## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the response must reach the home owner by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms, the pad rent stays the same, and the scheduled date of a subsequent increase does not change. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file — the only pad figures in this file are the UNVERIFIED site-copy ranges flagged in §5, exactly as this slug's own research documents them. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale (Z-240 for mobile homes, A-277 for modular in the program's sources; a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file** (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much is pad rent on a Langley mobile home under $500K?** No official source publishes a Langley pad-rent figure, average or range, so no figure is stated as fact here. This page's "$600–$900 per month" FAQ range and the pad-fees page's "$650–$950" range are both UNVERIFIED site copy and they conflict with each other (see §5). The figure that matters is the specific park's current rent in its written Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement. https://jaredgibbons.ca/cheap-mobile-homes-langley-under-500k.html (research file §7)
- **Q: Do I own the land under a park home in this price band?** No, in a pad-rental park. **You own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad** — the park owns the land, and your right to the site is the assigned pad tenancy under the MHPTA. Pioneer Park is the exception in form, not in land ownership: there you buy the home plus co-op shares, not land. (Section 2.)
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only; the proportional amount is park-specific, so ask the park for its last calculation). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does the park raise my pad rent when I buy the home?** No. On assignment under MHPTA s.28 the pad rent stays the same and the scheduled date of a subsequent increase does not change; assignment requires the landlord's prior written consent (or deemed consent, or an order of the director) and the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Q: Does the park have to approve me?** Yes — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent, requested on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no consent fee. At Pioneer Park the approval is the co-op's acceptance of the buyer as a member — a different gate, set by the co-op. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Which parks in this feed are actually 55+?** On the verified roster: the Brookswood pad-rental parks (Cedar Creek Estates, Fernridge Park, Pineridge) and the Pioneer Park co-op in Aldergrove. Langley Grove Estates and Aldergrove Mobile Home Park state no age restrictions in their listings, and Forest Green Estates' age rule is UNCONFIRMED. Every other card's "55+" phrase is listing copy only. (Section 2.)
- **Q: Can a park refuse me because of age?** In a 55+ park, yes on assignment: the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Regulation recognises a park where every site is reserved for a tenant who has reached 55 and allows the landlord to refuse an assignment where the proposed purchaser does not meet the park's age requirement. The strata exemptions do not automatically apply — ask the park for its rule in writing. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Q: What is the monthly cost stack besides the purchase price?** In structure, not figures: pad rent under the regulated formula, plus home insurance, plus property tax on the home's assessed value excluding the land in a park, plus utilities as the specific park's agreement allocates them. No dollar figure for any element is stated here, because no official source publishes a Langley figure for any of them. (Research file §5.)
- **Q: How is the purchase registered, and why does the CSA label matter?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal; a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold or redeveloped?** For a conversion of all or a significant part of the park, the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Are there market statistics for Langley mobile homes under $500K?** No — SnapStats has no manufactured-home category, so no manufactured-home sale counts, medians or days-on-market are published for Langley. The live page's investment/appreciation FAQ claims ("steady appreciation," "excellent affordable entry point") are site editorial, not sourced market facts, and are not adopted here.
