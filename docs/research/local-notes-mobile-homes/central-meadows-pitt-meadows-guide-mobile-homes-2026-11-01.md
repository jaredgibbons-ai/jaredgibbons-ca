## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (carried in full as the standing block; its application to the Meadow Highlands co-op form is UNCONFIRMED — see §2).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file, and no pad figure is documented in this guide slug's own research. For Meadow Highlands, the equivalent figure is the co-op's monthly Pad Assessment — set by the co-op, not by this formula. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; the 15-day dispute window and the additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the M1 files state them. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. How any closure framework interacts with a co-operative's own landholding at Meadow Highlands: UNCONFIRMED — a lawyer question on the co-op's documents. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. How the Registry step interacts with a co-op share transfer is a lawyer question on the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact. The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure — including a co-op share transfer — before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata; none is verified in Central Meadows).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning / OCP — Pitt Meadows frame.** Zoning Bylaw No. 2505, 2011 governs: houses sit under RS Large Lot Residential, R-3, R-4 and R-6 Urban Residential zones (R-3 up to 3 units per lot, R-4 up to 4 units, R-6 up to five units, or six where at least one unit meets the adaptable housing requirements in the BC Building Code on lots close to frequent transit; the 1,215 sq m minimum lot area in those zones is a subdivision minimum only), and RM-1 Multi-Family Residential 1 (townhouse) minimum lot area 0.4 ha. Manufactured-home ground specifically: the Residential Mobile Home (RMH) zone created by Zoning Amendment Bylaw 2991, 2024 for Meadow Highlands (§2 cross-reference). OCP: "I See Pitt Meadows 2040," Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 2864, 2020, adopted September 27, 2022 — the Municipal Town Centre designation covers this area. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/2025-04/Zoning Bylaw No. 2505, 2011 - Consolidated Version_0.pdf and https://www.pittmeadows.ca/ocp
- **Suites / SSMUH.** One secondary suite per principal dwelling unit, 33 to 90 sq m, inside the same building where the zone permits it, OR one garden suite per lot in the zones listed in the City's garden suite guide — a lot cannot have both; under provincial SSMUH rules applied citywide, lots 280 sq m or less allow up to 3 units, lots over 280 sq m up to 4 units, and lots over 280 sq m within 400 m of a frequent transit bus stop up to 6 units (SSMUH Zoning Amendment Bylaw No. 2986, adopted June 25, 2024). **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/docs/homes-development/information_sheet_-_planning_-_garden_suitesv4.pdf
- **Short-term rentals PROHIBITED (city layer).** Short-term rental accommodation is not a permitted use in Pitt Meadows — the City's zoning bylaw amendments staff report states the use is not permitted in the Zoning Bylaw. Price no Central Meadows purchase — co-op home, house or suite — on STR income. https://pub-pittmeadows.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=17699
- **Floodplain — ~95%+ governs.** The City's 2020 Business Plan states over 95% of the Pitt Meadows land base lies within the floodplain for the Fraser River and, to a smaller degree, the Pitt River — **this is the current figure; the guide page's "over 85%" Financial Plan figure is superseded and must not be used (see §5)**. The city is almost entirely protected by 60 km of dikes, plus ditches, pump stations and flood boxes; Central Meadows is in drainage Area 3 (Kennedy, including McKechnie) of the Pitt Meadows / Maple Ridge Diking District. The City also states 78% of Pitt Meadows land falls within the Agricultural Land Reserve; ALR status for a specific Town Centre parcel is a per-address check — UNVERIFIED at parcel level here. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/2022-03/pitt-meadows_business-plan_2020-web-full02.pdf and https://www.pittmeadows.ca/node/163
- **Servicing.** The City servicing-bylaw number is UNCONFIRMED in this program — no servicing standard is asserted for any Central Meadows parcel from a number; confirm subdivision and servicing requirements with the City for the specific lot. (Plain mobile file §3.)
- **GST on a manufactured home — only at cited-CRA level.** GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level (CRA GI-003 general level); anything finer is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established, including for a co-op share structure. (Standing brief.)

GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. A resale co-op/park home is not new housing for this rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What mobile-home buying actually exists in Central Meadows?** One community: Meadow Highlands Mobile Home Co-operative — a co-operative manufactured-home park of 162 homes where members hold shares in the co-op that collectively owns the land, documented at home in the plain Central Meadows mobile file. There is no verified pad-rental park in Central Meadows. (§2; https://meadowhighlands.ca/)
- **Q: In the co-op, do I own the land?** Not individually. The property — all 162 pads, roads, hall, pool, office, RV parking and infrastructure — is owned collectively by the 162 members; you purchase shares in the co-op and pay a monthly Pad Assessment based on your pad's size. That is neither a fee-simple lot nor a rented pad. https://meadowhighlands.ca/
- **Q: Is Meadow Highlands a 55+ park?** No — affirmatively not. No source states any age restriction, and the co-op maintains a kids' play area. It is a family/all-age co-op park. Do not confuse it with the Meadowlands Housing Co-operative on Advent Road, which IS 55+ — but is built row-homes, not a manufactured-home park. https://meadowhighlands.ca/ (plain mobile file §4.)
- **Q: Does the co-op have to approve me as a buyer?** Yes — on the co-op's own terms: once a purchase is tentatively agreed, the purchaser must be approved for membership by meeting with the Board of Directors before closing, and that meeting is a condition that must be included in all purchase agreements. That is a different process from a rental park's RTB Form 10 assignment consent. https://meadowhighlands.ca/
- **Q: The guide feed shows a manufactured-style home on Advent Road — is that a park home?** No park is named for it in any source checked. The card is feed listing text for an individual home in the Advent Road pocket (Central Meadows on the board classification); it is not Meadow Highlands and it is not a park listing on any source in this program. Its tenure is a title-and-documents question for that listing — UNVERIFIED here. (§2; research file §5.)
- **Q: In a normal BC pad-rental park, what would I own?** The home only. **You own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad** — the park owns the land, and your right to the site is the assigned pad tenancy under the MHPTA. Plainly, a home on a rented pad is not land ownership. Central Meadows has no verified park of that form. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up (in a rental park elsewhere)?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). That RTB cap governs pad tenancies; a co-op's Pad Assessment is set by the co-op. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How is a manufactured-home purchase registered, and why does the CSA label matter?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. How the Registry step interacts with a co-op share transfer is a lawyer question on the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: Can I short-term rent a Central Meadows home?** No. Short-term rental accommodation is not a permitted use in Pitt Meadows at all. https://pub-pittmeadows.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=17699
- **Q: Which schools serve a Meadow Highlands address?** Catchments are address-based in SD42 and no locator run for a park address is on record, so the park's catchment is UNVERIFIED — run the exact address. The settled locator chain in the area research is single-address only: 12007 Harris Road returns Pitt Meadows Elementary → Pitt Meadows Secondary. https://www.sd42.ca/school-locator/
