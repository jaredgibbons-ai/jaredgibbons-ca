## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. How any closure framework interacts with a co-operative's own landholding at a specific park: UNCONFIRMED — a lawyer question on the co-op's documents. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file.** The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure — including a co-op share transfer — before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. Anchor checks: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** — a $78,000 figure for $3.5M exists elsewhere in the wider program; it is an OPEN reconciliation item: follow $93,000 and flag the conflict wherever met. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata; none is verified in Pitt Meadows).** Form B diligence (the lot's strata fee, amounts owed, approved levies, CRF balance, parking/storage and insurance summary; the strata must provide the Form B within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Zoning / OCP — Pitt Meadows frame.** Zoning Bylaw No. 2505, 2011 governs: RS Large Lot Residential carries a minimum lot area of 0.2 ha; the R-3, R-4 and R-6 Urban Residential zones carry a minimum lot area for subdivision of 1,215 sq m and a minimum lot width for subdivision of 12 m, allowing up to three, four and six dwelling units (in R-6 the maximum is five units, or six where at least one unit meets the adaptable housing requirements in the BC Building Code) — and the 1,215 sq m figure is a subdivision minimum only, per the City's own SSMUH implementation update, not a development minimum on existing lots; RM-1 Multi-Family Residential 1 sets a minimum lot area of 0.4 ha for townhouse developments; A-1 General Agricultural sets a minimum lot area for subdivision of 8 ha and A-2 Large Lot Agricultural 16 ha. Manufactured-home ground specifically: the Residential Mobile Home (RMH) zone created by Zoning Amendment Bylaw 2991, 2024 for Meadow Highlands (§2). OCP: "I See Pitt Meadows 2040," Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 2864, 2020, adopted September 27, 2022; the North Lougheed Area Plan was adopted by OCP Amendment Bylaw No. 3060, 2026. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/2025-04/Zoning%20Bylaw%20No.%202505,%202011%20-%20Consolidated%20Version_0.pdf and https://www.pittmeadows.ca/homes-development/zoning-land-use/official-community-plan-0 (research file above)

Do not confuse it with the Meadowlands Housing Co-operative on the same side of Central Meadows, which IS 55+ — but is built row-homes, not a manufactured-home park. https://www.jagsidhu.com/listing/r3095727-19630-pinyon-lane-77-pitt-meadows-bc-v3y-1z3/brochure and https://www.chf.bc.ca/find-a-co-op/
- **Q: In a co-op park, do I own the land?** Not individually. The sibling record states members own shares and equity in the land, with no lease — you hold a co-op membership interest under the co-op's documents, not a fee-simple lot and not a rented pad. Read the co-op's membership, occupancy and share-transfer documents before comparing its price to either a pad-rental home or an own-land home. (~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/pitt-meadows-homes-for-sale-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md)
- **Q: In a normal BC pad-rental park, what would I own?** The home only. **You own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad** — the park owns the land, and your right to the site is the assigned pad tenancy under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act. Plainly, a home on a rented pad is not land ownership. Pitt Meadows has no verified park of that form; the question matters when cross-shopping Maple Ridge's parks. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up (in a rental park)?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only; the proportional amount is the park's own levy/regulated-utility change divided by its site count). For a co-op park, the equivalent question is the co-op's housing-charge history — ask the co-op, not the RTB table. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does a park have to approve me as a buyer?** In a rental park, yes — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent, requested on RTB Form 10, with a response due by the 10th day and silence deemed consent; no fee may be charged for consent. In a co-op, the approval is the co-op's membership process under its own rules — a different document set, confirmed with the co-op. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: How is a manufactured-home purchase registered, and why does the CSA label matter?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. A missing label is a red flag for financing and resale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: Does the floodplain change anything for a park home here?** Yes — it is a standard subject in this city, not an exception: the City's floodplain bylaw sets the Flood Construction Level at the designated flood level plus 0.6 m freeboard, and for a manufactured home the ground level or top of a pad must be no lower than that elevation. Taxes also carry a separate drainage levy ($0.1082 per $1,000, 2026). Check the FCL, the covenant on title and insurance early. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/2024-09/Floodplain%20Designation%20and%20Construction%20Control%20Bylaw%20No.%202384,%202008%20-%20Consolidated.pdf
- **Q: Can I short-term rent a Pitt Meadows home or suite?** No. Short-term rental accommodation is not a permitted use in Pitt Meadows at all — the prohibition is a city use rule, separate from any strata bylaw position, and it applies to park homes, houses and garden suites alike. https://pub-pittmeadows.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=17699
- **Q: Which schools serve a Meadow Highlands address?** Catchments are address-based in SD42 and no locator run for a park address is on record, so the park's catchment is UNVERIFIED — run the exact address. The one settled locator chain in the city research is single-address only: 11941 Harris Road returns Pitt Meadows Elementary → Pitt Meadows Secondary. https://www.sd42.ca/school-locator/ (research file above)
