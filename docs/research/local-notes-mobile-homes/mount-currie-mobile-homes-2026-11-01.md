## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Community and governance: Mount Currie is the main community of the Líl̓wat Nation (Ucwalmícwts-speaking; formerly styled the Mount Currie Indian Band). The Nation states the community is home to approximately 1,495 of its more than 2,275 citizens, and is named for Mount Currie, the 2,591 m mountain at the centre of Líl̓wat Traditional Territory. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/ (research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/mount-currie.md)
- Land status: the community sits on Líl̓wat reserve lands — 10 reserves totalling about 2,930 ha within a Traditional Territory of approximately 780,000 hectares. Reserve lands are federal lands set aside for the Nation; they are not a municipality. The surrounding unincorporated Pemberton Valley is SLRD Electoral Area C. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/ and https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf
- Neighbourhoods (per the Nation's Community Housing Plan): Mount Currie Village (IR 1 and IR 10), the centre; Xet̓ólacw Village (IR 6), the largest residential neighbourhood; Lillooet Lake Road (IR 3 and IR 8); and Kwetsa7 (IR 2). https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf
- Location: approximately 160 km north of Vancouver and approximately 35 km north of Whistler via Highway 99; the Village of Pemberton is a few kilometres west (exact road distance on an official source: UNVERIFIED). https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- Census: Statistics Canada treats "Mount Currie" as an Indian reserve census subdivision (code 5931843) within the SLRD; the 2021 Census Indigenous Population Profile counts 1,240 people in private households, median age 33.2. The Paragon sub-area name/code for Mount Currie could not be confirmed on a public official source: UNVERIFIED. https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/dp-pd/ipp-ppa/details/page.cfm?Lang=E&SearchText=&DGUID=2021A00055931843&GENDER=1&AGE=1&RESIDENCE=1&HP=0&HH=0
- Schools: Xet̓ólacw Community School is the Nation's own school in the community (grade range/enrolment on an official source: UNVERIFIED). Under the Local Education Agreement with School District 48 (Sea to Sky), Líl̓wat students also attend Signal Hill Elementary and Pemberton Secondary School in Pemberton; SD48's Bus 5 route runs "Owl Ridge, Reid Rd & Mt. Currie" to those schools. Ts̓zil Learning Centre, 125 Lillooet Lake Road, is the Nation's learning centre in partnership with Capilano University. https://lilwat.ca/departments/tszil-language-and-culture/advanced-education/ and https://22.files.edl.io/3fff/09/03/25/214527-07fe5ac2-2ab8-4eb4-9bfd-69ad562e6006.pdf and https://www.capilanou.ca/programs--courses/program-profiles/lilwat-nation-language--culture-certificate/
- Transit: BC Transit's Pemberton Valley Transit is cost-shared between BC Transit, the Village of Pemberton, the SLRD and the Líl̓wat Nation; local service connects Xit'olacw and Mt Currie to Pemberton, and a commuter service runs Pemberton–Whistler. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/889/862/pem_rg_sept2020.pdf

## 2. THE PARKS AND THE HOMES

**HONEST ZERO at the top: no manufactured-home park of any tenure form is on this ground, and there is no open-market manufactured-home stock here at all. That is the whole roster — none. What exists is Nation-administered housing on reserve land, documented below as its own system, never forced into a market tenure category.**

### The system that actually exists — Nation-administered housing (not a park, not market stock)

- **Tenure:** Nation-administered housing on reserve land — band housing, and member home ownership through the Nation's process with a Band Council Resolution and a Ministerial Loan Guarantee. This is neither freehold nor strata, and homes do not come to the open market for general buyers. There is **no bare-land market on reserve land**: serviced lots are allocated through the Lands Department land encumbrance check process (standard lot dimensions on an official source: UNVERIFIED). https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **The Nation's Five Steps to Home Ownership on Reserve** (documented per the Nation's own material): (1) meet the Housing Manager; (2) a land encumbrance check from the Lands Department confirming a serviced lot is available and registered to the person; (3) a Band Council Resolution (the Band Council acts as guarantor on the loan); (4) possible housing subsidies from Indigenous Services Canada and a Ministerial Loan Guarantee; (5) bank financing. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Manufactured/modular homes inside that system:** the Nation's home ownership material prices modular/pre-manufactured homes built to CSA Z-240 at about $160–$180 per square foot installed and hooked up (Nation guidance figures, carried as the Nation states them), noting modulars for Mount Currie must meet zone 6 construction standards and a roof snow load rating of 136 lb per square foot; new construction of a modest home is put at about $250–$300 per square foot including water, sewer and hydro connections. These are Nation guidance figures for its own process, not market prices. The Nation is building new housing in both the Mount Currie and Xet̓ólacw communities, with a goal of 12 units per year (Community Housing Plan 2018–2033). https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf and https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **Why the standard park block does not govern here.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, the Manufactured Home Registry and provincial property transfer tax are provincial systems for off-reserve tenure. On this ground, the research states the position plainly: standard municipal and regional district property taxes, and provincial property transfer tax, do not apply to reserve land the way they apply to fee-simple land off reserve; the Nation's own taxation laws or service fees for a specific lot are UNVERIFIED. Standard mortgage and foreclosure mechanics likewise do not apply the way they do off reserve, because the Band Council acts as guarantor and a Ministerial Loan Guarantee backs the loan. The full settled MHPTA block is carried below because it is this program's standing law for pad purchases — **it is carried as the off-reserve comparison frame only and is NOT asserted for any Mount Currie home.** https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf (research file §4.)
- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (off-reserve frame).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy (MHPTA s.28).** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. The decision-maker is the "director," never an "arbitrator." On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; the 15-day dispute window and the additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the program's M1 files state them. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry (off-reserve frame).** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. On this ground, the Nation's material works in CSA terms of its own — modular/pre-manufactured homes built to CSA Z-240 (§2) — and no MHR registration claim is made for an on-reserve home. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact; and for reserve land specifically, the research position (above) is that provincial PTT does not apply the way it applies off reserve, with the Nation's own laws/fees UNVERIFIED. The Province states expressly that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — an off-reserve point, relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land elsewhere, not to any Mount Currie home. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — off-reserve comparison frame only (e.g., a Pemberton purchase).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = **$12,000** (1% × $200,000 = $2,000, plus 2% × $500,000 = $10,000). Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000; $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (off-reserve frame only; no strata exists on this ground).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning / land use on this ground.** There is no municipal zoning bylaw for the reserve lands — land allocation runs through the Nation's Lands Department and Band Council processes. Whether the Nation has enacted a formal land code under the First Nations Land Management Act is UNVERIFIED. A community-specific floodplain map or flood construction rule for Mount Currie on an official source is UNVERIFIED; reserve lands are not in the provincial Agricultural Land Reserve (carried in the research as UNVERIFIED pending an official citation — any parcel question goes to the Nation's Lands Department). **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies**, and no SSMUH claim is made for reserve land. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **GST on a manufactured home — only at cited-CRA level (GI-003 general level), off-reserve frame.** GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer is UNCONFIRMED. Whether any GST program can attach to an on-reserve purchase by a citizen through the Nation's process is a per-transaction question for the Nation and the lender: UNVERIFIED, and none is asserted for Mount Currie.
- **First-home programs (off-reserve frame only).** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate is for new HOUSING (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. None of these is asserted for an on-reserve purchase (see the GST line above); the Nation's own route is the ISC subsidies and Ministerial Loan Guarantee inside the Five Steps. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Financing.** On reserve, financing runs through the Five Steps (Band Council as guarantor, Ministerial Loan Guarantee) — standard lender/mortgage-insurer rules for manufactured homes off reserve (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in this program: UNVERIFIED. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Foreclosure / court-ordered sale.** Standard BC court-ordered sale mechanics do not apply on reserve land the way they do off reserve (research §6, on the guarantor/guarantee structure). No registry or court-sale treatment is asserted for this ground: UNCONFIRMED / not applicable as documented.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can anyone buy a house in Mount Currie?** No. Mount Currie is the main community of the Líl̓wat Nation on reserve land; housing is allocated and financed through the Nation's own process for its citizens, starting with the Nation's Housing Manager and Lands Department — there is no fee-simple open market. Inquiries go to the Líl̓wat Nation (info@lilwat.ca, 604-894-6115, as the Nation publishes them). https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf and https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- **Q: Is there a mobile home park in Mount Currie?** No — none of any tenure form. Modular/manufactured homes exist inside the Nation's housing system (CSA Z-240, §2), placed on lots allocated through the Lands Department, not in a park and not for open-market purchase. (§2 above.)
- **Q: What about Mt. Currie View Village?** It is a Pemberton pad-rental community despite its name — a name trap, never Mount Currie stock. (§2 above.)
- **Q: How does home ownership work for Líl̓wat citizens?** Through the Nation's Five Steps: Housing Manager meeting, Lands Department land encumbrance check, Band Council Resolution (Council as guarantor), ISC subsidy and Ministerial Loan Guarantee checks, then bank financing. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Q: Do I own the land under a Mount Currie home?** No buyer owns reserve land in fee simple — land is reserve land administered by the Nation, allocated through its processes. There is no bare-land market on reserve land. (§§1–2 above.)
- **Q: Which schools serve Mount Currie?** Xet̓ólacw Community School in the community, and — under the Local Education Agreement with SD48 — Signal Hill Elementary and Pemberton Secondary School in Pemberton, with district bus routes serving Mount Currie. https://lilwat.ca/departments/tszil-language-and-culture/advanced-education/
- **Q: Is there bus service to Pemberton or Whistler?** Yes — Pemberton Valley Transit's local service connects Xit'olacw and Mt Currie to Pemberton, and a commuter service runs Pemberton–Whistler; the service is cost-shared by BC Transit, the Village of Pemberton, the SLRD and the Líl̓wat Nation. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/889/862/pem_rg_sept2020.pdf
- **Q: What are homes worth in Mount Currie?** UNVERIFIED — neither SnapStats report covers the Pemberton area, and MLS activity is not a meaningful measure of this community's Nation-administered housing. (§1 above.)
