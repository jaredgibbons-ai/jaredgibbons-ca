## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (no pad park is verified in this sub-area; the block is carried because buyers compare Braeside against pad parks elsewhere in Chilliwack).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date, may dispute a closure notice within the 15-day dispute window, and additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts apply only as the Act and the cited provincial pages state them. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. This applies to a Braeside home **if** that home is a manufactured home — check the individual home's registry record. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file** (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers — the Braeside form).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. Anchor checks: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (the Braeside form).** Form B diligence (the lot's strata fee, amounts owed, approved levies, CRF balance, parking/storage and insurance summary; the strata must provide the Form B within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. **For Braeside this block is the deciding test for its listing-stated 55+ label: only a registered 55+ bylaw read on the plan / Form B makes it real.** https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Zoning / OCP — Chilliwack frame.** OCP: Chilliwack 2050, Bylaw 2025, No. 5500. Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000: the hillside residential zones include HR (Hillside Residential) — minimum lot area 0.2 ha where served by a community water system and 0.4 ha for all other uses, minimum lot width 25 m — and SHR (Suburban Hillside Residential), whose minimum lot area is set by the zone's slope table, starting at 360 m² for the lowest slope band and rising to 990 m² for the highest. Which zone applies to a specific Little Mountain lot is per-parcel: UNVERIFIED. There is no province-wide park zoning — each municipality zones manufactured home parks in its own bylaw; no Chilliwack manufactured-home-park zone section for any Little Mountain address was opened in this program. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf (research file §4)
- **Hillside rules.** Development Permit Area 2 (Hillside and Upland Areas) requires a Geotechnical Report from a qualified professional with each application; Development Permit Area 5 (Hillside Development) applies to all lands in the City with slopes of 20% or more, and within a designated DPA a Development Permit is required before subdivision, construction, addition or alteration of a building, or alteration of land, except where exemptions apply. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2881

A bare-land strata purchase (Braeside) has no park-owner consent step — it has strata Form B diligence instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf

A missing label is a red flag for financing and resale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
