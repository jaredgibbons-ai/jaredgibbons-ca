## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; manufactured home site tenancies are covered by the MHPTA and landlords and tenants must have a written tenancy agreement for the site. A home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes and http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** Assignment of a manufactured home site tenancy agreement permanently transfers the pad tenancy to the buyer when the home is sold and remains in the park. Under s. 28 of the MHPTA a tenant may assign only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), a director's order, or where the tenancy agreement authorises it; before asking, the seller must give the proposed buyer written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. Note on wording, stated precisely: this slug's research quoted the older provincial information sheet's "an arbitrator's order" phrasing — the settled program position governs: the decision-maker is the **"director", never "arbitrator"**, and the research sheet's wording is not carried. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. A park's 55+ rule has its own legal footing: the MHPTA assignment rules expressly recognise a park where every pad is reserved for a tenant who has reached 55 (or 2+ tenants, at least one of whom has reached 55), as set out in s. 10(2)(b)(i) of the Human Rights Code — the park owner can refuse an assignment where no purchaser meets that age requirement. That is how the Surrey roster parks' 55+ rules operate: through park rules and assignment consent, not through a strata bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo108/loo108/161_2000_pit
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Landlords in manufactured home parks can only increase rent with 3 full months' notice, can increase rent every 12 months (once every 12 months), and the increase cannot exceed the yearly rent increase limit — the inflation rate (12-month average change in the BC all-items CPI) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park); a landlord may also apply to the Residential Tenancy Branch for an additional increase for expenses or capital expenditures. Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — provincial block plus the Surrey policy layer.** Provincially: a landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date, and may dispute within the 15-day dispute window; additional compensation in the amount of the assessed value of the home (less the $20,000) and the further $5,000 / 12-months'-pad-rent amounts apply only as the provincial pages state them. Surrey layer: the City's Manufactured Home Park Policy brochure states the property owner must give 12 months' notice of a final moving date using a Notice to End Tenancy, which can only be given after the owner receives all their permits, and the City policy requires an affordable housing program for residents when a park is redeveloped; Surrey's Tenant Protection Bylaw, 2025, No. 21788 does NOT apply to property governed by the MHPTA. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited sources. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_reg_21788.pdf
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** In BC, the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered; the Manufactured Home Registry maintains a central register of manufactured home ownership details, controls the movement of manufactured homes in BC, and improves the security of lenders financing purchases. The buyer and seller file the original signed and witnessed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership forms through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary); the Province directs buyers to make sure the CSA certification label is attached to the home at the point of sale. A home may not be sold unless it has the original legible CSA decal attached; if the label is removed or lost, the home must be re-certified — Technical Safety BC has a licensed electrical contractor inspect the wiring, and the electrical approval label prepared can be used in place of a CSA label to list the home for sale. A permit to move a manufactured home is issued by the Registry, a tax certificate for transport purposes is required (with exceptions including a move to a different pad within the same park), and the transport permit expires 30 days after issue. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value will not include the value of the land if the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner — which is why Westwood Estates listings can fold a "land portion of property taxes" into the pad rent. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. City of Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file** (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers, including a Sea Ridge Bays lot).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Do all Surrey "mobile homes" sit on rented pads?** No. This feed holds both tenures: park homes where the buyer owns the home and rents the site under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement governed by the MHPTA, and strata communities (Sea Ridge Bays' form) where the buyer owns the land and pays a strata or community fee instead of pad rent — plus manufactured homes on own fee-simple lots. Establish the tenure before anything else: the paperwork, financing and monthly costs are different for each. https://jaredgibbons.ca/surrey-mobile-homes.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf

- **Q: Can the park raise my pad rent when I buy?** No — on assignment of the site tenancy the pad rental for the new tenant stays the same, and the scheduled date for a subsequent rent increase does not change; increases otherwise require 3 full months' notice, at most once every 12 months, within the yearly limit (2.3% + proportional amount for 2026; 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027, percentages only) plus the proportional levy/utility amount, with further increases only through a Residential Tenancy Branch order. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Q: What are the carrying costs on top of the purchase?** Pad rent is the big one in a park, and it is park-specific: Westwood Court's listings state the pad fee includes water, garbage and management; Westwood Estates' listings state pad rent including the land portion of property taxes, garbage pickup, sewer and water — but the amounts stated across Westwood Estates listings conflict by home and date, and Newton MHP's current pad rent is UNCONFIRMED, so **no pad figure is published here as a rate for any park**. - **Q: Does the park have to approve me as a buyer?** Yes, in a park — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent; the seller must give you the park rules and tenancy agreement terms before asking. In a bare-land strata (Sea Ridge Bays) there is no park owner to approve you; the bylaws bind you from completion and diligence runs through the Form B. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: What if the home has lost its CSA label?** A manufactured home may not be sold unless it has the original and legible CSA decal attached (Z-240 for a mobile home, A-277 for a modular). If the label is removed or lost, the home must be re-certified: Technical Safety BC arranges a licensed electrical contractor's inspection, and the electrical approval label prepared can be used in place of a CSA label — a current Westwood Estates listing advertises exactly that history ("Electrical re-certification, silver label"). Check the label before anything else. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: How is the purchase registered?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the purchase is only effective once registered, through a lawyer or notary filing the Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership. Sellers: have the CSA label (or the Technical Safety BC approval label), the registry registration number and the tenancy agreement and park rules ready — those are the documents the buyer's lawyer, lender and the park's consent process will ask for first. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: Are pets allowed?** Park by park, and the roster parks allow some on listing-stated rules: Westwood Court — pets welcome; Westwood Estates — large dogs with park approval, no vicious breeds; Newton MHP — one pet allowed, subject to park rules (listing-stated in its home file); Sea Ridge Bays — two dogs with no size restrictions (listing-stated in its home file). Search the City's development applications for the park's address before buying. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
