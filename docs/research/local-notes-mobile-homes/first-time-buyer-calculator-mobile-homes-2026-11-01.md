## 2. THE PARKS AND THE HOMES

**TOPIC FILE — NO ROSTER, stated at the top: this file documents no park, counts no park and verifies no manufactured home. A first-time buyer pricing a manufactured home into this calculator meets three different tenure forms, and the tool's land-based arithmetic fits only one of them cleanly — which is the whole mobile-homes point of this file.**

### The three forms a first-time buyer might price into the tool

- **A pad-rental park home.** Plainly: **a home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home (a chattel) and rents the pad.** The calculator's PTT engine is built on interests registered at the Land Title Office (see §3); the PTT treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad is UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule in this program, so **a pad-home buyer must NOT assume the tool's PTT output — exemption included — describes their purchase** until a lawyer or notary confirms the treatment for the exact structure. The verified pad-rental stock lives in its home grounds (for example Newton Mobile Home Park, 6571 King George Blvd, in the West Newton mobile home file; Cottonwood Village, 7610 Evans Rd, in the West Chilliwack GUIDE mobile home file) — cross-referenced here only, never counted. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and (~/workspace/local-notes/mobile-homes/west-newton-guide-mobile-homes-2026-11-01.md; ~/workspace/local-notes/mobile-homes/west-chilliwack-guide-mobile-homes-2026-11-01.md)
- **A manufactured home on the buyer's own land.** This is the form the tool fits: a land interest registered at the Land Title Office, priced by the general PTT tiers, and — where the home is NEW — expressly inside the newly built home exemption, which includes "a manufactured home placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" that has not been occupied since placement. A person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may also be eligible for a refund of the tax paid. Own-land manufactured stock is documented as that form in the lots-and-land and acreages home files, never as park stock. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **A bare-land strata manufactured-home community.** The buyer owns the home AND the strata lot — a Land Title Office registration, so the tool's PTT frame applies as it does to any strata purchase, with the strata block in §3 alongside. Documented once per community in its home file (for example Baker Trail Village in the chilliwack-mobile-homes lots-and-land home; Sea Ridge Bays in the south-surrey-mobile-homes homes) — cross-referenced here only. (~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/chilliwack-mobile-homes-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md)
- The exemption thresholds shape which stock the tool's savings are largest on: the first-time program's full exemption runs to a fair market value of $835,000, while the newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 — a first-time buyer of a newly built home can be inside one program's range and outside the other's, so the tool must test both; both BC exemptions also set 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) as the size line, with a partial exemption possible above it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

### The calculator's own rules — as the official pages state them

- **PTT — the base calculation.** When a person purchases or gains an interest in property registered at the Land Title Office, the person or their legal professional must file a property transfer tax return and pay property transfer tax unless an exemption applies; PTT is based on the fair market value of the property (land and improvements) on the day it is registered, and it is not annual property tax. General rates as currently published: **1%** of fair market value up to and including $200,000; **2%** of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; **3%** of the portion greater than $2,000,000; plus a **further 2%** on the residential property value greater than $3,000,000 (only the residential portion on a mixed-class property). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Worked PTT examples — inputs stated, tier math shown.** Gross PTT, fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $300,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**; $700,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**; $800,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $600,000 ($12,000) = **$14,000**; $900,000 → **$16,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $1,800,000 → **$34,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000 (the further 2% on the $500,000 over $3,000,000) = **$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier** — a $78,000 figure carried in a townhomes sibling elsewhere in the program is an OPEN reconciliation item: follow $93,000 and flag the conflict wherever met. **First-time-buyer net example:** at an $800,000 fair market value meeting every full-exemption condition, the buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000, so the net PTT is the tax on the remaining $300,000 portion as the program anchors state it: **$6,000** (the $800K-cap anchor). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **BC first time home buyers' program (PTT exemption).** The program reduces or eliminates PTT on a first home; where only some purchasers qualify, only the qualifying purchasers' percentage of interest is eligible, and foreign entities and taxable trustees are not eligible. Buyer conditions: Canadian citizen or permanent resident; lived in BC for at least a year immediately before registration, or filed at least 2 income tax returns as a BC resident in the last 6 taxation years before registration; never owned a registered interest in a property that was the buyer's principal residence anywhere in the world; never received a first time home buyers' exemption or refund. Full-exemption property conditions: used only as the buyer's principal residence; fair market value **$835,000 or less** (effective April 1, 2024); **0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) or smaller**; only residential improvements — and where all conditions are met, the purchaser is **exempt from PTT on the first $500,000** of the purchase price. A partial exemption may apply where fair market value is more than $835,000 but less than $860,000, the property is larger than 0.5 hectares, and/or there is another building besides the principal residence. Keeping it: move in within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy as principal residence to the first anniversary of registration; the legal professional applies the exemption in the PTT return, and a buyer who qualified but did not apply at registration may apply for a refund from the first anniversary to 18 months after registration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **BC newly built home exemption (PTT).** Effective April 1, 2024, the full-exemption fair market value threshold is **$1,100,000**, with a partial exemption phasing out over the $50,000 range above it and complete elimination at **$1,150,000**. A newly built home includes a house constructed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since construction, a unit in a newly built condo building not occupied since construction, **a manufactured home placed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since placement**, and a building converted from non-residential to residential use not occupied as a residence since the conversion began. The buyer must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, the property in BC, used only as the buyer's principal residence, 0.5 hectares or smaller; occupancy mirrors the first-time program (move in within 92 days, occupy for the remainder of the first year). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Down payment and mortgage loan insurance (CMHC) — as THIS file's research verified from CMHC itself.** Mortgage loan insurance is required in Canada when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the purchase price; the insurance protects the lender rather than the buyer and allows qualification with as little as 5% down. Minimum down payment: homes $500,000 or less — 5%; homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999 — 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more — 20%, and mortgage loan insurance is not available. **Premium rates range from 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount**, based on the size of the down payment and the loan amount, and the premium can be paid in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Home owner grant (annual, not a purchase tax).** The grant reduces property taxes paid each year on a principal residence — an annual cost item met after purchase, not a closing cost, and a calculator should not net it against purchase taxes. As published (page last updated September 22, 2026): effective January 1, 2027, the regular grant amount is $570; the grant threshold for 2026 is $2,075,000 for a full grant, reduced by $5 for each $1,000 of assessed value over $2,075,000, and $0 if the residential or partitioned value is over $2,189,000; the claimant must be the registered owner, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, live in BC and occupy the residence as a principal residence, must pay at least $350 in property taxes before receiving the regular grant, and must apply each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **Additional PTT.** A foreign national, foreign corporation or taxable trustee must also pay additional property transfer tax on the fair market value of the residential portion of a property within a specified area of BC; the rate is published on the Province's separate additional-PTT page, which was not re-read for this file, so **the rate is UNVERIFIED here**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Taxes deliberately out of this tool's scope.** BC's speculation and vacancy tax (annual, on how owners use residential properties in designated areas) and the BC home flipping tax (on income from a short-held residential sale) are not purchase-time costs a first-time-buyer calculator computes, and the page title names only PTT, a GST rebate and the FHSA; whether the live tool mentions them is UNVERIFIED. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home

### The MHPTA block — what the calculator does not compute, and a park buyer still faces

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. **CORE TENURE LINE: a home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home (a chattel) and rents the pad.** No line in the calculator prices this tenure: the pad rent, the assignment consent and the park rules sit entirely outside its PTT/GST/FHSA arithmetic. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file; the pad-fee ranges carried in program page copy elsewhere ($650–$950 on the pad-fees page; $600–$900 in the cheap-mobile-homes FAQ) are UNVERIFIED site copy, are NOT documented in this slug's own research, and are never carried here as rates. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place BEFORE issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; the 15-day dispute window and the additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the program's M1 files state them. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the PTT treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file; pad-resale PTT is UNCONFIRMED — and BOTH flat page claims met in the program are UNVERIFIED**. The calculator's land-registration frame (above) is exactly why this matters: its PTT output presumes a Land Title Office registration. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **GST on a manufactured home — only as CRA sources support.** At the general level the program's CRA sources support: GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established. A NEW manufactured home placed on the buyer's own land is the case the federal rebates above are written for; a resale park home is not new housing. State the GST position in the contract.
- **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies** — it is a municipal lot-zoning framework, not a park-tenancy right, and it plays no part in this calculator's arithmetic.
- **Financing.** Lender and mortgage-insurer rules for manufactured homes (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in this program: UNVERIFIED — a first-time buyer financing a chattel purchase should confirm the lender's manufactured-home rules for the specific home and tenure before removing subjects, because the CMHC down-payment frame above presumes a mortgageable land interest.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much property transfer tax does a first-time buyer in BC actually save?** A qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price, provided the property's fair market value is $835,000 or less, it is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the buyer's principal residence; above $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. Worked at the anchor: an $800,000 purchase meeting every condition nets $6,000 of PTT against $14,000 gross. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: What are the general PTT rates the calculator starts from?** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% on the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% on the portion over $2,000,000 — plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000 where that applies. The exemption reduces the bill; it does not change the tiers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: I'm buying a manufactured home on a rented pad — does the calculator's PTT answer apply to me?** Not safely. **You would own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad — not the land** — and the PTT treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad is UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule in this program; the calculator's frame presumes a Land Title Office registration. Have a lawyer or notary confirm the tax and the Manufactured Home Registry handling for your exact purchase before you rely on any number the tool shows. (§3 above.)
- **Q: Is a newly built home treated differently from a resale home?** Yes, twice over. The newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 with a phase-out to $1,150,000 — and it expressly includes a manufactured home placed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since placement — while a new home may also attract GST, which the federal first-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate can offset by up to $50,000 for eligible buyers (thresholds flagged UNVERIFIED in this file's research, §3). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: How much can I put in a First Home Savings Account in the first year?** FHSA participation room in the first year you open the account is $8,000, and contributions are generally tax deductible — which is why the account rewards being opened before the purchase year, not at offer time. The $40,000 lifetime figure is the program standard but is flagged UNVERIFIED in this file's research because the CRA's detailed page could not be retrieved. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?** Yes — the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. The HBP withdrawal limit itself (program standard $60,000) is flagged UNVERIFIED in this file's research because the CRA's HBP page could not be retrieved. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Q: How much down payment do I actually need, and what does the insurance cost?** The floor is 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any portion above $500,000, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more, where insurance is unavailable. Under 20% down, CMHC mortgage insurance is required and its premium is published by CMHC as ranging from **0.6% to 4.5%** of the total mortgage amount, payable in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: Does the park have to approve me if my first home is in a park?** Yes — assignment of the pad tenancy needs the park owner's prior written consent on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent; and the seller must give you the written tenancy agreement and park rules before asking for that consent. None of that is in the calculator. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up after I buy?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Should I count the home owner grant as closing money?** No — it is an annual reduction of property tax on a principal residence ($570 regular grant as published, effective January 1, 2027), claimed each year after you own and occupy; a calculator should not net it against purchase taxes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
