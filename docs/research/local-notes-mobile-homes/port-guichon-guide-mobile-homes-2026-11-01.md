## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA) — the pad-tenancy block IN FULL.** The Act governs pad tenancies: it applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on in a manufactured home park; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. No verified pad-rental park exists on Port Guichon ground (§2), so this block is the frame a buyer needs if a pad-tenancy listing is ever marketed under this sub-area's name — and the test that exposes a mislabelled listing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **CORE DISCIPLINE — a home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership:** the buyer owns the home (a chattel) and rents the pad; the land stays the park owner's. There is no strata lot, no Form B, and no land title for the pad. Own-land stock is different in kind: a bare-land strata purchase carries Form B/fees/bylaws, and a co-op purchase carries shares/membership — neither park form is verified on this ground. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment on a sale — no new tenancy:** when a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is ASSIGNED to the purchaser, who becomes the park owner's tenant on the same terms from the assignment date. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park-owner consent — the 10-day rule:** assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on the approved form (RTB Form 10); the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Park rules:** park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY:** pad rent may be increased only once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped at the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) PLUS a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the Residential Tenancy Branch (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** NO pad-rent DOLLAR FIGURE is stated as fact anywhere in this file — no pad-rent figure for any park on this ground is documented in this ground's research file, and any live-page pad-rent range is UNVERIFIED site copy. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion — only as the Province states it:** a landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place BEFORE issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Anything finer for a specific park is UNCONFIRMED. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Age-restricted parks — the provincial general rule only:** in a designated 55-plus park, at least one tenant on the site must be 55 years of age or older. No specific park's age rule is stated from this general rule, and no 55+ stock of any kind is verified on this ground (§2). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry:** a manufactured home is factory-built to the CSA standard and registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the Province's central register of manufactured home ownership; a sale, transfer or purchase is ONLY effective if registered there, filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary) with the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home:** a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does NOT include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant

Under the standing correction, adoption of DSSS 8288 is UNCONFIRMED — the standalone lots sibling for this same ground holds it UNCONFIRMED too. Flagged here expressly as a conforming-pass item for the GUIDE lots sibling; this mobile-homes file follows UNCONFIRMED and asserts no subdivision-standards bylaw number. https://delta.civicweb.net/document/226157/
- **The historic name is the trap and the tell.** "Port Guichon" is a ferry-landing and railway-station name from the Canadian Register of Historic Places, carried today by an elementary school built as Fairview Elementary — a buyer searching the name finds history and a school, not a park, because there is no verified park on this ground to find. https://www.historicplaces.ca/en/rep-reg/place-lieu.aspx?id=6344 and http://pg.deltasd.bc.ca/
- **Delta's bylaw numbers need the same care as in every Delta file.** OCP = Bylaw 8400 (2024), consolidated, no lettered schedules (farm land is designated "Agriculture (A)" — there is no Tsawwassen-style area-plan schedule inside 8400 to cite for Ladner either); the ZONING bylaw number is CONTESTED (7600, 2017 vs "8400, 2025") and is never asserted here. What governs a parcel in practice is its own zone on the City maps and, at the west edge, its ALR status. https://maps.delta.ca/Utilities/ZoningPDF/RS5.pdf
- **There is no Ladner market table to price a mobile home from.** SnapStats has no Ladner section, no Port Guichon row and no mobile category at all — any "Port Guichon mobile home median" on a website is not a SnapStats figure and is UNVERIFIED. Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report (research file, §2).
- **The dike is a title item, not just a landscape item.** Section 219 flood-proofing covenants and dike-raising rights-of-way (to 5.5 m) are the documented Ladner pattern — on river-side lots in this pocket, the flood covenant search is as much a part of diligence as any park-rules read would be in a pad purchase elsewhere. https://delta.civicweb.net/document/104061/
