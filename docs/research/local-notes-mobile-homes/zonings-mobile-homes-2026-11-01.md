## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. Zoning (this page's subject) governs where a park may sit; the MHPTA governs the tenancy inside it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the response must reach the home owner by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file — this slug's research documents no pad range, so none is mentioned at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. The 15-day dispute window and the additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the M1 files state them. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a NEW manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked examples, inputs stated (fully residential, no exemption): $500,000 = **$8,000**; $700,000 = **$12,000**; $800,000 = **$14,000** (a fully qualifying first-time buyer at that price point: $6,000, per the program anchors); $1,500,000 = **$28,000**; $2,000,000 = **$38,000**; $3,500,000 fully residential = **$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. Bare-land strata park LAND is documented once in the lots homes (the 16-entry roster lives in the lots-and-land PROGRAM-SUMMARY); this topic file documents no land. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning frames the directory summarizes (official-source level only, per this slug's research).** Surrey: Zoning By-law No. 12000's residential zones R1 to R7 replaced the RF, RH and RQ zones on July 8, 2024 under the provincial SSMUH legislation (RF-12/RF-12C→R4; RF-SD→R6), with former codes shown on the directory's cards; a secondary suite in Surrey is capped at 40% of the principal dwelling unit's habitable floor area — a "90 m² cap" is NOT current (the 90 m² figure on this page is the ALR additional-residence limit, a different rule). Township of Langley: Zoning Bylaw No. 2500, with the rural family (RU-1 minimum new lot 1.7 ha; RU-2/RU-3/RU-4/RU-5/RU-8/RU-11 at 8 ha) and the R-CL compact-lot family (R-CL s.403 = 220 m² minimum / 464.5 m² maximum LOT area; SR-3B added by Bylaw No. 6144, July 27, 2026, per its card); Township SSMUH Bylaw 6020 has NO 6-plex tier and the Township suite cap is 120 m². Abbotsford: the A1 card states agriculture with one single detached dwelling, a secondary suite (to 100 m² per the card — that city's own bylaw figure as the page gives it, NOT the ALR suite rule and NOT Surrey's 40% rule) and one accessory farm dwelling unit, with an 8.0 ha minimum new lot; the A2 minimum rises to 16 ha. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** Servicing per the VERIFIED LEDGER where a frame needs it: Township Bylaw 5382 CONFIRMED; Surrey Bylaw 8830 (1986); Richmond Bylaws 8751/8752 VERIFIED; Abbotsford Development servicing number UNCONFIRMED; Delta DSSS 8288 adoption UNCONFIRMED. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html and https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/12000
- **ALR frame (verified, ALC).** On ALR land, one principal residence is permitted without an ALC application at a maximum total floor area of 500 m² (no more than 1,000 m² of fill), a secondary suite is permitted only when located within the principal residence, and at most one additional residence is permitted of up to 90 m² on properties 40 ha or less or up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha; ALC rules apply in addition to municipal zoning, and local governments can add further restrictions. Strata plans in the ALR cause subdivision per ALC Information Bulletin 09 s.6; most subdivision of ALR land needs ALC approval. Manufactured homes on farm ground are supported only via the ALR Use Regulation s.34(3) / ALC Policy L-25 manufactured-home residence route plus the Non-Adhering Residential Use route — a general farm-worker manufactured-housing entitlement is UNCONFIRMED. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **GST on a manufactured home — only at the settled general level.** At the general level CRA sources support (GI-003 level): GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established. State the GST position in the contract. (Standing brief.)
- **First-home programs.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate is for new HOUSING (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Financing.** Lender and mortgage-insurer rules for manufactured homes (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in this program: UNVERIFIED — confirm financing for the specific home and tenure before removing subjects.
- **Foreclosure / court-ordered sale — where met.** BC court-ordered sales follow the Supreme Court process (Rules 21-7 / 13-5, Practice Direction PD-66, Form 60). REGISTRY LEDGER unblended: Abbotsford HAS a Supreme Court registry (the exception); Surrey/Delta/Mission NONE (mapping UNVERIFIED); Chilliwack + Maple Ridge UNCONFIRMED; Langley NOT in the ledger — no single registry position is asserted for this multi-city topic file. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: We're considering a manufactured home — what does the zoning tell us?** Which park form is lawful where: parks sit in dedicated manufactured-home zones (Abbotsford RSMH/RSH and RH1, Township MH-1, Surrey RM-M in this directory). The zoning tells you the park can exist there; your actual rights as a pad tenant come from the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and the park's own rules — and a park's zone says nothing about whether it is age-restricted. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **Q: In a pad-rental park, do I own the land?** No: **you own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad** — the park owns the land, and your right to the site is the assigned pad tenancy under the MHPTA. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does a park have to approve me as a buyer?** Yes, in a pad-rental park — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent, requested on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent. Build the 10-day window into subject dates. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: My Surrey listing says RF-12 — what zone is that now?** R4: Surrey's R1–R7 zones replaced the RF, RH and RQ zones on July 8, 2024, with RF-12 and RF-12C becoming R4 and RF-SD becoming R6; the directory's Surrey cards show the former code on each card so old listing text can be translated. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **Q: How big can a secondary suite be?** It depends which regime governs, and the three on this page must not be mixed: in Surrey the cap is 40% of the principal dwelling unit's habitable floor area (a "90 m² cap" is not current); on ALR land a suite is permitted only within the principal residence under the ALC frame; and Abbotsford's A1 card states a suite of up to 100 m² under that city's bylaw as the page summarizes it. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: What homes does the ALR allow on a farm parcel?** One principal residence of up to 500 m² total floor area (no more than 1,000 m² of fill), a secondary suite only inside that principal residence, and one additional detached residence of up to 90 m² (parcels of 40 ha or less) or 186 m² (parcels over 40 ha), without an ALC application — with municipal zoning still able to limit further. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Can I rely on a zone card instead of checking with the municipality?** No — summaries may not reflect recent amendments (the Township's SR-3B card alone records a July 27, 2026 addition), and one segment of this directory could not even be re-read for the research file (see FILE STATUS). Confirm the zone, permitted uses and ALR status with the municipality and the ALC for the specific parcel before you buy. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **Q: How is a manufactured-home purchase registered, and why does the CSA label matter?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: What happens if a park is sold or redeveloped?** For a conversion of all or a significant part of the park, the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
