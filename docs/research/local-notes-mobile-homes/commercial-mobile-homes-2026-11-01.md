## 2. THE PARKS AND THE HOMES

**TOPIC FILE — NO ROSTER, stated plainly at the top. This file verifies no park, counts no park and names no park as commercial stock. What follows is the tenure distinction this topic turns on, and nothing more.**

### A park as a commercial asset is not a pad tenancy — the distinction, stated plainly

- **Two different purchases share one name.** (a) A **pad tenancy**: a resident owns a manufactured home as a chattel and rents the pad it sits on — **a home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership** — under the MHPTA. (b) A **park as a commercial asset**: an investor buys the park's land (and its rental business) in a commercial transaction; the buyer of the park does not thereby become a pad tenant, and the purchase is not governed by the MHPTA as a tenancy. What the MHPTA governs is the tenancies INSIDE the park — each resident's pad tenancy continues under the Act whoever owns the park, and a park sale does not end those tenancies. (Standing MHPTA frame: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

*The full MHPTA block is carried here because it is the rules frame for the tenancies inside any park a commercial buyer acquires — §3 governs the residents' position, not the investor's purchase, and each bullet says which it touches.*

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (residents' tenancies).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. A park investor steps into the landlord side of tenancies this Act already governs. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director," never "arbitrator." https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** For a park investor this cap is the revenue frame — pad revenue cannot be re-priced to market on acquisition. No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support (the investor-side risk, stated exactly).** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. A buyer underwriting a park for redevelopment value carries this notice-and-compensation cost by law, not by negotiation. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. The Registry registers the HOMES; the park LAND transfers on title — the two systems run in parallel inside one operating park. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file**. The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. PTT on the park LAND itself in a commercial acquisition is a separate, transaction-specific question for the buyer's legal professional — no commercial PTT position is stated in this slug's research and none is asserted here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. Anchor checks: $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** — the $78,000 figure for $3.5M lives ONLY in the south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m TOWNHOMES sibling elsewhere in the program; no sibling of THIS slug uses $78,000 as its $3.5M result (the commercial acreages sibling mentions it only as that flagged reference), this file follows $93,000, and the reconciliation is OPEN. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata; none is documented for this topic).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies** — it is a lot-zoning framework, not a park-tenancy right, and it does not create redevelopment rights over a sitting park's tenancies. (Standing brief position.)
- **GST on a manufactured home — only at cited-CRA level.** At the general level CRA sources support: GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer for a specific manufactured home or a specific commercial park transaction is UNCONFIRMED until the property's and seller's status are established. (Standing brief.)
- **First-home programs — the boundary, stated so they are never misapplied to this feed.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate is for new HOUSING (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate, and none of these programs is a commercial-investor program. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Financing.** Lender and mortgage-insurer rules for manufactured homes (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in this program: UNVERIFIED — confirm financing for the specific home and tenure before removing subjects. Commercial park financing is a separate lender question again, and no source opened for this slug states it. (Standing brief.)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: If I buy a manufactured-home park as an investment, do I become a pad tenant?** No. You buy the land and the rental business in a commercial transaction. The pad tenancies are the residents' — each resident owns their home as a chattel and rents the pad from whoever owns the park, under the MHPTA — and those tenancies continue on a park sale. (Section 2; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes)
- **Q: Can a new park owner raise pad rents to market?** No. Pad rent may increase once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Can a park owner refuse a resident's home sale to a new buyer?** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent, requested on RTB Form 10, with a response due by the 10th day and silence deemed consent; no fee may be charged for consent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: What does it cost to close or convert a park?** On the official sources: all necessary permits and approvals first, 12 months' notice, and $20,000 compensation per tenant payable on or before the notice's effective date, with early move-out on at least 10 days' notice. Finer detail is UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does this commercial page list parks for sale?** No park listing is documented in this slug's research at all — the feed as read carries Business, Office, Industrial, Retail, Business with Property and Land Commercial cards, and no manufactured-home park is named in the research. Treat any park that surfaces in the live feed as an individual listing to verify in Paragon, not as stock this file has verified. (Research file §§1, 5.)
- **Q: Are there commercial statistics for parks — prices, cap rates, vacancy?** No. Commercial figures are UNVERIFIED for this page: they are not in SnapStats' residential extraction and no official commercial source opened for this slug supports them. (Research file §2.)
- **Q: Is a bare-land strata community the same investment as a pad park?** No. In a bare-land strata the lots are owned strata lots (Form B, strata fees, registered bylaws); there is no park owner collecting pad rent and no MHPTA tenancy. The forms are never mixed in this program. (Section 2.)
- **Q: How is a resident's home sale inside a park registered?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — effective only once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, with the CSA label attached at sale. The park land itself transfers separately, on title. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
