## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), carried VERBATIM as every open-houses topic file carries it:** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, states that the disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Representation at an open house.** In British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and the Province's Buying and selling a home page directs prospective purchasers to BCFSA for information on purchasing property in British Columbia. A licensed real estate professional in BC must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party to the transaction; BCFSA also states that professionals cannot represent two or more clients in the same transaction whose interests are in conflict except in the rarest of circumstances, and that dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 — so the host cannot simply "also represent" a buyer who walks in. In practice: chatting factually with the host at a Chilliwack open house does not by itself make you their client — but once you start sharing your motivation, budget or needs with a licensee who represents the seller, the disclosure rules engage. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home
- **Identification or sign-in for open-house visitors:** NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED as a rule; a host may still ask, as a matter of the seller's instructions, but that practice is not in an official source. (Research file §4.)
- **MHPTA — the governing Act the moment the open house is a park home.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. **CORE TENURE LINE: a home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home (a chattel) and rents the pad.** An open house changes none of this: the host's factual answers are not the tenancy agreement or the park rules. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file; the pad-rent range carried in Chilliwack page copy elsewhere in the program ($550–$950) is UNVERIFIED site copy, is NOT documented in this slug's own research, and is never carried here as a rate. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place BEFORE issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; the 15-day dispute window and the additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the program's M1 files state them. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file; pad-resale PTT is UNCONFIRMED**. The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. Anchor checks: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** (a $78,000 figure carried in a townhomes sibling elsewhere in the program is an OPEN reconciliation item: follow $93,000 and flag the conflict wherever met). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata — the Baker Trail Village form; it does NOT govern a pad-rental park home seen at an open house).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning / OCP — Chilliwack frame.** OCP: **Chilliwack 2050, Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 5500**, presenting the long-term vision and growth strategy for the whole city, including long-term growth strategies for residential (urban, rural and hillside), commercial, industrial and institutional uses and the protection of the Agricultural Land Reserve. Zoning: **Bylaw No. 5000** — the R1-A (Urban Residential) Zone sets a minimum lot area of 500 m² on community water and sewer and a minimum lot width of 15 m on the same services; the R1-C (Urban Infill) Zone sets a minimum lot area of 300 m² on an interior lot and 360 m² on a corner lot. SSMUH: **Bylaw 5395 (adopted June 18, 2024)**. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies** — it is a lot-zoning framework, not a park-tenancy right. https://chilliwack.com/ocp and https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Do I need my own agent to attend a Chilliwack open house — including at a park home?** No official source requires it. Rule 5-10(3) excuses disclosure of representation where a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or answering general factual questions — but the moment you share your motivation, financial qualifications or needs, that changes, and BCFSA requires unrepresented consumers to be told the risks of dealing with a licensee who represents the other party. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: How do I know the open house time on the page is current?** Each listing card shows an "OPEN HOUSE:" label with a day, a date and start and end times, but the research found one displayed card with no open-house label and could not confirm every card in the full feed carries one — check the page the day you plan to go and confirm the time privately with the listing brokerage before travelling. https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwack-open-houses.html
- **Q: Do I have to sign in or show ID at an open house?** No BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source states an identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors — UNVERIFIED as a rule; an individual host may still ask, on the seller's instructions. (Research file §4.)
- **Q: At a park home's open house, do I own the land if I buy?** No. **You own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad — that is not land ownership.** There is no Form B and no strata fee in a pad-rental park; the diligence set is the tenancy agreement, the written park rules and the Manufactured Home Registry record. (At a bare-land strata community such as Baker Trail Village the answer is different — you own the strata lot — which is why the tenure form must be confirmed park by park.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: What should I ask the host at a manufactured-home open house?** Facts the host can answer on the spot: the current pad rent for that site and what it includes, the park rules on pets, rentals of the home and buyer approval, and whether the tenancy agreement and park rules are available in writing — the seller must give the proposed purchaser both before asking the park owner to consent to assignment. Anything about your motivation, budget or needs goes to your own agent, not the seller's host (Rule 5-10(3), §3). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up after I buy?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only; no dollar rate is published as fact). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does the park have to approve me?** Yes — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent. An open-house conversation is not that consent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Which Chilliwack areas can I cover in one trip?** Group by the City's own OCP neighbourhood plan areas — Chilliwack Proper and Fairfield Island, Downtown, Eastern Hillsides, Sardis, South Vedder and Yarrow, plus the Agricultural Area; Sardis-side areas (Sardis, Garrison Crossing, Promontory, Vedder) cluster south of Highway 1 and Chilliwack Proper clusters north of it. https://chilliwack.com/ocp
- **Q: If I buy after an open house in Chilliwack, when are property taxes due?** The City mails tax notices at the end of May and taxes are due the first working day after July 1 each year; for 2026 the City published a 5% penalty for taxes outstanding after July 2, 2026 and an additional 5% after August 4, 2026, and your lawyer or notary allocates the bill between buyer and seller on the Statement of Adjustments. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2522
- **Q: What if the park behind the listing is redeveloped?** The provincial frame: permits and approvals first, 12 months' notice to end tenancy, $20,000 compensation per home on or before the effective date, and early move-out on 10 days' notice. An open house is a viewing, not diligence — search the City's development applications for the park's address before offering. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The disclosure rule has an open-house carve-out with a tripwire.** Rule 5-10(3) excuses disclosure of representation only while the host is hosting an advertised open house or giving factual answers to general questions — the moment the host solicits or receives your motivation, financial qualifications or needs, the exception ends. That is the legal line between small talk and sharing with the seller's side, and it is the same at a park home's open house as at any other. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download (~/workspace/local-notes/condos/surrey-open-houses-condos-2026-11-01.md)
- **Schools are address-based — and Chilliwack's catchments are moving.** Chilliwack is School District 33 (SD33); the district's Find Your School page states that a family enters its street address into the School Locator to find the catchment school. SD33 states that beginning in the fall of 2026 the district will review school catchments for Watson Elementary, Unsworth Elementary, Tyson Elementary and Stitó:s Lá:lém Totí:lt, with any recommended adjustments to be presented to the Board by the spring of 2027, and that changes to the Cheam Elementary catchment are already approved, effective September 2027 — note the exact address at any open house and run the locator the same day. School names are carried here ONLY as the district itself names them; no catchment is asserted for any address in this file. https://sd33.bc.ca/index.php/find-your-school
- **Anchors for a city-wide run.** Chilliwack General Hospital, 45600 Menholm Rd, is a community hospital with emergency and acute care (Fraser Health); the Chilliwack Urgent and Primary Care Centre is at #104 - 7955 Evans Road; the City's two leisure centres are the Chilliwack Landing Leisure Centre, Unit #1, 9145 Corbould St, and the Cheam Leisure Centre, 45501 Market Way; the Chilliwack Library (Fraser Valley Regional Library) is at 45860 First Avenue.
