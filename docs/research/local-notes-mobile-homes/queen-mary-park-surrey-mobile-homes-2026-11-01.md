## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. On assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, and the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase does not reset. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director — the decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator"), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — provincial block, plus the Surrey policy layer that applies to Westwood Estates.** Provincially: a landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date, and may dispute within the 15-day dispute window; additional compensation in the amount of the assessed value of the home (less the $20,000) and the further $5,000 / 12-months'-pad-rent amounts apply only as the provincial pages state them. Surrey layer: the City's Manufactured Home Park Policy brochure states the property owner must give 12 months' notice of a final moving date using a Notice to End Tenancy, which can only be given after the owner receives all their permits, and the City policy requires an affordable housing program for residents when a park is redeveloped; Surrey's Tenant Protection Bylaw, 2025, No. 21788 does NOT apply to property governed by the MHPTA. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited sources. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_reg_21788.pdf
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. A home may not be sold unless it has the original legible CSA decal attached (Z-240 for a mobile home, A-277 for a modular); if the label is removed or lost, the home must be re-certified — Technical Safety BC has a licensed electrical contractor inspect the wiring, and the electrical approval label prepared can be used in place of a CSA label to list the home for sale. A permit to move a manufactured home is issued by the Registry, a tax certificate for transport purposes is required (with exceptions including a move to a different pad within the same park), and the transport permit expires 30 days after issue. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner — which is why Westwood Estates listings can fold a "land portion of property taxes" into the pad rent. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file** (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: At Westwood Estates, do I own the land?** No. **You own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad** — the park owns the land, and your right to the site is the assigned pad tenancy under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act. Plainly, a home on a rented pad is not land ownership. (Section 2; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes)
- **Q: What is the park's address — 8224 or 8254 134 Street?** Both appear on current listings for the same park, with different postal codes — an open conflict, confirmed live again on a Nov 2, 2026 search check. Confirm the civic address and pad number from the park office; do not navigate or write an offer off a listing's address alone. https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3121759-103-8224-134-street-surrey-british-columbia-v3w4v1-249800/brochure
- **Q: What do I pay each month in the park?** Pad rent under the tenancy agreement, plus the home's property tax and insurance. The listings checked state the pad rent includes the land portion of property taxes, garbage pickup, sewer and water — but the amounts stated across listings conflict ($822, $912.52 as a Jan 1, 2027 figure, $915 and $935 a month, for different homes and dates), and **no one of them is published here as the park rate**. Confirm the current figure, what it includes, and the increase history for the exact site in the tenancy documents before offering. (Section 2.)
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only; the proportional amount is the park's own levy/regulated-utility change divided by its site count, so dollar increases differ park by park). On assignment the pad rent stays the same for the new tenant and the next increase date does not reset. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does the park have to approve me as a buyer?** Yes — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent, requested on RTB Form 10, with a response due by the 10th day and silence deemed consent; no fee may be charged for consent. Build the 10-day window into subject dates. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Is Westwood Estates really 55+?** Listings describe it as a 55+ age-restricted park and MLS data records "Senior Community: Yes" — AS listing-stated. A park's age rule lives in its park rules and tenancy documents, not in a strata bylaw, so the written park rules are the deciding document; the MHPTA assignment rules recognise a 55+ park (at least one tenant on the site 55 or older) and let the owner refuse an assignment where no purchaser meets the requirement. Get the park's current rule in writing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: My spouse is under 55 — can we both live there?** In a strata, provincial rules exempt a younger spouse or partner. In Westwood Estates, the park's own rules govern who may reside — the strata exemptions do not automatically apply to a pad tenancy, so ask the park for its rule in writing before writing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws (~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/queen-mary-park-surrey-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md)
- **Q: Can I rent out a park home, or keep a large dog?** Listings state large dogs are allowed with park approval (no vicious breeds) — AS listing-stated park rules. Renting out a home on a pad is a park-rules and tenancy-agreement question — confirm with the park before pricing any rental income; a park's no-rentals rule is a tenancy/park rule and is not touched by the province-wide voiding of strata rental bans. (Section 2.)
- **Q: How is the purchase registered, and why does the CSA label matter?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. A lost label must be re-certified through Technical Safety BC's electrical inspection route (a current park listing advertises exactly that "silver label" history). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold or redeveloped?** Provincially: all permits and approvals first, 12 months' notice, and $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date. Surrey adds its Manufactured Home Park Policy layer (12 months' notice of a final moving date, only after all permits are received, with an affordable housing program required on redevelopment), and Surrey's Tenant Protection Bylaw 21788 expressly does NOT cover MHPTA property. Search the City's development applications for the park's address before buying. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
- **Q: Which schools serve a Westwood Estates address?** Catchments are address-based in SD36 and no locator run for a Queen Mary Park address is on record, so the park's catchment is UNVERIFIED — run the exact address. The Ministry-listed schools in or beside the sub-area are named in §1; a 55+ park's family catchment is rarely the deciding factor, but confirm it if it matters to the household. (Research file §4.)
