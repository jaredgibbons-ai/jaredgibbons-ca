## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

### The presale frame — as this slug's research and siblings carry it

- **REDMA block.** Under BC's Real Estate Development Marketing Act, the developer must give the buyer a disclosure statement before signing (as the live page states it, attributed there to BCFSA presales information); **REDMA s.21 — the buyer may rescind by written notice within 7 days** (the siblings state: within 7 days of the later of signing the purchase agreement and acknowledging receipt of the disclosure statement); **REDMA s.18 — deposits are held in trust**. The page describes deposits as "usually 10%–20% total, staged over construction and held in trust" — that range is page copy, UNVERIFIED as a rule in this slug's research (no BCFSA, BC Laws or Province page stating the legal specifics was opened in that pass); the disclosure statement for the specific project governs its deposit schedule. Verify current REDMA rules on BCFSA's site and have a lawyer read the disclosure statement inside the rescission window. https://jaredgibbons.ca/south-surrey-new-construction-presales.html and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **New-home warranty:** the live page lists a 2-5-10 warranty (2 years materials and labour, 5 years building envelope, 10 years structure) as site copy attributed on the page to BCFSA presales information; the research file carries those terms as site copy only — UNVERIFIED on an official source in that pass. https://jaredgibbons.ca/south-surrey-new-construction-presales.html
- **The regulator:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and BCFSA states a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. The live page states the developer pays the buyer's agent's fees and that registration order matters for representation — register with your own agent before a first presentation-centre visit. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers (research file §4.)

### The MHPTA block — full (governs a pad purchase; no pad product is sold on this page)

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. A presale contract is neither — it is a REDMA developer contract (above) until completion. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director — the decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator"), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and no fee or benefit may be charged for consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes tenant on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file — no pad figure is documented in this slug's own research, so none is mentioned at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. The 15-day dispute window and the additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the M1 files state them. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a NEW manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. The live page's Newly Built Home Exemption thresholds (site copy: no PTT on a qualifying new principal residence up to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000) were not opened on a Province page in this slug's research — verify against the Province for your own purchase. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers, including a completed presale strata lot).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential purchase with no exemption = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. The $3.5M/$78,000 error is NOT met in this slug's own siblings. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata; for a presale strata, a 55+ bylaw in any case exists only once a strata is registered, confirmed on the Form B after completion).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning / OCP — two municipalities, never mixed.** Surrey buildings sit under the Surrey 2050 OCP, Bylaw No. 21900 (the former Bylaw No. 18020 is REPLACED and cited only as predecessor, never as current) and Surrey Zoning Bylaw, 1993, No. 12000. Surrey SSMUH / suites (Surrey lots only): a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots within 400 m of a frequent bus stop, under the July 8, 2024 changes; a secondary suite's habitable floor area may not exceed 40% of the principal dwelling unit. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** Servicing: Surrey Bylaw No. 8830 (1986) in the verified ledger. Along King George Boulevard, bus rapid transit is being designed from Surrey City Centre to Semiahmoo Town Centre — stated as site copy on the live page, citing a City of Surrey source dated May 2026 on the page; the BRT plan and timing were not opened on a City of Surrey or TransLink page in this pass: UNVERIFIED on an official source in this file. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing and https://jaredgibbons.ca/south-surrey-new-construction-presales.html
