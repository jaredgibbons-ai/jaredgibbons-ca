## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

### The page's own regime — Residential Tenancy Act, official-source level only (as the research documents it)

- **Residential rent increases (RTA — NOT the pad rule):** the 2026 limit for residential tenancies is 2.3% and the published 2027 limit is 2.2%; rent can be increased only once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' written notice on form RTB-7 showing the exact dollar amount and effective date; only the current year's limit can be used (no carry-forward, no rounding up); a larger increase requires an application on form RTB-52 or the tenant's voluntary written agreement; a tenant does not have to pay an increase above the legal limit or given without proper notice. (RTB Rent increases page, as cited on the live page.) https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Tenancy agreements:** a landlord must prepare a written agreement for every tenancy on the RTB-1 template or an equivalent containing all standard terms required by the RTA; both parties sign and date it; the landlord must give the tenant a copy within 21 days of signing; a tenancy can be month-to-month (periodic) or fixed term. (RTB Tenancy agreements page, as cited on the live page.) https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Condition inspections and deposits:** landlord and tenant inspect together at move-in and move-out and complete the Condition Inspection Report (RTB-27); the landlord gives the tenant a copy of the move-in report within 7 days; to keep the right to claim against a deposit a landlord must offer at least 2 opportunities to inspect, using form RTB-22 when the tenant was unavailable. A security deposit can be no more than half of the first month's rent, and a pet damage deposit the same cap no matter how many pets (no pet damage deposit for certified guide or service dogs, no other pet fees); a landlord cannot keep a deposit without the tenant's written permission or an RTB order; once the landlord has the tenant's forwarding address in writing (form RTB-47), deposits must be returned with any interest within 15 days if there are no issues, failing which the tenant can apply through the direct request process and the landlord may be ordered to pay double; deposit interest for 2026 is 0% per the page. Deposit interest for years before 2026 at a year-by-year level: UNVERIFIED in the research. (RTB pages, as cited on the live page.) https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Ending a tenancy — landlord's notices (RTA):** only for reasons allowed under the RTA, on the correct form, in writing, signed and dated, with unit address, effective date and reason; eviction notices cannot be served by text message. RTB-30 (10 Day Notice for Unpaid Rent or Utilities — tenant has 5 days to pay or dispute); RTB-33 (One Month Notice for Cause — 10 days to dispute); RTB-32L / RTB-32P (Three Month Notice for landlord's use / purchaser's use — must be generated through the RTB web portal, a non-generated notice is legally unenforceable; 3 months' notice, 21 days to dispute, one month's rent compensation, the person moving in must live in the unit at least 12 months, and personal-occupancy notices are not allowed in some buildings with 5 or more rental units); RTB-29 (Four Month Notice for Demolition or Conversion — permits/approvals required, 30 days to dispute, one month's rent compensation; major renovations/repairs are a separate process requiring an RTB order of possession). (RTB Evictions pages, as cited on the live page.) https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Ending a tenancy — tenant's notice and mutual agreement (RTA):** no RTB form is required for a tenant's own notice (writing, name, date, unit address, move-out date, signature; for month-to-month, received at least one full rental month before the effective date and before the day rent is due); a fixed-term tenancy cannot be ended early by tenant's notice alone except in specific situations. On form RTB-8 (Mutual Agreement to End a Tenancy) neither side can be required to sign, mutual agreement carries neither side's notice compensation, and a text message is not a legal way to end a tenancy by mutual agreement. (RTB pages, as cited on the live page.) https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Repairs (RTA):** landlords are responsible for most repairs including heating, plumbing, common areas and normal wear and tear; tenants are responsible for minor upkeep and damage caused by them, their pets or their guests; regular requests should be in writing; emergency repairs can be arranged by the tenant at reasonable cost after 2 phone attempts and a reasonable wait to reach the landlord's emergency contact. (RTB Repairs and maintenance page, as cited on the live page.) https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Dispute resolution (RTA):** four processes — direct request (written submissions only, narrow situations), facilitation, participatory hearing, and expedited hearing (RTB tries to schedule within 12 days); monetary claims must generally be $35,000 or less, with a limited exception for certain 12-month compensation claims; applications online with a Basic BCeID or on paper (RTB-12 series); tenants must keep paying rent during the process; orders are enforced through the courts. **Wording flag, stated exactly:** the live page / research describe the participatory hearing as producing a "binding arbitrator decision" and the RTB-52 route as an "arbitrator application" — that is the **RTA page's own wording as the research records it**, carried here as a page statement; it is NOT MHPTA wording, and in the MHPTA block below the decision-maker is the **director**, never "arbitrator." The two regimes' words are not blended in this file. (RTB Tenancy dispute resolution page, as cited on the live page.) https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Buying or selling a tenanted property (RTA):** a landlord cannot end a tenancy just because they want to sell; on a sale the buyer becomes the new landlord and the tenancy continues on the same terms; showings and open houses need written notice to the tenant or the tenant's permission; the owner at the time the tenant moves out is responsible for returning the deposits, so buyer and seller may deal with deposits in their contract or at closing. (RTB Sell a rental property page, as cited on the live page.) https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Currency of the forms:** the RTB updates its forms; whether each form number and version described on the page is still the current published version at rebuild time is UNVERIFIED in the research — a per-form check against the official RTB Tenancy forms page. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html

### The mobile-homes regime — the settled MHPTA block in full

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. **CORE TENURE LINE: a home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home (a chattel) and rents the pad.** https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY (the MHPTA rule, distinct from the RTA rule above).** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (12-month average change in the BC all-items CPI) plus a proportional amount (change in local government levies plus change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** The percentages coincide numerically with the RTA limits in this period; the proportional amount is MHPTA-only, and the two caps are not the same rule. No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — s.42 block.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Additional amounts as the settled block states them: an additional assessed-value amount and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts, with a 15-day dispute window. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the PTT treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad; both flat page claims met program-wide are UNVERIFIED. The one express provincial point: the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — own-land placement, not a resale on a rented pad. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 including the further 2% tier** (a $78,000 figure at a $3.5M input in a townhomes sibling elsewhere is an ERROR — follow $93,000; see §5 check). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata; this topic file verifies none).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **GST on a manufactured home — only at cited-CRA level (GI-003 general level).** GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established. (Program condos siblings.)
- **First-home programs.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate is for new HOUSING (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Does this page cover my manufactured home park tenancy?** No — the page is a Residential Tenancy Act page, and the research states the scope note expressly: manufactured home park tenancies sit under the separate MHPTA and are not the page's subject. Use §3's MHPTA block for a pad tenancy, and the RTB manufactured-homes pages, not the page's RTB-7 framing. (Research file §1.)
- **Q: How much can rent be increased in BC in 2026?** For a residential (RTA) tenancy: up to 2.3% (2027: 2.2%), once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' written notice on RTB-7. For a manufactured home **pad**: 2.3% **plus a proportional amount** for 2026 (2.2% plus proportional for 2027) under the MHPTA — same headline percentage, different rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How much can a landlord charge for deposits?** Under the RTA as the page states it: a security deposit of no more than half the first month's rent, and a pet damage deposit of no more than half the first month's rent regardless of the number of pets; deposits (with any interest) must be returned within 15 days after the forwarding address is received in writing, if there are no issues. Pad-tenancy deposit treatment is an MHPTA question — do not import the RTA figures into a park file without an MHPTA source. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Q: Can a landlord end a tenancy because the property is being sold?** Under the RTA, no: the tenancy continues and the buyer becomes the new landlord; a purchaser's-use notice (RTB-32P, portal-generated) is available only once all conditions of sale are met and the purchaser or a close family member genuinely intends to move in for at least 12 months. A park home sale is different again — it is an **assignment** of the pad tenancy with park-owner consent on Form 10, not an RTB-32P notice. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Q: Never serve a personal-use notice off a downloaded PDF — why?** RTB-32L and RTB-32P must be generated through the RTB web portal, and the RTB says a notice other than a generated one is legally unenforceable. (Research file §6.)
- **Q: Who decides a manufactured home park dispute — an arbitrator or the director?** The settled MHPTA wording in this program is **"an order of the director."** The resource page's "arbitrator" language belongs to its RTA dispute-resolution description and is flagged in §3; it is never carried into the MHPTA block. (Settled block; research file §4.)
- **Q: Are the form versions on the page current?** UNVERIFIED at rebuild time in the research — the RTB updates its forms, so download the current version from the official RTB Tenancy forms page and check per form. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
