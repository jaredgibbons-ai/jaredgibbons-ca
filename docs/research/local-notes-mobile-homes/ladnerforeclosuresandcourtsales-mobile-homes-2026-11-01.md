In BC a "court-ordered sale" is a sale conducted under a court order inside a foreclosure proceeding; it is not a separate process, and "bank foreclosure" is a misnomer — sales run through the court, not the bank. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **The settled court process — carried in full.** A foreclosure proceeding is started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents (Rule 21-7(2)), and a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 (Rule 21-7(4.1)). The court may order that a respondent pay what is due within a redemption period the court fixes — the Rules state no fixed length — and that in default the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption (Rule 21-7(5)(b)); in default of payment, a final order of foreclosure may be granted (Rule 21-7(6)). A party of record may apply at any time for an order that the property be sold (Rule 21-7(7)), and the person having conduct of a sale may apply for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser, even before the redemption period expires (Rule 21-7(9)). The sale mechanics — conduct of sale, court directions, reserve/upset price, bid and offer rights, and Form 60 certification — sit in Rule 13-5 and the Supreme Court's foreclosure Practice Direction PD-66, carried here as the settled program treatment. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Form B on a court sale — UNCONFIRMED.** In an ordinary strata resale, an owner, purchaser or authorized person may request a Form B Information Certificate and the strata corporation must provide it within 7 days (maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page). No official source read in this program states that a court-ordered sale will produce a Form B or a full strata package — what documents are produced is a per-file fact stated in the listing/file documents. For a fee-simple property there is no strata and no Form B question at all; for a bare-land strata lot the question applies in the same UNCONFIRMED way, and the form is never reclassified. (~/workspace/local-notes/condos/ladnerforeclosuresandcourtsales-condos-2026-11-01.md; ~/workspace/local-notes/townhomes/ladnerforeclosuresandcourtsales-townhomes-2026-11-01.md)
- **PTT and GST on a court sale — UNCONFIRMED.** No official source read in this program states a special PTT or GST rule for a purchase through a court-ordered sale, and this slug's own research could not load the Province's PTT page or an official GST source on 2026-11-01 — a buyer's lawyer must confirm both on the specific property before an offer. The general PTT rates (for transfers where PTT does apply) are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million — worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential purchase with no exemption = $2,000 + $10,000 = **$12,000**; anchors $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier** (the $78,000 townhomes-sibling figure elsewhere is an OPEN reconciliation item — follow $93,000, flag where met). **Conforming flag, stated plainly: this slug's condos sibling states the general PTT structure as "3% to $3 million, 5% above" — that shorthand is WRONG (it compresses 3% above $2M plus the further 2% above $3M into a single tier) and is never carried in this file; the sibling is a conforming-pass item.** https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (carried in full as the standing block; it governs form (a) in §2 wherever a pad tenancy exists).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser; before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules. How an assignment interacts with a court-ordered sale of the home itself: UNCONFIRMED — per-file legal advice. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or deemed consent, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the response must reach the home owner by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of day 10 is deemed consent, and no fee or benefit may be charged for consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula (BC all-items CPI 12-month average change + proportional amount = change in local government levies + change in regulated park utilities, divided by number of sites). Verified live from RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** Converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park requires all necessary government permits and approvals BEFORE issuing notice, 12 months' notice, and $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer detail UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; the original signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (lawyer/notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached at the point of sale. For a court-ordered sale of a home on a rented pad, how the vesting order and the Registry transfer interact is a per-file question — UNCONFIRMED as a general rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners; the assessed value excludes the land value when the home is in a park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule** (distinct from the court-sale position above, which is also UNCONFIRMED). No primary source opened in this program settles PTT on a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact here. The Province states expressly that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies to a bare-land strata lot in a court file in the same UNCONFIRMED-document way as §3's Form B point).** Form B diligence in an ordinary resale (within 7 days of request, max $35 + up to 25¢/page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01, with spouse/caregiver/legacy exemptions incl. the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning / OCP — Delta frame (for any Ladner parcel in a proceeding).** OCP: Bylaw No. 8400 (2024), NO lettered schedules. The Delta ZONING bylaw number is CONTESTED and is never asserted here. SSMUH: Bylaws 8394 + 8395. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** Delta DSSS 8288, 2024 adoption is UNCONFIRMED — held, never asserted. Delta 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty after that date and a further 5% after September 2, 2026 — arrears handling on a specific court-ordered sale is a per-property check, UNVERIFIED here. https://www.delta.ca/services/property-taxes-utilities/property-taxes

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b). Ask which stage the specific file is at before spending money. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What does court confirmation mean for my offer on a Ladner home?** The person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7(9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires, so until the order is made the sale is not final. Do not book movers on acceptance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** The bid and offer mechanics sit in Rule 13-5 and Practice Direction PD-66, carried in this file as the settled program treatment (§3) — this slug's own research could not retrieve those texts and holds the mechanics UNVERIFIED in its own pass, so get the seller's counsel's bid instructions for the specific file in writing before spending on inspections, and treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for. (Research file UNVERIFIED items 4–5; §3.)
- **Q: Which court registry holds a Ladner foreclosure file?** Read it off the file itself on Court Services Online. Delta has NO Supreme Court registry in the program ledger and the serving-registry mapping is UNVERIFIED — never assume a neighbouring city's registry. Court Services Online eSearch shows the file number, registry location, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure". https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: Will I get a Form B and depreciation report for a court-ordered strata property?** UNCONFIRMED — that is the honest answer at topic level. In an ordinary resale the strata must provide a Form B within 7 days of a proper request; no source in this program guarantees a court sale will produce a full package, and what is produced is a per-file fact. (§3.)
- **Q: What PTT and GST will I pay on a court-ordered purchase?** UNCONFIRMED for court-ordered sales specifically — have your lawyer cost both on the specific property before you set your ceiling. The general PTT tiers are in §3; do not use any "3% to $3M, 5% above" shorthand — it is wrong. (§3.)
- **Q: If the property is a manufactured home on a rented pad, what am I actually buying in a court sale?** The home as a chattel, with the pad position governed by the MHPTA tenancy — not land ownership. How the court file, the Manufactured Home Registry transfer and the pad tenancy assignment fit together on that file is UNCONFIRMED as a general rule: per-file legal advice before offering. (§§2–3.)
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up, if the home sits on a rented pad?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Are court-ordered Ladner homes cheaper — is there a foreclosure discount figure?** UNVERIFIED — no official source publishes Ladner foreclosure counts or discount statistics, and SnapStats has no foreclosure category and no South Delta section. Any discount claim is characterization, not a sourced figure. (Research file §2.)
