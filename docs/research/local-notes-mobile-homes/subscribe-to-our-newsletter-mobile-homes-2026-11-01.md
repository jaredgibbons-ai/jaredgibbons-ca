## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA block, carried in full as the standing block (the law a mobile-home reader of this newsletter is governed by elsewhere; nothing on this page engages it).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the response must reach the home owner by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of day 10 is deemed consent, and no fee or benefit may be charged for consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes tenant on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula (BC all-items CPI 12-month average change + proportional amount = change in local government levies + change in regulated park utilities, divided by number of sites). Verified live from RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated anywhere in this file — and no pad range is mentioned at all, because no pad figure is documented in this slug's own research. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** Converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park requires all necessary government permits and approvals BEFORE issuing notice, 12 months' notice, and $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer detail UNCONFIRMED beyond cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; the original signed Bill of Sale + Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (lawyer/notary); the CSA certification label must be attached at point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** Assessed/taxed like other residential property; assessed value excludes the land value when the home is in a park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner; Home Owner Grant available for a principal residence, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles PTT on a home-only purchase on a rented pad; NO flat claim either way is stated as fact. The Province states expressly that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a NEW home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer/notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% first $200,000, 2% $200,000–$2M, 3% above $2M, plus further 2% on the residential portion above $3M. Worked example, inputs stated: $700,000 fully residential, no exemption = $2,000 + $10,000 = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 FTHB figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier**. - **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 + up to 25¢/page); depreciation reports 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01, with spouse/caregiver/legacy exemptions incl. May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **CASL — the law that actually governs THIS page (CRTC framework).** Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) and its regulations target spam, computer programs installed without consent, and unsolicited redirections; the CRTC is the regulator that publishes the compliance framework. The core rule for a message like this newsletter: when sending a commercial electronic message (e.g. email or text), you must (1) obtain prior consent from the recipient (either express or implied); (2) provide identification and contact information; and (3) include a working unsubscribe mechanism. Violations may result in monetary penalties. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti/reg.htm (research file §4)
- **CASL — what counts as a commercial electronic message.** The CRTC FAQ states CASL applies when (1) a commercial electronic message (CEM) is (2) sent to an electronic address — and a CEM includes offers to purchase, sell, barter or lease land or an interest in land, and messages promoting a person as someone who does those things. A REALTOR®'s newsletter carrying latest listings and market updates, sent to subscribers' email addresses, sits squarely in that definition on the CRTC's own examples. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **CASL — express vs implied consent, and the burden of proof.** There are two types of consent under CASL — express and implied; express consent does not expire, however the recipient has the right to withdraw their consent at any time; express consent can be obtained in writing or orally, and in either case the onus is on the person sending the message to prove they obtained consent. A voluntary signup through this page is the classic express-consent route — and the sender, not the subscriber, carries the burden of proving it. An unsubscribe mechanism must be included in CEMs; a clear and prominent hyperlink allowing unsubscribe with a simple click is the CRTC's example; the mechanism must be "readily performed" — simple, quick and easy; the unsubscribe link must remain valid for at least 60 days after the CEM is sent; and an unsubscribe request must be processed without delay, and no later than 10 business days after receiving it. - **Privacy — PIPEDA federally, BC's own law provincially.** The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada states PIPEDA sets the ground rules for how private-sector organizations collect, use and disclose personal information in for-profit, commercial activities across Canada; personal information includes any factual or subjective information, recorded or not, about an identifiable individual. Businesses subject to PIPEDA must follow its 10 fair information principles: 1. Accountability; 2. Identifying purposes; 3. Consent; 4. Limiting collection; 5. Limiting use, disclosure, and retention; 6. Accuracy; 7. Safeguards; 8. Openness; 9. Individual access; 10. Challenging compliance. BC (with Alberta and Quebec) has its own private-sector privacy law deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA, and organizations subject to it are generally exempt from PIPEDA for in-province handling, while cross-border handling remains subject to PIPEDA. A BC REALTOR®'s subscriber list therefore sits primarily under BC's provincial regime for in-province handling, with PIPEDA behind it for cross-border handling; this file states the framework only and gives no legal advice on a specific list.
