- **A resale manufactured home in a park is not new housing, and this rebate does not attach to it.** The live page's own "stock" is new housing only — newly built or substantially renovated homes bought from a builder (including on leased land), owner-built homes on land the buyer owns or leases, and co-operative housing shares for new units; resale homes are expressly excluded, with the page noting resale homes are not subject to GST. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the response must reach the home owner by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of day 10 is deemed consent, and no fee or benefit may be charged for consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; the 15-day dispute window and the additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the M1 files state them. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. That newly built home exemption's own thresholds were verified on the Province's page in an earlier batch and are not restated as figures here, because the live gst-rebate page does not raise them and they were not re-opened in this slug's pass (research file §4, stated plainly). A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million — verified in this slug's research pass on the Province's page. The BC first-time home buyers' program, verified in the same pass: exempt on the first $500,000 where the fair market value is $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000, on the Province's stated qualifying conditions; the page's "up to $8,000" saving is the general-rate tax on $500,000 ($2,000 at 1% plus $6,000 at 2%). Worked examples, inputs + tier math: $500K → $8,000; $700K → $12,000; $800K → $14,000 ($6,000 for a fully qualifying first-time buyer at that cap); $900K → $16,000; $1.0M → $18,000; $1.2M → $22,000; $1.5M → $28,000; $1.8M → $34,000; $2.0M → $38,000; $3,200,000 → $78,000 (CORRECT for that input — never flagged); $3.5M fully residential → **$93,000 including the further 2% tier** ($2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** This is a BC-wide topic file with no single-city frame: no city OCP, zoning, suite cap, STR licence or servicing bylaw is asserted for this slug, and none is imported from any sibling's city frame.
- **GST — THE CENTRE OF THIS FILE, carried per this research's own CRA verification status.**
  - **FTHB GST/HST rebate — VERIFIED in this pass** (CRA News No. 122 + the CRA tax-tips page both opened): Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**. Together with the GST/HST new housing rebate, it provides 100% of the GST (or the federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated single unit residential complex or residential condominium unit valued up to $1 million; between $1 million and $1.5 million the rebate is gradually reduced; no FTHB rebate at $1.5 million or more. Maximum $50,000 — consistent with 100% of the 5% GST on a $1 million home in BC. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
  - **Eligibility — VERIFIED:** at least 18, Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and not have lived in a home — in or outside Canada — that they or their spouse or common-law partner owned or jointly owned as a primary place of residence in the purchase year or the preceding four calendar years; the buyer must meet the existing new housing rebate's criteria (or would, if its ceiling were $1.5 million instead of $450,000); a builder agreement on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (owner-built: construction begins in the same window); construction begins before 2031 and is substantially completed before 2036; the buyer is the first to occupy after substantial completion; and the buyer must not have previously applied for an FTHB rebate. The rebate also generally covers a home bought on land leased from the builder and a co-operative housing share for a new unit. Claiming: usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction; a builder can credit it at closing (Form GST190); otherwise the buyer applies directly (GST190, or GST191 for an owner-built home). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much GST can a first-time buyer in BC get back on a new home?** Up to $50,000, VERIFIED: together with the new housing rebate, the FTHB rebate returns 100% of the GST on a new or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Does the rebate apply to a resale manufactured home in a park?** No — resale homes are not new housing and are not subject to GST on the page's own position; the rebate's stock is new housing only. A NEW manufactured home's position depends on its exact structure and is UNCONFIRMED on any park-specific source — confirm before subjects. (§2.)
- **Q: Who counts as a first-time buyer for this rebate?** VERIFIED: at least 18, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, with no home lived in as a primary residence that they or their spouse or common-law partner owned in the purchase year or the preceding four calendar years. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: What happened to the old GST new housing rebate?** It still exists, VERIFIED in this pass: generally 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, gradually reduced between $350,000 and $450,000, none at $450,000 or more. The FTHB rebate is the top-up to 100% for qualifying first-time buyers — do not double-count the two. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: My agreement was signed before March 20, 2025 — do I qualify?** No on the verified condition: eligibility turns on a builder agreement on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, with construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036, no matter the price. The live page's finer assignment-sales wording is UNVERIFIED (§3). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: How and when do I claim?** VERIFIED: usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction — a builder can credit the rebate at closing (Form GST190), or you apply directly to the CRA (GST190; GST191 for an owner-built home). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
- **Q: Can I stack the GST rebate with BC's PTT exemption?** They are separate federal and provincial programs, both verified in this pass: the BC first-time program exempts PTT on the first $500,000 at a fair market value of $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000, and nothing in either program's conditions as read bars claiming both. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Are the Home Buyers' Plan figures on the page safe to rely on?** Not from this file: the CRA HBP page failed to load in this pass (HTTP 403), so the page's HBP figures are UNVERIFIED site copy here, however familiar they look. The same holds for the page's tax-credit figures, which were not re-read on a CRA page in this pass. (§3.)
- **Q: If I buy in a pad-rental park, do I own the land?** No: **you own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad** — the park owns the land, and your right to the site is the assigned pad tenancy under the MHPTA. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
