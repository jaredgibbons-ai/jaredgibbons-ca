### How a manufactured home meets this topic — process terms only

- Any mortgaged property type can be the subject of a Rule 21-7 proceeding, which by its terms covers "mortgaged property" without a property-type limit. A manufactured home on a RENTED PAD adds a second layer a fee-simple home does not have: the home is a chattel registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the pad is a tenancy under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, and a court file touching the home does not by itself transfer the pad tenancy — the assignment/consent machinery in §3 still applies, and the park's own position must be confirmed on the specific file. **A home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home (a chattel) and rents the pad.** https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 (research file §5)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **The settled court process (standing frame).** BC foreclosure proceedings are started by petition in the Supreme Court under Rule 21-7 (1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents (Rule 21-7 (2)); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 (Rule 21-7 (4.1)). The court may order a respondent to pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period the court fixes, failing which the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption (Rule 21-7 (5)(b)) — the Rules state no set length for the redemption period. Court-ordered sales run under Rule 13-5 (conduct of sale, court directions, reserve or minimum price, bid and offer rights, sale by contract conditional on court approval) with Form 60 certification, under the Supreme Court's foreclosure Practice Direction PD-66. **Research-stance note:** subrules 21-7 (6), (7) and (9) and the Rule 13-5 / PD-66 texts were UNVERIFIED in this slug's own research pass (429 stop; Rule 13-5 not opened) — the treatment above is the program's settled treatment from earlier opens, carried per the brief, and the specific file's conduct-of-sale lawyer's written process governs any live deal. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Court records.** Court Services Online is BC's electronic court registry; its eSearch services are available to anyone with Internet access, the file details shown for Supreme Court civil files include the file number, registry location, party names and filed documents, and its file classes include "H - Foreclosure." Read the court file before offering — the petition, the amount certified due and the orders made are the facts of the deal. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. On a court-ordered sale of a park home, whether and how that assignment interacts with the court's vesting order on the specific file is a lawyer-confirmed, file-specific question: UNCONFIRMED as a general rule. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director," never "arbitrator." https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file — this slug's research documents no pad range, so none is mentioned at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. How a court vesting order is reflected in the Registry on a specific park-home file: confirm with the lawyer/notary on that file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / GST on a court-ordered purchase — UNCONFIRMED.** No official source read for this topic states a special property transfer tax or GST rule for a purchase through a court-ordered sale, and this file does not invent one — the research file opened no PTT or GST source at all (its UNVERIFIED item 9), and this slug's condos and townhomes siblings state the same UNCONFIRMED position. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is there a list of Maple Ridge park homes in foreclosure?** No — this topic file carries no roster, and no official source publishes foreclosure counts for Maple Ridge. - **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — it is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5)(b), within which the respondent must pay what is due and for costs or be foreclosed of the equity of redemption. Ask which stage the file is at before spending money. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Is my accepted offer on a court-ordered sale final?** No — treat it as not final until the court confirms it on that file. The settled program treatment places confirmation in the Rule 21-7 / Rule 13-5 process under PD-66; this slug's research could not re-read the confirmation texts (429), so get the conduct-of-sale lawyer's written approval process for the specific file before booking movers or spending on inspections. (Section 3; research file §6.)
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** Plan for the possibility. Bid rights sit in the court's sale directions (Rule 13-5) and PD-66 — neither text was opened in this slug's research pass, and the live page's description of sealed, unconditional competing bids at the hearing is the page's own statement, UNVERIFIED as a general rule here. Get the seller's counsel's bid instructions for the specific file in writing before you spend. (Research file §§4, 6.)
- **Q: If the court-sale home is in a park, do I still need the park's consent?** The pad tenancy transfers by assignment, which needs the park owner's prior written consent on RTB Form 10 (10-day rule, silence deemed consent, no fee) — and how that interacts with the court's vesting order on a specific file is UNCONFIRMED as a general rule. Confirm both tracks with your lawyer before the approval hearing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Do I pay PTT or GST differently because it's a court sale?** UNCONFIRMED — no official source read for this topic states a court-sale-specific PTT or GST rule. Budget on the general rules in §3 and have a BC real-estate lawyer confirm the treatment on the specific file before the approval hearing. (Section 3.)
- **Q: Is a court-ordered sale automatically a bargain?** No published source supports a standard foreclosure discount. Price against the general market — Maple Ridge detached sold at 97% sale-to-list and attached at 94% in September 2026 — and against the specific home's condition, as-is risk and occupancy, which is where any discount actually comes from. (Research file §6; SnapStats GVR September 2026.)
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold or redeveloped?** For a conversion of all or a significant part of the park, the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date. A court sale of one home in a park does not change that framework. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
