## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (the corridor's park homes are exactly this case).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. Whether and how the Act's park-owner consent and closure machinery applies to a leasehold / First Nations Lease pad (the Oasis listing flag in §2) is a structure-specific question for the park's own documents and a lawyer — UNCONFIRMED at that finer level in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file; the listing-text range the plain research documents is flagged in §5 as UNVERIFIED listing copy only, exactly as the plain file flags it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place BEFORE issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; the tenant's dispute window is 15 days, and the Act's additional assessed-value amount and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts apply as the provincial pages state them. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. On this rural ground the tax notice itself comes from the Province (see the tax bullet below). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land transfers on this ground).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata; none is verified on this ground).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

(Research file §§1, 4.)
- **ALR block — farm ground on this corridor.** Farmland parcels in the ALR follow the ALC housing frame: one principal residence at a maximum of 500 m² plus at most one additional residence of 90 m² (parcels ≤40 ha) or 186 m² (parcels >40 ha). - **GST on a manufactured home — only at cited-CRA level.** GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level (CRA GI-003 general level); anything finer is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established. - **First-home programs.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate is for new HOUSING (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. - **Property taxes / holding cost — RURAL frame (Mission's City penalty expressly NOT applied).** A property not located in a city, town, district or village is in a rural area: its owner receives the property tax notice from the Province's Surveyor of Taxes Office and pays property taxes to the Province, not to the FVRD or the City of Mission. Under the Taxation (Rural Area) Act, s.11, rural property taxes are due and payable on July 2 of the year they are first levied, a 5% penalty is added to any portion that remains unpaid on July 2, and a further 5% penalty is added to any portion that remains unpaid on October 31. **The City of Mission's flat 10% penalty is a municipal penalty for City of Mission parcels and is expressly NOT applied to this rural ground** — the two regimes are DISTINCT and are never blended in this file. A park home's holding cost is pad rent plus the home's own tax/insurance. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/rural-area and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Are Dewdney and Deroche part of Mission?** No. Listings carry Mission mailing addresses and the board groups the sub-area under Mission for reporting, but Dewdney and Deroche are unincorporated FVRD Electoral Area G communities — the FVRD's housing-needs report lists Area G as "Nicomen Island, Deroche, Dewdney, Hatzic Island" — and the City of Mission does not govern them. Zoning, permits, servicing and taxes run through the FVRD and the Province. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Document~Library/EA%20Housing%20Needs%20Report.pdf
- **Q: In Oasis Country Estates or Nicomen Park, do I own the land?** No. **You own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad** — the park owns (or leases) the land, and your right to the site is the assigned pad tenancy under the MHPTA. Plainly, a home on a rented pad is not land ownership. Both parks are documented in the plain home file as listing-text tenure records only — confirm tenure, including the First Nations Lease flag one Oasis listing carries, from the park's own documents before writing. (§2.)
- **Q: What do I pay each month in a park here?** Pad rent under the tenancy agreement, plus the home's property tax and insurance. Pad figures appear in this corridor's listing text for individual homes — they are flagged in §5 as UNVERIFIED listing copy only, never as a park rate; confirm the current figure, what it includes, and the increase history for the exact site in the tenancy documents before offering. (§§2, 5.)
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only; the proportional amount is the park's own levy/regulated-utility change divided by its site count, so dollar increases differ park by park). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does the park have to approve me as a buyer?** Yes — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent, requested on RTB Form 10, with a response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, and no fee charged for consent. Build the 10-day window into subject dates. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Is Oasis Country Estates a 55+ park?** UNCONFIRMED. Listings distinguish an "adult side" from a "family section," but no source checked states the adult side's exact age number — get the park rules in writing before relying on any age position. Nicomen Park / Nicomen Village carries no age restriction in any listing checked and is carried as a family park record. (§2.)
- **Q: Who do I pay property tax to on a Dewdney or Deroche property?** The Province: rural-area properties receive their tax notice from the Surveyor of Taxes Office and pay the Province, not the FVRD or a municipality; rural taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty on amounts unpaid on July 2 and a further 5% on amounts unpaid on October 31 — NOT the City of Mission's flat 10% penalty, which does not apply to this rural ground. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/rural-area and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01
- **Q: How is a park-home purchase registered, and why does the CSA label matter?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. A missing label is a red flag for financing and resale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold or redeveloped?** For a conversion of all or a significant part of the park, the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date, with a 15-day dispute window for the tenant. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
