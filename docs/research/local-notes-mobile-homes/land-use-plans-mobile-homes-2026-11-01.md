- **The ALR layer crosses all four municipalities.** Where land is in the Agricultural Land Reserve, ALC rules apply on top of the municipal plan and zoning — generally no more than one residence per ALR parcel, a principal residence built without an ALC application capped at 500 m² total floor area, and one additional detached residence up to 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less or 186 m² on larger parcels. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase in any of the four municipalities.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. No municipal land use plan creates, changes or ends a pad tenancy. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated anywhere in this file; no pad figure is documented in this slug's own research, so none is mentioned at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place BEFORE issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; the 15-day dispute window and the additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the program's M1 files state them. The permits-first gate is where a municipality's land use plans touch a park home directly. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles PTT on a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact; pad-resale PTT is UNCONFIRMED, and BOTH flat page claims met in the program are UNVERIFIED.** The Province states expressly only that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — a rule for a NEW home on OWN land, not a resale home on a rented pad. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** — the $78,000 figure lives ONLY in the south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m TOWNHOMES sibling and is flagged, not followed, in this slug's acreages sibling; follow $93,000. The "3% to $3M, 5% above" shorthand is WRONG, never carried, and NOT met in this slug's siblings. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **GST on a manufactured home — only as CRA sources support.** At the general level: GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established.
- **First-home programs.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate is for new HOUSING (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate. **Conforming note for this slug:** the land-use-plans acreages sibling notes "March 12 noted in-line in some sources — March 13 governs" — that is a correct governing statement, not a carried March 12 claim, so no new conforming item arises from it; any flat March 12 assertion met anywhere would be flagged and never carried. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Financing.** Lender and mortgage-insurer rules for manufactured homes (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in this program: UNVERIFIED.
- **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies** in any of the four municipalities — it is a lot-zoning framework, not a park-tenancy right.
- **Court sales, where met.** BC court-ordered sales follow the settled Supreme Court process (Rules 21-7 / 13-5, Practice Direction PD-66, Form 60); Form B treatment and PTT/GST treatment on a court-ordered sale are UNCONFIRMED. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Which municipalities does this page cover?** Four, per the live page's title: Surrey, Langley, Abbotsford and Delta — each planning through its own OCP and zoning bylaw; there is no single shared land use plan document for the four. https://jaredgibbons.ca/land-use-plans.html
- **Q: What is an Official Community Plan?** In Delta's own description — the verified example here — an OCP describes where and what type of housing can be added across the city and contains long-term objectives and policies guiding decisions about land use, development, services, amenities and infrastructure. https://www.delta.ca/building-development/housing-our-future
- **Q: What is Delta's current land use plan?** OCP Bylaw No. 8400 (2024), adopted July 8, 2024: a single consolidated plan with the Land Use Plan at s. 1.5, designations at s. 1.5.1 on Map 2, and no Schedule D.1 or community area-plan schedules (those belonged to the superseded Bylaw No. 3950). https://hdp-ca-prod-app-delta-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/8917/7677/0068/OCP_2024_web.pdf
- **Q: How do I check zoning and future land use for an Abbotsford property?** The City's Development Planning pages direct property checks to the City's property information search or a property search in WebMap for current zoning, future land uses and development permit areas. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/development-planning
- **Q: Does an OCP designation settle what I can build?** No. In the verified Delta model the designation is the first of three layers — designation (s. 1.5.1 / Map 2), then the parcel's zone, then any Development Permit Area — and on ALR land the ALC's rules apply on top of all three. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: I found a Surrey or Langley plan claim in an offer — can I check it against this file?** Only at standing-frame level: this slug's research opened NO Surrey or Langley municipal source, so their OCP numbers and structures are UNVERIFIED in this pass. Never cite a plan you have not dated — the dating check (current bylaw vs superseded bylaw) has to be run in the municipality's own current documents before a plan claim goes into an offer or a listing. (Research file §§4, 6.)
- **Q: Do land use plans affect a manufactured home on a rented pad?** Not the tenancy itself — the MHPTA governs it (assignment on Form 10, the 10-day consent rule, the pad-rent cap, park rules). Plans touch a park home mainly at closure/conversion, where MHPTA s.42 requires all government permits and approvals BEFORE the landlord issues notice. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Is "Langley" one planning jurisdiction?** No — the City of Langley and the Township of Langley each have their own OCP and zoning bylaw. Confirm which municipality the parcel is in before opening either plan. (Research file §6.)
