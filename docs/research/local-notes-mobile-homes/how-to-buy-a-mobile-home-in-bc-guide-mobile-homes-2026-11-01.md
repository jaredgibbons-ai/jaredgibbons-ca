## 1. THE PROVINCIAL FRAME — WHERE MANUFACTURED HOMES SIT IN BC

- There are no streets or boundaries for this file: the research file's scope is a buyer's guide to purchasing a manufactured home (the term BC legislation uses; "mobile home" is the everyday term) anywhere in British Columbia — "there is no MLS sub-area for this guide and no single municipality — the rules below are the provincial framework, with municipal zoning varying by city." https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes (Research file §1)
- The two ownership forms the guide is built around: (1) you own the home and rent the pad (site) it sits on in a manufactured home park — the land is the park owner's; (2) you own both the home and the land it sits on. https://jaredgibbons.ca/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-guide.html (Research file §1)
- Which law applies follows the ground, not the home: the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA) applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land you own is dealt with through the land title system instead; and where a person rents BOTH the home and the site from the same owner, the Residential Tenancy Act — not the MHPTA — applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- Terminology caution: a manufactured home is factory-built to the CSA standard and registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; it is not the same product class as a site-built home, and the registry/CSA-label rules in §3 are what separate a registrable manufactured home from an unregistered unit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry (Research file §1)

### (a) The tenure line — the core discipline of this program

- **A home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership.** In a pad-rental park the buyer owns the home — a chattel, registered in the Manufactured Home Registry — and rents the pad from the park owner under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement. There is no land title, no strata lot and no Form B in this form. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Own-land manufactured-home stock is a different stock.** In a bare-land strata park the buyer owns the lot as a strata lot — Form B, strata fees and registered bylaws apply, and there is no pad rent. In a co-operative the land is held through the co-op and the buyer holds a co-op interest plus the home. On fee-simple land the buyer owns the parcel outright. The lots-and-land sibling maps all three ground-holding forms in full. (~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-guide-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md, §2)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES — THE MHPTA BLOCK IN FULL

- **The governing statute for pad tenancies is the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA).** It applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the owner also owns is dealt with through the land title system instead, and if a person rents both the home and the site from an owner, the Residential Tenancy Act applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Buying in a park is an assignment, not a new tenancy (MHPTA s.28).** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply to the tenancy. On assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Park owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director — the Act's word is "director," never "arbitrator"). The request is made in writing on the approved form (RTB Form 10); the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received; silence by the end of the 10th day means consent is deemed to have been given; and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment, the purchaser becomes the tenant of the park owner and assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the tenancy agreement from the assignment date, on the same terms as before. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Pad rent increases — percentages ONLY, never a dollar figure.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' written notice, capped by formula — the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park); the landlord may not round up and may not carry forward an unused portion of a prior year's limit. **The caps verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): 2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **NO pad-rent DOLLAR figure is published as fact in this program.** No official or operator source states one for any park. The live site's own pages carry conflicting unsourced ranges — the Langley pad-fees page states $650–$950/mo while the cheap-mobile-homes-langley-under-500k page's FAQ states $600–$900/mo, and a Chilliwack page states $550–$950/mo — **all UNVERIFIED site copy, in conflict with each other, adopted nowhere.** The figure that matters is the specific park's current rent in its written Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement. (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/mobile-home-parks-fraser-valley.md, §5)
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only with proper notice (see the April 30, 2024 change in §5), and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s.42).** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; the dispute window for a 12-month notice is 15 days; and additional compensation of the assessed value of the home minus the $20,000 may be ordered where the tenant could not obtain the permits needed to move the home or could not move it to another site within a reasonable distance, with a further $5,000 or 12 months' pad rent, whichever is greater, payable if the landlord does not proceed with the conversion purpose within a reasonable period. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **The home as chattel — the Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Age rules in parks are park designations, not strata bylaws.** The Province states the general rule for a designated 55-plus park: at least one tenant on the site must be 55 years of age or older. Individual parks can state their occupancy rule more strictly in their own documents, so the park's own tenancy agreement and rules are the operative document. No official source names a specific age-restricted park for this topic file — park names at topic level rest on the sibling files in §2, each with its basis stated. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Strata-form parks carry the BC strata block instead.** Where the park form is strata (a bare-land strata such as Sea Ridge Bays): Form B Information Certificate diligence (fees, amounts owed, approved levies, CRF balance, insurance summary; within 7 days); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; and only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01 — legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and the May 1, 2023 expanded exemptions for a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the resident cared for before they turned 19). A 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** The provincial small-scale multi-unit housing rules govern owned lots and strata ground; a pad tenant holds no land interest for SSMUH to attach to. (Program standing position; municipal frames are carried in the area files.)
- **Property tax and the Home Owner Grant.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment — stated ONLY as official sources support.** The Province's newly built home exemption from property transfer tax expressly includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) as a newly built home — relevant to buyers placing a NEW manufactured home on their own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. For a resale home on a rented pad, the purchase is of the home and an assigned tenancy, not a registered transfer of land: at exactly that level, it is generally not a PTT transaction on land. **Both stronger pad claims carried on program pages are UNVERIFIED wherever met:** the Abbotsford page's flat "PTT does not apply on a pad" claim and the Chilliwack page's opposite PTT-on-a-pad claim — neither is adopted here or in any file in this program. Bare-land strata lot transfers (which DO transfer a registered strata lot), partial interests and any specific transaction's treatment are UNCONFIRMED here — confirm against BC PTT guidance / a lawyer before writing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general tiers, for the owned-land forms, with the program's worked anchors:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2M, 3% above $2M, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3M. OPEN reconciliation item: a townhomes sibling elsewhere carries $78,000 for the $3.5M input — this program follows $93,000 and flags the conflict wherever met. First-time buyers' exemption: no tax on the first $500,000 at $835,000 or less, phasing to $860,000; newly built home exemption full to $1,100,000, phasing to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: If I buy a mobile home in a park, do I own the land?** No. You own the home — a chattel registered in the Manufactured Home Registry — and rent the site under a pad tenancy agreement; the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on. The exceptions prove the rule: in a bare-land strata such as Sea Ridge Bays you own the lot as a strata lot, and in a co-operative such as Pioneer Park the land is held through the co-op. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes (Research file Q1)
- **Q: Can the park owner refuse to let me buy a home in the park?** The sale needs the park owner's prior written consent to assign the tenancy — but the owner must respond within 10 days of the written request (RTB Form 10), silence counts as consent given, no fee can be charged for consent, and if consent is unreasonably withheld the home owner can apply for dispute resolution. Listings phrase this as "subject to park approval." https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf (Research file Q2)
- **Q: How much is pad rent?** No dollar figure is stated in this file, because no official or operator source publishes one for any park — and the live site's own pages conflict ($650–$950/mo on the Langley pad-fees page vs $600–$900/mo in the cheap-mobile-homes FAQ, both unsourced). Get the current pad rent from the park's written Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement for the specific home, never from a web range. (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/mobile-home-parks-fraser-valley.md, §5)
- **Q: How much can my pad rent go up each year?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the inflation rate plus a proportional amount for the park's changes in local government levies and regulated utilities, divided by the number of sites in the park — verified at 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027. On assignment, your pad rent and the next increase date carry over unchanged from the seller's tenancy. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes (Research file Q3)
- **Q: What paperwork should the seller hand me before I write an offer?** The written tenancy agreement and the written park rules — the regulation requires the seller to give the proposed purchaser both before the park owner is even asked to consent to the assignment — plus the current pad rent and the date of the last increase, so the increase formula can be checked. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Q: What happens to me if the park is sold or redeveloped?** A sale of the park alone does not end your tenancy — your assigned tenancy continues on the same terms. A conversion of all or a significant part of the park is different: the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay you $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; you may leave early on at least 10 days' written notice, and additional compensation may be ordered where the home cannot be moved. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes (Research file Q4)
- **Q: How is the purchase actually registered?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered. Your lawyer or notary files the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the home must carry its CSA certification label at the point of sale — a missing label is a red flag for financing and resale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry (Research file Q5)
- **Q: How is financing different for a home on a rented pad?** The home is registered in the Manufactured Home Registry rather than dealt with as land-title real property, and lender/insurer rules for manufactured homes (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in this research — UNCONFIRMED. Confirm financing for the specific home and tenure before removing subjects. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: A directory calls a park 55+, but a listing in it says no age restrictions. Which is right?** Treat the park as UNCONFIRMED until its own rules settle it — that exact conflict occurred in this research at Breakaway Bays in South Surrey (directory: 55+ / 345 sites; MLS listing: "No age or rental restrictions" / "Family Park"; the MLS basis governs and the park is NOT verified 55+). Ask the park for its current tenancy agreement and park rules in writing before relying on either claim. https://www.mhbo.com/mobile-home-park/45813-breakaway-bays-1840-160th-street-surrey-bc-v4a-4x4
- **Q: Can I resell my home in the park later?** Yes, on the same machinery you bought through: the sale is an assignment of your pad tenancy requiring the park owner's prior written consent (10-day response, deemed consent on silence, no consent fee), the transfer is effective only when registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, and the CSA label must be attached at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: Do I pay property tax on a home I own on a rented pad?** Yes — a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land. Some parks include the land portion of property taxes inside the pad rent (Westwood Estates and Val Maria listings both state this, as listing-stated inclusions for specific homes) — check what the specific park's pad rent includes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **June 6, 2018 — closure compensation replaced and increased.** The Province's information bulletin states the new compensation for tenants of manufactured home parks facing eviction took effect June 6, 2018: compensation on a park closure/conversion became a flat $20,000, increased from the previous requirement of 12 months of pad rental costs; the same changes require park owners to compensate tenants for the assessed value of the home over $20,000 where the manufactured home cannot be relocated, clarify that a tenant unable to relocate the home is not responsible for disposal costs, and set bad-faith closure compensation at 12 months' pad rental or $5,000, whichever is greater. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2018MAH0066-001111
- **2019 — rent increases tied to inflation only.** The Province's September 4, 2019 release states rent increase limits are tied only to the inflation rate (the 12-month average percentage change in the BC Consumer Price Index), and that for manufactured home park tenancies the rate is that inflation figure plus a proportional amount for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees — the formula structure still in use, whose verified outputs are 2.3% + proportional (2026) and 2.2% + proportional (2027). https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2019MAH0130-001702
- **April 30, 2024 — park rule changes limited to once a year.** The Province announced April 30, 2024 that changes to the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Regulation mean park owners may change park rules only once per year, must give tenants three months' notice of a rule change (up from two weeks), and must give that notice on a form approved by the Residential Tenancy Branch. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2024hous0072-000659
- **October 20, 2025 — Surrey's new tenant-protection framework expressly leaves parks to the provincial Act.** Surrey adopted its Tenant Protection Bylaw No. 21788 and Rental Replacement Policy No. O-63 on October 20, 2025 for multi-unit rental redevelopment, and the bylaw does not apply to property governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act — a dated reminder that park residents' redevelopment protection remains the provincial 12-month notice and $20,000 framework plus any city park policy, not the new city bylaw. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-development-application-process/submitting-a-development-application/rental-housing-redevelopment
- **The consent clock is the park equivalent of a subject period, and most buyers have never heard of it:** the park owner has 10 days to respond to an assignment request on RTB Form 10, and silence works FOR the buyer (deemed consent). A deal structured without that window in its dates is structured wrong. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **The tax assessment quietly tells you the tenure:** in a park, the assessed value does not include the land, because the home owner does not own it — the cleanest one-line test of pad tenure on a tax notice. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **The site's own directory cannot be checked against any registry — because none exists.** The 73-entry Fraser Valley parks directory (§2(c)) is site copy with internal duplicates and a two-postal-code conflict on the same park; treat it as a starting list, confirm the park's legal name and address from the listing and the park itself, and never treat a directory entry — or a directory age line — as verification. (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/mobile-home-parks-fraser-valley.md, §§5–6)
