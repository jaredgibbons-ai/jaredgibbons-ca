### The process position for a manufactured home

- Any mortgaged property type can be the subject of a Rule 21-7 proceeding, which by its terms covers "mortgaged property" without a property-type limit — so a manufactured home is not excluded from the process by its form. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- The tenure split decides what a court sale can actually transfer, and it must be checked file by file: a manufactured home on the owner's OWN land is land-plus-home, and the vesting order deals with title; a home on a RENTED PAD is different stock — **a home on a rented pad is not land ownership (the buyer owns the home as a chattel and rents the pad)** — and how a court sale interacts with the pad tenancy, the park owner's consent position and the Manufactured Home Registry record for that home is UNCONFIRMED in this program and must be confirmed by the buyer's lawyer on the specific file. (Section 3; standing brief.)
- Tsawwassen's one manufactured-home tenure record — Tsawwassen RV Resort (4761 Nulelum Way, Tsawwassen First Nation Lands), documented in its Tsawwassen North home as an all-ages RV resort — is not a City of Delta park, MHPTA applicability on TFN Lands is UNVERIFIED, and no foreclosure position for it is stated here. https://www.mhbo.com/mobile-home-park/83798-tsawwassen-rv-resort-4761-nulelum-way-tsawwassen-bc-v4m-4g9 (~/workspace/local-notes/mobile-homes/tsawwassen-north-guide-mobile-homes-2026-11-01.md)
- Condition reality (a structural feature of the process, not a statistic): the seller in a foreclosure sale is not an owner preparing the home for market in the ordinary way — the sale runs inside a court proceeding under Rule 21-7, and buyers should expect the conditions in the court-approved form for that file, not a standard residential contract. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- Title outcome: on confirmation, the court's order directs the disposition of the proceeds and vests title in the purchaser — the vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- Occupancy: whether a property is vacant or occupied is a matter of the individual court file, not a published statistic — UNVERIFIED for any general statement. (Research file §5.)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser; before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. How assignment interacts with a COURT-ordered sale of a park home is UNCONFIRMED (see §2) — do not assume the ordinary assignment path governs a vesting order. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (ordinary assignment).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the response must reach the home owner by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of day 10 is deemed consent, and no fee or benefit may be charged for consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes tenant on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula (BC all-items CPI 12-month average change + proportional amount = change in local government levies + change in regulated park utilities, divided by number of sites). Verified live from RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42).** Converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park requires all necessary government permits and approvals BEFORE issuing notice, 12 months' notice, and $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer detail UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; the original signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (lawyer/notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners; the assessed value excludes the land value when the home is in a park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles PTT on a home-only purchase on a rented pad; no flat claim either way is stated as fact. The Province states expressly that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a NEW home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** (the $78,000 figure in the south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m townhomes sibling is an OPEN reconciliation item — follow $93,000; it is not met on this ground). The townhomes siblings' "3% to $3M, 5% above" shorthand is WRONG wherever met and is never carried here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata; none is verified in Tsawwassen).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 + up to 25¢/page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01, with spouse/caregiver/legacy exemptions incl. the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Foreclosure process — the settled treatment carried in this file.** A foreclosure proceeding is started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7 (1); all persons whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). The court may order payment of what is due within a redemption period the court fixes (no fixed length in the Rules) — Rule 21-7 (5) (b); in default, a final order of foreclosure may be granted — Rule 21-7 (6); a party of record may apply at any time for an order that the property be sold — Rule 21-7 (7); and the person with conduct of sale may apply, even before the redemption period expires, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7 (9). Sale mechanics (conduct of sale, court directions, reserve/upset price, bid and offer rights, Form 60 certification) sit in Rule 13-5 and Practice Direction PD-66 — carried here as the settled program treatment; this slug's own research did not re-verify those texts (see FILE STATUS). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5) (b). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What does court confirmation mean for my offer?** The person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7 (9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires, so until the order is made the sale is not final. Do not book movers on acceptance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** Competing-bid mechanics sit in the court's sale directions under Rule 13-5 and PD-66 (the settled treatment) — get the seller's counsel's bid instructions for the specific file in writing before spending on inspections. This slug's own research held the bid-rights wording UNVERIFIED in its pass; the file-specific instructions govern either way. (Research file §7; FILE STATUS above.)
- **Q: Where can I check the court file for a Tsawwassen foreclosure?** Court Services Online offers eSearch to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." Which registry serves Delta is UNVERIFIED as a stated mapping — Delta has NO registry in the program ledger. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: If the court sale is a manufactured home on a rented pad, what am I actually buying?** The home as a chattel, plus whatever pad-tenancy position the court file and the park's consent process produce — NOT the land. That interaction is UNCONFIRMED in this program; have your lawyer read the court file, the tenancy agreement and the Manufactured Home Registry record before you set a ceiling. (Section 2.)
- **Q: Are court-ordered sales "bank sales" with a set discount?** No on both counts. In BC the sale runs through the court inside a foreclosure proceeding, not through the bank, and no official source publishes Tsawwassen foreclosure counts or discount-to-market statistics — any discount figure for Tsawwassen is UNVERIFIED. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: What taxes do I pay on a court-ordered purchase?** UNCONFIRMED for court sales in this program — PTT and GST treatment for the specific property must be costed by your lawyer before you offer; the general PTT tiers in §3 apply to own-land transfers where PTT does apply, but do not assume them onto a court sale without that advice. (Research file §4.)
- **Q: How is a manufactured home purchase registered?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA label must be attached at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: Can I get a mortgage on a park home bought this way?** UNVERIFIED at official-source level in this program — chattel financing treatment, home-age and foundation requirements are lender-specific, and a court-sale timeline adds its own constraint. Confirm financing for the specific home and tenure before removing subjects. (Standing brief.)

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **"Bank foreclosure" is a misnomer in BC.** Sales run through the Supreme Court under Rule 21-7, with the court confirming the sale and vesting title — the process facts, not a bank's listing decision, decide the timeline. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
