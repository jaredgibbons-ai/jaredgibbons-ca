### Pad ground is NOT land — the tenure line, stated plainly

- **A home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home as a chattel and rents the pad.** A pad listing is not a land listing: no title to the ground transfers, the pad is a tenancy under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act assigned from the seller with the park owner's consent (Section 3), and the home's ownership transfers through the Manufactured Home Registry. A buyer reading a Langley "land" search who lands on a pad-priced listing is looking at a home-only purchase with a monthly tenancy underneath it — a different product from every parcel on this topic page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Own-land manufactured stock is the land-side form:** a manufactured home on land the buyer owns (fee simple, a bare-land strata lot, or a co-operative share) transfers through the land title system and is documented in this program in the area home files — never blended with pad stock. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (carried in full as the standing block; it governs the cross-referenced parks above, NOT the land on this topic page).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY (the only pad-rent content in this file).** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file — pad ground is a tenancy cost, not a land price. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file**. The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — directly relevant to this land topic: it speaks to placing a NEW manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — the closing tax on an actual land purchase from this page.** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked examples, inputs and tier math stated: a $800,000 parcel → 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $600,000 ($12,000) = **$14,000**; a $1,200,000 parcel → $2,000 + 2% × $1,000,000 ($20,000) = **$22,000**. Anchor checks: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** — a townhomes sibling elsewhere in the program carries $78,000 for $3.5M; that figure is an OPEN reconciliation item: follow $93,000 and flag the conflict wherever met. First-time buyers' exemption: full on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), partial to $860,000, 0.5 ha or smaller, principal residence only. Newly built home exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 — including the documented route where a person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may be eligible for a refund of the tax paid. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only where manufactured stock is a bare-land strata, never to a pad tenancy).** Form B diligence (the lot's strata fee, amounts owed, approved levies, CRF balance, parking/storage and insurance summary; the strata must provide the Form B within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 (short-term restrictions may still apply by bylaw); only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements

SSMUH Bylaw 6020 was adopted November 18, 2024 and contains NO 6-plex tier: 3 units on lots of 280 m² or less, 4 units on lots under 4,050 m²; added-unit eligibility requires connection to both municipal water and sewer (roughly 55% of Township lots, about 15,000 lots; Brookswood about 15%, Fort Langley about 22%, Walnut Grove about 99%, Murrayville about 98%). ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is a manufactured-home park pad a piece of land I can buy?** No. **A home on a rented pad is not land ownership** — you would own the home as a chattel and rent the pad under an MHPTA tenancy assigned from the seller. The parcels on this topic page are land: title transfers at the Land Title Office. The two products share search terms, not tenure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Which Langley parks can I cross-reference from here, and where are they documented?** The Brookswood-area 55+ pad parks (Cedar Creek Estates, Pineridge, Fernridge Park), Langley Grove Estates in Otter District, and the Pioneer Park co-op in Aldergrove are cross-referenced in Section 2 to their home grounds, where their park/homes side is documented and any count lives. Cedar Lane Park, Deer Creek Estates, Forest Green Estates and Riverglen are candidates only — UNCONFIRMED — and Aldergrove Mobile Home Park is checked and NOT 55+ on listing evidence. Basis: Section 2.
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up in a Langley park?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only; no pad-rent dollar figure is published as fact in this file). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does a Langley park have to approve me as a buyer?** In a pad-rental park, yes — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent, and the seller must give you the written tenancy agreement and park rules before asking. A co-op purchase (Pioneer Park's form) runs on the co-operative's own membership process, not the Form 10 route — confirm that process with the co-op. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Can I put a manufactured home on a Langley lot I buy?** That is a parcel-level zoning and (on reserve land) ALC read, not a topic-level rule: the Township and City zoning bylaws govern what dwelling forms a lot permits, ALR land carries the 500 m² principal-residence cap with the suite inside it only, and the Province's newly built PTT exemption expressly contemplates "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied). Whether a specific lot permits a manufactured home is UNCONFIRMED until that lot's zone and ALR status are read. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: What is the smallest rural lot I can create in the Township?** It depends on the zone under Zoning Bylaw No. 2500, s. 110.1: RU-1 requires 1.7 ha for a new lot, RU-2 and RU-3 require 8.0 ha, SR-2 requires 8,094 m² and SR-1 requires 3,716 m² — and ALR land also needs Agricultural Land Commission permission to subdivide. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/resources/social-sustainability/housing/TOL-Council-Report---Provincial-Housing-Legislation,-June-10,-2024.pdf
- **Q: Who approves a subdivision in Langley?** The Approving Officer, as required under the Land Title Act — in the Township the process runs application, review, decision letter, conditions, final approval and registration in the Land Title Office; in the City, Bylaw 3300 adds that meeting the minimum lot standards does not bind the Approving Officer to approve. https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/subdivision.aspx
- **Q: What taxes do I plan for on a Langley land purchase?** PTT on the general tiers in Section 3 (worked examples: $800,000 → $14,000; $1,200,000 → $22,000, with the first-time and newly built exemptions tested before assuming the full bill, including the vacant-land refund route once a newly built home is on the land), and GST at CRA GI-003 level — developer/subdivided lots generally taxable, an individual's personal-use sale generally exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html

- **Q: Can a park close and end my tenancy?** Yes, on the statutory route: all permits and approvals in place before notice, 12 months' notice, and $20,000 compensation payable on or before the effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. That closure risk attaches to pad tenure only — it is one of the structural differences between pad ground and the land on this topic page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
