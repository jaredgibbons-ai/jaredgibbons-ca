## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (carried in full as the standing block; no pad purchase is counted in this band file).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file (see §5 on non-documentation). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place BEFORE issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; the 15-day dispute window and the additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the program's M1 files state them. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file. The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers, including the condos this band actually surfaces).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: **the band cap itself, $800,000, fully residential with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $600,000 ($12,000) = $14,000** (the program's $800K-cap anchor; $6,000 is the figure at that price point for a fully qualifying first-time buyer). Anchor checks: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** (tier math: $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000). **$78,000 flag — met in this slug's acreages sibling:** that sibling, following the program anchor, notes a townhomes sibling elsewhere carries $78,000 for $3.5M; the $78,000 figure lives in the **south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m TOWNHOMES sibling** (a different slug from surrey-homes-over-1-8m). Reconciliation is OPEN: this file follows **$93,000** and flags $78,000 wherever met, never the reverse. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-time buyers' PTT program in this band.** The full exemption requires a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), the property 0.5 hectares or smaller with only residential improvements, used only as the buyer's principal residence, and the exemption applies to the first $500,000 of the purchase price; a partial exemption is available above $835,000 and below $860,000 — so a qualifying first-time buyer can in principle use the program anywhere in this band, subject to all the program's other conditions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata; none is counted in this band, and the block governs the strata condos the band does surface).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. BC strata disputes can go to the Civil Resolution Tribunal. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/resolving-disputes/the-civil-resolution-tribunal
- **Zoning / OCP — Maple Ridge frame.** Zoning Bylaw No. 7600-2019 governs (its s.402.8(h) prohibits rental accommodation (suites) on floodplain properties). **OCP: CONTESTED (7060-2014 vs 7998-2024) — flagged here, resolved nowhere in this file;** the 55-plus sibling for this slug cites 7060-2014 from the City's own OCP chapter documents, which is one side of that contest, not a resolution. Albion Area Plan adopted October 28, 2025. **Lougheed Transit Corridor ADOPTED January 27, 2026 (Bylaw 7781-2021; corridor ends at 221 Street — does NOT reach Whonnock);** contrary readings met in cityofmapleridge research and in three Maple Ridge guide research files elsewhere in the program are flagged wherever met and never followed — this slug's own research does not address the corridor plan. Servicing: Bylaw 4800-1993, stated from a City source only. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** https://mapleridge.ca/your-government/plans-strategies/official-community-plan-area-plans/area-neighbourhood-plans/town
- **Floodplain at the south edge of the core.** The Port Haney / River Road edge sits along the Fraser River — the flood construction level is 6.7 m geodetic (including freeboard), a covenant on title identifies construction in the floodplain, and no habitable floor space may be located below the flood construction level on affected lots — for a strata building, flood status is a building-level check on title and in the strata documents. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/floodplain-construction-0
- **GST on a manufactured home — only at cited-CRA level.** GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer for a specific manufactured home is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established. State the GST position in the contract. (Standing brief.)

GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. **Verification status in this file: carried per this slug's own research and siblings, which state the March 13, 2026 CRA date (the condos sibling notes secondary sources saying March 12 — CRA governs).** A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How many Maple Ridge condos are for sale under $800,000 right now — and how many are mobile homes?** Neither figure can be stated from the sources used here. - **Q: Is a park home under $800K the same kind of purchase as a condo under $800K?** No. The condo is a strata lot on title, diligenced through the Form B and strata documents. **The park home is a chattel on a rented pad — you own the home and rent the pad; that is not land ownership** — diligenced through the tenancy agreement, the written park rules and the Manufactured Home Registry. Same price band, different law. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Can a first-time buyer use the PTT exemption under $800K?** In principle, yes — the program's full exemption runs to a fair market value of $835,000 (exempting the first $500,000 of price), with a partial exemption to $860,000, provided the buyer meets all the program's personal and property conditions; the exemption is claimed through the buyer's legal professional on the PTT return. At the $800,000 cap with no exemption the general PTT is $14,000; the program anchor for a fully qualifying first-time buyer at that price point is $6,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: What PTT do I budget at the cap?** Inputs stated: $800,000, fully residential, no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $600,000 ($12,000) = **$14,000**. Every price in this band is taxed entirely in the 1% and 2% tiers — the 3% tier and the further 2% over $3,000,000 do not arise in this band. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Does the park have to approve me as a buyer?** In a pad-rental park, yes — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent, decision-maker the director. A strata condo purchase has no equivalent park-owner consent step. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only; the proportional amount is the park's own levy/regulated-utility change divided by its site count). No pad-rent dollar figure is published as fact in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How is a park-home purchase registered, and why does the CSA label matter?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. A missing label is a red flag for financing and resale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold or redeveloped?** For a conversion of all or a significant part of the park, the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date. Search the City's development applications for the park's address before buying. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes

Confirm financing for the specific home and its pad tenure before removing subjects. (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-1/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-guide.md)
