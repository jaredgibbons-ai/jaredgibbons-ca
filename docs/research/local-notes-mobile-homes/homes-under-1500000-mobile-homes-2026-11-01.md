## 2. THE PARKS AND THE HOMES

### The tenure split inside one price band — the core fact of this file

- **A home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home as a chattel and rents the pad.** The purchase is a home-only sale: the pad is a tenancy under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, assigned from the seller with the park owner's consent (Section 3), and the home's ownership transfers through the Manufactured Home Registry, not the land title system. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes

Own-land manufactured stock is never mixed with pad stock in this program. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file**. The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply, and the band arithmetic.** Property transfer tax is payable when a person purchases or gains an interest in property registered at the Land Title Office, unless an exemption applies; it is based on the fair market value of the property (land and improvements) on the day of registration, and it is not the same as annual property taxes. General rates: 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. **Band consequence: a purchase under $1,500,000 only ever meets the 1% and 2% tiers** — the 3% tier starts above $2,000,000 and the further 2% never applies in this band. Worked examples, inputs and tier math stated: $500,000 → 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $300,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**; $700,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**; $800,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $600,000 ($12,000) = **$14,000** ($6,000 for a fully-qualifying first-time buyer after the $8,000 saving); $900,000 → **$16,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,400,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $1,200,000 ($24,000) = **$26,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **The two BC purchase exemptions sit inside this band.** First time home buyers' program: where all conditions are met, the purchaser is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price; a full exemption requires a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), a property of 0.5 hectares or smaller containing only residential improvements and used only as the buyer's principal residence, and a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000. Newly built home exemption: effective April 1, 2024, the fair market value threshold for a full exemption is $1,100,000, with a partial exemption phasing out over the $50,000 above it and complete elimination at $1,150,000 for qualifying purchasers. A buyer above $1,150,000 gets neither exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Additional property transfer tax — rate UNVERIFIED here:** a foreign national, foreign corporation or taxable trustee must also pay additional PTT on the fair market value of the residential portion of a property within a specified area of BC; the rate is published on the Province's separate additional-PTT page, which was not re-read for this slug's research, so the rate is UNVERIFIED in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **The $1.5 million line is also the federal insured-mortgage line.** CMHC states mortgage loan insurance is required in Canada when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the purchase price; the insurance protects the lender, not the buyer. CMHC's published down-payment rules: homes $500,000 or less require a minimum 5% down payment; homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999 require 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require a minimum down payment of 20% and mortgage loan insurance is not available. Every home on this page is, by the page's own price filter, on the insurable side of the line — a buyer here can purchase with less than 20% down, paying an insurance premium CMHC puts at **0.6% to 4.5%** of the total mortgage amount, payable in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only where the manufactured stock is a bare-land strata).** Form B diligence (the lot's strata fee, amounts owed, approved levies, CRF balance, parking/storage and insurance summary; the strata must provide the Form B within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 (short-term restrictions may still apply by bylaw); only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies** — it is a lot-zoning framework, not a park-tenancy right. This band spans two municipalities' ground: Surrey's frame (OCP Bylaw 21900; Zoning Bylaw 12000; SSMUH amendments July 8, 2024; secondary suite cap 40% of the principal dwelling) and Langley's two frames (Township of Langley Zoning Bylaw 2500 with SSMUH Bylaw 6020; Langley City Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300 by number, SSMUH Bylaw 3284, suite the lesser of 100 m² and 40%) — never blended, and parcel eligibility is read per property. https://www.surrey.ca/ and https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/zoning-bylaw.aspx
- **GST on a manufactured home — only at cited-CRA level.** At the general level CRA sources support: GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer for a specific manufactured home (vendor status, whether the home was previously occupied, placement on own land) is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established. State the GST position in the contract. (Standing brief.)
- **First-home programs.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate is for new HOUSING (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12 — a March 12 statement met anywhere is a conforming-pass item, never carried): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Financing a manufactured home.** Lender and mortgage-insurer rules for manufactured homes (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in this program: UNVERIFIED — confirm financing for the specific home and tenure before removing subjects. The CMHC insured-mortgage line above governs price eligibility; it does not by itself confirm that a specific chattel home is financeable. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained

- **Q: If I find a park home under $1.5M, what am I actually buying?** The home only, as a chattel — **a home on a rented pad is not land ownership**: you own the home and rent the pad. The seller must give you a copy of the written tenancy agreement and the written park rules before asking the park owner to consent to the assignment, and the transfer registers in the Manufactured Home Registry, not on land title. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: Does the park have to approve me as a buyer?** Yes, in a pad-rental park — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent, and on assignment you become the tenant on the same terms. There is no equivalent park-owner approval for an own-land or strata purchase. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up each year?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only; no pad-rent dollar figure is published as fact in this file). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How secure is my tenancy if the park is sold or closes?** The tenancy is an assignment of the existing agreement on the same terms, and park rules can change only once a year with 3 full months' notice. If the park owner converts the park to another use, all permits and approvals must be in place before notice, the notice period is 12 months, and $20,000 compensation is payable on or before the effective date; you may move early on at least 10 days' notice, paying rent to the move-out date. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Can I get a mortgage on a manufactured home in this band?** The band itself is inside CMHC's insurable price range (under $1,500,000), but lender and insurer treatment of a specific manufactured home — chattel vs land-home, age, foundation — is UNVERIFIED at official-source level in this program. - **Q: Can I resell my home inside the park later?** Yes — a park home resells by assignment of the pad tenancy: your buyer needs the park owner's consent on the same Form 10 / 10-day process, receives the tenancy agreement and park rules from you first, and the home's transfer must be registered in the Manufactured Home Registry to be effective, with the CSA label attached at sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry

For a home-only purchase on a rented pad, PTT treatment is UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule — a lawyer or notary confirms it for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
