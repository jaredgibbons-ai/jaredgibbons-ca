### The tenure line, stated for this topic

- **A home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home (a chattel) and rents the pad; the buyer does not own the land.** A buyer who meets a manufactured home in a Tsawwassen-area search and assumes they are buying land is making the most expensive mistake this topic can produce: pad ground carries a Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act tenancy (Section 3), not a title. No pad-rental park is counted, listed or priced in this file, because this is a land file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file**. The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — directly relevant to this topic: placing a NEW manufactured home on the buyer's own Tsawwassen lot is the own-land case, not a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. Anchor checks: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M**. No sibling of this slug carries the $78,000 figure or the wrong "3% to $3M, 5% above" shorthand — checked and stated in §5. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata, as at Beachcomber Cottages).** Form B diligence (the lot's strata fee, amounts owed, approved levies, CRF balance, parking/storage and insurance summary; the strata must provide the Form B within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements

The Delta ZONING bylaw number is CONTESTED in this program and is never asserted here — the research file's zone sheets and minimums are carried in §1 as the research states them, and the parcel's zone is confirmed on the City zoning maps. https://hdp-ca-prod-app-delta-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/8917/7677/0068/OCP_2024_web.pdf and https://maps.delta.ca/Utilities/ZoningPDF/RS1.pdf
- **SSMUH / added units.** Delta's small-scale multi-unit housing bylaws are Bylaws 8394 and 8395 (adopted June 24, 2024): Delta allows up to four units on all single-detached and duplex lots — added-unit permission is not permission to subdivide, and subdivision still runs against the zone minimums in §1. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies** — it is a lot-zoning framework, not a park-tenancy right. https://delta.civicweb.net/document/235301/ and http://www.delta.ca/building-development/housing-our-future

A specific parcel's reserve status is checked on the ALC maps by PID or civic address. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/application-and-notice-process/applications/required-documents/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is a Tsawwassen "lot" with a manufactured home on it actually land I own?** Only if title says so. If the home sits on a rented pad, **you own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad — that is not land ownership**, and this topic file counts no such ground as land. If the home sits on a fee-simple lot or a bare-land strata lot (the Beachcomber Cottages form, documented in its lots home), you own land and the purchase runs on title. Check title, tenure and any Manufactured Home Registry record before pricing. (Section 2; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes)
- **Q: Can I place a new manufactured home on a Tsawwassen lot I buy?** That is the own-land structure the Province's newly built home exemption language expressly contemplates — "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — subject to the parcel's zone, any SD2/SD3 hazard permit area, streamside setbacks within 30 m of a stream, and, on an ALR parcel, the 500 m² principal-residence cap and ALC rules. Confirm the exact structure with the City, a lawyer/notary and the lender before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up, if I buy on a pad elsewhere in Delta?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). A Tsawwassen lot purchase has no pad rent at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does a park have to approve me as a buyer?** In a pad-rental park, yes — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent. On a lot purchase there is no park owner to approve you; a bare-land strata purchase binds you to the bylaws from completion instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Can I subdivide a farm parcel near Tsawwassen for a family manufactured home?** Only within tight limits as the research states them: a new A1 lot needs 8 ha, OCP compliance and approval under the Agricultural Land Commission Act where applicable, A3 zoning does not permit subdivision, and reserve land generally needs ALC permission to subdivide. https://maps.delta.ca/Utilities/ZoningPDF/A1.pdf
- **Q: What extra rules apply to a bluff or beach lot for a manufactured home?** On the west shore, DPA SD2 addresses mud flows, debris flows, erosion and land slip, with development subject to a Professional Engineer's conditions and a 7.5 m setback from the natural boundary of the sea; on Boundary Bay, SD3 sets a 2.9 m datum floor level, a 2.3 m minimum land surface elevation and a 7.5 m tidal setback — confirm both with the City before writing, not from a listing's wording. https://hdp-ca-prod-app-delta-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/8917/7677/0068/OCP_2024_web.pdf
- **Q: Which schools serve a Tsawwassen lot?** Catchments are address-based in SD37 and follow the parcel's address — verify the specific parcel address in the SD37 School Locator before marketing school access; no locator run for a lot address is on record in this file, so any specific catchment is UNVERIFIED here. https://www.deltasd.bc.ca/schools/school-locator/
- **Q: Are there market statistics for Tsawwassen land or mobile homes?** No figure is stated in this file: SnapStats does not cover South Delta / Tsawwassen and publishes no vacant-land or mobile-home category, so land counts, price per acre and days on market are UNVERIFIED. ## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The same "land" search spans two incompatible rule sets.** An urban lot's potential is set by its zone minimum (as low as 335 m² for a new RS7 lot) and the four-unit added-housing permission, while a farm parcel's potential is set by the 8 ha A1 subdivision minimum and the ALC's housing and subdivision rules — price the parcel in front of you against its own system, never the other one's. https://maps.delta.ca/Utilities/ZoningPDF/RS4.pdf and https://maps.delta.ca/Utilities/ZoningPDF/A1.pdf
- **Hazard overlays sort the urban stock more than street names do.** West-shore lots carry SD2 geotechnical conditions and the 7.5 m sea setback, and Boundary Bay lots carry SD3 floor-level and tidal-setback rules, before any design work starts — including where the home to be placed is a manufactured home. https://hdp-ca-prod-app-delta-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/8917/7677/0068/OCP_2024_web.pdf
- **Delta's flood system is the quiet diligence file.** The City is the diking authority and operates and maintains over 63 kilometres of flood protection infrastructure, 31 drainage pump stations and approximately 538 kilometres of drainage infrastructure, and every property within 30 m of a stream, including sloughs and ditches, falls within the Streamside Protection and Enhancement Development Permit Area — the setback check applies on farmland ditches as well as urban lots. http://www.delta.ca/services/water-sewer-drainage/sanitary-sewer-drainage and https://www.delta.ca/sites/default/files/2023-06/Form%20T1%20-%20SPEA%20DPA%20Application%20Guide%20-%20May%202023.pdf
