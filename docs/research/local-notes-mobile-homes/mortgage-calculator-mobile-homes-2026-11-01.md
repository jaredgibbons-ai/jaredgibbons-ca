## 2. THE PARKS AND THE HOMES

**Topic file — NO park roster, stated plainly at the top. Verified parks counted here: 0.** A calculator page holds no stock. What this section documents is the one thing a manufactured-home buyer must understand before using any payment tool, including this one: the tool's published rules are written for land-title purchases, and the two manufactured tenures price differently underneath the same arithmetic.

### The tenure split the calculator does not show

- **A home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home as a chattel and rents the pad.** A chattel purchase has no land component: the loan is against the home alone, the pad rent is a continuing tenancy cost on top of any loan payment, and the transfer registers in the Manufactured Home Registry, not on land title. A monthly-payment estimate that shows only the loan payment understates a pad buyer's true monthly carry by the whole pad rent — and no pad-rent dollar figure is published as fact in this file, because pad rents are park-specific and none is documented in this slug's research. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **A manufactured home on the buyer's OWN land is a land-home purchase.** The mortgage is against home and land together on title, the strata block in Section 3 applies where the lot is a bare-land strata lot, and the calculator's insured-mortgage rules (down-payment tiers, premium, stress test) are the rules written for exactly this form. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Financing treatment by tenure — UNVERIFIED at official level, stated honestly:** lender and mortgage-insurer rules for manufactured homes (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in this program. The research for this slug verifies the general insured-mortgage framework (Section 3) — it does NOT verify that any specific chattel home qualifies under it. Confirm financing for the specific home and tenure with the lender before removing subjects; do not let a calculator output stand in for that confirmation. (Standing brief; research file above.)
- **The price bands shape what the tool must branch on:** at $500,000 the down-payment tier changes; at $1,500,000 insurance disappears and 20% down becomes mandatory; at 20% down the insurance premium disappears and the amortization menu widens — three hard branch points any correct calculator has to encode. Manufactured homes sit at the lower end of the price spectrum, so the first branch point — the 5% / 5%-plus-10% tier split at $500,000 — is the one a park-home or own-land manufactured buyer meets most often. (Research file above, §5.) https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **What the tool does NOT decide:** a calculator computes payments and guideline ratios; it does not approve a mortgage. OSFI's framework leaves the underwriting decision with the federally regulated lender applying Guideline B-20, and the insurer (CMHC or another approved insurer) sets its own eligibility on insured loans — the tool's output is an estimate to take to a lender or broker, not a qualification. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

### The MHPTA block — the pad side of the purchase this calculator cannot price

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file**. The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption

### The calculator's rule set — re-read on official pages Nov 1, 2026

- **How interest is calculated — semi-annual compounding.** Under the federal Interest Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. I-15, s.6, where principal and interest secured by a mortgage on real property are payable on a blended-payment plan, no interest is chargeable unless the mortgage contains a statement showing the principal and the rate of interest chargeable on it, "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" — the statutory basis for the Canadian convention that fixed mortgage rates are stated as nominal rates compounded semi-annually, which is why a Canadian payment calculation converts the nominal rate to a monthly rate using semi-annual compounding rather than dividing by 12. The live page states the same convention for fixed rates; its variable-rate monthly-compounding line is a lender-practice statement, not a statutory rule, and is UNVERIFIED as a rule. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **Minimum down payment (CMHC).** For homes $500,000 or less, a minimum 5% down payment; for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require a minimum down payment of 20% and mortgage loan insurance is not available. CMHC's published worked example: on a $750,000 home the required minimum is $25,000 (5% of $500,000) plus $25,000 (10% of $250,000) = $50,000; a buyer with $60,000 saved (8% down) still needs mortgage loan insurance because the down payment is under 20%. The Department of Finance Canada confirms the same structure as the insured-mortgage rule in effect with the December 15, 2024 cap increase. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Mortgage loan insurance (CMHC) — thresholds and premiums.** CMHC states mortgage loan insurance is required in Canada when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the home's purchase price; the insurance protects the lender, not the buyer, and allows a buyer to qualify with a down payment as low as 5%. Insurance is needed where the down payment is less than 20% AND the home price is $1.5 million or less; it is not available at all on homes priced over $1.5 million. Premiums as published by CMHC: based on the size of the down payment and the loan amount — the smaller the down payment, the higher the premium rate — **premium rates range from 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount**, payable in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. CMHC's worked example: a $690,000 mortgage (on the $750,000 purchase above, $60,000 down) at a 4% premium rate carries a $27,600 premium. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained

That range does not match CMHC's own published range of 0.6% to 4.5% re-read for this slug, and the 0.20% 30-year surcharge was not confirmed on a CMHC or Finance page in this pass. **This file adopts CMHC's published range only; the live page's range and the surcharge are UNVERIFIED and must not be quoted to a buyer as CMHC's figures.** The other two approved insurers (Sagen and Canada Guaranty) are named on the live page; their premium schedules were not read in this pass and no figure from them is stated here. - **The mortgage stress test — OSFI Guideline B-20 / minimum qualifying rate.** OSFI's minimum qualifying rate (MQR) for uninsured mortgages is currently **the greater of the mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25%** — the buffer is currently 2% and the floor currently 5.25%, both reviewed by OSFI at least annually. OSFI obliges federally regulated lenders to apply the test, and Guideline B-20 sets the expectation that lenders apply the MQR to most newly underwritten residential mortgagors. Insured mortgages are tested the same way: FCAC states federally regulated entities require borrowers to pass a stress test at the higher of 5.25% or the contract rate plus 2%, and that "that's the case for insured and uninsured mortgages". https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **The renewal-switch exception, and what re-triggers the test.** OSFI does not expect lenders to apply the MQR to an uninsured straight switch at renewal — a switch to another federally regulated lender with no increase to the amortization period and no increase to the loan amount. FCAC adds the related triggers: a borrower must pass the stress test again on a refinance or on taking out a home equity line of credit. Provincially regulated lenders (e.g., credit unions) are not federally regulated; FCAC notes lenders that aren't federally regulated may also ask a borrower to pass a stress test — whether any given BC credit union applies it is lender-specific and UNVERIFIED here. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Amortization rules.** Insured mortgages are those with less than 20% down, available only on homes priced at $1.5 million or less (the insured price cap, raised from $1 million effective December 15, 2024). Eligibility for 30-year insured amortizations extends to **all first-time homebuyers** and to **all buyers of new builds**, effective December 15, 2024, building on the August 1, 2024 measure for first-time buyers purchasing new builds. The exact general insured maximum (25 years) for insured borrowers outside those two groups was NOT stated on a regulator page re-read in this pass and is NOT asserted as a verified figure here; the Department of Finance's detailed first-time-homebuyer criteria for the 30-year measure were likewise not re-read — UNVERIFIED, and no definition is encoded from secondary sources. The live page's statement that 30 years is available to any buyer at 20%+ down on an uninsured mortgage is a lender-practice statement, not a rule re-read here. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **Affordability ratios — GDS and TDS (FCAC).** Gross Debt Service: total monthly housing costs should not be more than 39% of gross household income; monthly housing costs are the mortgage payments, property taxes, heating costs and 50% of condo fees (if applicable). Total Debt Service: total debt load should not be more than 44% of gross income — monthly housing costs plus all other debts. FCAC frames 39% and 44% as guidelines a borrower should stay within, not hard approval cut-offs — a borrower may still qualify with ratios slightly higher but increases the risk of taking on more debt than they can afford; FCAC's own Mortgage Qualifier Tool uses the same 39% / 44%. The live page's GDS/TDS description matches the FCAC composition (principal and interest, property taxes, heating estimate, 50% of strata fees) — consistent with the official source. Whether a lender counts PAD RENT inside these ratios for a chattel loan is not stated on any official page read in this pass: UNVERIFIED — confirm with the lender. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html and https://itools-ioutils.fcac-acfc.gc.ca/mq-hq/MQCalc-EAPHCalc-eng.aspx
- **PTT general rates — closing-cost cross-reference only (the live page defers PTT to the First-Time Buyer Calculator).** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential property value greater than $3,000,000. PTT is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office and is separate from annual property taxes. Worked examples, inputs and tier math stated: $500,000 → 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $300,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**; $900,000 → **$16,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $3,500,000 fully residential → **$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** (tier math: $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000). The exemptions (first-time buyers', newly built home) are researched in the companion first-time-buyer-calculator file, not restated here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

For a manufactured home specifically, at the general level CRA sources support: GST applies to new construction, not resales — anything finer for a specific home is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established. **First-home program anchors, carried at the program's settled figures:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; GST New Housing Rebate 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000; Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What rate does the stress test use?** The greater of your mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25% — OSFI's minimum qualifying rate, applied by federally regulated lenders to insured and uninsured mortgages alike; OSFI reviews the 2% buffer and the 5.25% floor at least annually. Run the payment twice: once at your contract rate (what you will actually pay) and once at the qualifying rate, because the second number is the one the lender uses to size your approval. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: What is the minimum down payment in Canada?** 5% on homes of $500,000 or less; on homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000; at $1,500,000 or more, a minimum of 20% — and mortgage loan insurance is not available at that level. Know which side of $500,000 your price sits on: on CMHC's own $750,000 example the minimum is $50,000, not the $37,500 a flat 5% would suggest. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: When do I need mortgage loan insurance, and what does it cost?** Whenever your down payment is under 20% on a home priced at $1.5 million or less. CMHC publishes premium rates ranging from **0.6% to 4.5%** of the total mortgage amount, depending on the down payment size and loan amount; the premium can be paid up front or added to the mortgage — and if it is added, it raises both the balance and the payment the stress test is applied to. Note the defect in Section 3: this site's calculator page states a different range (2.80%–4.00% + 0.20%) that does not match CMHC's published range — budget from CMHC's figures, not the page's. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: Can I get a 30-year amortization?** On an insured mortgage (under 20% down), 30-year amortizations have been available to all first-time homebuyers and to all buyers of new builds since December 15, 2024; on an uninsured mortgage (20% or more down) 30 years is a lender offering rather than an insured-program rule, so it is confirmed with the lender, not the calculator. A longer amortization lowers the payment at any given rate and raises total interest over the life of the loan. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Q: How much of my income can go to housing and debt?** FCAC's guidelines: total monthly housing costs (mortgage payments, property taxes, heating, 50% of condo fees) should not exceed 39% of gross household income (GDS), and total debt load including all other debts should not exceed 44% (TDS); the ratios are calculated at the qualifying rate, not your contract rate, and slightly higher ratios may still qualify but carry more risk. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: I'm buying a home in a manufactured home park — can I just use this calculator?** Use it for the loan arithmetic only, with two cautions. - **Q: Does the park have to approve my purchase before I finalize financing?** In a pad-rental park, the tenancy side runs on its own track: assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent — and your seller must give you the written tenancy agreement and park rules before asking. Build that consent step into your subject timeline alongside the financing condition. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up while I'm paying down the loan?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). That percentage cap, not a dollar figure, is the honest way to model pad-rent growth in a long-term budget. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: At renewal, will I face the stress test again?** Not on a qualifying uninsured straight switch: OSFI does not expect the MQR to be applied where you switch an uninsured mortgage to another federally regulated lender with no increase to the loan amount or amortization. A refinance or a new HELOC re-triggers the test — model the two paths separately before you renew. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: What closing costs sit outside the mortgage math?** BC property transfer tax on the general tiers in Section 3 (for example $500,000 → $8,000 before any exemption), 5% GST on a newly built home, and the legal/notary, title insurance, inspection and tax-adjustment categories — the live page's $1,500–$2,500 legal/notary range is UNVERIFIED as a figure, so budget those items from quotes, not from the page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
