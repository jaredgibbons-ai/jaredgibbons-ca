### Manufactured homes on farm ground — ONLY as ALC sources support

- **The general position:** a manufactured home on a farmer's OWN fee-simple farm parcel (where ALC and zoning permit it) is own-land form, not a pad tenancy: the buyer buys land + home, land-title and Manufactured Home Registry handling both apply, and the ALR residence caps in §3 govern what may be built or placed. It is a different stock from a park home on a rented pad.
- **Route 1 — ALR Use Regulation s.34(3) / ALC Policy L-25:** a manufactured home used as a residence in the ALR is addressed through the ALR Use Regulation s.34(3) manufactured-home residence provisions and ALC Policy L-25, with the Non-Adhering Residential Use application route where the use does not adhere to the regulation — the poultry-farms treatment in this program is the model for this route. Whether any specific Ladner Rural parcel qualifies: UNCONFIRMED parcel by parcel — confirm with the ALC before pricing a placement. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Route 2 — Delta A1 migrant farm worker housing (the one place this slug's own research expressly requires manufactured homes):** in the A1 zone, migrant farm worker housing is permitted only where, among other criteria, the lot is in the Agricultural Land Reserve with farming as a permitted use, the lot is classified as a farm under the Assessment Act, written ALC approval has been obtained, the farm employing the workers is at least 8 ha and the lot is at least 4 ha; **new buildings for it must be manufactured homes on a temporary foundation or footings**. - **A general farm-worker manufactured-housing entitlement: UNCONFIRMED.** Beyond the A1 migrant-housing criteria and the s.34(3) / L-25 route above, no source opened for this file establishes a general right to place manufactured housing for farm workers on Delta farm ground — do not assume one. - **A1 residential frame the manufactured routes sit inside:** one farm house per lot is permitted, with a maximum total floor area of 500 m²; the farm home plate maximum is 3,600 m² for a farm house with or without an accessory farm dwelling unit (5,000 m² where an additional farm house is also on the plate); one accessory farm dwelling unit per lot is permitted, at a maximum of 90 m² on lots of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on lots over 40 ha, located on the same farm home plate as the farm house, and ALC approval is required for more than one accessory farm dwelling unit; an additional farm house (180 m² maximum on lots under 8 ha, 233 m² on lots of 8 ha or more) requires a Professional Agrologist's report supporting its necessity and written ALC approval. Accessory uses include a farm house accessory to farming, one secondary suite accessory to a farm house, an accessory farm dwelling unit, migrant farm worker housing, and equestrian facilities to a maximum of 40 permanent stalls accessory to farming. https://maps.delta.ca/Utilities/ZoningPDF/A1.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (no pad purchase exists in Ladner Rural; carried in full as the standing block).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns — the farm-parcel form here — is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director — the decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator"), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file, and no pad range is documented in this slug's own research, so none is mentioned at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date, and may dispute within the 15-day dispute window; additional compensation in the amount of the assessed value of the home (less the $20,000) and the further $5,000 / 12-months'-pad-rent amounts apply only as the provincial pages state them. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. This registry step applies to a manufactured home placed on the buyer's own farm parcel as well as to a park home — the land transfers on title, the home registers in the Registry. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **ALR RULES IN FULL — this file's core rule set for the farm ground.** Subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s. 6; Strata Property Act, s. 239; Agricultural Land Commission Act, s. 21(1)). Residence caps: one principal residence per parcel, maximum 500 m² total floor area, with a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; one additional detached residence is permitted without an application at 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller and 186 m² or less on larger parcels, where Delta bylaws also permit it. A specific parcel's ALR status is checked on the ALC maps by PID or civic address; the ALC states its online tools are for general reference only, with official confirmation from its Mapping and GIS team. Delta's A1 subdivision rule sits on top of this: in the A1 zone (s. 10.10.8), subdivision is not permitted except where it complies with the Official Community Plan Bylaw and has been approved under the Agricultural Land Commission Act if applicable, and the minimum lot size that may be created by subdivision is 8 ha, with a home site severance exception under ALC policy. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps and https://maps.delta.ca/Utilities/ZoningPDF/A1.pdf
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — full three-tier form, never a flat figure.** Gross annual farm income required: $2,500 on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the farm operation for farm purposes in excess of 4 ha; $10,000 on an operation under 0.8 ha — applications by October 31 for the next taxation year. A flat "$2,500+" line is an error wherever met.
