## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **The open-house rule that governs this topic — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), carried as this slug's research re-verified it at source:** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, provides that a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. In practice: chatting factually with the host does not by itself make you their client — but once you share your motivation, budget or needs with a licensee who represents the seller, the disclosure rules engage. BCFSA states that a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Identification / sign-in:** an identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors is NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED as a rule (a host may still ask, on the seller's instructions; that practice is not in an official source). https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (carried in full as the standing block; it governs any park home a visitor might view, none being documented on this feed).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** On a park-home sale the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser; before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Prior written consent (or deemed consent, or an order of the **director** — never "arbitrator"), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; response must reach the home owner by the 10th day after the request is received; silence by end of day 10 = deemed consent; NO fee or benefit may be charged for consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes tenant on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Part of the tenancy agreement; changeable only once a year with 3 full months' notice; enforceable only if fairly applied to all tenants, clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, properly noticed, and not changing a material term. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula (BC all-items CPI 12-month average change + proportional amount). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure anywhere in this file — none is documented in this slug's research, so none is mentioned. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42).** All necessary permits/approvals BEFORE notice, 12 months' notice, $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer detail UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A manufactured-home sale/transfer/purchase is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; original signed Bill of Sale + Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership filed through a qualified supplier (lawyer/notary); CSA certification label must be attached at point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** Assessed/taxed like other residential property; assessed value excludes the land value when the home is in a park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner; Home Owner Grant available for a principal residence, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles PTT on a home-only purchase on a rented pad; **no flat claim either way is stated as fact** (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The Province states expressly that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a NEW home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer/notary must confirm for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates (own-land / strata-lot transfers — the form of every listing documented on this feed).** 1% first $200,000, 2% $200,000–$2M, 3% above $2M, plus further 2% on the residential portion above $3M. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**.
