## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA block, carried in full as the standing block (it governs any pad-rental comparable behind this feed).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the response must reach the home owner by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of day 10 is deemed consent, and no fee or benefit may be charged for consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes tenant on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula (BC all-items CPI 12-month average change + proportional amount = change in local government levies + change in regulated park utilities, divided by number of sites). Verified live from RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated anywhere in this file — and, under this program's cleaner discipline, no pad range is mentioned at all in this topic file, because no pad figure is documented in this slug's own research. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** Converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park requires all necessary government permits and approvals BEFORE issuing notice, 12 months' notice, and $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer detail UNCONFIRMED beyond cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; the original signed Bill of Sale + Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (lawyer/notary); the CSA certification label must be attached at point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** Assessed/taxed like other residential property; assessed value excludes the land value when the home is in a park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner; Home Owner Grant available for a principal residence, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles PTT on a home-only purchase on a rented pad; NO flat claim either way is stated as fact. The Province states expressly that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a NEW home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer/notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates (own-land / strata-lot transfers — the transfers behind most cards on this page).** 1% first $200,000, 2% $200,000–$2M, 3% above $2M, plus further 2% on the residential portion above $3M. Worked example, inputs stated: $700,000 fully residential, no exemption = $2,000 + $10,000 = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 FTHB figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier** (tier math: $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000). The $78,000-for-$3.5M figure lives in the south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m TOWNHOMES sibling; reconciliation OPEN — follow $93,000. **This topic's own acreages sibling actually meets the $78,000 figure** (it names that townhomes sibling and follows $93,000), so it is flagged here as met-in-sibling; no sold-properties sibling asserts $78,000 as its own result. The townhomes siblings' "3% to $3M, 5% above" shorthand is WRONG — never carried; it is NOT met in any sold-properties sibling checked. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax (~/workspace/local-notes/acreages/sold-properties-acreages-2026-11-01.md)
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 + up to 25¢/page); depreciation reports 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01, with spouse/caregiver/legacy exemptions incl. May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Who owns and runs the MLS® system (this topic's governing frame).** The Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA), a trade association, owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service® ("MLS®") and REALTOR®, and comprises over 100,000 brokers and agents across 100 local boards and associations; to use the MLS® system agents must be CREA members and comply with CREA's rules, and must also be members of a local board and adhere to both CREA's rules (such as the REALTOR® Code) and their local board's rules. For this site, the boards named in the page's own Reciprocity notice are GVR, FVREB and CADREB. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage (research file §4)
- **The 2010 CREA consent agreement — cited as a governance milestone only.** In early 2010 the Commissioner of Competition applied to the Competition Tribunal alleging CREA abused a dominant position; in October 2010 a consent agreement resolved the concerns, registered for a 10-year period, requiring CREA not to adopt, maintain or enforce rules denying agents the ability to provide mere postings or discriminating against agents who offer them. Careful reading: that agreement was about listing services and display of a seller's information — it is NOT the source of any current BC sold-data display rule, and what followed its 10-year term is UNVERIFIED in this file. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Sold-data display — the documented precedent is Ontario's, not BC's.** The Competition Bureau's TREB litigation concerned VOW policies prohibiting display of sold and "pending sold" homes and other listing states; the Tribunal rejected TREB's privacy rationale as a pretext and required certain data fields to be provided in VOW feeds and displayable on any device. Careful reading: a Greater Toronto Area VOW precedent — NOT a statement of any FVREB/GVR/CADREB rule. **No BC board's sold-display rule is stated in this file, because neither board publishes its MLS® rules on a public page that could be read for this research — UNVERIFIED.** Whether sold listings specifically require a separate board permission or form in FVREB/GVR is likewise UNVERIFIED. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **The BC regulator's consumer rules around any sale on this page.** Real estate trading services in BC are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients and of the risks for unrepresented consumers, and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
