## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **What Surrey counts as a secondary suite (City page, opened in this slug's research).** A dwelling unit that is an accessory use, with habitable floor area not exceeding 40% of the principal dwelling unit, within a single family, semi-detached or duplex residential building, and not a separate strata lot. The current cap is the 40% rule in Surrey Zoning Bylaw No. 12000; a "90 m²" cap is NOT current and is not stated as a rule anywhere in this file. Only one secondary suite is permitted per single family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit; a secondary suite is not permitted within a coach house or garden suite; one suite may be permitted in dwellings within small-scale, multi-unit housing zones, including Comprehensive Development zones; and each application is reviewed for legal restrictions such as Land Use Contracts and Building Schemes. To become legal the owner must meet the conditions of zoning, hold the necessary building permits, meet the BC Building Code, and pay the secondary suite utility and service fees. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- **SSMUH frame (City page).** Surrey adopted its small-scale multi-unit housing zoning changes on July 8, 2024 — a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop; the new zoning does not retroactively legalize suites built without permits. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** Servicing: Surrey Subdivision and Development Bylaw No. 8830 (1986) in the verified ledger. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase (contrast: it does not govern a duplex purchase).** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. A half-duplex purchase is a land-title purchase — no MHPTA assignment, no park-owner consent, no pad tenancy. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy (pad purchases only).** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the **director** — never "arbitrator"), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. No "arbitrator" wording appears in this slug's research; the director position governs. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file — none is documented in this slug's own research, so none is mentioned at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the M1 files state them, with the 15-day dispute window. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure. A half-duplex purchase is an ordinary land transfer for PTT purposes under the general rates below. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is a half duplex with a suite a mobile home?** No. A half duplex is one half of a two-unit building bought through the land title system (fee-simple style / undivided interest, or a strata lot); a manufactured home is a factory-built home bought as a chattel on a rented pad, as a bare-land strata lot, or on its own lot, and registered in the Manufactured Home Registry. The two stocks never merge. (§2 above.)
- **Q: How big can a secondary suite be in a Surrey duplex?** Its habitable floor area cannot exceed 40% of the principal dwelling unit, it must sit within the duplex building, and it cannot be a separate strata lot. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- **Q: Can a Surrey half duplex have two secondary suites?** No — only one secondary suite is permitted per single family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit, and a suite is not permitted inside a coach house or garden suite. A card advertising both a legal suite and a second guest suite needs the second unit's status explained in writing before subjects come off. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- **Q: The listing says the suite is "legal" — can I rely on that?** No. Verify the suite, don't trust the listing word: pull the City registration status and the permit history — the page itself says a listing note is not proof of permits, and Surrey's new zoning does not legalize an unpermitted suite retroactively. The permitted status of any individual suite on this page is UNVERIFIED. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **Q: Is there a duplex-only sales figure for South Surrey?** No. SnapStats publishes Detached and Condos & Townhomes splits only — September 2026 community medians were $1,628,000 detached and $700,000 for Condos & Townhomes — and no duplex-only figure exists in program sources. Do not price from a "duplex median" — none exists; price from the property's own half-duplex comparables. (SnapStats FVR September 2026 report; research file §2.)
- **Q: What time is the open house?** Confirm the time with the listing brokerage before going: on this feed, the top time block and the listing-text time disagreed on every card that showed both (§5). https://jaredgibbons.ca/south-surrey-duplexes-with-suites-for-sale.html
- **Q: If I buy in a park instead, do I own the land?** In a pad-rental park, no: **you own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad** — the park owns the land, and your right to the site is the assigned pad tenancy under the MHPTA. That is the opposite tenure from this feed's duplexes, where the purchase includes the land interest. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up (for a park purchase, not a duplex)?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does a park have to approve me as a buyer?** Yes, in a pad-rental park — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent, requested on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent. No equivalent approval step exists on an ordinary duplex purchase. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Can I get a mortgage on a park home, or on an undivided-interest duplex?** UNVERIFIED at official-source level in this program for both forms — chattel financing treatment and undivided-interest lending are lender-specific. Confirm financing for the specific home and its exact tenure before removing subjects.
