## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director," never "arbitrator." https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer closure detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. City of Mission taxes carry the FLAT 10% penalty in the taxes bullet below; a park parcel whose City/FVRD jurisdiction is UNVERIFIED (§2) cannot be assigned a penalty regime until its jurisdiction is confirmed — the rural regime is NOT applied to it by default. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file** (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers, including a Mission condo in this band).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked examples, inputs stated (fully residential, no exemption unless stated): $500,000 = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $300,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**; $800,000 (this band's cap) = $2,000 + 2% × $600,000 ($12,000) = **$14,000** — and because the band caps at $800,000, a qualifying first-time buyer anywhere in the band sits under the $835,000 full-exemption value ceiling, so the **$6,000 FTHB-cap example** applies at that price point for a fully qualifying buyer (exemption on the first $500,000; confirm the buyer's own conditions); $1,000,000 = **$18,000**; $1,200,000 = **$22,000**; $1,500,000 = **$28,000**; $1,800,000 = **$34,000**; $2,000,000 = **$38,000**; $3,500,000 fully residential = **$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** — the $78,000 figure lives ONLY in the south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m TOWNHOMES sibling; it was NOT met in this slug's own siblings (grep-checked), so it is not flagged against this slug, reconciliation remains OPEN program-wide, and $93,000 is followed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I'm shopping this page for a condo under $800K — is a park home the same purchase?** No. This band's feed is apartment condos (strata lots, Form B, strata fees). A Mission park home is a different purchase entirely: **you own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad**, under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, with no strata lot and no Form B. The parks are cross-referenced in §2 only so the two forms are never confused. (Sections 2–3.)
- **Q: Does "under $800K" tell me which Mission condos or parks are in my budget?** No — SnapStats publishes no band cut and no condo-only cut, and this file counts 0 buildings and 0 parks in the band. Every band status is UNCONFIRMED per parcel; the live feed is the only place current in-band supply can be seen. (Section 1; research file §2.)
- **Q: Can a first-time buyer use the PTT exemption under $800K in Mission?** In principle, yes — the full exemption runs to a fair market value of $835,000 (exempting the first $500,000 of price), with a partial exemption to $860,000, provided the buyer meets all the program's personal and property conditions; at the band's $800,000 cap the worked figure for a fully qualifying buyer is $6,000 (§3). Confirm the buyer's own position with a legal professional before writing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up, if I buy in a Mission park instead?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). Park-wide pad rent at Mission MHP, Oasis Country Estates and Nicomen Park is UNCONFIRMED — confirm the specific pad's figure in its tenancy documents. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does the park have to approve me as a buyer?** In a pad-rental park, yes — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent. A condo purchase needs no strata-council approval to buy, though the bylaws bind the buyer from completion. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Is Oasis Country Estates a 55+ park?** Only in part, on the sources: listings distinguish an "adult side" from a "family section," and no source checked states the adult side's exact age number — calling the whole park "55+" is UNCONFIRMED. Mission MHP's 55+ position is listing-stated; Nicomen Park is a family park. Get the specific park's current written rules. (Section 2.)
- **Q: How is a park-home purchase registered, and why does the CSA label matter?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA certification label must be attached at the point of sale. A condo purchase registers on land title instead — a different system entirely. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: What happens if a Mission park is sold or redeveloped?** For a conversion of all or a significant part of the park, the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date. Search the City's development applications for the park's address before buying. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Can I get a mortgage on a park home?** UNVERIFIED at official-source level in this program — chattel financing treatment, home-age and foundation requirements are lender-specific. Confirm financing for the specific home and its pad tenure before removing subjects. (Standing brief.)
- **Q: Which schools serve a Mission condo address?** Catchments are address-based in SD75 and only the downtown sample (7466 Welton St → École Mission Central Elementary / Heritage Park Middle School / Mission Senior Secondary) is on record — single-address only. Run the exact condo address in SD75's locator. https://www.mpsd.ca
