### Manufactured homes on farm ground — the sourced positions only

- **Additional manufactured home on a farm: family occupancy only.** Under the ALR Use Regulation's additional-residence provisions (s. 34(3) as summarised in ALC Policy L-25), an additional residence that is a manufactured home and not a pre-existing structure is permitted only if it is 9 m or less in width and is used only by the owner or specified relatives of the owner (parent, grandparent or great-grandparent, sibling, or child, within the regulation's meaning). ALC Policy L-25 adds that a manufactured home permitted as an additional residence may only be occupied by the property owner or the owner's immediate family, and if it is no longer occupied as permitted it must be demolished, removed, or converted to a non-residential use. That is an occupancy rule in the opposite direction from a park: it restricts WHO may live in the home; it creates no tenancy, no pads and no park. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf (~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/poultry-farms-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md)
- **Outside those conditions, ALC permission is the route.** An additional residence that does not fit the permitted conditions needs ALC permission through a Non-Adhering Residential Use application, and the Commission must not grant it unless the residence is necessary for a farm use. Any farm-worker housing proposal for a specific poultry operation therefore runs through that necessity test on that parcel — it is not a listing assumption and not a bylaw entitlement stated in this file. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_policy_l-26_-_non-adhering_residential_use_applications.pdf (55-plus sibling above)
- **What is actually being bought (the farm package a manufactured home would sit inside):** land (usually ALR), the barn package (barns are "necessary farm use structures" for an intensive livestock operation under the ALR Use Regulation), the residence, and quota where the seller includes it — note the Abbotsford West listing behind the current pages states quota is not included, which is a common structure. Operation types: broiler grower operations (the BCCMB sector), broiler hatching egg (breeder) operations, turkey operations, and layer (egg) operations — each under its own board and quota system. Tenure: operations sit on freehold farm land, almost always in the ALR in the Fraser Valley belt; the licence side is annual — hatcheries, growers, transporters and processors are licensed by the BCCMB on an annual basis. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html and https://bcchicken.ca and https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf (research file §5)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **ALR RULES IN FULL — the governing frame for a manufactured home on Fraser Valley poultry ground:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps as the ALC states them: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m²; a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Additional manufactured-home residence — the mobile-specific ALR rule (carried from §2 because it decides this page type).** ALR Use Regulation s. 34(3) as summarised in ALC Policy L-25: a non-pre-existing manufactured home as an additional residence is permitted only at 9 m or less in width and only for occupancy by the owner or specified relatives; once it is no longer occupied as permitted it must be demolished, removed, or converted to a non-residential use; anything outside those conditions needs a Non-Adhering Residential Use approval the Commission must not grant unless the residence is necessary for a farm use. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_policy_l-26_-_non-adhering_residential_use_applications.pdf
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure:** $2,500 of farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the farm operation for farm purposes in excess of 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha — Classification of Land as a Farm Regulation, B.C. Reg. 411/95, s.5, with applications by October 31. https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf (~/workspace/local-notes/acreages/poultry-farms-acreages-2026-11-01.md)
- **The ALR barn rule.** Under the Agricultural Land Reserve Use Regulation, B.C. Reg. 30/2019, s.5, the use of agricultural land to construct, maintain or operate a structure, other than a residential structure, that is necessary for a farm use is designated as a farm use and, under s.4, may not be prohibited by a local government enactment; s.5 states for greater certainty that this includes a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation — the wording under which a poultry barn is treated as a farm building in the ALR. The ALC's guidance says the same in plain terms: such a structure may not be prohibited by a local government bylaw, but it may be regulated, and it must be shown to be required for the farm use on the property and in line with the level of that farm use. Precision carried from the research file: a rule stated in the words "poultry barn" in the ALR Use Regulation is UNVERIFIED — s.5 covers a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019 and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/763/2025/10/Necessary-Farm-Use-Structures-in-the-ALR-Guidelines.pdf
- **MHPTA — stated in full, and its boundary on farm ground stated plainly.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. **CORE TENURE LINE: a home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home (a chattel) and rents the pad.** On poultry ground this Act is normally NOT the frame: a farm's manufactured home is an ALR additional residence on the owner's own land (above), not a pad tenancy — the MHPTA block below governs only if the buyer separately buys into a park, which is town stock documented in other files, never on this topic. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy (park purchases only).** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser; before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (park purchases only).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence deemed consent, no fee or benefit for consent; on assignment the purchaser becomes tenant on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules (park purchases only).** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only with 3 full months' notice, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY (park purchases only).** Once every 12 months, at least 3 full months' notice, capped at inflation (12-month average change in the BC all-items CPI) plus a proportional amount (change in local government levies plus change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of sites): verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (park purchases only) — only as official sources support.** Permits and approvals first, 12 months' notice to end tenancy, $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; early move-out on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry — applies to a manufactured home on farm ground too.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. Property tax: the owner is assessed and taxed like other residential owners, excluding the land value where the home is in a park not on reserve land or on another's land; a principal-residence manufactured home can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **The chicken board — from THIS slug's own opened sources only.** The British Columbia Chicken Marketing Board (BCCMB) is a non-profit agricultural commodity board that monitors and regulates the production and marketing of chicken in British Columbia; its jurisdiction covers all activities of broiler chicken production in BC from the time the day-old chick arrives on the farm, and its mandate includes overseeing policy, managing risk, and upholding national programs for supply management, food safety and animal welfare. In its own reporting its role covers setting provincial chicken prices, determining quota, allocating quota to growers, managing the quota system and collecting levies, for the period from the day-old chick arriving on farm until the chicken arrives at the processing plant; hatcheries, growers, transporters and processors are licensed by the board on an annual basis. https://bcchicken.ca and https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf (research file §4)
- **What broiler quota is, who counts as a grower, and how quota moves — BCCMB General Orders, named source.** Quota is the amount of chicken, expressed in kilograms of live weight, that the board allots to a grower for production and marketing; the General Orders name two classes — mainstream quota and specialty quota — with mainstream covering regular broilers, roasters, Cornish, or certified organic chicken. A "grower" is a person engaged in the production of chicken to whom the board has allotted quota or issued a permit that has not been cancelled; the Orders include parts on quota allotment to growers, transfer of quota with registered premises, and acquisition or disposition of quota without registered premises. The board's role is to approve quota transactions, ensuring growers are in good standing and production is allocated appropriately for each growing cycle, and quota transfers are facilitated privately rather than through a board-run central platform (board consultation paper). A quota exchange is in development (board Policy Directions Update: the board directed staff to develop a BC Chicken Quota Exchange platform) — whether the exchange is operating is NOT CONFIRMED. Do not price quota from listings: no official source publishes quota prices. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Policy-Directions-Update-Sep-2026.pdf
- **Hatching eggs and turkey — from THIS slug's own opened sources only.** The BC Broiler Hatching Egg Commission covers the step before the grower — its regulated product is a broiler hatching egg, a saleable chick or a broiler breeder; for mainstream production the system is hen-based (one quota unit equals one placeable broiler breeder hen) and for specialty production it is hatched chick-based (one quota unit equals one hatched chick). The British Columbia Turkey Marketing Board runs the turkey scheme; turkey quota in plain terms is the number of pounds of turkey meat a turkey producer is entitled to market in intraprovincial trade through normal marketing channels, or to have marketed on the producer's behalf by the board, during a specified period of time. Oversight: the BC Farm Industry Review Board is an independent administrative tribunal that supervises the regulated marketing system and reviews a board's decision on appeal, with appeals to be filed within 30 calendar days of the board's final decision. Egg (layer) sector quota transfer rules from the BC Egg Marketing Board's own published material: UNVERIFIED for this file (research file UNVERIFIED list). https://bcbhec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2022-Annual-Report.pdf and https://bcturkey.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/174_66.pdf and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/organizational-structure/ministries-organizations/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/npma-appeals
- **Municipal zoning — per labelled example only, never blended.** This topic covers BC as a whole and has no single municipality, so there is no one municipal zoning bylaw, OCP, SSMUH bylaw or servicing bylaw for it, and none is asserted topic-wide. Labelled example: Chilliwack's Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000 defines POULTRY as any domesticated fowl including chickens, turkeys, ducks, geese and swans, and lists General Agriculture as a permitted principal use in its AL, AU and AS zones. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies** and is a host-municipality lot framework — read in the host municipality's own file, never here. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf (research file §4)
- **Biosecurity and avian influenza — sale-timing rules a farm buyer cannot skip.** BC Gov poultry guidance defines biosecurity as management practices that reduce disease and pests entering a farm and limit their spread within and beyond the farm, using a Controlled Access Zone for the production area and a more restricted Restricted Access Zone where birds are housed. Highly pathogenic avian influenza is a reportable disease in Canada and cases must be reported to the CFIA; the CFIA establishes primary control zones where avian influenza has been detected and permits are required to move birds, their products and by-products through a control zone; on a farm under quarantine, no birds, bird products such as eggs, or bird by-products such as manure may enter or leave the property without CFIA permission — which can affect bird placement, showings and sale timing on a poultry farm, including any residence move tied to the sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/ai_enhanced_biosecurity_for_small_scale_poultry_producers.pdf and https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is there a mobile-home park on a poultry farm I can buy into?** No — not in any source in this program. Manufactured-home parks are a town product here; no park sits on a working poultry parcel in this topic's research or siblings. A manufactured home on a farm is an ALR additional residence under family-occupancy conditions, not a pad in a park. (Section 2; ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/poultry-farms-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md)
- **Q: Can I put a manufactured home on the farm for my parent, or for myself while the kids run the operation?** Possibly — the ALR Use Regulation permits an additional manufactured-home residence only within its conditions: 9 m or less in width, and occupied only by the owner or specified relatives (the regulation's list includes a parent). Outside those conditions, an additional residence needs ALC permission through a Non-Adhering Residential Use application, and the Commission must not grant it unless the residence is necessary for a farm use. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_policy_l-26_-_non-adhering_residential_use_applications.pdf
- **Q: Can I use a manufactured home on the farm for farm workers instead?** Only as official sources support — and they support no general entitlement in this file. The sourced routes are the family-occupancy additional residence (owner / specified relatives only) or a Non-Adhering Residential Use approval granted only where the residence is necessary for a farm use, decided parcel by parcel by the ALC. Anything broader is UNCONFIRMED — do not buy on the assumption that worker housing in a manufactured home is permitted as of right. (Section 3.)
- **Q: What happens to the manufactured home when the family member moves out?** Under ALC Policy L-25, a manufactured home permitted as an additional residence may only be occupied by the property owner or the owner's immediate family, and if it is no longer occupied as permitted it must be demolished, removed, or converted to a non-residential use. That condition survives resale and belongs in the purchase conversation, not after it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf
- **Q: What am I actually buying when I buy a "poultry farm"?** A package: fee-simple land (usually ALR), the barn package, the residence, and quota where the seller includes it — plus a step into an annually licensed grower role. Any one element can be excluded (the Abbotsford West feed listing states quota is not included). This file documents no roster — each operation is diligenced as its own package. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html
- **Q: Does the quota automatically come with the farm?** No. Quota transfers require board approval, and the BCCMB General Orders deal separately with transfer of quota with registered premises and acquisition or disposition of quota without registered premises — listings may also state quota is not included. Establish exactly what quota is in the deal, in writing: class (mainstream vs specialty), kilograms live weight, and which transfer case applies. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Q: The feed card says "poultry farm" — is it a working operation?** Not on the feed's word alone. - **Q: Can I stratify the farm, or split the residence off?** No, on ALR land — residences in the ALR cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)), subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval, and the housing caps are one principal residence to 500 m², a suite inside that residence only, and an additional residence of 90 m² (40 ha or less) or 186 m² (over 40 ha). https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Can a municipality stop me building a poultry barn in the ALR?** Under the ALR Use Regulation, B.C. Reg. 30/2019, a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation is a designated farm use that a local government enactment may not prohibit — though it may be regulated, and the structure must be required for the farm use and in line with its level. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019
- **Q: What happens to a poultry farm sale during an avian influenza outbreak?** HPAI is a reportable disease; in a CFIA primary control zone, permits are required to move birds, eggs and by-products, and on a quarantined farm nothing bird-related enters or leaves without CFIA permission — which can delay placements, showings and completion, including a residence move tied to the sale. Confirm the property's status before scheduling placements or a completion date. https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected
