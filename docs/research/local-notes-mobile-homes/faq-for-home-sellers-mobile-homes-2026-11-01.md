## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase/sale.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the seller owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. A seller FAQ written for land sales does not displace this Act for a park home. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator" — any "arbitrator" wording met in research is flagged, never followed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified from the RTB: **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file, and none is documented in this slug's research. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts apply as the Act states, with a 15-day dispute window. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact; both flat page claims met in the program are UNVERIFIED. The page's own repeated statement — "property transfer tax is paid by the buyer, not the seller" — matches the Province's frame for land transfers: PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact pad-sale structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000; $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. **Grep-checks stated explicitly for this slug:** the PTT shorthand (all forms) appears in this slug's siblings ONLY inside flagging sentences (the townhomes sibling flags an "Over $3M — 5%" row as a compression, documenting no such row in this slug's research) — NOT a new carrier; the $3.5M/$78,000 error is NOT met as this slug's own sibling rate (sibling hits are flagging sentences naming a south-surrey townhomes sibling and following $93,000). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Selling without a licence (the page's "Do I need a REALTOR®?" answer, verified at the regulator).** A person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves — for example, by selling or renting out their own property — while a person must not provide real estate services to or on behalf of another, for or in expectation of remuneration, unless licensed or exempt. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information
- **The regulator's consumer framework (BCFSA).** A licensed real estate professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; the representation, duties and BCFSA complaint-route disclosures must happen at the outset of dealings. This page states no commission rate or amount — consistent with that framework, which places the commission question in the listing conversation. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Cooling-off / deposits (BCFSA).** Where a buyer rescinds an accepted offer under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining deposit balance to the buyer, with no separate release form; otherwise a deposit is held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder, and is not automatically returned if subjects are not removed. The live FAQ page does not itself raise the rescission period; the sibling selling page does. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **Listing-contract length — UNVERIFIED page statements (owned by this file).** The page states Fraser Valley and Greater Vancouver listing contracts are typically 4–6 months, with a claimed 60-day minimum under the named board's rules. No board rule page was opened in the research pass, so both the typical length and the minimum as stated are UNVERIFIED, and the board name used ("Real Estate Board of Greater Vancouver") is dated wording against the current Greater Vancouver REALTORS® name. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata; no such community attaches to this topic slug).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning / SSMUH note.** Surrey/Township SSMUH frames in the area files do not attach to this topic slug, and **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies** in any case.
- **GST — only at cited-CRA level (GI-003 general level).** GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer is UNCONFIRMED. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate is for new HOUSING (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate. **March-12 grep-check for this slug: stated explicitly — no sibling of this slug carries March 12 as its own Bill C-4 date; March 13, 2026 per CRA governs.** https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I'm selling a home on a rented pad — what am I actually selling?** The home as a chattel, plus an assignment of your pad tenancy — not land. **You own the home and rent the pad**; the buyer becomes the pad tenant on the same terms once the park owner consents (or consent is deemed given) and the transfer is registered in the Manufactured Home Registry. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: Does the park have to approve my buyer?** Yes — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent, requested on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent. Build the 10-day window into subject dates; it is a seller-side timing item the land-sale FAQ does not mention. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Why isn't my home selling? (the page's own first answer)** The page's answer: generally because it is overpriced relative to comparable recent sales and current competition, not marketed well enough, or does not show well; the fix starts with a home evaluation and an honest comparison against recent sales, active listings and expired or terminated listings. For a park home, the comparable set is homes in parks on the same tenure — a land-home price case does not transfer. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: Do I need a REALTOR® to sell my home in BC?** No — BCFSA's licensing guidance confirms a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves, such as selling their own property; a licence (or an exemption) is required to provide real estate services to or on behalf of another person for remuneration. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information
- **Q: What are the costs of selling?** The page lists real estate commission (split between the listing agent and the buyer's agent), cleaning, staging and preparation, legal fees and disbursements, and a possible mortgage penalty if the mortgage is broken early — and states no commission figure, which BCFSA's framework places in the listing conversation. For a park home, add the registry filing through a lawyer or notary. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Do I have to accept an offer?** The page's answer: no — on receiving an offer you can accept it as-is, counter, or reject it altogether; a counter voids the previous offer. The page's further claim — that a seller "may be required to accept" a cash offer at list price matching listed "cash" terms, with advice to set terms as "TBD" — is a listing-contract legal-effect claim with no BCFSA, BC Laws or board source opened in the research pass: UNVERIFIED as stated (§5). Read your own listing contract before relying on any general statement. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: Who pays the property transfer tax when I sell?** On a land sale, the buyer: PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office. For a home-only pad sale, PTT treatment is UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule (§3) — confirm the exact structure with a lawyer or notary. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up for the buyer after I sell?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
