## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

### The settled court process (carried in full)

- **Step 1 — the petition.** "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7(1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). Rule 21-7 sets out no demand step before the petition; in the standard mortgage terms prescribed under the Land Title Act, if a default occurs, the lender may "demand payment of all the mortgage money," apply to the court for an order that the land be sold on terms approved by the court, or apply to the court to foreclose the borrower's interest in the land. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo61/loo61/53_90_03
- **Step 2 — the order and the redemption period.** The court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period that the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the respondent's equity of redemption — Rule 21-7(5)(b); the court may determine or certify the amount due and pronounce judgment for it — Rule 21-7(5)(c) and (e). The Land Title Act refers to the court's order in a foreclosure as an "order nisi," with a later final order of foreclosure — Land Title Act, s.271(1). The redemption period has **no fixed length in the Rules** — it is the period the court fixes in its order; interest is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem the property — Law and Equity Act, s.19. (The Law and Equity Act's 6-month redemption direction in s.16 covers a vendor's proceeding under an agreement for sale, NOT the Rule 21-7 mortgage process — research file, UNVERIFIED list.) https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol33/consol33/96250_18 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Final order; applying for a sale instead.** In default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7(6). A party may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7(7). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale.** If the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell the property "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5(3); the court's directions for a sale may appoint the person who is to have conduct of the sale, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of the value of the property — Rule 13-5(4). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Court approval of the sale; Form 60.** The manner of sale the court fixes may be sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5(4)(b); the person having conduct of a sale may apply to the court, even though the time for redemption has not expired, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7(9); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in **Form 60** by the person having conduct of the sale — Rule 13-5(6). On confirmation, the vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Bids; PD-66.** The court's directions for a sale may define "the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids" — Rule 13-5(4)(d); the Supreme Court's civil practice direction **PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings,"** governs practice in these proceedings and replaced the earlier sealed-bid practice direction PD-62, per the Court of Appeal. The timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66: the text of PD-66 was not retrieved in the research file — UNCONFIRMED beyond the point above. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 and https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm
- **Where the proceeding is filed; registry position.** Unless the court otherwise orders, a foreclosure proceeding is commenced at the Supreme Court registry in the municipality where the land is located, if there is one there, and otherwise at a registry in the judicial district in which the land is located, and applications in the proceeding are heard at that registry — Law and Equity Act, s.21(2). **REGISTRY LEDGER: Langley is NOT in the program's registry ledger** — the ledger confirms Abbotsford HAS a registry and that Surrey / Delta / Mission have NONE (mapping UNVERIFIED); it makes no Langley entry, and the research file likewise does not settle which registry serves Langley as a stated mapping (UNVERIFIED item 7). No registry is asserted for a Langley file in this file. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01 (research file §4 and UNVERIFIED list.)
- **Court records.** Court Services Online is British Columbia's electronic court registry; its eSearch services are available to anyone with Internet access, the file details it shows for Supreme Court civil files include the file number, registry location, names of parties and list of filed documents, and its file classes include "H - Foreclosure." It publishes no area statistics. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do

### The MHPTA block (applies to a park home in this process, alongside the court process)

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. How this assignment step interacts with a court-conducted sale of the home in a specific file: UNCONFIRMED on any source opened — the court file and the park's position must both be read. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the M1 files state them, with the 15-day dispute window. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. How registry filing is sequenced with a court vesting order in a specific park-home file: UNCONFIRMED — confirm with the lawyer handling the file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b), and interest runs to the date full payment is made to redeem. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Q: What does "subject to court approval" mean for my offer?** Where the court has directed sale by contract conditional on the approval of the court (Rule 13-5(4)(b)), the person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7(9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — until that order, the sale is not final. Do not book movers on acceptance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** The court's sale directions may define the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids (Rule 13-5(4)(d)), and PD-66 governs foreclosure proceedings — so a competing bid at the approval stage is a real possibility. Price with room, or know your ceiling in advance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: Where can I check the court file for a Langley foreclosure?** Court Services Online offers eSearch to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." Which registry serves Langley is NOT settled in the registry ledger — the file itself, once found, states its registry. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do
- **Q: If the home is in a park, do I also need the park's consent?** The MHPTA assignment machinery (park-owner consent on RTB Form 10, 10-day rule) governs a park-home sale in the ordinary course; how it interacts with a court-conducted sale in a specific file is UNCONFIRMED on any source opened — read the court file and confirm the park's position before spending on inspections. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: Can the owner redeem and end the deal?** Yes — the owner may redeem by paying what is due plus interest to the date of payment (Law and Equity Act, s.19), and the person with conduct of sale may apply to confirm a sale even before the redemption period expires. Ask which stage the file is at before spending. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Q: Do I pay PTT or GST on a court-ordered park home, and will I get a Form B?** UNCONFIRMED — all three. No source opened in this program settles PTT or GST on a court-ordered sale of a manufactured home, or Form B treatment on a court sale. Budget on your lawyer's advice for the specific file, not on a general rule. (§3 above.)
- **Q: What conditions will my contract carry?** The court's conditions, not the standard ones: the particulars and conditions of sale are settled by court direction, including any reserve or minimum price (Rule 13-5(4)). Read them line by line with your lawyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: Can I get a mortgage on a court-ordered manufactured home?** UNVERIFIED at official-source level in this program — chattel treatment and age/foundation requirements are lender-specific, and the confirming-order timeline must fit the lender's commitment. Confirm financing for the specific home, tenure and file before removing subjects. (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-1/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-guide.md)
