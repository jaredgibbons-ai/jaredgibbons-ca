## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified from the RTB: **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file, and none is documented in this slug's research. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts apply as the Act states, with a 15-day dispute window. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year; the regular grant amount is $570 effective January 1, 2027 and the 2026 full-grant threshold is $2,075,000 assessed value, per the Province frame relied on in the research. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact; both flat page claims met in the program are UNVERIFIED. The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates + the first-time buyers' program (verified — Province).** General rates: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million, on fair market value on the day of registration. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential purchase with no exemption = **$12,000**; with the first-time exemption (exempt on the first $500,000) = **$4,000** — the legs of the page's own worked example, which follow the verified rates. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000; $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. First-time buyers' program: a qualifying buyer (Canadian citizen or permanent resident; lived in BC at least a year immediately before registration or filed at least 2 income tax returns as a BC resident in the last 6 taxation years; never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world; never received the exemption before) is exempt on the first $500,000 where the property is used only as a principal residence, has a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), is 0.5 hectares or smaller and contains only residential improvements; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and occupy the home as a principal residence to the first anniversary of registration to keep the full exemption. Newly built home exemption: full threshold $1,100,000, phasing out over the $50,000 range above it and eliminated at $1,150,000; the page's FAQ figure of "up to $20,000" at the $1,100,000 threshold is arithmetically consistent with the verified general rates. **Grep-checks stated explicitly for this slug:** the PTT shorthand (all forms) appears in this slug's siblings ONLY inside flagging sentences — NOT a new carrier; this slug's own page DOES carry the one-line "Over $3,000,000 — 5%" compression as its own table row (documented in its own research, §1) — that is a page simplification flagged here, not the shorthand as a sibling rate. The $3.5M/$78,000 error is NOT met as this slug's own sibling rate (sibling hits are flagging sentences naming a south-surrey townhomes sibling and following $93,000). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Down payment and mortgage insurance + the CMHC error (owned by this file).** Mortgage loan insurance is required when a down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more (where insurance is not available); **CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount**, and the premium can be paid in full or added to the mortgage. - **FTHB GST/HST rebate — new HOUSING only (verified — CRA).** Bill C-4, which introduced the rebate, received Royal Assent on **March 13, 2026 per CRA** (never March 12). Together with the GST/HST new housing rebate it provides 100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million (maximum $50,000), is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more; eligibility turns on a builder agreement entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036, and the buyer being at least 18, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and a first-time buyer under the four-calendar-year look-back; claiming is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction, by builder credit at closing or directly to the CRA (Form GST190; Form GST191 for owner-built homes). **A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate.** The phase-out mechanism between $1 million and $1.5 million is stated by CRA only as "gradually reduced" — any straight-line formula is an UNVERIFIED page statement. GST New Housing Rebate (pre-existing): generally 36% of the GST paid, maximum $6,300, gradually reduced between $350,000 and $450,000, not available at $450,000 or more. **March-12 grep-check for this slug: stated explicitly — no sibling of this slug carries March 12 as its own Bill C-4 date; sibling hits are check sentences stating March 13 governs, and this slug's research states March 13 only.** https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I'm a first-time buyer looking at a resale home in a park — do I get the GST rebate?** No. The FTHB GST/HST rebate is for new HOUSING — a newly constructed or substantially renovated home under a builder agreement on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate, whatever your first-time status. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: What about a brand-new manufactured home placed on my own land?** That is the one manufactured-home form the Province names expressly: the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied), with a full PTT threshold of $1,100,000 phasing out to $1,150,000 — and a new home may also attract GST, which the FTHB rebate can offset by up to $50,000 for an eligible buyer. Confirm the exact structure with a lawyer or notary. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: How much PTT does a first-time buyer in BC actually save?** A qualifying buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 where the property's fair market value is $835,000 or less, it is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the buyer's principal residence; above $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. For a home-only pad purchase, PTT treatment is UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule (§3) — do not assume the land exemption applies to the chattel structure without advice. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?** Yes — the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal; FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000. The HBP figures themselves are UNVERIFIED in this file on the CRA-403 basis (§3). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Q: The program page says mortgage insurance runs 2.80% to 4.00% — is that right?** Not against CMHC's published frame: CMHC publishes 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount. The page's range is UNVERIFIED site copy in conflict with the CMHC frame, owned and flagged in this file (§§1, 3, 5) — price a purchase on the CMHC range and the lender's actual premium for the loan-to-value tier. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: What would I own in a pad-rental park?** The home (a chattel) only — **you rent the pad and do not own the land**; the existing pad tenancy is assigned to you under the MHPTA with the park owner's consent (Form 10, 10-day window), and the transfer is effective only once registered in the Manufactured Home Registry. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: What happened to the old GST new housing rebate?** It still exists on its own terms: generally 36% of the GST paid, to a maximum of $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and unavailable at $450,000 or more. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
