## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file — this slug's own research documents no pad figure, so none is mentioned at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. The 15-day dispute window and the additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the M1 files state them. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers, including a Sea Ridge Bays lot).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential purchase with no exemption = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies to Sea Ridge Bays as a bare-land strata, and to any strata lot in this feed).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning / SSMUH — Surrey frame.** Zoning By-law No. 12000 governs Surrey ground; OCP = Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted June 15, 2026 per the siblings) — Bylaw No. 18020 is REPLACED and is never cited as current. SSMUH changes adopted July 8, 2024: a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots within 400 m of a frequent bus stop; a secondary suite's habitable floor area may not exceed 40% of the principal dwelling unit. Servicing: Surrey Subdivision and Development Bylaw, 1986, No. 8830 VERIFIED in the ledger. Surrey short-term rentals are permitted, owner-only; the licence figure is carried in the siblings as $358/yr with a $350/yr proposal figure disclosed as a conflict — check the City licence page before quoting either. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** White Rock ground sits under White Rock's own bylaws (Zoning 2024 No. 2506; OCP 2017 No. 2220). https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/12000 and https://www.whiterockcity.ca/514/Resources-Documents (siblings above)
- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3).** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report, provides that a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about their motivation, financial qualifications or needs. BCFSA states a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers (research file §4)
- **GST on a manufactured home — only at cited-CRA level.** GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established. (Research file §4, sibling frame.)
- **First-home programs.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate is for new HOUSING (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Financing.** Lender and mortgage-insurer rules for manufactured homes (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in this program: UNVERIFIED — confirm financing for the specific home and tenure before removing subjects.

- **Q: Does the hosting agent have to give me a disclosure of representation at an open house?** Not for the hosting itself: Rule 5-10(3), as set out in the Superintendent's November 2017 consultation report, provides that disclosure is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions — unless the licensee solicits or receives information from you about your motivation, financial qualifications or needs. Use the open house for facts; save your story for your own agent. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Q: Who does the agent hosting an open house represent?** The seller, unless you have your own client relationship with a licensee: BCFSA states a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers (research file §7)
- **Q: If I buy in a Surrey pad-rental park, do I own the land?** No: **you own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad** — the park owns the land, and your right to the site is the assigned pad tenancy under the MHPTA. At Sea Ridge Bays the form is different: you own the home AND the strata lot. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does a park have to approve me as a buyer?** Yes, in a pad-rental park — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent, requested on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent. Build the 10-day window into subject dates. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: What happens if a park is sold or redeveloped?** For a conversion of all or a significant part of the park, the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How is a manufactured-home purchase registered, and why does the CSA label matter?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: Which schools serve a South Surrey address?** Catchments are address-based in SD36. One settled single-address chain is on record inside this page's scope: 1785-148 St → H.T. Thrift Elementary / Semiahmoo Secondary. Every other address's catchment is UNVERIFIED — run the exact address in the SD36 locator. (Research file §4.)
