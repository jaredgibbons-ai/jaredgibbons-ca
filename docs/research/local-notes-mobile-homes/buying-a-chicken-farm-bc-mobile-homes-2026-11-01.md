## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

### The full ALR block — the frame that governs any manufactured home on farm ground

- **ALR housing frame (province-wide, ALC):** on ALR land, one principal residence is permitted without an ALC application at a maximum total floor area of 500 m² (no more than 1,000 m² of fill), a secondary suite is permitted only when located within the principal residence, and at most one additional residence is permitted of up to 90 m² on properties 40 ha or less or up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha; local government bylaws may further regulate or restrict residences, and may not be more permissive than the ALC's requirements. The guide's statement that ALR rules generally allow barns, a principal residence and, with restrictions, additional dwellings is consistent with this frame at that general level. ALR residences cannot be stratified without ALC permission (program farm treatment). https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Manufactured homes on farm ground — the only routes.** ALR Use Regulation s.34(3) as summarised in ALC Policy L-25: a non-pre-existing manufactured home as an additional residence is permitted only at 9 m or less in width and only for occupancy by the owner or specified relatives (parent, grandparent or great-grandparent, sibling, or child, within the regulation's meaning); if it is no longer occupied as permitted it must be demolished, removed, or converted to a non-residential use. Anything outside those conditions needs a Non-Adhering Residential Use (NARU) approval, which the Commission must not grant unless the residence is necessary for a farm use. **A general farm-worker manufactured-housing entitlement is UNCONFIRMED.** https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf
- **ALR subdivision:** strata plans in the ALR cause subdivision per ALC Information Bulletin 09 s.6 (direct-read confirmed Nov 1, 2026), and subdivision of ALR land generally requires ALC approval — relevant to any guide reader assuming a farm parcel can be split for a second homesite. (Research file §4.)
- **BC Assessment farm classification (province-wide):** a voluntary program; income thresholds are $2,500 if the total area of the farm operation is between 0.8 ha and 4 ha, $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if the operation is greater than 4 ha, and $10,000 if the operation is less than 0.8 ha; applications must be submitted to BC Assessment by October 31 for consideration for the next assessment and tax year, and a buyer who plans to continue farming a farm-classified property must submit a General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Parcel-level checks:** whether a specific parcel is in the ALR, its zoning, servicing and floodplain position, and its farm-class status are UNVERIFIED at parcel level — each is a per-parcel check with the applicable local government, the ALC, BC Assessment and title. (Research file §4.)

### The settled MHPTA block — stated in full, for the pad-tenancy contrast only (no pad tenancy is established on any farm ground in this slug)

- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns (including farm land) is dealt with through the land title system and, in the ALR, the ALR block above instead. **CORE TENURE LINE: a home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home (a chattel) and rents the pad.** https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the **director** — never "arbitrator" in the MHPTA block. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (12-month average change in the BC all-items CPI) plus a proportional amount (change in local government levies plus change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation — s.42 block.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Additional amounts as the settled block states them: an additional assessed-value amount and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts, with a 15-day dispute window. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the PTT treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad; both flat page claims met program-wide are UNVERIFIED. The one express provincial point: the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied). For a farm purchase specifically: **how PTT and GST are apportioned between land, buildings, residence and quota is UNVERIFIED in this file** — no Province farm-transfer tax source was opened for this slug; have an agricultural lawyer and accountant model it before an offer is written. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply.** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential purchase with no exemption = **$12,000**. Anchors: $500K→$8,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 first-time-buyer figure at that price point in the program anchors); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 including the further 2% tier** (a $78,000 figure at a $3.5M input in a townhomes sibling elsewhere is an ERROR — follow $93,000; see §5 check).
