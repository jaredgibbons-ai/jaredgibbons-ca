## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **MHPTA block, carried in full because east-side buyers cross-shop Abbotsford's parks.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the response must reach the home owner by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. The decision-maker is the "director", never "arbitrator". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date; the provincial pages also provide a 15-day window to dispute the notice and additional compensation routes (an assessed-value-based amount where the home cannot be relocated, and a $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amount in the circumstances those pages describe) — finer detail UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry — the rule that matters for an own-lot east-side manufactured home.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. Whether a specific east-side manufactured home remains Registry-registered as a chattel or has been de-registered and affixed to its fee-simple land is UNCONFIRMED until the Registry and land title records for that home are read — the two positions diligence, finance and tax differently. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles PTT on a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so **no flat claim either way is stated as fact in this file** (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED; the Abbotsford pad-page PTT line belongs to the separate abbotsford-mobile-homes page lineage and is NOT documented for this slug — see §5). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Worked examples (inputs + tier math): $500,000 fully residential, no exemption = $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**; $700,000 = **$12,000**; $800,000 = **$14,000** (a qualifying first-time buyer at $800,000 pays **$6,000** after the first-$500,000 relief); $900,000 = **$16,000**; $1.0M = **$18,000**; $1.1M = **$20,000**; $1.2M = **$22,000**; $1.5M = **$28,000**; $1.8M = **$34,000**; $2.0M = **$38,000**; $3.5M fully residential = **$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata block (applies if an east-side buyer instead buys in a bare-land strata elsewhere, and to east-side strata product such as Eden Court's form — never to a pad tenancy).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page; fee, amounts owed, approved levies, CRF balance, parking/storage, insurance summary); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse/caregiver/legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning / OCP — Abbotsford frame.** Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014 governs (RS3 540 m², RS5 420 m², RS6 compact lots). OCP: **Abbotsforward 2050 is CURRENT (adopted Dec 2025)**; this slug's research cites the OCP as "Bylaw No. 2600-2016, as updated" and the live page has carried "2500-2016" — both stale/conflicted, flagged in §5, resolved nowhere in the research and carried nowhere here. The McKee Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 3400-2023), Schedule E of the OCP, covers the mountainside; the OCP includes a Steep Slope Development Permit Area and protects Clayburn Village through a Heritage Conservation Area. Zoning Bylaw s.140.3.8 permits one secondary suite in all single detached dwellings (City-report criteria: at least 400 m² lot, at least 12.0 m frontage, a Collector or Local road, not on a cul-de-sac bulb); Abbotsford's suite cap is **under 50%** of the principal unit (changed from 100 m²), and a coach/garden suite is capped at **15% of lot area to a maximum of 100 m² — Abbotsford's rules ONLY**. **SSMUH bylaw number: UNCONFIRMED** — this slug's research asserts "3560-2024" as ESTABLISHED; the standing program correction holds the number UNCONFIRMED on City pages, the source/disk position governs, and the number is never asserted in this file (the assertion is flagged in §5). **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** The Development/servicing bylaw number is likewise UNCONFIRMED. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400140 and https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2025bschedulea (research file above)
- **GST on a manufactured home — only at cited-CRA level.** At the general level CRA sources support: GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level; anything finer for a specific manufactured home is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established.
