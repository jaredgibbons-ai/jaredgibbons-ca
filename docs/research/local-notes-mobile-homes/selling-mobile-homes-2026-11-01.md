## 2. THE PARKS AND THE HOMES

**TOPIC FILE — NO ROSTER at the top: this file verifies no park. Selling a manufactured home follows the same eight-step shape the page describes, but the park-specific steps below replace the ordinary title-transfer steps — a seller who skips them has not actually prepared the sale.**

### What selling a park home actually involves (process position, no roster)

- **A pad-rental home sells as a chattel plus an assigned tenancy — not as land.** The seller sells the home (registered in the Manufactured Home Registry) and the buyer takes an assignment of the existing pad tenancy; there is no land title transfer for the pad. **TENURE LINE: a home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home as a chattel and rents the pad** — and the seller's marketing must say so plainly, because a buyer who believes they are buying land has been mis-sold the tenure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Park-owner consent is a sale step with a clock.** Before completion the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules, then request the park owner's consent on RTB Form 10; the response is due by the 10th day, silence is deemed consent, and no fee may be charged for consent (§3). A selling timeline that does not build in the 10-day window is incomplete. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **A bare-land strata manufactured home sells as strata, not as a park tenancy** — Form B, registered bylaws and strata fees govern, and there is no pad assignment (§3 strata block). South Surrey's verified example, Sea Ridge Bays, is documented AT HOME in the south-surrey-mobile-homes topic file and is cross-referenced here only as the form contrast — never counted in this topic file. (~/workspace/local-notes/mobile-homes/south-surrey-mobile-homes-mobile-homes-2026-11-01.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT MOBILE HOMES

- **The regulator's framework for any sale (BCFSA, opened in this slug's research).** In BC a licensed real estate professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; disclosure of whether the professional can represent the consumer, the duties owed, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA must happen at the outset of dealings, and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Cooling-off / rescission (BCFSA, opened in this slug's research).** If a buyer rescinds an accepted offer under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining balance of the deposit to the buyer, with no separate release form signed by either party. A buyer's deposit is usually held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder; where subject clauses are not removed, the deposit is not automatically returned and both parties must sign a release, except where the buyer exercises the right of rescission. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **BC home flipping tax (Province page, opened in this slug's research).** The tax applies to profit from selling a BC property (including presale contracts) owned for less than 730 days, under the Residential Property (Short-Term Holding) Profit Tax Act effective January 1, 2025; the rate is 20% of net taxable income for a property disposed of within 365 days and gradually decreases over the next 365 days, with no tax at 730 days; a return must be filed within 90 days of the sale where the seller is subject to the tax or an exemption applies only after filing. The page's shorter wording ("20% of the profit," "drops to zero by day 730") is a simplification of "net taxable income" and the filing conditions as the Province states them. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/bc-home-flipping-tax
- **MHPTA — the governing Act for a pad purchase.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the buyer owns is dealt with through the land title system instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park-owner consent and the 10-day rule (MHPTA s.28 / RTB Form 10).** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the **director** — never "arbitrator"), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day is deemed consent, and the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant on the same terms. No "arbitrator" wording appears in this slug's research; the director position governs. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Park rules.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and with 3 full months' notice to tenants, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
- **Pad-rent increases — percentages ONLY.** Once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). Verified live from the RTB (Nov 1, 2026): **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as a park rate anywhere in this file — none is documented in this slug's own research, so none is mentioned at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion / compensation (MHPTA s.42) — only as official sources support.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. Additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts are stated only as the M1 files state them, with the 15-day dispute window. Finer detail: UNCONFIRMED beyond the cited provincial pages. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Home-as-chattel / Manufactured Home Registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax on a park home.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **PTT / transfer treatment of a manufactured home — UNCONFIRMED as a flat rule.** No primary source opened in this program settles the property transfer tax treatment of a home-only purchase on a rented pad, so no flat claim either way is stated as fact (both flat page claims in the program are UNVERIFIED). The one PTT point the Province states expressly is that the newly built home exemption includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to placing a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. A lawyer or notary must confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **PTT general rates — for transfers where PTT does apply (own-land / strata-lot transfers).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $700,000 fully residential own-land purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. - **Strata-form parks — BC strata block (applies only if a manufactured-home community is a bare-land strata).** Form B diligence (within 7 days of request, max $35 plus up to 25¢ per page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with spouse / caregiver / legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and does NOT convert to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning / SSMUH — the two frames this page spans.** Surrey: OCP Bylaw No. 21900 (never 18020 as current), Zoning Bylaw No. 12000, suite cap 40%, SSMUH adopted July 8, 2024, servicing Bylaw No. 8830 (1986). - **GST on a manufactured home — only at cited-CRA level.** GST applies to new construction, not resales — a used home is generally exempt and a new home is taxable only as sourced at that general level (CRA GI-003 general level); anything finer is UNCONFIRMED until the home's and seller's status are established.
- **First-home programs.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate is for new HOUSING (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA**, never March 12): 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. A resale park home is not new housing for this rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What does it cost to sell a home in BC, according to this page?** The page lists, attributing the list to BCFSA: legal or notary fees for the title transfer, legal fees to discharge the mortgage, GST on legal fees, any lender prepayment penalty, and the seller's share of the current year's property taxes if unpaid — and states no commission figure at all (§5 defect 2). BCFSA requires commission and fee information to be given to the consumer in the listing conversation, so the commission figure is a listing-appointment answer, not a page answer. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html
- **Q: Can a buyer back out after I accept their offer?** For most homes, yes, inside the Home Buyer Rescission Period: the buyer may rescind within three business days after acceptance, in writing, paying 0.25% of the offer price, and the brokerage then pays that fee to you directly from the deposit with no release form required. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **Q: Do I pay tax if I sell soon after buying?** Possibly the BC home flipping tax: it applies to profit on a BC property owned less than 730 days, at 20% of net taxable income within 365 days, gradually decreasing to no tax at 730 days, with a return due within 90 days of the sale where the tax or a filing-based exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/bc-home-flipping-tax
- **Q: If I sell a park home, do I sell the land too?** No — on a rented pad you sell the home and assign the pad tenancy; the park keeps the land. **You own the home (a chattel) and rent the pad** for the whole time you live there. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does a park have to approve my buyer?** Yes, in a pad-rental park — assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent, requested on RTB Form 10, response due by the 10th day, silence deemed consent, no fee for consent. Build the 10-day window into the sale timeline before listing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up while my home is listed?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the provincial cap — 2.3% + proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional amount for 2027 (percentages only). A buyer inherits the tenancy on its existing terms, so a pending increase is a disclosure point, not a renegotiation. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold or redeveloped after I list?** For a conversion of all or a significant part of the park, the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: How is a manufactured-home sale registered?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered, filed by a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: How is the list price decided?** From sold evidence, as the page itself frames it: what similar homes nearby recently sold for and what is listed now, delivered as a price range with reasons — ask for that reasoning in writing at the pricing step. For a park home, the comparable set is in-park and same-tenure sales, not fee-simple houses on their own lots. https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html
