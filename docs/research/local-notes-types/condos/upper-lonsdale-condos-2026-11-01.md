## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. At Somerset Green it is also where the 1987-vs-1989 build-year conflict gets settled against the strata plan. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral by vote; since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — that is why the "rentals not allowed" element of Kings Court's directory line in Section 2 is VOID (long-term rentals are allowed; short-term rentals can still be restricted). **Age bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw, with exemptions under Strata Property Regulation 7.01 for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. The bylaw restricts RESIDENTS, not owners — people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Extra layer at Vista 29 — a covenant, not just a bylaw:** the District's 2004 approval for the Vista 29 site included a restrictive covenant limiting occupancy to households where one member is 55 years of age or older, alongside the CD 52 zoning. Have the covenant on title reviewed as well as the strata bylaws — the two are separate instruments. https://councilarchive.dnv.org/upload/documents/council_reports/512271.pdf
- **CITY rules (south of 29th Street — the Somerset Green side):** the City's Official Community Plan is the Official Community Plan, 2014, No. 8400, amended December 8, 2025 (Amendment Bylaw 2025, No. 9114); City zoning is Zoning Bylaw No. 6700 (1995). On December 8, 2025 the City adopted the Ground Oriented (GO) Zone, increasing permitted units from 3 to 4 on nearly 4,300 residential properties and allowing up to 6 units per lot on approximately 600 properties. Secondary suites have been legal in City single-family dwellings since 1993 — one suite per property, the owner must reside at the property — and an accessory coach house requires a front lot line of at least 10 m plus a Development Permit and a Building Permit. Never cite these City rules for a District address. https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/Housing/Official-Community-Plan-2014-No-8400-Amendment-Bylaw-2025-No-9114-City-Initiated-Amendment-Related-t.pdf and https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/Housing/HTO-Year-2-Progress-Report-July-1,-2025-June-30,-2026.pdf and https://www.cnv.org/Business-Development/Permits-Inspections/building-permits/secondary-suites and https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/Accessory-Coach-Houses/Coach-House-Information-Handout.ashx
- **DISTRICT rules (29th Street north — the Vista 29 / Churchill House side):** the District's Official Community Plan is Bylaw 7900 and its zoning is Zoning Bylaw 3210; Queensdale is one of the OCP's six Village Centres. Since June 2024, single-family properties within the Urban Containment Boundary may hold a principal dwelling plus a secondary suite plus a coach house (3 units), with suites up to 49% of the house and coach houses up to two storeys. Slope is the defining hazard condition: the District designates Natural Hazard Development Permit Areas and publishes property-level GeoWeb hazard reports, so steep lots should be checked before additions or rebuilds. Never cite these District rules for a City address. https://docs.dnv.org/documents/official-community-plan.pdf and https://docs.dnv.org/documents/rtc-bylaws-8787-8799.pdf and https://docs.dnv.org/documents/zoning-guide-single-family-residential-1-5.pdf
- **Property taxes — both municipalities, different bills:** 2026 taxes were due July 2, 2026 in both, with a 5% penalty after July 2, 2026 and a further 5% after September 1, 2026. City 2026 residential total rate: 3.34028 per $1,000 of assessed value, plus a separate residential storm drainage rate of 0.10697 per $1,000; the City illustrates an average residential property assessed at $1,387,684 paying $4,790 in total 2026 taxes. District 2026 residential total rate: 3.28195 per $1,000 (municipal portion 1.83419); District single-family utilities include water at $932.80 and sewer/drainage at $1,255.60. https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/City-Finances/2026/2026_TAX_RATE_SUMMARY_CITY_OF_NORTH_VANCOUVER-2.PDF and https://docs.dnv.org/documents/2026-tax-rates.pdf and https://docs.dnv.org/documents/tax-newsletter-2026.pdf
- **GST and rebates:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — all three counted buildings are resale stock. First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) — 100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements March 20, 2025–2031; GST New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC) — worked example with inputs stated:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Inputs: fair market value $835,000 (the First-Time Home Buyers' Program ceiling, illustration only — no area price is stated or implied), fully residential, no exemption claimed: $2,000 (1% × $200,000) + $12,700 (2% × $635,000) = **$14,700 PTT**. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **HBP / FHSA / CMHC:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.60%–4.50%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- **No Vancouver Empty Homes Tax:** Upper Lonsdale is in the City and District of North Vancouver, not the City of Vancouver — Vancouver's Empty Homes Tax does not apply here. No source is cited for a negative; the municipal identity is established in Section 1. **No market figures:** SnapStats does not cover North Vancouver, so no median, sales-ratio or trend figure is quoted anywhere in this file. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Which condo buildings are verified by name in Upper Lonsdale?** Three counted entries: Somerset Green (121 West 29th St, City side, 55+), Vista 29 (188 West 29th St, District side, 55+, plan BCS2494) and Kings Court (106 West Kings Rd, general-market, built 1990). The building at 105 West Kings Rd is carried UNVERIFIED on a single listing and is not in the count; Chartwell Churchill House is rental, not buyable. (Section 2 sources.)
- **Q: Somerset Green and Vista 29 face each other across West 29th Street — are they in the same municipality?** No. Somerset Green is south of West 29th Street and is described in the District's own report as a seniors' condominium development "in the City of North Vancouver"; Vista 29 was rezoned by the District (CD 52) on the block between West Queens Road and West 29th Street. Different OCP, zoning bylaw, permit desk and tax rate apply to each — City total residential rate 3.34028 vs District 3.28195 per $1,000 for 2026. https://councilarchive.dnv.org/upload/documents/council_reports/512271.pdf and https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/City-Finances/2026/2026_TAX_RATE_SUMMARY_CITY_OF_NORTH_VANCOUVER-2.PDF and https://docs.dnv.org/documents/2026-tax-rates.pdf
- **Q: Do I have to be 55 to buy in a 55+ building, or only to live there?** Only to live there. The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the bylaw applies to residents, not ownership. Somerset Green listings phrase it as "one resident must be 55+"; Vista 29 listings say "One owner to be 55+" — the registered bylaws govern over listing wording. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Is Vista 29's 55+ rule only a strata bylaw?** No — it has a second layer: the District's approval for the site included a restrictive covenant limiting occupancy to households where one member is 55 or older, in addition to the strata's own bylaws. Have both reviewed on a purchase. https://councilarchive.dnv.org/upload/documents/council_reports/512271.pdf
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ building here?** Long-term, yes — every strata rental-restriction bylaw in BC became invalid on November 24, 2022, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. The tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw (or an exemption) — and at Vista 29, the occupancy covenant as well. The same void rule is why Kings Court's directory line "rentals not allowed" is flagged VOID in its Section 2 entry. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: Is Chartwell Churchill House something I can buy?** No. It is a private-pay rental retirement residence at 150 West 29th St, registered with the Assisted Living Registry (operator Chartwell Master Care LP, class SN), offering independent living and assisted living in rental suites — not strata ownership. https://chartwell.com/bc/north-vancouver/churchill-house
- **Q: My unit is on one side of 29th Street and an identical one is on the other — are the taxes the same?** No. Both municipalities set 2026 taxes due July 2, 2026 (5% penalty after July 2, further 5% after September 1), but the City total residential rate is 3.34028 per $1,000 plus 0.10697 storm drainage, against the District's 3.28195 per $1,000 — and District single-family utilities (water $932.80; sewer/drainage $1,255.60) are billed on the District's own schedule. Confirm which municipality the specific unit is taxed in. https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/City-Finances/2026/2026_TAX_RATE_SUMMARY_CITY_OF_NORTH_VANCOUVER-2.PDF and https://docs.dnv.org/documents/2026-tax-rates.pdf
- **Q: What slope or hazard checks should I do on an upper-Lonsdale purchase?** On the District side, pull the property-level GeoWeb hazard report and check whether a Natural Hazard Development Permit Area applies — slope DPAs can add geotechnical cost to additions and rebuilds. For a strata purchase, the Form B package and the most recent depreciation report are the building-level equivalents. https://docs.dnv.org/documents/zoning-guide-single-family-residential-1-5.pdf and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Q: Which schools serve an Upper Lonsdale condo address?** School District 44 on both sides of the municipal line: Westview Elementary (641 17th Street West, south of the sub-area), Larson Elementary (2605 Larson Road, dual-track English/French Immersion), École André-Piolat (380 West Kings Road, francophone K–12), and Carson Graham Secondary (2145 Jones Avenue) for most addresses. Catchments are address-based — check the exact address, and check easternmost addresses against the Argyle boundary. https://www.sd44.ca/school/larson/About/ and https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/044
- **Q: How do I commute downtown without driving?** Route 230 buses run the full length of Lonsdale Avenue past the 29th Street and Kings Road blocks, down to Lonsdale Quay, where the SeaBus crosses to Waterfront Station in 12 minutes, every 15 minutes during the day and every 10 minutes at peak. By car, the Highway 1 interchanges at Lonsdale and Westview are the highway routes. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt230.pdf and https://www.translink.ca/translink/schedules-and-maps/seabus

- **Somerset Green has two addresses.** The complex is listed as 121 West 29th St, but units are also addressed as 2800 Chesterfield Ave — a listing for 201–2800 Chesterfield Ave describes the same Somerset Green building. Searching only one address misses listings under the other. https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3120637-201-2800-chesterfield-avenue-north-vancouver-british-columbia-v7n4m1-735000/brochure
- **Somerset Green's build year is not consistent across sources.** Most current listings state 1987; at least one current listing (MLS R3117942) states 1989. This file quotes neither as settled — the strata plan and Form B settle it. Its 143-unit count comes from the District's 2004 council report, a figure most listing copy never mentions. https://councilarchive.dnv.org/upload/documents/council_reports/512271.pdf and https://www.robbiejohal.com/listing/r3117942-121-w-29-street-322-north-vancouver-bc-v7n-4l6/brochure
- **Vista 29 was purpose-built as 55+, not converted.** The District's Dec 22, 2004 report shows the project was designed from the start for ageing in place, with the 55+ occupancy covenant written into the rezoning alongside the CD 52 zoning — the covenant survives independently of any later bylaw change, which is why a Vista 29 purchase needs both instruments reviewed. https://councilarchive.dnv.org/upload/documents/council_reports/512271.pdf
- **A building directory can be a decade out of date on rentals.** Kings Court's directory entry still records "rentals not allowed" — a restriction that has been void province-wide since November 24, 2022. Any directory bylaw line for any building in this file has to be read against that date before it is repeated. http://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/kings-court-106-west-road and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The two recreation anchors point in opposite directions.** The City-side anchor is the new Harry Jerome Community Recreation Centre at 130 East 23rd Street, just south of the sub-area, opened July 25, 2026 — with its aquatics centre still in final checks at opening and fall programming expected. The District-side anchor is the Delbrook Community Recreation Centre at 851 West Queens Road, west along Queens Road from the 29th Street cluster. Which centre is "yours" follows the same 29th Street line as the taxes. https://www.cnv.org/City-Hall/News-Room/News-Releases/2026/6/3/New-HJCRC-and-Silver-Harbour-Seniors%E2%80%99-Activity-Centre-to-Open-July-25,-2026 and http://www.nvrc.ca/facilities-fields/accessibility
- **The 29th Street tax difference is small but real, and it is permanent.** For 2026 the City total residential rate is 3.34028 per $1,000 (plus 0.10697 storm drainage) against the District's 3.28195 — two otherwise comparable 55+ units across West 29th Street from each other are taxed by different municipalities at different rates, on the same July 2 due date. https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/City-Finances/2026/2026_TAX_RATE_SUMMARY_CITY_OF_NORTH_VANCOUVER-2.PDF and https://docs.dnv.org/documents/2026-tax-rates.pdf
- **The avenue's famous elevation figure has no official source.** The top of Lonsdale at Rockland is routinely quoted at about 850 feet, but no City, District or provincial source stating it was found in either research pass — this file carries it as UNCONFIRMED rather than as a fact, and a buyer pricing a "view premium" should verify the specific unit's view in person, in winter, when the upper blocks sit in a different snow band than the waterfront.
