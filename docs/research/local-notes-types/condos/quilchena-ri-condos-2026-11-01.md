## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

*No condo building is verified in this pocket, so this section states the BC strata framework that would govern any strata unit a buyer finds marketed under the Quilchena name (most likely in adjoining areas), plus the Richmond rules that govern the pocket itself.*

- **Form B and strata records:** on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate; the BC Real Estate Association advises reviewing the Form B for the most recent depreciation report, the strata's financial statements, the CRF balance and planned work to identify future special levies. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports:** effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Minimum CRF contribution:** effective November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund in the annual budget. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/more-help-and-support/subscribing-for-updates
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void:** since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw; short-term rentals can still be restricted. Any directory line stating "rentals not allowed" for a Richmond strata reflects old bylaw text, not current law. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ is the only legal age bylaw:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. This is the provision under which a building like Quilchena Green (Terra Nova) holds its verified 55+ status — confirm the registered bylaw on the Form B for any specific building. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City plans and zoning:** Richmond's 2041 Official Community Plan is Bylaw 9000, with area and sub-area plans held in Schedule 2 under Bylaws 7100 and 9000; the Laurelwood Sub-Area Plan is the City sub-area plan for the Granville Avenue / Railway Avenue bend. Zoning is Richmond Zoning Bylaw 8500 — Single Detached (RS1/RS2), Section 8.1, is the common single-family zoning in this pocket, with minimum lot areas by subzone including 360 m² (RS1/B, RS2/B), 550 m² (RS1/E), 828 m² (RS1/F) and 2,000 m² (RS1/G), and a secondary suite as a permitted secondary use. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quilchena-ri.md)
- **Suites / SSMUH:** the City states at least one secondary suite and/or accessory dwelling unit such as a coach house is allowed per lot on covered lots, with Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (RSM) zoning applied under Bill 44 and provincial minimums of three units on lots 280 m² or less and four units on larger lots — the realistic "multi-unit" route in this pocket is a house lot, not a condo building. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quilchena-ri.md)
- **Flood:** Richmond is made up of islands within the floodplain of the Fraser River at an average of 1 metre above sea level; the City's flood protection system includes 49 km of dikes designed to handle a 1:500 flooding event, with dike raising from 3.5 metres to 4.7 metres under way. All lands within Richmond are designated flood plain under Flood Plain Designation and Protection Bylaw No. 8204, which sets flood construction levels that habitable floors must meet unless an exemption applies. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quilchena-ri.md)
- **Richmond property taxes and utilities:** 2026 property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on unpaid 2026 taxes after July 2, 2026 and a further 5% penalty on any outstanding balance after Tuesday, September 1, 2026. Annual flat-rate utility payments were due March 31, 2026, with a 10% discount only for payments received on or before the due date; the City's discounted cost for a typical single-family metered residence for 2026 totals $2,251 per year (water $631, sewer $835, garbage/recycling/environmental programs $443, flood protection $342). (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quilchena-ri.md)
- **GST on new condos:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 (some secondary sources say March 12 — CRA governs). The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less (maximum saving $8,000), partial $835,000–$860,000, none at $860,000+. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www.equitylawgroup.ca/news/closing-costs-bc-home-purchase and https://danmarusin.com/blog/bc-property-transfer-tax-2026/
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. No approved major project or presale specific to the Quilchena pocket was confirmed on a City source. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quilchena-ri.md) and https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60% (LTV up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), 4.50% at the top band with a non-traditional down payment, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
