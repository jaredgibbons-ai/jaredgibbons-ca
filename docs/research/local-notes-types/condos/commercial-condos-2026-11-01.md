## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **The regulator's framework applies to commercial trading services as it does to residential:** in BC, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings — whether the professional can represent the consumer as a client, the duties to clients and non-clients, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA — and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **GST on commercial purchases differs from residential resale — the headline difference:** a resale home is not taxed with GST, but a sale of commercial real property is a taxable supply. CRA's GST/HST return instructions state the mechanism a commercial buyer actually uses: line 205 is completed where the buyer is a registrant, purchased taxable real property made in Canada, is required to pay the tax directly to CRA, and the use or supply of the property in the course of commercial activities will be more than 50% — i.e. a registrant buyer using the property primarily in commercial activity self-assesses the GST on its own return (and claims any corresponding input tax credit there) instead of paying it to the seller at closing. The treatment of any specific deal turns on the buyer's registration status and use: confirm with a tax professional — no deal-specific GST conclusion is stated in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/instructions-preparing-return.html
- **Commercial strata basics, where a unit is a strata lot:** the Strata Property Act governs nonresidential strata lots as it governs residential ones, and the Act expressly contemplates strata corporations with sections representing the different interests of owners of residential strata lots and owners of nonresidential strata lots, and of nonresidential owners who use their lots for significantly different purposes (SPA Part 11, ss. 190–191). For a strata commercial unit, the Form B route (SPA s. 59) is the diligence document: an owner, purchaser or authorized person may request it, the strata corporation must provide it within 7 days, and the maximum charge is $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. Practically: a mixed-use or commercial strata purchase needs the corporation's Form B **and**, where sections exist, the relevant section's Form B. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol17/consol17/98043_11 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Strata rules that do NOT carry over from the residential files:** the residential standard block's renter and first-buyer items are home-buyer rules, not commercial ones — the Nov 24, 2022 voiding of rental-restriction bylaws, the 55+ age-bylaw frame (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2), the First-Time Home Buyers' GST Rebate and PTT exemption, the Newly Built Home Exemption, the Home Buyers' Plan and the FHSA are residential programs and are not stated here as applying to a commercial purchase. Depreciation reports (every five years for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024) and the minimum CRF contribution (10% of the operating fund since November 1, 2023) are Strata Property Act rules that do apply to a strata corporation holding a commercial unit, on the same terms as a residential one. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Property Transfer Tax:** the Province's general tiers (1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million) are published for property transfers generally; how PTT applies to a specific commercial or mixed-use transfer (including any residential component) is a conveyancing question for that deal — no deal-specific PTT conclusion is stated in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Listing-data notice the page carries:** the footer states listing data comes in part from the MLS® Reciprocity program of GVR, FVREB or CADREB, that the boards do not warrant its accuracy, and that the materials may not be reproduced without the boards' express written consent. CREA owns the MLS® and REALTOR® trademarks, and agents must be members of a local board and CREA to access the MLS® system. https://jaredgibbons.ca/commercial.html and https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is a commercial strata unit a condo?** Not a residential one. An industrial bay, office or retail strata lot is owned under the Strata Property Act like a condo, but it is nonresidential product — different permitted use, different financing, and GST on the purchase where a home resale has none. This series counts only residential apartment condos; commercial strata units are expressly excluded from every roster. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol17/consol17/98043_11
- **Q: What kind of page is this — residential or commercial?** Commercial. The page is headed "Commercial Properties for Sale & Lease in the Fraser Valley," its cards are labelled Business, Office, Industrial, Retail, Business with Property and Land Commercial, its MLS® numbers are C-prefixed commercial numbers, and its editorial section is headed "Commercial Real Estate Opportunities." https://jaredgibbons.ca/commercial.html
- **Q: Does the page cover only the Fraser Valley?** No — on the page's own evidence it covers the Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver: the editorial text says "across the Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver," and the first page of the feed itself includes North Vancouver, Burnaby and Coquitlam listings alongside Delta, Surrey and Abbotsford ones. https://jaredgibbons.ca/commercial.html
- **Q: Why do some listings show no price?** Several for-lease cards on the first page display the price as masked ("$[***]") in the page text; the page does not explain the masking, so the lease rate, term and inducements for those properties can only come from the listing agent — a masked lease card and a priced sale card are not comparable figures. https://jaredgibbons.ca/commercial.html
- **Q: Do I pay GST when I buy a commercial unit?** Generally yes, commercial real property is a taxable supply — unlike a resale home. How it is paid differs: a GST/HST registrant buyer who will use the property more than 50% in commercial activities self-assesses on line 205 of its GST/HST return (claiming any corresponding input tax credit there) instead of paying the tax to the seller at closing. Non-registrant and mixed-use cases differ — get tax advice on the specific deal before writing. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/instructions-preparing-return.html
- **Q: What diligence document do I get for a strata commercial unit?** The Form B Information Certificate (Strata Property Act s. 59): strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies, CRF balance, insurance summary, budget, rules and the most recent depreciation report — provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. Where the corporation has sections (residential vs nonresidential), request a Form B from both the corporation and the relevant section. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Q: If I contact Jared about one of these listings without my own agent, who represents me?** No one, until a client relationship is formed. BCFSA requires a BC licensee to work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, to disclose at the outset whether they can represent you and what duties are owed to clients and non-clients, and to tell unrepresented consumers the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party; dual agency is prohibited in BC except in the rarest of circumstances. The framework is the same for commercial trading services as for residential. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Are there market statistics for commercial property on this page?** No sourced commercial statistics are stated in this file: commercial figures are not in SnapStats' residential extraction used by this program, and no official commercial source opened for the research supports figures for this page — vacancy, lease-rate, cap-rate and absorption figures for any municipality are UNVERIFIED here.
