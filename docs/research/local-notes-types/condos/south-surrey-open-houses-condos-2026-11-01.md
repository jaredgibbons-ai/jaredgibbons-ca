## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3):** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, states that the disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/south-surrey-open-houses.md)
- **Representation at an open house:** a licensed real estate professional in BC must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party to the transaction. The regulator is the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA). The live page's own "Before you walk in" copy is consistent with that frame: it warns that the listing agent works for the seller, and advises asking for the Form B, depreciation report and minutes at a strata open house. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://jaredgibbons.ca/south-surrey-open-houses.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/south-surrey-open-houses.md)
- **Identification or sign-in for open-house visitors:** NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED as a rule; a host may still ask, as a matter of the seller's instructions, but that practice is not in an official source. How open-house dates and times are set and entered for publication on the brokerage/board side is likewise UNVERIFIED on an official source. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/south-surrey-open-houses.md)
- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. This is the document step after the open house — the open house shows the unit, the Form B shows the building. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain one every five years, no deferral by vote. **Minimum CRF contribution:** at least 10% of the operating fund since November 1, 2023. In this file's roster that lands hardest on the oldest buildings — Semiahmoo Estates (1990 vs 1991) and Suncliff Place (2002). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 — no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw, whatever older directory or bylaw text says; short-term rentals can still be restricted. Flagged in-entry in Section 2: the Semiahmoo Estates directory "rentals not allowed" line is VOID for long-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 — only 55+ is lawful; exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children, with Reg 7.01 exemptions. Semiahmoo Estates, Suncliff Place and The Capistrano are all presented as 55+ in their sources — confirm on each Form B whether the registered bylaw is a 55+ bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City plans and zoning — two municipalities, never mixed:** Surrey buildings sit under the Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted June 15, 2026) — the former PlanSurrey 2013 OCP, Bylaw No. 18020, is REPLACED and cited only as predecessor — and Surrey Zoning Bylaw, 1993, No. 12000. White Rock buildings (The Capistrano) sit under White Rock's OWN bylaws: Zoning Bylaw 2024 No. 2506 and OCP 2017 No. 2220 — never Surrey's. Surrey STRs are permitted, owner-only, at $358/yr under Surrey's rules — Surrey ground only, and only as the program's research states it; White Rock's STR rules are a separate municipal check. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/r133-plansurrey-2013-official-community-plan-surrey-2050-official-community-plan-alignment-related-amendments
- **Home Buyer Rescission Period:** the live page describes BC's 3-business-day rescission period at 0.25% of the price, with leasehold, auction and court-ordered exceptions — recorded here as the page's site copy. https://jaredgibbons.ca/south-surrey-open-houses.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/south-surrey-open-houses.md)
- **GST on new homes:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 (the March 12 date in some secondary sources is superseded). The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial $835,000–$860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18 — distinct from the 3-business-day Home Buyer Rescission Period the page describes for resales; do not confuse the two windows. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. https://homecalc.ca/first-time-home-buyer-programs-canada
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premium range 0.60%–4.50%. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

Use the open house for facts; save your story for your own agent.
