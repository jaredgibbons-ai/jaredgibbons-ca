## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

The condo-buyer essentials the resources on this hub support — the diligence a buyer works through with the mortgage broker, inspector, lawyer/notary and insurer the page points to.

- **Form B diligence (the core strata document):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote; and since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — any directory or listing line stating "rentals not allowed" is VOID for long-term rentals; short-term rentals can still be restricted. **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 allow only a 55-or-older strata residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City plans and zoning for the hub's ground:** Surrey-side properties sit under the Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted June 15, 2026 — Bylaw 18020 is the replaced predecessor only) and Surrey Zoning By-law No. 12000; White Rock properties sit under White Rock's own OCP and zoning bylaws; Langley City and Township of Langley properties sit under those municipalities' own plans and bylaws — confirm the parcel's municipality first. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/official-community-plan (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.md)
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC) — worked example with its inputs stated:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000; the tax is based on fair market value on the day the transfer is registered at the Land Title Office unless an exemption applies, and in most cases a legal professional completes the transfer. Worked example — inputs: fair market value $700,000, fully residential, no exemption claimed: $2,000 (1% × $200,000) + $10,000 (2% × $500,000) = **$12,000 PTT**. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.md)
- **GST and rebates:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 — some secondary sources say March 12, CRA governs): 100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements March 20, 2025–2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **HBP / FHSA / CMHC:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.60%–4.50%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- **Presales (REDMA):** where a buyer uses this hub while buying a developer-contract presale, REDMA s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **The regulator behind the professionals:** in BC, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), which states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. Whether a licensee may accept a referral fee or other benefit from a professional they recommend, and on what disclosure terms: UNVERIFIED in the research file — no BCFSA source stating that rule was opened, so no rule is stated here. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.md)
- **Pad-rent parks (MHPTA), for completeness:** no park is part of this hub; where pad-rent parks are discussed in this program, MHPTA pad-rent increases are limited to 2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.md)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Who are the professionals on this page?** None are named. The page lists thirteen categories — Mortgage Brokers; Home Inspectors; Lawyers & Notaries; Appraisers; Surveyors; Oil Tank Removal; Handyman Services; Professional Painters; Junk / Rubbish Removal; Pressure Washing; Insurance Providers; Professional Organizers; Home Staging Services — and states Jared can connect you with trusted local professionals if you contact him. https://jaredgibbons.ca/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.md)
- **Q: Which areas does this resource hub cover?** As the page states it: South Surrey, White Rock, Langley City and the Township of Langley, for people buying, selling or maintaining a home. That span crosses three SnapStats communities, which is why this file states no single market figure for the page. https://jaredgibbons.ca/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.md)
- **Q: What is the first document to order on a condo resale?** The Form B Information Certificate (SPA s. 59): strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the budget, rules and most recent depreciation report attached — within 7 days, maximum $35 plus 25¢/page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Q: What does a buyer actually pay in property transfer tax?** On the worked example in Section 3 — inputs: $700,000 fair market value, fully residential, no exemption — the bill is $12,000. The general rates are 1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000, calculated on fair market value at registration unless an exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.md)
- **Q: Can a first-time buyer get the PTT or GST back?** Potentially both: the First-Time Home Buyers' Program fully exempts PTT on the first $500,000 at FMV ≤$835,000 (partial to $860,000), and the Bill C-4 First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate returns 100% of GST on a new home to $1M (maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements March 20, 2025–2031). Eligibility is fact-specific — confirm with the lawyer/notary. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: A listing says "rentals not allowed" — can I still rent out a condo I buy?** Yes for long-term rentals: rental-restriction bylaws have been void since November 24, 2022 anywhere in BC. Short-term rentals can still be restricted, and at a 55+ building the tenant must still satisfy the age bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: What protections do I have when dealing with a real estate professional from a referral?** BCFSA states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and work for only the buyer or the seller in a transaction — and consumers can file a complaint with BCFSA. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.md)
- **Q: On a strata purchase, can I book painters or repair work that touches the building exterior?** Check the strata frame first: owners own their strata lot and together own the common property as a strata corporation, so work touching common property is the corporation's to approve, not the individual owner's. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.md)
- **Q: How much do the services on this page cost, and how fast will I get a name?** UNVERIFIED — the page publishes no prices, fees, timelines or response commitments for any of its thirteen categories, and how the referral list is maintained cannot be confirmed from the page itself; the page's only stated mechanism is to contact Jared for a recommendation. https://jaredgibbons.ca/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.md)
- **Q: I'm buying a presale — what cooling-off right do I have?** REDMA s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing the contract and acknowledging receipt of the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
