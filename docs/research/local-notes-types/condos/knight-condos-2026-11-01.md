# Knight — Condos: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named buildings themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- "Knight" is the Knight Street corridor in Vancouver East: the major north–south arterial running from the Clark Drive / Great Northern Way area south through Kensington-Cedar Cottage, Sunset and Victoria-Fraserview to the Knight Street Bridge over the Fraser River's north arm to Richmond. The City lists "Knight" as a recognised smaller area, but it is not one of the City's 22 distinct local areas. A block-by-block board mapping of how far east and west of the street the sub-area reaches: UNVERIFIED. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)
- Where the condo stock actually concentrates: overwhelmingly at one node — **Kingsway / King Edward Avenue at Knight Street**, where the King Edward Village development holds the corridor's apartment condos above its retail podium. Elsewhere the corridor's residential form is detached houses on the side streets, with townhouse infill (Knight 18, The Charles) on and just off the street, and commercial / mixed-use frontage on the arterial itself. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)
- Cross streets that structure the corridor: East Broadway, Kingsway, East 33rd Avenue, East 41st Avenue, East 49th Avenue and SE Marine Drive. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)
- Transit: the corridor is crossed by the R4 41st Ave RapidBus and the 41 at East 41st Avenue, the 49 at East 49th Avenue, and the 9 at East Broadway; no SkyTrain station sits on the corridor. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Form B and strata records:** on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate; the BC Real Estate Association advises reviewing the Form B for the most recent depreciation report, the strata's financial statements, the CRF balance and planned work to identify future special levies. At King Edward Village the depreciation report is also where podium, parkade and retail-interface obligations surface — read which costs sit with the residential strata versus the retail podium. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports:** effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt — every complex in Section 2 is above the threshold. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Minimum CRF contribution:** effective November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund in the annual budget. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void:** since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw; short-term rentals can still be restricted. The King Edward Village listing line "Rental Friendly (Nothing Less than 30 days)" is exactly that split in practice: long-term tenancies cannot be banned, sub-30-day rentals can. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://faithwilson.com/listings/516-4078-knight-street/
- **55+ is the only legal age bylaw:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. No Knight corridor complex in Section 2 is published as 55+ in the sources checked — age bylaws: UNCONFIRMED per building; check the Form B. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City zoning:** Vancouver's zoning is the Zoning and Development By-law No. 3575. The residential side streets sit under the R1-1 District Schedule — minimum site area 306 m² for a single detached house or duplex and for a multiple dwelling of 3 to 4 units, 464 m² for 5 units, 557 m² for 6 to 8 units; a single detached house may include a secondary suite and a laneway house, and a multiplex may have up to 6 units or up to 8 rental units. The King Edward Village site itself is a site-specific CD-1 district — the retail-podium source records Zoning CD-1 (420) and listings record CD-1 — and the Knight Street arterial frontage's own district schedules are UNVERIFIED in the research pass. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md) and https://d2wsxqxx9m1aa9.cloudfront.net/wdd6e4gmkti1c942ocnfr9ttta9g
- **Area plans:** the Kensington-Cedar Cottage, Sunset and Victoria-Fraserview / Killarney community visions apply to the local areas the corridor runs through, alongside the citywide Vancouver Official Development Plan, which replaced the Vancouver Plan on March 31, 2026; a Knight-corridor-specific adopted plan: UNVERIFIED. The June 2025 Conwest referral also records that Council repealed the Kensington-Cedar Cottage Community Vision in November 2023 — do not cite the old vision as current policy for the King Edward node. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md) and https://council.vancouver.ca/20250603/documents/rr10.pdf
- **Floodplain:** the corridor's south end descends to the Fraser River's north arm at the Knight Street Bridge; the City's designated floodplain includes the coastal floodplain along the Fraser River in South Vancouver, with Vancouver Building By-law setback and flood construction level requirements for parcels in it. Whether a specific parcel is in the floodplain: UNVERIFIED — check the City dataset. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)
- **Vancouver Empty Homes Tax:** every residential property owner must submit an annual property status declaration; properties deemed or declared empty in the 2025 reference year are taxed at 3% of the property's 2025 assessed taxable value, the declaration deadline for 2025 was February 3, 2026, and the 2025 Empty Homes Tax payment was due April 16, 2026. The City's tax is separate from the provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax, which may also apply. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/evidence-exemptions.aspx and (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)
- **Vancouver property taxes:** 2026 advance taxes were due February 3, 2026 and main taxes July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty on the unpaid balance after each due date; the home owner grant was also due July 3, 2026. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)
- **GST on new homes:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — relevant to the 2026-built Knight 18 townhomes if bought from the developer. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 (some secondary sources say March 12 — CRA governs). The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less (maximum saving $8,000), partial $835,000–$860,000, none at $860,000+. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www.equitylawgroup.ca/news/closing-costs-bc-home-purchase
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. (BC standard block, batches C1–C3.)
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60% (LTV up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), 4.50% at the top band with a non-traditional down payment, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Where are the Knight corridor's condos, actually?** At one node: King Edward Village at Knight Street, Kingsway and East King Edward Avenue — 398 residences in buildings of 5, 7, 12 and 17 storeys over a retail podium. The rest of the corridor is side-street houses and townhouse infill. https://d2wsxqxx9m1aa9.cloudfront.net/wdd6e4gmkti1c942ocnfr9ttta9g and https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/1503kingsway-appendixc.pdf
- **Q: Are 4028 Knight, 4078 Knight, 1432 Kingsway and 1483 King Edward different buildings?** They are addresses within the one King Edward Village development by the Aquilini Group — listings under all four addresses carry the King Edward Village name. Price and compare them as one development's phases/faces, not as four competing buildings. https://faithwilson.com/listings/516-4078-knight-street/ and https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3109152-516-4028-knight-street-vancouver-british-columbia-v5n5y8-555000/brochure
- **Q: What year was King Edward Village built?** Sources conflict — 2007 in a 4078 Knight Street listing, 2008 in 4028 Knight Street listings, 2009 for the retail podium in the brokerage source — consistent with phased completion. The conflict is flagged in Section 2; the specific building's strata plan and Form B govern. https://faithwilson.com/listings/516-4078-knight-street/
- **Q: Can I rent out a King Edward Village condo?** Long-term, yes — rental-restriction bylaws have been unenforceable since November 24, 2022, and the building is marketed as rental friendly with a nothing-less-than-30-days term, which is the enforceable short-term restriction. Vancouver's Empty Homes Tax declaration still applies if the unit sits empty. https://faithwilson.com/listings/516-4078-knight-street/ and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Are pets allowed at King Edward Village?** UNCONFIRMED from the public sources checked — no citable building source in this pass states the pet bylaw. Read the registered bylaws in the strata package before writing. Pet bylaws remain enforceable in BC. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)
- **Q: Is the 24-storey tower across from Kingcrest Park coming?** The Conwest application for 4001–4009 Knight Street (233 strata units) was referred to public hearing with a staff recommendation to **refuse** it (June 3, 2025 report). Treat it as a refused-recommendation proposal, not as incoming supply. https://council.vancouver.ca/20250603/documents/rr10.pdf
- **Q: What is the building proposed at 1406 East King Edward?** BC Housing supportive housing — approximately 90 studio homes with supports in the fact sheet (109 modular units in the later application report), operated by non-profit societies. It is non-market housing, not condos for sale. https://www.bchousing.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Vancouver-East-King-Edward-Fact-Sheet.pdf
- **Q: Which schools serve a Knight corridor address?** SD39 catchments are address-based. Schools in the local areas the corridor runs through include Sir Alexander Mackenzie Elementary (960 East 39th Avenue) and Sir Richard McBride Elementary (1300 East 29th Avenue) in Kensington-Cedar Cottage, and the Sunset elementaries to the south-west; John Oliver Secondary (530 East 41st Avenue) sits west of the corridor. The locator result for the research file's sample address is UNVERIFIED — run the exact address in the VSB locator. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)
- **Q: How noisy / busy is it?** Knight Street is one of Vancouver's heaviest truck and commuter arterials and part of the Highway 99 corridor via the Knight Street Bridge — frontage exposure is the corridor's defining trade-off, and the research file's pricing instruction is to never comp a Knight-frontage home directly against a side-street home without a frontage adjustment. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The grocery store, library and bank are inside the condo development.** King Edward Village's podium holds the Save-On-Foods, the Vancouver Public Library branch and services that listings describe as "right at your door step" — the amenity is the podium tenancy mix (the retail podium is 92.20% occupied in the brokerage source, with Save-On-Foods, TD, Service Canada and the library occupying 71.81% of it), so a change in podium tenancies changes daily life in the towers above. https://faithwilson.com/listings/516-4078-knight-street/ and https://d2wsxqxx9m1aa9.cloudfront.net/wdd6e4gmkti1c942ocnfr9ttta9g
- **City staff counted this node as the template — then repealed its vision.** Council's 2007 neighbourhood-centres report records the Knight and Kingsway plan adding capacity for about 800 further ground-oriented homes on top of the over-370 units on the King Edward Village site; the 2025 Conwest referral records that the Kensington-Cedar Cottage Community Vision that framed the node was repealed in November 2023. Policy citations for this corner need a date check. https://council.vancouver.ca/documents/pe4_003.pdf and https://council.vancouver.ca/20250603/documents/rr10.pdf
- **The health centre is on the street itself.** South Community Health Centre, 6405 Knight Street at East 49th Avenue, provides public and community health services for South Vancouver from an address on the corridor — one of the few civic anchors physically on Knight Street. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)
- **Knight 18's name and its phase count do not match — check the disclosure.** The current 3430 Knight Street listing names the project "Knight 18" while recording 7 units in the development phase shown and a 2026 build year; a buyer needs the disclosure statement / strata plan to reconcile the full collection size before relying on either figure. https://www.estateblock.com/vancouver/3430-knight-st-1975057
- **The corridor's south end is a bridge, not a boundary street.** Knight Street carries Highway 99 traffic over the Knight Street Bridge to Richmond — for King Edward Village owners that is the Richmond/YVR route from the front door, and for side-street owners it is the source of the truck traffic the research file prices as frontage exposure. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/knight.md)
