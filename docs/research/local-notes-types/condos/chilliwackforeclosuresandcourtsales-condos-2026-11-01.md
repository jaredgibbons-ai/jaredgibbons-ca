- **Strata lien and priority — only as the Act states it:** under Strata Property Act s. 116, a strata corporation may register a Certificate of Lien against a strata lot for unpaid strata fees, a special levy, a reimbursement of work costs under s. 85, or the lot's share of a judgment against the strata corporation, and on registration the lien ranks in priority to every other lien or registered charge except to the extent it is for a lot's share of a judgment against the strata corporation, a Crown lien or charge that is not a mortgage, or a lien under the Builders Lien Act. Fines and the costs of remedying a contravention cannot be the subject of that lien (s. 116(3)(c)). How those priorities play out on a specific foreclosure file is governed by that file's pleadings and sale order — UNCONFIRMED for any specific unit here. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol31/consol31/98043_06

Published case commentary on BC court-ordered strata sales (Toronto Dominion Bank v. Madson (2001); Canada Trustco Mortgage Co. v. Gies (2001)) turned on the terms of the sale order and whether the strata appeared and objected — secondary commentary, not a rule for a specific file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-f-certificate-of-payment and https://www.cwilson.com/fine-and-form-fs/

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

### The court layer (settled treatment, as in the earlier Langley foreclosure file in this series)

- **How a foreclosure starts:** a proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property, or for redemption, must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7(1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). If the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation, a person who subsequently registers an interest need not be served, is bound by an order made in the proceeding, and may file a response to petition — Rule 21-7(4). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Order nisi and the redemption period:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption — Rule 21-7(5)(b); the court may also make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7(5)(a) — and determine summarily, or order an account taken and certified, the amount due — Rule 21-7(5)(c). The Land Title Act refers to the court's order in a foreclosure as an "order nisi" (s. 271(1)). The Rules state no fixed length for the redemption period — it is the period the court fixes — and interest is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act, s. 19). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol33/consol33/96250_18 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Final order and applying for a sale:** in default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7(6); a party may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7(7). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5(3). The court's directions may appoint who has conduct, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of value — Rule 13-5(4). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Court approval of your purchase:** the manner of sale the court fixes may be sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5(4)(b); the person with conduct may apply, even before the redemption period expires, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7(9); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in Form 60 — Rule 13-5(6). The vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Competing bids:** the court's directions may define "the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids" — Rule 13-5(4)(d); the Supreme Court's practice direction PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings," governs practice in these proceedings, and PD-66 replaced the earlier sealed-bid practice direction PD-62. The timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66 were not retrieved from the court's website for the research file — UNVERIFIED beyond the rule text. The live page's account of sealed, subject-free backup offers submitted on the morning of a hearing is the page's own account and is not adopted here. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 and https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/chilliwackforeclosuresandcourtsales.md)
- **Where it is filed and where to read it:** unless the court otherwise orders, a foreclosure proceeding is commenced at the Supreme Court registry in the municipality where the land is located, if there is one there, and otherwise at a registry in the judicial district in which the land is located (Law and Equity Act, s. 21(2)). Chilliwack has a Supreme Court registry — Chilliwack Law Courts, 46085 Yale Road (Province courthouse directory, this pass) — but the registry for a specific file is read off that file, not assumed. Court Services Online eSearch is open to anyone with Internet access and shows the file number, registry, party names and filed documents for Supreme Court civil files, class "H - Foreclosure." https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01 and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/justice/courthouse-services/courthouse-locations?keyword=court&keyword=registries

### The strata layer

- **Form B (Strata Property Act s. 59):** in an ordinary resale the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate within 7 days (maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page), disclosing fees, amounts owed, approved levies, unfiled bylaw amendments, proceedings, parking/storage allocations and insurance summaries. Its availability in a court-ordered sale is UNCONFIRMED — see Section 2. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years with no deferral vote, and since November 1, 2023 stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF annually. The roster's oldest buildings (Mountain View Estates, 1988/1989; Creekside Estates, 1993) are in the age band where those reports drive levy risk — and in a court sale, where the document package may be thin, the current depreciation report is the document to chase first. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rentals and age:** long-term rental-restriction bylaws are void since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44) — flag any building-directory "rentals not allowed" line (for example Mountain View Estates') as void in-entry. The only valid age bylaw is 55+ (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2), with exemptions from May 1, 2023 for younger spouses/partners, live-in caregivers, legacy residents and defined children — an age bylaw, unlike a rental line, can still bind a court-sale buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City plans and taxes:** the Chilliwack 2050 Official Community Plan is Bylaw No. 5500 (Sardis plan Schedule B5, Agricultural Plan Schedule B1 — never "Schedule D / Bylaw 5024"); zoning is Zoning Bylaw No. 5000; Chilliwack's SSMUH bylaw is Bylaw 5395 (adopted June 18, 2024). None changes the court process — they matter only to what a buyer could later do with the lot. Chilliwack property taxes: 5% penalty on taxes outstanding after July 2, 2026 and an additional 5% after August 4, 2026. https://chilliwack.com/ocp and https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2522

### Taxes on a court-ordered condo purchase

- **PTT and GST treatment of court-ordered sales specifically: UNCONFIRMED.** No official source read for this file states a special Property Transfer Tax or GST rule for a purchase through a court-ordered sale, and this file does not invent one. The general rules a buyer's lawyer and notary will apply are: BC PTT at 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million, with the First-Time Home Buyers' Program (full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial $835,000–$860,000) and the Newly Built Home Exemption (full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000); GST at 5% on new housing and not on resales; the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4; 100% of GST to $1 million, maximum $50,000, phasing out to $1.5 million; agreements March 20, 2025–2031; Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122); the GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out); Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.60%–4.50%. Whether a court vesting order changes any of that for a given purchase is a question for the buyer's lawyer on the specific file — UNCONFIRMED here. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA), where relevant:** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. A court-ordered resale of an existing condo is not a presale; the point matters only if the unit is developer-held new product. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is a court-ordered condo sale just a normal purchase with a discount?** No. The contract is conditional on court approval where the court has fixed sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" (Rule 13-5(4)(b)), the seller is the person with conduct of sale selling under court-settled conditions (Rule 13-5(3)–(4)), and there is no published statistic in any official source for a "typical discount" in Chilliwack — any discount claim is UNCONFIRMED. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can my accepted offer still lose?** Yes, two ways the rules themselves create: the person with conduct may apply to confirm a sale even before the redemption period expires (Rule 21-7(9)), but until the confirming order is made the sale is not final; and the court's directions may define rights to bid, make offers or meet bids (Rule 13-5(4)(d)), so a competing bid at the approval stage is a real possibility in this process. The PD-66 timing/manner for competing bids: UNVERIFIED beyond the rule text. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can the owner still save the condo after I offer?** The redemption right runs until the process ends it: the court fixes a redemption period in the order nisi (Rule 21-7(5)(b)), interest runs to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act, s. 19), and only in default of payment within that period may a final order of foreclosure be granted (Rule 21-7(6)). Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Q: How long is the redemption period on the Chilliwack file I'm looking at?** Whatever that file's order says. The Rules state no set length — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b) — so anyone quoting a standard period for a specific file without reading its order is guessing. Read the order on Court Services Online or through counsel. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Will I get the full strata documents — Form B, minutes, depreciation report?** UNCONFIRMED as a general promise. In an ordinary resale the strata must provide a Form B within 7 days of a proper request; no official source read for this file guarantees a court sale will produce a full package, and the conditions of sale are court-settled particulars that may differ from a standard contract. Price the information risk, and read the conditions of sale line by line with a lawyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: How do I check the court file myself, and where is the Chilliwack registry?** Court Services Online eSearch is open to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry location, party names and the list of filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." The Province's courthouse directory lists Chilliwack Law Courts (Provincial and Supreme Courts) at 46085 Yale Road — read the registry off the specific file on eSearch rather than assuming it. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/justice/courthouse-services/courthouse-locations?keyword=court&keyword=registries
- **Q: Is the title clean if I win?** That is what the confirming order does: on the Rule 21-7(9) application the court may order the sale confirmed, direct the disposition of the proceeds and vest title in the purchaser; the result of the sale is certified in Form 60 (Rule 13-5(6)). The vesting order, not the listing, is the title-cleaning step. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What about strata fees or levies the old owner owed?** The strata's lien under Strata Property Act s. 116 covers unpaid strata fees, special levies and the other s. 116 amounts and ranks ahead of most other liens and charges, with the three exceptions the Act lists; fines cannot be liened. What is actually paid out of a specific sale, and in what order, is set by that file's sale order — get the Form F position and the order's payment directions before the approval hearing. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol31/consol31/98043_06
- **Q: Do I pay PTT or GST differently because it's a court sale, and is the unit vacant?** PTT/GST: UNCONFIRMED — no official source read for this file states a court-sale-specific rule; budget on the general rules in Section 3 and have a BC real-estate lawyer confirm the treatment on the specific file before the approval hearing. Vacancy/inspection: occupancy is a matter of the individual court file, not a published statistic — UNVERIFIED as a general statement; inspection and access terms live in the court-settled conditions of sale for that file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
