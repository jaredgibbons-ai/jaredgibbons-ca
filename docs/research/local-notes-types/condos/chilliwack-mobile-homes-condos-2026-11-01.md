# Chilliwack Mobile Homes — Condos: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named buildings themselves.

**Framing note, stated up front: this is a TOPIC file, not an area file, and its core distinction is legal, not geographic.** A manufactured home on a rented pad in a park is governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA) — the buyer owns the home and holds a site tenancy under park rules. A manufactured home in a bare-land strata is governed by the Strata Property Act (SPA) — the buyer owns the home AND a strata lot under registered bylaws. The two regimes have different rent/fee rules, different age-rule machinery, different consent rules on resale, and different documents. Nothing in this file blends them. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **The park-rule vs SPA-bylaw distinction — the core of this file.** Inside a pad-rent park, the age rule, pet rule and approval process are PARK RULES under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and the site tenancy agreement. The Strata Property Act's age-bylaw machinery — SPA ss. 123.1/123.2, the Regulation 7.01 exemptions, the Form B — does not govern a pad tenancy at all. Inside a bare-land strata (Baker Trail Village), the reverse is true: registered SPA bylaws govern, the Form B is the diligence document, and MHPTA pad-rent rules do not apply because there is no pad rent. Establish which regime a listing is in before applying any rule in this section. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/chilliwack-mobile-homes.md)
- **MHPTA pad-rent limits:** landlords in manufactured home parks can only increase rent with 3 full months' notice, can increase rent once every 12 months, and the increase cannot exceed the yearly rent increase limit; the 2026 limit for manufactured home site tenancies is 2.3% plus a proportional amount for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees, and the 2027 limit is 2.2% plus the same proportional amount; standard increases are notified on RTB Form 11a, and a landlord may apply to the RTB (Form 52) for an additional increase above the limit for qualifying expense or capital reasons. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment on a park sale (MHPTA s. 28):** a tenant may assign a manufactured home site tenancy agreement, or sublet the site, only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed obtained under the Regulation), an order of the director, or authorization in the tenancy agreement itself; consent may be withheld only in prescribed circumstances, and under s. 28(3) the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting. The consent request runs on RTB-10. There is no equivalent park-consent step in a strata sale. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/chilliwack-mobile-homes.md)
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s. 42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute the notice within 15 days, and may in defined circumstances apply for additional compensation. The conversion notice is issued on RTB-31. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Registration and the CSA label:** manufactured homes include modular and mobile homes; mobile homes are built under CSA Z-240 standards and modular homes under CSA A-277 standards; in B.C., the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered in the Manufactured Home Registry. The home may not be sold unless it has the original and legible CSA decal attached; if the label is lost, the home must be re-certified — Technical Safety BC has a licensed electrical contractor inspect the wiring and an electrical approval label is prepared, which can be used in lieu of a CSA label (the route behind the "Silver Label" shorthand). A permit to move a manufactured home is issued by the Registry and expires 30 days after issue. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **No pad-rent figure is stated as a Chilliwack fact in this file.** The live page states monthly pad fees in Chilliwack and Sardis parks "typically range from $550 to $950 per month." No official source publishes Chilliwack pad rents and no park's rent roll was opened in this pass, so that range is UNVERIFIED. The only pad figure that matters is the specific site's tenancy agreement figure, confirmed with the park. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/chilliwack-mobile-homes.md)
- **For the strata side (Baker Trail Village and any bare-land strata): Form B** — Strata Property Act s. 59 — states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance, and a summary of the strata's insurance, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain one every five years, no deferral by vote. **Minimum CRF contribution:** at least 10% of the operating fund since November 1, 2023. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 — in a STRATA. That voiding does not create a right to rent out a home in a pad-rent park: Mt Cheam's "no rentals" position, for example, is a park rule/tenancy matter, not a strata bylaw, and it is not voided by the strata amendment. Short-term rentals can still be restricted in a strata. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 — only 55+ is lawful IN A STRATA; exemptions for younger spouse/partner, live-in caregiver, legacy residents and defined child cases (May 1, 2023 expansion). Cottonwood Village's 55+ is a park rule, not an SPA bylaw, so the Regulation 7.01 spouse/caregiver exemptions do not automatically apply inside the park — read the park rules instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Are there any condos in this file?** No — zero apartment-condo buildings, stated plainly. This topic's stock is manufactured homes in two tenures: pad-rent parks (you own the home, rent the site) and one verified bare-land strata (Baker Trail Village, where you own the land too). Neither is a condo. Sources: Section 2 entries.
- **Q: What is the difference between a park rule and a strata bylaw — why does it matter?** Because almost every protection a buyer assumes comes from one regime or the other, not both. In a park: MHPTA governs — pad-rent caps (2026: 2.3% plus proportional; 2027: 2.2% plus proportional), park consent to assign on resale (RTB-10, no consent fee under s. 28(3)), and 12 months' notice plus $20,000 compensation on a park conversion. In a strata: SPA governs — Form B, depreciation reports, CRF rules, and the ss. 123.1/123.2 age-bylaw frame with its exemptions. A park's 55+ rule carries none of the strata exemptions automatically. Sources: Section 3 entries.
- **Q: Which Chilliwack park is actually 55+?** One verified park: Cottonwood Village (7610 Evans Rd — 143 units, clubhouse, First Nations leased land, listing-stated lease expiry 2076 plus an option to extend), where you own the home and rent the pad. Every other park checked by name is below 55+ or all-ages: Rainbow Estates (40+ on the specialist list, with the feed itself conflicting 40+ vs 45+), Country Park Village (45+), Baker Trail Village (no age restrictions), Mt Cheam (families allowed). Sources: Section 2 entries.
- **Q: Where is Cottonwood Village — Sardis or West Chilliwack?** UNRESOLVED in this file. The two systems name the same ground differently, and the park is documented in this city-wide topic file so neither sub-area file has to win the argument. Sources: Section 2 entry.
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up each year?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, and no more than the yearly limit set by the Residential Tenancy Branch: for manufactured home site tenancies the 2026 limit is 2.3% plus a proportional amount for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees, and the 2027 limit is 2.2% plus the proportional amount; increases above the limit require an RTB order. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: What pad rent should I expect in a Chilliwack park?** No figure is stated in this file: no official source publishes Chilliwack pad rents, and the live page's $550–$950 range is UNVERIFIED here. The figure that governs is the specific site's tenancy agreement — confirm the current pad rent, what it includes, and its increase history with the park for the exact site before offering. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/chilliwack-mobile-homes.md)
- **Q: Can the park refuse to let me buy — or refuse my buyer later?** Assignment of a pad tenancy needs the park owner's prior written consent under MHPTA s. 28, and consent may be withheld only in prescribed circumstances, with no fee chargeable for consent under s. 28(3). In a 55+ park, age is part of that consent picture through the park's own rules — get the park rules and the tenancy agreement in writing before writing an offer. In Baker Trail Village (strata) there is no park consent step at all. Sources: Section 3 entries.
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in Cottonwood Village?** Park age rules operate through the park's own rules, not through the strata spouse exemption — the provincial younger-spouse exemption in Regulation 7.01 is a strata rule and does not automatically apply inside a park. Read Cottonwood Village's current park rules for how a younger spouse is treated before relying on it. Sources: Section 3 entries.
- **Q: Why does the CSA label matter when buying a manufactured home?** The CSA label is the home's certification decal — Z-240 series for mobile homes, A-277 series for modular homes. The Province states a manufactured home may not be sold unless the original legible CSA decal is attached; if it is lost, the home must be re-certified, with Technical Safety BC arranging an electrical inspection and an approval label that can be used in lieu of the CSA label to list the home for sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: What happens if a park is converted or closed?** A park conversion needs all government permits and approvals in place first, then 12 months' notice covering a full year and $20,000 compensation per home under MHPTA s. 42, with defined additional-compensation routes where a home cannot be moved or no site is available within a reasonable distance. Confirm the park's ownership and any known redevelopment signals as part of due diligence. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
