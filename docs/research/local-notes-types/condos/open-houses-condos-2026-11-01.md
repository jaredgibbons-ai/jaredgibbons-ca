## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

Framed for an open-house visitor: what the host may and may not do, and what to ask for once a unit interests you.

- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3):** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report, provides that a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about their motivation, financial qualifications or needs. Use the open house for facts; save your story for your own agent. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Representation at an open house:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/open-houses.md)
- **Identification or sign-in for open-house visitors:** not stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED as a rule; a host may still ask, on the seller's instructions, but that practice is not in an official source. How open-house dates and times are set and entered for publication on the brokerage/board side is likewise UNVERIFIED on any BC Gov or BCFSA source. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/open-houses.md)
- **What to ask for after the open house — Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. The open house shows the unit; the Form B shows the building. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote — ask for the current report at or after the open house; since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — if a host or a directory line at an open house says "rentals not allowed," that line is VOID for long-term rentals. **Age bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children — ask whether a 55+ bylaw is registered before falling for a unit in a seniors-positioned building. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City plans and zoning (no single city governs this page):** an open-house unit's zoning and OCP designation are its own municipality's — for example Surrey 2050 OCP Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted June 15, 2026) and Surrey Zoning By-law No. 12000 for a Surrey unit; Maple Ridge Zoning Bylaw No. 7600-2019 for a Maple Ridge unit; New Westminster Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001 and OCP "Our City 2041" Bylaw No. 7925, 2017 for a New Westminster unit; Port Coquitlam Zoning Bylaw 2008 No. 3630 and OCP Bylaw 2013 No. 3838 for a Port Coquitlam unit. Check the unit's own city file in this program. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/official-community-plan
- **GST and rebates:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — a developer unit at an open house can carry GST where the resale next door does not. First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 — some secondary sources say March 12; CRA governs) — 100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements March 20, 2025–2031; GST New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC) — worked example with inputs stated:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Inputs: fair market value $600,000 (a stated round-number illustration — not a market figure for any area on this page), fully residential, no exemption claimed: $2,000 (1% × $200,000) + $8,000 (2% × $400,000) = **$10,000 PTT**. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** a presale presentation centre is not an open house in a finished home — where a purchase is on a developer contract, REDMA s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Pad rents (MHPTA) — if a park home is on the feed:** manufactured-home pad rent increases are capped at 2.3% plus the proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus the proportional amount for 2027. A park home has no Form B, no depreciation report and no CRF — the diligence documents are the park rules and the tenancy agreement. (Standing program rule.)
- **HBP / FHSA / CMHC:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.60%–4.50%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
