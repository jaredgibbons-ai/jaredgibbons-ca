## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

- **Stated plainly: there is no condo building list for this topic — count 0 by design.** This is a lots-and-land file. A vacant lot has no building, no Form B building package and no apartment-condo stock; apartment-condo buildings in Langley belong in the Langley area condo files in this series, not here. Verified named apartment-condo count for this topic file: **0 by design**.
- **The strata angle that does apply: bare-land strata (a strata of lots).** The Strata Property Act defines a "bare land strata plan" as a strata plan on which the boundaries of the strata lots are defined on a horizontal plane by reference to survey markers and not by reference to the floors, walls or ceilings of a building, or any other strata plan defined by regulation to be a bare land strata plan. In a bare-land strata, the strata lot is essentially a plot of land: the plan is filed before any homes are constructed, the lot owner builds (or contracts to build) the home on the lot, and the common property can include roads, streetlights, a septic system, a clubhouse, playground and forested areas. You cannot tell by looking whether detached homes sit on a bare-land strata — the words "bare land strata" appear on the first page of the strata plan. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol27/consol27/98043_01 and https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/
- **What that means for a Langley lot buyer:** if a lot is a bare-land strata lot, there IS a strata corporation — with bylaws, strata fees for the common property, and the Section 3 strata rules — even though there is no building. If the lot is freehold, none of that applies. The title and, where one exists, the strata plan decide which purchase you are in; the research file records that bare-land strata lots exist in some newer Langley communities but publishes no named bare-land strata complex for this topic: any named complex here would be UNCONFIRMED, and none is listed. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

### Strata Property Act — the bare-land strata block

- **Form B — Information Certificate (s.59).** Where a lot is a bare-land strata lot, a resale purchase still runs through the strata: the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fee, any amount owed, any special levy already approved, the CRF balance, and — since April 1, 2023 — a summary of the strata's insurance coverage. For a freehold lot there is no Form B at all. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports.** Effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. This applies to a bare-land strata corporation as it does to a building strata — its report covers the common property (roads, systems, amenities), not the houses on the lots. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Strata fees and the CRF.** Since November 1, 2023, strata corporations must contribute a minimum of 10% of the annual operating fund to the contingency reserve fund when approving budgets. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void.** Since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata corporation in BC may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — existing ones are invalid; short-term rentals can still be restricted. **Age bylaws — 55+ only (ss.123.1 / 123.2):** the only permitted age-restriction bylaw is 55+, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner and defined children. Both rules apply to a bare-land strata exactly as to a condo building. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Insurance difference a bare-land buyer must know:** the Strata Property Act requires the strata corporation to insure common property, common assets, and buildings shown on the strata plan — in a bare-land strata the house sits entirely inside the strata lot and is not shown as a building on the plan, so the lot owner insures the house; the strata does not. https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/

### Land-side rules the research file verifies

- **Township SSMUH — Bylaw 6020.** Township of Langley Zoning Bylaw No. 2500 Amendment (Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing) Bylaw No. 6020 (adopted November 18, 2024, per the assignment's standing correction) implements the provincial SSMUH mandate under Local Government Act s.481.3, which requires the Township to permit up to four units on existing single-family detached and duplex lots; the Township's June 10, 2024 council report estimates approximately 55% of the Township's single-family and duplex lots (~15,000 properties) will qualify for 3 to 4 units, with eligibility varying by municipal water and sewer servicing (Brookswood ~15% and Fort Langley ~22% eligible; Walnut Grove ~99% and Murrayville ~98%). **No 6-plex tier exists in Bylaw 6020** — the Township report frames the bylaw as a 3-to-4-unit regime, unlike the City of Langley's transit-area framework. Added-unit housing eligibility requires a property to be connected to both municipal water and sewer. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/resources/social-sustainability/housing/Report-to-Council----Small-Scale,-Multi-Unit-Housing,-June-10,-2024.pdf and https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/small-scale-multi-unit-housing.aspx
- **Zoning — the two Langleys.** Township: Zoning Bylaw 1987 No. 2500 "applies to the entirety of the Township." City: Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300 "applies to the entire area within the boundaries of the City of Langley" and repeals Zoning Bylaw 1996 No. 2100 (the City's media library still serves a consolidated Bylaw 2100 file — Bylaw 3300 is the current source). Minimum lot sizes (Bylaw 2500, s.110.1, as reproduced in a Township council report): SR-1 — 3,716 m²; SR-2 — 8,094 m²; SR-3 — 1,765 m²; RU-1 — 1.7 ha; RU-2 — 8.0 ha; RU-3 — 8.0 ha; urban R-1B/R-1C/R-2 — 464.5 m². City (Bylaw 3300): R1 Suburban Residential — 557 m² minimum lot size; R2 Townhouse Residential — 1,000 m²; R3 Low Rise Residential — 1,000 m². https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/zoning-bylaw.aspx and https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/zoning-bylaw
- **Subdivision.** All Township subdivisions must be approved by the Approving Officer under the Land Title Act, through application, review, decision letter, conditions, final approval and registration. City Bylaw 3300 adds that meeting the minimum lot standards does not bind the Approving Officer to approve. Development cost charges: Township DCC Bylaw No. 5897; City DCC Bylaw 2024 No. 3256. Servicing upgrades are determined under the Township's Subdivision and Development Servicing Bylaw 2019 No. 5382. https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/subdivision.aspx
- **ALR position — ALC Information Bulletin 09 (issued October 18, 2022).** The ALC's bulletin on subdivision and plans that cause subdivision in the ALR states the governing frame: a person must not subdivide agricultural land unless permitted by the Commission after an application, or permitted under s.3 of the ALR General Regulation (Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)); an approving officer under the Land Title Act, the Local Government Act or the Strata Property Act is prohibited from approving a subdivision of ALR land unless so permitted (ALCA s.18(5)); and the bulletin's contents include a dedicated section, "Strata Plans Cause Subdivision" — i.e., **creating a bare-land strata on ALR land is itself a subdivision and needs the same ALC permission.** With 235 km² of the Township in the ALR, this is the rule underneath most rural Langley lot-and-strata questions. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf and https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/about-the-township.aspx
- **ALR housing rules.** A principal residence built without an ALC application must have a total floor area of 500 m² or less, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill; a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence; and one additional detached residence is permitted up to 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or smaller or 186 m² on larger parcels, where Township bylaws also permit it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Environment and servicing on lots.** Township development permit types include Streamside Protection and Enhancement (Schedule 3 of the Township OCP, for watercourses not in the ALR); a permit is required to cut or remove a protected tree under Tree Protection Bylaw 2019, No. 5478. A home not connected to a community sewer must use an onsite sewage system complying with the Sewerage System Regulation, designed and installed by an Authorized Person (Fraser Health); the provincial GWELLS registry holds recorded wells, and non-domestic groundwater use requires a water licence under the Water Sustainability Act. https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/development-activity.aspx and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/health-topics-a-to-z/onsite-sewerage-systems/regulatory-requirements-for-onsite-sewerage-systems

### Taxes and rebates — purchase

- **GST on land and new homes.** GST treatment of a vacant-lot purchase depends on the seller and the lot's history — no single rate is stated here because no source in the research file states one for land: UNCONFIRMED for any specific parcel; confirm with the buyer's legal professional. For a newly built home on the lot: 5% GST applies to new construction. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million — PTT is payable on a land purchase registered at the Land Title Office unless an exemption applies. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less, partial $835,000–$860,000 (principal-residence and other conditions apply — a vacant lot purchase's eligibility is a PTT-return question: UNCONFIRMED here for bare land). Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000, for a qualifying newly built home. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Presales (REDMA).** The 7-day rescission and disclosure-statement regime applies to developer marketing of strata lots, including bare-land strata lots sold by an owner developer; no Langley bare-land presale project is named in this file: UNCONFIRMED. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles.** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year — both are for a qualifying home purchase, and whether a bare lot alone qualifies is a program-conditions question: do not assume it. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC).** Published premiums run 0.60% to 4.50% of the mortgage amount for insured home purchases; vacant-land financing is a lender-specific product and CMHC's home-purchase schedule is not stated here as a land-loan rule. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Are there any condo buildings in this file?** None — count 0 by design. This is a lots-and-land topic; the only strata form a lot can take is a bare-land strata lot (Section 2). Langley apartment condos live in the Langley area condo files in this series. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/langley-lots-land.md)
- **Q: What is a bare-land strata lot, in plain terms?** A plot of land that is a strata lot: the Strata Property Act defines a bare land strata plan by lot boundaries drawn on a horizontal plane by survey markers, not by a building's floors, walls or ceilings. You own the lot, you build the house, and a strata corporation owns the common property (roads, systems, amenities) and charges strata fees. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol27/consol27/98043_01
- **Q: How do I tell if a Langley lot is bare-land strata or freehold?** Not by looking — detached homes on a bare-land strata look like any detached homes. The words "bare land strata" appear on the first page of the strata plan at the Land Title Office; if the homes are drawn on the plan, it is a building strata instead. https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/
- **Q: Who insures the house on a bare-land strata lot?** You do. The strata corporation insures common property, common assets and buildings shown on the strata plan — a house entirely inside a bare-land strata lot is not shown on the plan, so it is the lot owner's policy, not the strata's. https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/
- **Q: Can I turn ALR land into a bare-land strata?** Only with ALC permission: ALC Information Bulletin 09 treats strata plans as plans that cause subdivision, and subdivision of ALR land requires Commission permission after an application unless the ALR General Regulation permits it — an approving officer (including under the Strata Property Act) cannot approve it otherwise. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Q: How much of Langley is in the ALR?** The Township of Langley states 235 square kilometres of the Township is within the Agricultural Land Reserve; check a specific parcel on the Agricultural Land Commission's maps. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/about-the-township.aspx and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Q: What is the smallest rural lot I can create in the Township?** It depends on the zone under Zoning Bylaw No. 2500, s.110.1: RU-1 requires 1.7 ha for a new lot, RU-2 and RU-3 require 8.0 ha, SR-2 requires 8,094 m² and SR-1 requires 3,716 m² — and ALR land also needs Agricultural Land Commission permission to subdivide. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/resources/social-sustainability/housing/TOL-Council-Report---Provincial-Housing-Legislation,-June-10,-2024.pdf
- **Q: Does SSMUH let me put four homes on a Township lot?** Bylaw 6020 implements the provincial mandate to permit up to four units on existing single-family detached and duplex lots — but eligibility turns on municipal water and sewer servicing (about 55% of Township lots, ~15,000 properties, are estimated to qualify; Brookswood ~15%, Fort Langley ~22%, Walnut Grove ~99%, Murrayville ~98%), added-unit housing requires connection to both municipal water and sewer, and **there is no 6-plex tier in Bylaw 6020**. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/resources/social-sustainability/housing/Report-to-Council----Small-Scale,-Multi-Unit-Housing,-June-10,-2024.pdf
- **Q: Can I build a second home on a rural Langley lot?** On ALR land, one additional detached residence is permitted without an ALC application where the conditions are met — 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller, 186 m² or less on larger parcels — and Township bylaws must also permit it; the principal residence is capped at 500 m², and a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Two Langleys, two current zoning bylaws — and a stale one still online.** Township land sits under Zoning Bylaw 1987 No. 2500; City land under Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300, which repealed Bylaw 1996 No. 2100 — yet the City's media library still serves a consolidated Bylaw 2100 file. Quote Bylaw 3300 for the City. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/zoning-bylaw
- **A strata plan is a subdivision in the ALR — even with no building.** ALC Information Bulletin 09 (October 18, 2022) devotes a section to "Strata Plans Cause Subdivision": filing a bare-land strata plan on reserve land triggers the same ALC permission requirement as a conventional subdivision. Most lot buyers never hear this until the application stage. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **SSMUH eligibility in the Township is a servicing map, not a slogan.** The Township's own estimate puts Brookswood at ~15% and Fort Langley at ~22% of single-family/duplex lots eligible for 3–4 units, against ~99% in Walnut Grove and ~98% in Murrayville — because eligibility follows existing municipal water and sewer. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/resources/social-sustainability/housing/Report-to-Council----Small-Scale,-Multi-Unit-Housing,-June-10,-2024.pdf
- **Meeting the minimum lot size does not guarantee a subdivision.** City of Langley Bylaw 3300 states expressly that the bylaw does not bind the Approving Officer to approve a subdivision that meets the minimum standards — and Township subdivisions run a seven-stage Approving Officer process ending in Land Title Office registration. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/zoning-bylaw and https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/subdivision.aspx
- **The rural sub-area rows are a thin market — do not read them as land demand.** In September 2026 the rural detached rows were Salmon River 22 inventory / 5 solds, Campbell Valley 16 / 0, County Line Glen Valley 0 / 1, Otter District 2 / 0 — and they are residential rows, not land statistics in any event. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/langley-lots-land.md)
- **A lot's school catchment follows its address.** School District 35 catchments are address-based and checked in the SD35 School Locator — verify the specific parcel address before marketing school access for a buildable family lot. https://www.sd35.bc.ca/school-locator
- **Provincial dates that changed a bare-land strata lot's rule book:** rental bylaws void November 24, 2022; Form B insurance summary April 1, 2023; wider 55+ exemptions May 1, 2023; 10% CRF minimum November 1, 2023; depreciation reports every five years from July 1, 2024; Township SSMUH Bylaw 6020 adopted November 18, 2024. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
