## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**Zero strata-condo stock in this topic, stated plainly at the top — with one named exception, stated just as plainly. A manufactured home in a South Surrey park is not a condo: for pad-rental product there is no strata corporation, no Form B, no contingency reserve fund and no depreciation report — the buyer owns a manufactured home and rents the pad under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA). The one exception is Sea Ridge Bays (15875 20 Avenue), a bare-land STRATA manufactured-home community: there the buyer owns the home AND the strata lot, pays a strata fee instead of pad rent, and the strata regime applies. It is the one strata-form exception in this topic — and it is NOT pad-rental. The parks below are otherwise documented as tenure traps — product a buyer can mistake for strata ownership — not as condo buildings, and none is counted as an apartment condo.**

### The core distinction — park rule vs strata bylaw

- In a strata, an age restriction is a registered bylaw: BC law allows only a 55-or-older residency bylaw (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2), it is disclosed on the Form B, and the May 1, 2023 exemptions for younger spouses/partners and defined children apply to it. In a manufactured home park, the 55+ rule is a **park rule** set by the park owner under the MHPTA tenancy — it is not an SPA bylaw, no Form B discloses it, and the SPA exemptions do not govern it; the written park rules, which the seller must hand to the proposed buyer before assignment is even requested, are the document that governs. That is also why Sea Ridge Bays' 55+ position is a different legal object from any park's: as a bare-land strata, its age rule (as presented in listings) sits in the strata framework, with the SPA exemptions — confirm the registered bylaw on its Form B. Treat any "55+ strata" language in a pad-rental park listing as a category error. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

Read this section as "rules a condo buyer expects, and what actually applies instead" — the Strata Property Act block is stated because buyers arrive with condo assumptions; each park item names the manufactured-home rule that replaces it, and the Sea Ridge Bays exception names where the strata rules genuinely do apply.

- **Pad tenancy law — the regime that governs the park product.** Manufactured home site tenancies are covered by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA); landlords and tenants must have a written tenancy agreement for the site. On a sale where the home stays in the park, assignment of the site tenancy permanently transfers the pad tenancy to the buyer; under MHPTA s. 28 a tenant may assign only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it; before asking, the seller must give the proposed buyer written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent; and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/south-surrey-mobile-homes.md)
- **Pad rent increases — verified limits.** Landlords in manufactured home parks can increase rent only with 3 full months' notice, at most once every 12 months, and not above the yearly rent increase limit, plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park); further increases only by Residential Tenancy Branch order. The limits: **2026 = 2.3% plus the proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% plus the proportional amount.** Freshly re-verified against the Province's page in this pass. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Worked example (illustrative inputs, stated — NOT a South Surrey park's rent):** a pad rent of $800 per month, with an increase taking effect in 2026. The percentage component is 2.3% × $800 = $18.40, so the rent may rise to $818.40 per month **plus** the proportional amount — the park's own change in local government levies plus regulated park utility fees, divided by that park's site count. The proportional amount cannot be computed from any published South Surrey figure, because no official source publishes a park's levy/utility change for this calculation. The $800 input is an arithmetic illustration only. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s. 42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute the notice within 15 days, may in defined circumstances apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent. Surrey's Tenant Protection Bylaw, 2025, No. 21788 does NOT apply to property governed by the MHPTA. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_reg_21788.pdf
- **Form B — there is none for a park home, and there IS one at Sea Ridge Bays.** A Form B Information Certificate (Strata Property Act s. 59 — fees, amounts owing, approved special levies, CRF balance, insurance summary, with rules, budget and the most recent depreciation report attached; within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page) exists only for strata lots. The park-home equivalents are the written park rules, the site tenancy agreement, the home's Manufactured Home Registry record and its CSA label status. At Sea Ridge Bays, the bare-land strata exception, a Form B is exactly what a buyer should demand — the tenure decides which document set exists. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF — strata-only rules, live only at the exception.** The rule that BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years (since July 1, 2024, no deferral vote) and contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund (since November 1, 2023) binds strata corporations only — including the Sea Ridge Bays strata. A pad-rental park has no depreciation report and no CRF; the park's capital plans are the landlord's, and a buyer's protection is the MHPTA rent-increase regime above, not a strata report. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void — a strata rule, not a park rule.** Since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw. That voiding is a Strata Property Act change; it does not rewrite a park's rules — and it cuts both ways at Breakaway Bays, where the listings themselves conflict on whether any rental restriction exists at all (see Section 2). Whether a park home may be rented out is governed by the park rules and the site tenancy agreement — read them, do not import the strata rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ — park rule vs SPA bylaw.** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw in a strata, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children — that framework is the one that applies at Sea Ridge Bays, subject to its registered bylaws. A park's 55+ restriction is a park rule under the MHPTA, set in the written park rules — the SPA exemption list does not govern it. And at Breakaway Bays the prior question is unanswered: whether any age rule exists there at all is in conflict between the directory and the listings (Section 2). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Registration is what makes the sale effective, and the CSA label is a sale gate.** In BC, the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered with the Manufactured Home Registry, through a lawyer or notary filing the original signed and witnessed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership forms. Manufactured homes include modular and mobile homes; the CSA label states the standard — modular homes under A-277, mobile homes under Z-240. A home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal; a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification. A permit to move a home is issued by the Registry, a tax certificate for transport purposes is required (with exceptions including a move to a different pad within the same park), and the transport permit expires 30 days after issue. This registry step applies to manufactured homes in every tenure in this file — park, own-land and bare-land strata alike. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Taxes on a home in a park.** The Province states a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value will not include the value of the land if the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/south-surrey-mobile-homes.md)
- **City zoning and plans.** Surrey's current plan is the Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted June 15, 2026) — never cite the replaced Bylaw No. 18020 as current. Surrey Zoning By-law, 1993, No. 12000 carries a dedicated zone for this housing form (Part 19, Zone RM-M, Manufactured Home Residential Zone) — but the live page's claim applying RM-M to the specific South Surrey parks in this feed, and its claim that White Rock's Zoning Bylaw 2506 has no manufactured-home-park zone, were not read in the bylaw texts in the research pass: UNVERIFIED as applied. - **GST and purchase taxes.** 5% GST applies to new construction, not ordinary resales; how GST and Property Transfer Tax apply to a specific manufactured-home purchase depends on whether land transfers with the home (a park unit transfers no land; a Sea Ridge Bays unit transfers a strata lot) — no source in the research file settles the treatment for a specific unit: UNCONFIRMED per unit; take conveyancing advice before writing. For qualifying new homes generally: the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (CRA News No. 122: Royal Assent March 13, 2026); the GST New Housing Rebate returns 36% to a $6,300 maximum across $350,000–$450,000. BC Property Transfer Tax generally runs 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million and 3% above, with a First-Time Home Buyers' exemption (full to $835K FMV, partial to $860K) and a Newly Built exemption (full $1.1M, gone $1.15M) where land is acquired. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. Manufactured-home resales are not REDMA presales — stated so a buyer does not assume a 7-day rescission exists on a park purchase; subject periods are contractual here. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html

- **Q: What happens if a South Surrey park closes or is converted?** Under s. 42 of the MHPTA, as the Province states it: the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, then give 12 months' notice and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the effective date; tenants may leave early on 10 days' written notice, may dispute within 15 days, and may in defined circumstances claim additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000; if the landlord does not proceed within a reasonable period, a further payment of the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent applies.
