## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **The regulator's framework applies from the outset of dealings:** in BC, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings — whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA — and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. Practical consequence for this page: commission and fee information is part of what BCFSA requires a licensee to give a consumer, but the page itself states no fee or commission figure — so any fee discussion for a seller reading this page happens off-page, in the listing conversation, and no fee is stated in this file. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://jaredgibbons.ca/marketing-plan.html
- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), where it is supported:** the page does not name open houses at all (Section 2), so no open-house practice is claimed for the plan. Where an open house is held for a listing, the settled rule in this program is Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report: the disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Form B — the document a condo seller's marketing has to survive:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale condo sale the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance, and a summary of the strata's insurance, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. Photography and promotion bring the buyers; this package is what their agents price against. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain one every five years, no deferral by vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. **Minimum CRF contribution:** at least 10% of the operating fund since November 1, 2023. A current depreciation report is part of the document package a listing presents — its findings shape the questions a marketed listing will face. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/ and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since Nov 24, 2022 — marketing copy for a condo listing that repeats a directory or bylaw line stating "rentals not allowed" is repeating old bylaw text, not current law; short-term rentals can still be restricted. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 — only a 55-or-older residency bylaw is lawful; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. Where a condo being marketed is in a 55+ building, the lawful buyer pool is defined by this bylaw — a fact the marketing has to state accurately, from the registered bylaws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **GST on new condos only:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — so a seller's GST position depends on whether the unit is a developer sale or a resale, and listing copy must not blur the two. First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) — 100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031; GST New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial $835,000–$860,000, none at $860,000+. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, gone at $1,150,000. These are buyer-side taxes, stated here because pricing strategy — the plan's stated starting point — is set against the buyer's total cost, not the list price alone. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Presales (REDMA):** where the home being marketed is a developer-contract presale rather than a listed resale, the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18 — a different sale from the MLS® listing route this page describes. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles and mortgage insurance:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.60%–4.50%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- **Listing-data notice the site carries:** the standard site footer notice states the data relating to real estate on the website comes in part from the MLS® Reciprocity program of either the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® (GVR), the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board (FVREB) or the Chilliwack and District Real Estate Board (CADREB); listings held by participating firms are marked with the MLS® logo and include the listing agent's name; the representation is based on board-generated data the boards do not warrant for accuracy; and the materials may not be reproduced without the boards' express written consent. CREA owns the MLS® and REALTOR® trademarks, and agents must be members of a local board and CREA to access the MLS® system. https://jaredgibbons.ca/marketing-plan.html and https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
