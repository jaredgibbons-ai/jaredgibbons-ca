## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3):** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, states that a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Representation at an open house:** a licensed real estate professional in BC must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party to the transaction. The regulator is the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and the Province's Buying and selling a home page directs prospective purchasers to BCFSA for information on purchasing property in BC. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home
- **Identification or sign-in for open-house visitors:** NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED as a rule; a host may still ask, as a matter of the seller's instructions, but that practice is not in an official source. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-8/tsawwassen-open-houses.md)
- **How the dates and times get onto the page:** UNVERIFIED — how open house dates and times are set and entered for publication on the brokerage / board side, and how ShowingTime and TouchBase schedule, publish or announce open houses in this market, are not documented on any BC Gov, BCFSA or board public source found for the research. What is documented is the output: listings carrying an OPEN HOUSE label with a date and start and end times — with the one-hour card-vs-text discrepancy flagged in Section 1. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-8/tsawwassen-open-houses.md)
- **Form B and strata records:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. This is the document step after the open house — the open house shows the unit, the Form B shows the building. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote. In Tsawwassen's roster this lands hardest on the oldest buildings — Tsawwassen Mews (1977), The Lexington (1987), Tsawwassen Greene (1990), Royal Oaks (1992) and Heron Place (1992). **Minimum CRF contribution:** at least 10% of the operating fund since November 1, 2023. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void:** since November 24, 2022, no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw; short-term rentals can still be restricted. Building directories still show "rentals not allowed" for Tsawwassen Greene and Heron Cove — that reflects old bylaw text, not current law, and an open-house host repeating it is repeating an unenforceable line. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ is the only legal age bylaw:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. Of the roster, only The Lexington carries a stated 55+ restriction in listing sources — confirm on each Form B whether a registered 55+ bylaw exists before attending an open as an under-55 occupant. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Delta plan and zoning — number CONTESTED, flagged as required:** OCP Bylaw No. 8400 (2024), consolidated, no lettered schedules (the former Tsawwassen Area Plan was Schedule D.1 under the previous OCP, Bylaw No. 3950); farm land designated "Agriculture (A)". City of Delta pages cite the Zoning Bylaw as No. 7600, 2017, while an internal title says "8400, 2025" — the collision is flagged here and neither number is asserted as settled. SSMUH Bylaws 8394 and 8395, adopted June 24, 2024. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-8/tsawwassen-open-houses.md)
- **Flood frame:** Delta is the diking authority and operates over 63 km of flood protection infrastructure, 31 drainage pump stations and approximately 538 km of drainage infrastructure; hazard DPA SD3 (Boundary Bay Foreshore and the Southlands — 2.9 m GSC datum floor level, 2.3 m minimum land surface elevation, 7.5 m tidal setback) and SD2 (English Bluff escarpment) are the overlays an open-house visitor on the shore or bluff side is walking through. http://www.delta.ca/services/water-sewer-drainage/sanitary-sewer-drainage
- **Delta property taxes:** 2026 due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty after and a further 5% after September 2, 2026. https://www.delta.ca/services/property-taxes-utilities/property-taxes
- **GST on new condos:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026. The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less, partial $835,000–$860,000, none at $860,000+. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust — the frame if an open-house-style event is a developer sales event rather than a resale open. https://northam-law.com/pre-sale-condo-purchases-in-british-columbia-key-legal-protections-for-buyers/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60% (LTV up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), 4.50% at the top band with a non-traditional down payment, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Do I need my own agent to attend a Tsawwassen open house?** No official source requires it. BCFSA's Real Estate Rules excuse disclosure of representation where a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or answering general factual questions — but the moment you share your motivation, financial qualifications or needs, that changes, and BCFSA requires unrepresented consumers to be told the risks of dealing with a licensee who represents the other party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Q: Is the host at an open house the seller's agent?** The host is a licensee connected to the listing side of the transaction; BCFSA requires a licensee to work for either the buyer or the seller and to inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients — assume anything non-factual you tell the host can reach the seller. Use the open house for facts; save your story for your own agent. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: How do I know the open house time is current?** Carefully, on this feed: each listing carries an OPEN HOUSE label with a date and start and end times, but on the cards read for the research the card time and the time inside the listing's own text differ by one hour, and the page states no feed update interval — confirm the actual time with the listing brokerage before you go. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-8/tsawwassen-open-houses.md)
- **Q: Do I have to sign in or show ID at an open house?** No BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source states an identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors — UNVERIFIED as a rule; an individual host may still ask, on the seller's instructions. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-8/tsawwassen-open-houses.md)
- **Q: Which Tsawwassen areas appear on the open-house feed?** On the cards read for the research: Tsawwassen Central, Tsawwassen North, English Bluff and Cliff Drive, across condos, townhomes and detached houses — the feed is Tsawwassen-wide and changes as listings come and go. The condo events, on the roster, can only come from the Central, East and North buildings in Section 2. https://jaredgibbons.ca/tsawwassen-open-houses.html
- **Q: How should I group a Tsawwassen open-house run?** On the community's actual geography as the research frames it: Tsawwassen North (the Highway 17 side), Tsawwassen Central and Cliff Drive (the 56 Street / 12 Avenue side) and English Bluff (the west bluff) — group by those clusters and use 56 Street and 12 Avenue as the spine, rather than zig-zagging the peninsula; use dead time at South Delta Recreation Centre (1720 56 Street) or Boundary Bay Regional Park's Centennial Beach and Dyke Trail. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-8/tsawwassen-open-houses.md)
- **Q: The host says the building bans rentals — can I buy as an investor?** Yes for a long-term tenancy — rental-restriction bylaws have been unenforceable since November 24, 2022 anywhere in BC, whatever a directory or a host says (Tsawwassen Greene and Heron Cove directories both say "rentals not allowed"). What can still bind you: a short-term rental bylaw, and a 55+ age bylaw limiting who may live in the unit (The Lexington). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Can I buy at The Lexington if I am under 55?** The bylaw restricts who may live in the unit, not who may own it; a 55+ bylaw is the one age bylaw BC law still allows (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2), with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (since May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. Whether you can occupy, or only own and rent to a qualifying tenant, depends on the registered bylaw on the Form B — ask before the open house, not after. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Are pets allowed in these buildings?** It varies and pet bylaws remain enforceable in BC (unlike rental bylaws). Published examples: Windsor Woods — small pets to 30 lbs per a listing; Oliva — 1 dog to 22 lbs or 2 cats per a listing; Royal Oaks, Heron Cove, Tsawwassen Greene — directory records pets allowed with restrictions; Windgrove — directory and listing conflict, UNCONFIRMED. For the other roster buildings, pet rules are UNCONFIRMED from public sources — read the registered bylaws in the strata package. Sources: Section 2 entries.
- **Q: What should I ask for after I like a unit at an open house?** The strata package behind the Form B: the depreciation report (mandatory every five years for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024), financial statements, CRF balance and planned work — the items BCREA advises reviewing to identify future special levies. In the 1977–1992 buildings (Tsawwassen Mews, The Lexington, Tsawwassen Greene, Royal Oaks, Heron Place) that package matters more than the staging. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
