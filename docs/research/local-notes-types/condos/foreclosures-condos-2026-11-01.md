## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- This file covers buying a condo anywhere in the Fraser Valley through a BC Supreme Court court-ordered sale inside a foreclosure proceeding. The governing rules are provincial, not municipal: the Supreme Court Civil Rules (notably Rule 21-7 on foreclosure proceedings and Rule 13-5 on court-ordered sales), the Law and Equity Act, and the Land Title Act's reference to the order nisi govern this topic in every Fraser Valley community exactly as elsewhere in BC. The process facts in this file are the BC facts; there is no Fraser Valley variant. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md) and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- Terminology this file uses exactly: in BC a "court-ordered sale" is a sale conducted under a court order inside a foreclosure proceeding, not a separate process, and almost every BC foreclosure sale is "subject to court approval" because the sale contract is conditional on the court approving it. "Bank foreclosure" is a misnomer in BC — sales run through the court, not the bank, and the proceeding is started by petition, not by a bank. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md) and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

### The court layer (from BC Laws sources, as settled in the program's earlier foreclosure files)

- **How a foreclosure starts:** a proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property, or for redemption, must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7(1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner may also join a claim arising out of the mortgage or collateral security and a person liable to pay the mortgage debt — Rule 21-7(3). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Later interests and the information notice:** if the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation against the mortgaged property, a person who subsequently registers or files an interest in a land title office need not be served with the petition, is bound by an order made in the proceeding, and may file a response to petition — Rule 21-7(4); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Order nisi and the redemption period:** the court may make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7(5)(a); order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption — Rule 21-7(5)(b); and determine summarily, or order that an account be taken of and that a registrar certify, the amount due — Rule 21-7(5)(c). The Land Title Act refers to the court's order in a foreclosure as an "order nisi" (s. 271(1)). The Rules state no fixed length for the redemption period — it is the period the court fixes — and interest is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act, s. 19). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol33/consol33/96250_18 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Final order and applying for a sale:** in default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7(6); a party may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7(7). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5(3). The court's directions may appoint who has conduct, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of value — Rule 13-5(4). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Court approval of your purchase:** the manner of sale the court fixes may be sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5(4)(b); the person with conduct may apply, even before the redemption period expires, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7(9); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in Form 60 — Rule 13-5(6). The vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Competing bids:** the court's directions may define "the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids" — Rule 13-5(4)(d); the Supreme Court's practice direction PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings," governs practice in these proceedings. The timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66 were not retrieved from the court's website for the earlier files — UNVERIFIED beyond the rule text. The live page's statement that competing offers can come in at the courthouse on the day an offer is heard is consistent with that bid-rights frame but is recorded as the page's own account of the day's mechanics. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 and (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md)
- **Where it is filed and where to read it:** unless the court otherwise orders, a foreclosure proceeding is commenced at the Supreme Court registry in the municipality where the land is located, if there is one there, and otherwise at a registry in the judicial district in which the land is located (Law and Equity Act, s. 21(2)). Which registry serves a given Fraser Valley property as a stated mapping is UNVERIFIED in this file. Court Services Online eSearch is open to anyone with Internet access and shows the file number, registry, party names and filed documents for Supreme Court civil files, class "H - Foreclosure." https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01 and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do

### The strata layer

- **Form B — the limit that matters most in a court sale:** in an ordinary resale, an owner, purchaser or authorized person may request a Form B Information Certificate (Strata Property Act s. 59) and the strata corporation must provide it within 7 days (maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page), disclosing fees, amounts owed, approved levies, unfiled bylaw amendments, proceedings, parking/storage allocations and insurance and electrical-planning summaries, with the most recent depreciation report attached. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Form B in a court-ordered sale — UNCONFIRMED as a general rule:** no official source read for this program's foreclosure files states that a court-ordered sale will produce a full Form B and strata document package, or that it will not. The seller in a court sale is the person with conduct of sale under court directions (Rule 13-5(3)), not the strata-lot owner in the ordinary course, and the conditions of sale are the court-settled particulars (Rule 13-5(4)) — so a buyer must not assume the usual document package will be available, complete or current. What documents a specific court sale offers is a fact of that sale's conditions — read them, and treat any general claim either way as UNCONFIRMED. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Form F is a separate, litigated point:** published case commentary on BC court-ordered strata sales turns on the Form F Certificate of Payment (Strata Property Act s. 115) — whether a strata must deliver a Form F where fines are outstanding was decided differently on different facts in Toronto Dominion Bank v. Madson (2001) and Canada Trustco Mortgage Co. v. Gies (2001), turning on the terms of the sale order and whether the strata appeared and objected. That is secondary legal commentary, not a rule a buyer can rely on for a specific file — the sale order's own terms govern. https://www.cwilson.com/fine-and-form-fs/
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years with no deferral vote, and since November 1, 2023 stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF annually. In a court sale, where the document package may be thin (above), the current depreciation report is the document to chase first. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rentals and age:** long-term rental-restriction bylaws are void since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44) — a directory or bylaw line saying "rentals not allowed" on a court-sold condo does not bind the buyer. The only valid age bylaw is 55+ (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2, Regulation 7.01, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and, from May 1, 2023, younger spouses/partners and defined children) — and unlike the rental line, a registered 55+ bylaw can still bind a court-sale buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **"As-is" conditions and subjects:** the live page states buyers buy as-is with no warranties and no repair negotiations, and that there are no subjects once an offer goes to court — recorded as the page's own editorial position. No official source read for this file states a standard conditions form or its terms as a general rule: UNCONFIRMED as a stated process rule. Expect the conditions in the form for the specific file, confirmed with a lawyer, not a standard residential contract. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md) and https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html

### Taxes on a court-ordered purchase

- **PTT and GST treatment of court-ordered sales specifically: UNCONFIRMED.** No official source read for this program's foreclosure files states a special Property Transfer Tax or GST rule for a purchase through a court-ordered sale, and this file does not invent one. The general rules a buyer's lawyer and notary will apply are: BC PTT at 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million, with the First-Time Home Buyers' Program (full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial $835,000–$860,000) and the Newly Built Home Exemption (full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000); GST at 5% on new housing and not on resales; the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4; 100% of GST to $1 million, maximum $50,000, phasing out to $1.5 million; agreements March 20, 2025–2031; Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 — the March 12 secondary date is superseded); the GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out); Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.60%–4.50%. Whether a court vesting order changes any of that for a given purchase is a question for the buyer's lawyer on the specific file — UNCONFIRMED here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Presales (REDMA) do not govern a court sale:** REDMA's 7-day rescission period (s. 21, deposits in trust under s. 18) is the frame for developer-contract presales, not for a purchase confirmed by court order — do not assume a rescission right exists in a court-ordered purchase. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent (Rule 21-7(2)), and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition (Rule 21-7(4.1)). (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md) and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Is a court-ordered sale just a normal purchase with a discount?** No. The contract is conditional on court approval where the court has fixed sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" (Rule 13-5(4)(b)), the seller is the person with conduct of sale selling under court-settled conditions (Rule 13-5(3)–(4)), and there is no published statistic in any official source for a "typical discount" — any discount claim is UNCONFIRMED. The live page's own caution stands as editorial: "foreclosures aren't always cheap, and they're rarely as dramatic as people expect." https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 and (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md)
- **Q: Can my accepted offer still lose?** Yes, two ways the rules themselves create: the person with conduct may apply to confirm a sale even before the redemption period expires (Rule 21-7(9)), but until the confirming order is made the sale is not final; and the court's directions may define rights to bid, make offers or meet bids (Rule 13-5(4)(d)), so a competing bid at the approval stage is a real possibility in this process. Get the specific file's bid instructions from the seller's counsel, confirmed by your own lawyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can the owner still save the property after I offer?** The redemption right runs until the process ends it: the court fixes a redemption period in its order (Rule 21-7(5)(b)), interest runs to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act, s. 19), and only in default of payment within that period may a final order of foreclosure be granted (Rule 21-7(6)). Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** Whatever the specific file's order says. The Rules state no set length — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b) — so anyone quoting a standard period for a specific file without reading its order is guessing. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md) and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Will I get the full strata documents — Form B, minutes, depreciation report?** UNCONFIRMED as a general promise. In an ordinary resale the strata must provide a Form B within 7 days of a proper request (Strata Property Act s. 59, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page, depreciation report attached); no official source read for this program's foreclosure files guarantees a court sale will produce a full package, and the conditions of sale are court-settled particulars that may differ from a standard contract. Price the information risk, and read the conditions of sale line by line with a lawyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Q: How do I check the court file myself?** Court Services Online eSearch is open to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry location, party names and the list of filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." The petition, the amount certified due and the orders made are the facts of the deal. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do
- **Q: Is the title clean if I win?** That is what the confirming order does: on the Rule 21-7(9) application the court may order the sale confirmed, direct the disposition of the proceeds and vest title in the purchaser; the result of the sale is certified in Form 60 (Rule 13-5(6)). The vesting order, not the listing, is the title-cleaning step. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Do I pay PTT or GST differently because it's a court sale?** UNCONFIRMED — no official source read for this program states a court-sale-specific PTT or GST rule. Budget on the general rules in Section 3 and have a BC real-estate lawyer confirm the treatment on the specific file before the approval hearing, not after. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Is the unit vacant, and can I inspect inside?** Occupancy is a matter of the individual court file, not a published statistic — UNCONFIRMED as any general statement. Inspection and access terms live in the court-settled conditions of sale for that file; if the conditions do not offer an inspection, do not assume one can be added. Do the diligence the conditions allow before the final offer, as the live page itself advises — financing approval, inspection and title review up front. ## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **There is no official Fraser Valley foreclosure list to shortcut the work.** Court Services Online shows individual files (class "H - Foreclosure") but publishes no area statistics, and SnapStats has no foreclosure category — the file-by-file eSearch is the only official window, which is why the first buying step on any court-ordered listing is to read the court file before offering. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do and (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md)
- **The practice direction changed:** the Supreme Court's civil practice direction PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings," governs practice in these proceedings and replaced the earlier sealed-bid practice direction PD-62, per the Court of Appeal — advice written around the old sealed-bid process is out of date. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm (Langley foreclosures file: ~/workspace/local-notes/condos/langley-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales-condos-2026-11-01.md)
- **"Bank foreclosure" language misleads BC buyers twice over:** the sale is not run by a bank, and it is not final when a contract is signed — it is a Supreme Court proceeding started by petition (Rule 21-7(1)) and, where the court so directs, a sale by contract conditional on the court's approval (Rule 13-5(4)(b)). US-style foreclosure content describes a different legal system. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md)
- **The first order can already be final.** The court's powers under Rule 21-7(5) include making a final order of foreclosure at the first stage (Rule 21-7(5)(a)) — do not assume every file a listing points to is at an early, reversible stage; a sale application can also be made at any time (Rule 21-7(7)), and a sale can be confirmed even before the redemption period expires (Rule 21-7(9)). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **A later-registered interest does not slow the proceeding.** Once a certificate of pending litigation is registered, a person who subsequently registers an interest need not be served, is bound by orders made, and may file a response to petition (Rule 21-7(4)) — a title search mid-file can change without anyone owing the buyer notice. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
