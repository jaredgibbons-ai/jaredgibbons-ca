- **CASL's three requirements (CRTC).** Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) and its regulations target spam, computer programs installed without consent, and unsolicited redirections; the CRTC is the regulator that publishes the compliance framework. The CRTC states the core rule for a message like this newsletter: when sending a commercial electronic message (e.g. email or text), you must (1) obtain prior consent from the recipient (either express or implied); (2) provide identification and contact information; and (3) include a working unsubscribe mechanism. - **Why a real estate newsletter is a commercial electronic message.** The CRTC's FAQ defines the trigger: CASL applies when (1) a commercial electronic message (CEM) is (2) sent to an electronic address — and a CEM includes offers to purchase, sell, barter or lease land or an interest in land, and messages promoting a person as someone who does those things. A REALTOR®'s newsletter carrying latest listings and market updates, sent to subscribers' email addresses, sits squarely in that definition on the CRTC's own examples. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Express consent through this page.** The CRTC FAQ states there are two types of consent under CASL — express and implied; express consent does not expire, however the recipient has the right to withdraw their consent at any time; express consent can be obtained in writing or orally, and in either case the onus is on the person sending the message to prove they obtained consent. A voluntary signup through this page is the classic express-consent route — and the sender, not the subscriber, carries the burden of proving it. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

### Strata Property Act — resale and ownership

- **Form B — Information Certificate (s.59).** On a resale, the strata corporation provides a Form B stating the unit's monthly strata fee, any amount owed, any special levy already approved, the CRF balance, parking and storage entitlements, any judgments/proceedings, and — since April 1, 2023 — a summary of the strata's insurance coverage. A newsletter "latest listings" item is a pointer; the Form B is the disclosure behind any condo it features. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Depreciation reports.** Effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Strata fees and the CRF.** Since November 1, 2023, strata corporations must contribute a minimum of 10% of the annual operating fund to the contingency reserve fund when approving budgets. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void.** Since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata corporation in BC may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit short-term rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws — 55+ only (ss.123.1 / 123.2).** The only permitted age-restriction bylaw is 55+, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner and defined children. The bylaw governs who may reside, not who may own. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

### Taxes and rebates — purchase

- **GST — resale vs new.** Resale condos are generally exempt from GST; new construction carries 5% GST in BC.
- **FTHB GST Rebate (Bill C-4).** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122) rebates 100% of the GST on a new home valued up to $1M (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5M, for agreements entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST New Housing Rebate (the older rebate, still in place).** 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, phasing out for homes between $350,000 and $450,000.
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — general rates.** 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion to $2M, 3% above $2M, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3M. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **PTT — first-time home buyers' program.** Exempting PTT on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), partial between $835,000 and $860,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **PTT — newly built home exemption.** Full exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), eliminated at $1,150,000. The two BC exemptions are mutually exclusive — elect the larger. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption

### Presales and assignments — REDMA

- **Disclosure and 7-day rescission.** A developer must give the buyer a disclosure statement filed with BCFSA before the contract binds; the buyer may cancel within 7 days after the later of signing the purchase agreement and receiving/acknowledging the disclosure statement, with the deposit refunded from trust. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers

### Financing supports

- **FHSA.** $8,000 of participation room in the first year the account is opened, lifetime $40,000. **HBP.** Up to $60,000 withdrawn from an RRSP for a first home; CRA confirms FHSA and HBP can be combined for the same home. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **CMHC mortgage insurance.** Required when the down payment is under 20% on a home priced $1.5M or less; published premiums run 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained

### Topic-specific: CASL and privacy — the law that governs this page

- **CASL's three requirements, restated for the sender:** obtain prior consent (express or implied), provide identification and contact information, and include a working unsubscribe mechanism — with monetary penalties for violations, per the CRTC. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti/reg.htm
- **Onus of proof sits with the sender:** express consent can be obtained in writing or orally, and in either case the onus is on the person sending the message to prove they obtained consent — the signup flow behind this page needs to create a durable consent record, because the promise on the page is only half the obligation. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Unsubscribe timelines are the yardstick:** the link must remain valid for at least 60 days after the CEM is sent, and a request must be processed without delay, and no later than 10 business days after receiving it. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **The BC privacy wrinkle, from the OPC itself:** British Columbia (with Alberta and Quebec) has its own private-sector privacy law that has been deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA, and organizations subject to a substantially similar provincial law are generally exempt from PIPEDA with respect to collection, use or disclosure that occurs within that province — while businesses handling personal information that crosses provincial or national borders remain subject to PIPEDA regardless. A BC REALTOR®'s subscriber list therefore sits primarily under BC's provincial regime for in-province handling, with PIPEDA behind it for cross-border handling; this file states the framework only and does not give legal advice on which statute governs a specific list. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/?wbdisable=false
