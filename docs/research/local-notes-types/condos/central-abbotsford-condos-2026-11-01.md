# Central Abbotsford — Condos: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named buildings themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- "Central Abbotsford" is the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board sub-area name used in the SnapStats FVR September 2026 report; it is not confirmed as an official City neighbourhood name — the City plans the core through the City Centre Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2913-2019) and the Historic Downtown Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2920-2019). https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/community-planning/neighbourhood-plans
- The City's own commercial study describes the City Centre as roughly bordered by Peardonville Road and Mill Lake to the south, Ware Street, Mill Lake Road and Horn Street to the east, Parkview Street and Tims Street to the west, and a line roughly parallel to George Ferguson Way and Maclure Road to the north, centred on South Fraser Way. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/docs/business-development/HDNP%2BCommercial%2BMarket%2BStudy.pdf
- South Fraser Way is the commercial backbone; other main streets in plan material are George Ferguson Way, McCallum Road, Clearbrook Road, Ware Street and Old Yale Road, with Highway 1 interchanges at McCallum and Clearbrook. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/docs/business-development/HDNP%2BCommercial%2BMarket%2BStudy.pdf
- Where the condo stock concentrates: (1) the Gladwin Road / Mill Lake corridor south of South Fraser Way — the area's towers and low-rises (Mahogany, Regency Park, Natura) all sit on or just off Gladwin; (2) Historic Downtown around Montrose Avenue, South Fraser Way and the Rail District lands between Pine Street and Morey Avenue (Montvue, The Mason, Upper Montrose); (3) the McCallum Road / King Road edge by the University of the Fraser Valley (College Park). Sources: the building entries in Section 2 below.
- This is Abbotsford's attached-housing centre of gravity: 256 attached listings and 35 attached sales in September 2026, the most of any Abbotsford sub-area — as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report (file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-1/central-abbotsford-guide.md).
- Bourquin Exchange, the central transit exchange, is in the area, and BC Transit routes serving Abbotsford include 1 (Highstreet/UFV), 2 (Highstreet/McMillan), 4 (Saddle/Montrose), 5 (Clearbrook/Bourquin via Hospital), 12 (UFV/Bourquin), 17 (Industrial/Bourquin), 21 (Aldergrove/Abbotsford) and 31 (Abbotsford/Mission). https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/cfv_map_21.pdf
- There is no SkyTrain or West Coast Express station in Abbotsford. (Abbotsford East research file, read 2026-11-01: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-1/abbotsford-east-guide.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Form B and strata records:** on a resale condo purchase the seller's strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate; the BC Real Estate Association advises reviewing the Form B for the most recent depreciation report, together with the strata's financial statements, the contingency reserve fund (CRF) balance and planned work, to spot future special levies. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports are now mandatory on a cycle:** effective July 1, 2024, all BC strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years and can no longer defer by a 3/4 vote; the report projects 30 years of repair/replacement costs and must include at least three CRF cash-flow funding models. Stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Minimum CRF contribution:** effective November 1, 2023, strata corporations must contribute a minimum of 10% of the operating fund to the CRF when approving the annual budget. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/more-help-and-support/subscribing-for-updates
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void:** as of November 24, 2022 (Bill 44, Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022), no strata may enforce a bylaw restricting long-term rentals; stratas may still restrict short-term rentals. This is why older Abbotsford buildings whose bylaws or directory listings still say "rentals not allowed" (e.g. Regency Park, Section 2) are not actually rental-restricted for long-term tenancies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ is the only legal age bylaw:** under Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 a strata may only restrict residents to age 55 or older; any lower age bylaw (19+, 40+, 45+) is invalid, and exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children of a qualifying resident. The bylaw governs who may live in the unit, not who may own it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City zoning and plans:** Abbotsford Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014 governs; the central area is planned under the City Centre Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2913-2019) and Historic Downtown Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2920-2019) within the Abbotsforward 2050 Official Community Plan (Bylaw 2600-2016, as updated — see Section 5 for the December 2025 replacement). Historic Downtown designations include Urban 1 – Midrise (up to 6 storeys) and Urban 2 – Ground Oriented (rowhouses, townhouses, multiplexes, duplexes). https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/Historic%20Downtown%20Neighbourhood%20Plan%20Final%20Event%20Boards.pdf
- **SSMUH:** in June 2024 Council adopted Zoning Bylaw amendments to implement provincial small-scale multi-unit housing and Transit-Oriented Areas legislation (the task brief names this as Bylaw 3560-2024, adopted June 25, 2024 — the bylaw number/date are UNCONFIRMED on the City pages opened for this file; the June 2024 adoption itself is City-confirmed). SSMUH product in Abbotsford cannot be stratified as individual strata lots in the form the City first described, so SSMUH multiplexes are generally not condos. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/making-progress-housing-abbotsford
- **GST on new condos:** GST (5%) applies to new construction, not resales. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Making Life More Affordable for Canadians Act) rebates 100% of the GST on a new home up to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out in a straight line to $1.5 million, for purchase agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. Note: sources checked give Royal Assent as March 12, 2026 (the commissioning brief said March 13 — the March 12 date is what CRA-facing sources publish). https://rncanada.ca/blog/first-time-home-buyer-gst-rebate
- **The older GST New Housing Rebate still exists underneath:** 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, phasing out between $350,000 and $450,000 of value — at Abbotsford condo prices it rarely pays anything, and the FTHB rebate is the operative program for eligible first-time buyers. https://raincityproperties.com/journal/gst-new-home-rebate-bc-first-time-buyer-2026
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** standard rates are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% from $2 million to $3 million and 5% above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: no PTT on the first $500,000 if fair market value is $835,000 or less (maximum saving $8,000), partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000, none at $860,000 or more. Newly Built Home Exemption: full exemption up to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/bc-property-transfer-tax-exemptions-guide/ and https://www.kraftmortgages.ca/blog/first-time-home-buyer-guide-surrey-bc-2026
- **Presales (REDMA):** under the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21, a presale buyer gets a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing the purchase agreement and acknowledging receipt of the disclosure statement, with the full deposit returned on a valid rescission; deposits must be held in trust (REDMA s. 18). Assignments are governed by the purchase contract itself — developer consent and fees are contract terms, not a statutory right, and UNCONFIRMED for any specific Abbotsford project until its disclosure statement is read. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan allows a $60,000 RRSP withdrawal per buyer; the First Home Savings Account allows $40,000 lifetime contributions at $8,000 per year. https://homecalc.ca/first-time-home-buyer-programs-canada
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** premiums on the published CMHC schedule run from 0.60% of the loan (loan-to-value up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), and 4.50% at 90.01%–95% LTV with a non-traditional down payment; add 0.20% for an amortization over 25 years where that surcharge applies. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: The building's bylaws say "no rentals." Can I still rent out my condo?** For a long-term tenancy, yes — every residential rental-restriction bylaw in BC became unenforceable on November 24, 2022, even if the strata has never amended the bylaw text. Short-term rentals can still be banned by bylaw, and a 55+ age bylaw (like Regency Park's) still governs who may live in the unit, tenant or owner. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/prohibition-on-rental-and-age-restrictions-in-strata-buildings-556/
- **Q: What does the strata fee actually cover, and how do I check it?** The fee split between the operating fund and the CRF is on the Form B, which also states the current CRF balance; since November 1, 2023 the CRF contribution must be at least 10% of the operating fund. Coverage items (water, gas, caretaker, amenities) vary by building and are UNCONFIRMED for any Central Abbotsford building until its Form B is read. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/more-help-and-support/subscribing-for-updates
- **Q: How do I know if a big special levy is coming?** Read the depreciation report through the Form B: since July 1, 2024 every strata with 5+ lots must have one on a five-year cycle, with a 30-year cost projection and three CRF funding models that show whether the plan is "pay as you go," increased fees, or special levies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Q: Are pets allowed?** Building by building. The directory records Regency Park as pets not allowed; for Mahogany, Montvue, The Mason, Natura and College Park, pet rules are UNCONFIRMED from public sources — the registered bylaws attached to the strata documents govern. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/index.php/building/regency-park-3170-gladwin-road
- **Q: Is parking underground or surface, and is storage included?** It tracks building form: The Mason has two levels of underground parking; Mahogany's parking arrangement is UNCONFIRMED from the sources checked; College Park lists storage among its amenities. The specific stall and locker assigned to a unit are stated on the Form B — never assume from the listing. https://www.mikestewart.ca/presale/the-flats-at-the-rail-district/ and https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/index.php/building/college-park-33728-king-road?prompt=login
- **Q: What insurance risk do condo buyers miss?** The strata's master policy deductible — especially the water-damage deductible — can be charged back to an owner whose unit causes a loss, so buyers line up their own unit-owner policy against the deductible amount shown in the strata documents. The deductible figure for any specific Central Abbotsford building is UNCONFIRMED until its documents are read; the Form B / strata insurance summary in the document package is where it appears. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Q: I'm buying a presale at The Mason or Montvue. What protects me?** REDMA: a filed disclosure statement before you are bound, deposits held in trust, and a 7-day rescission right from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement. Completion dates, assignment consent and assignment fees are contract/disclosure terms — read them in the disclosure statement, not the brochure. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Q: Can I use the new GST rebate on a new Central Abbotsford condo?** If you are an eligible first-time buyer and the agreement was signed on or after March 20, 2025, yes — 100% of the 5% GST back up to a $1 million price (max $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million; the BC Newly Built Home PTT exemption can stack on top (full to $1,100,000). Resale condos charge no GST at all. https://rncanada.ca/blog/first-time-home-buyer-gst-rebate and https://www.kraftmortgages.ca/blog/first-time-home-buyer-guide-surrey-bc-2026
- **Q: Which schools does a Central Abbotsford condo feed into?** Catchments are address-based in SD34. Locator runs in the area research file: 33130 Bevan Ave returns Godson Elementary → Abbotsford Middle → Abbotsford Senior Secondary, and 32161 Dormick Avenue returns Dormick Park Elementary → Abbotsford Middle → Abbotsford Senior Secondary. Run the condo's own address before relying on a school. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-1/central-abbotsford-guide.md)
- **Q: Will downtown noise and new construction affect resale?** The Historic Downtown plan deliberately adds mid-rise housing (Urban 1 – Midrise up to 6 storeys) around the core, and the City is realigning Montvue Avenue and building West Railway Plaza at Montvue and Essendene — more neighbours and more construction are the plan, not an accident. Check the plan designation for the blocks around any building you are buying into. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/projects-go/montvue-avenue-and-west-railway-plaza

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Abbotsford replaced its Official Community Plan in December 2025.** After more than two years of work, Council adopted the new Abbotsforward 2050 OCP in December 2025 (public hearing November 25, 2025); it emphasizes more apartments, townhouses and SSMUH in priority areas and enables the 37,185 units the City's 2024 Interim Housing Needs Report targets over 20 years. Condo buyers should read designations under the new OCP, not the 2016 plan most older pages still describe. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/making-progress-housing-abbotsford and https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/city-begins-implementing-updated-official-community-plan
- **The Rail District is a 10-to-12-year build-out, not a finished neighbourhood.** The 19-acre former Clayburn Brick Plant site between Pine Street and Morey Avenue is planned as five neighbourhoods in nine phases over 10 to 12 years; The Mason (completed spring 2026) is only the first condo building, and the community amenity building, The Station, serves all five planned neighbourhoods. Early buyers are buying into a construction zone with an amenity payoff at the end. https://www.vancouvernewcondos.com/properties/rail-district-abbotsford/ and https://storeys.com/rail-district-the-mason-abbotsford/
- **Downtown's main intersection is being rebuilt.** The City is realigning Montvue Avenue — removing the single-lane connection to Essendene Avenue, connecting Montvue directly to West Railway Street, and turning the closed street section into West Railway Plaza, a public gathering/market space — as part of the Historic Downtown Neighbourhood Plan. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/projects-go/montvue-avenue-and-west-railway-plaza
- **The OCP growth direction adds a new centre east of downtown:** Abbotsforward 2050 states a new mixed-use centre between the hospital and Mill Lake Park and a Primary Transit Corridor along South Fraser Way between Clearbrook and McCallum Road — the corridor the Gladwin Road condo cluster already sits beside. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/city-begins-implementing-updated-official-community-plan
- **Highway 1 work touches the McCallum edge:** the BC Government's Highway 1 Fraser Valley program includes a new interchange at Highway 11, which affects commute patterns for the College Park / UFV-side condos. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/transportation-infrastructure-projects/highway-1-fraser-valley/documents/2026-09-22-september-fraser-valley-hwy1-fact-sheet.pdf
- **Federal Housing Accelerator money is spent, not pending:** the City was approved for up to $25.6 million in federal Housing Accelerator Fund money and reported in October 2026 that all HAF initiatives are complete, with affordable and multi-family units permitted above the program's expectations — part of why so much new mid-rise product (Montvue, The Mason, Aria rentals) landed in the core at once. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/making-progress-housing-abbotsford
