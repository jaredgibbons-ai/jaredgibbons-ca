## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3):** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, states that the disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Representation at an open house:** a licensed real estate professional in BC must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party to the transaction. The regulator is the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and the Province's Buying and selling a home page directs prospective purchasers to BCFSA for information on purchasing property in BC. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home
- **Identification or sign-in for open-house visitors:** NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED as a rule; a host may still ask, as a matter of the seller's instructions, but that practice is not in an official source. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/maple-ridge-open-houses.md)
- **Form B and strata records:** on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate; the BC Real Estate Association advises reviewing the Form B for the most recent depreciation report, the strata's financial statements, the CRF balance and planned work to identify future special levies. This is the document step after the open house — the open house shows the unit, the Form B shows the building. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports:** effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. In Maple Ridge's verified roster this lands hardest on the oldest buildings — The Willows (1987), Riverside (1988), Rosewood Court (1991) and Emerald Manor (1997). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Minimum CRF contribution:** effective November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund in the annual budget. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void:** since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw; short-term rentals can still be restricted. Building directories still show "rentals not allowed" for Rosewood Court and Fraser Glen — that reflects old bylaw text, not current law, and an open-house host repeating it is repeating an unenforceable line. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ is the only legal age bylaw:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. Five of this file's ten verified apartment entries are presented as 55+ communities (the Fraserview Village cluster) — confirm on each Form B whether the registered bylaw is a 55+ bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City zoning and plans:** Maple Ridge Zoning Bylaw No. 7600-2019 governs. The City's Official Community Plan citation is CONTESTED in the research (Bylaw 7060-2014 vs Bylaw 7998-2024) — flagged as contested, never asserted as one number. https://mapleridge.ca/your-government/plans-strategies/official-community-plan-area-plans/area-neighbourhood-plans/town
- **Maple Ridge property taxes:** due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty applied July 3, 2026 and an additional 5% penalty September 2, 2026 on unpaid current taxes. https://mapleridge.ca/2329/Due-Dates-and-Penalties-Late-Payments
- **GST on new condos:** 5% GST applies to new construction (developer sales at the newest downtown buildings), not resales. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 (some secondary sources say March 12 — CRA governs). The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less (maximum saving $8,000), partial $835,000–$860,000, none at $860,000+. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60% (LTV up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), 4.50% at the top band with a non-traditional down payment, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Do I need my own agent to attend a Maple Ridge open house?** No official source requires it. BCFSA's Real Estate Rules excuse disclosure of representation where a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or answering general factual questions — but the moment you share your motivation, financial qualifications or needs, that changes, and BCFSA requires unrepresented consumers to be told the risks of dealing with a licensee who represents the other party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Q: Is the host at an open house the seller's agent?** The host is a licensee connected to the listing side of the transaction; BCFSA requires a licensee to work for either the buyer or the seller and to inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients — assume anything non-factual you tell the host can reach the seller. Use the open house for facts; save your story for your own agent. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: How do I know the open house time is current?** Each listing on the maple-ridge-open-houses page carries an OPEN HOUSE label with a date and start and end times, and the page states the feed updates every 15 minutes — check the page the day you plan to go. https://jaredgibbons.ca/maple-ridge-open-houses.html
- **Q: Do I have to sign in or show ID at an open house?** No BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source states an identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors — UNVERIFIED as a rule; an individual host may still ask, on the seller's instructions. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/maple-ridge-open-houses.md)
- **Q: Which Maple Ridge areas can I cover in one trip?** Group by the listing pools: East Central and West Central held the deepest inventory of both property types in September 2026 (82 detached / 120 attached and 66 detached / 78 attached respectively), with Albion, Cottonwood and Silver Valley as the eastern detached group — inventory counts only, and for condos specifically the verified buildings all sit in East Central, so a condo run is a downtown run. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/maple-ridge-open-houses.md)
- **Q: So many Maple Ridge condo open houses are in 55+ buildings — can I buy if I am under 55?** Five of the ten verified apartment entries here are presented as 55+ (the Fraserview Village cluster: Rosewood Court, Emerald Manor, The Willows, Castlemount, Riverside). A 55+ bylaw is the one age bylaw BC law still allows (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2), with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (since May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children — whether you can occupy, or only own and rent, depends on the registered bylaw on the Form B. Ask before the open house, not after. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Are pets allowed in these buildings?** It varies and pet bylaws remain enforceable in BC (unlike rental bylaws). Published examples: Rosewood Court — directory records pets allowed with restrictions; The Willows — a listing describes two cats or two dogs (or one of each); one listing aggregator records cats OK at 11893 227 Street (Brickwater). For the other buildings in Section 2, pet rules are UNCONFIRMED from public sources — read the registered bylaws in the strata package. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/rosewood-court-22611-116-ave
- **Q: The directory says a building bans rentals. If I buy as an investor, can I rent it out?** Yes for a long-term tenancy — rental-restriction bylaws have been unenforceable since November 24, 2022 anywhere in BC. What can still bind you: a short-term rental bylaw, and a 55+ age bylaw limiting who may live in the unit (which decides your tenant pool in the Fraserview Village buildings). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: What should I ask for after I like a unit at an open house?** The strata package behind the Form B: the depreciation report (mandatory every five years for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024), financial statements, CRF balance and planned work — the items BCREA advises reviewing to identify future special levies. In the 1987–1997 buildings (The Willows, Riverside, Rosewood Court, Emerald Manor) that package matters more than the staging. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Q: I saw a rural Maple Ridge open house — anything extra to check?** Yes: properties along the Fraser River frontage sit in a floodplain where the flood construction level is 6.7 m geodetic (including freeboard), a covenant on title identifies construction in the floodplain, and no habitable floor space may be located below that level on affected lots — ask for the title and flood status on any river-edge open house, not after subjects. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/floodplain-construction-0

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The disclosure rule has an open-house carve-out with a tripwire.** Rule 5-10(3) excuses disclosure of representation only while the host is hosting an advertised open house or giving factual answers to general questions — the moment the host solicits or receives your motivation, financial qualifications or needs, the exception ends. That is the legal line between small talk and sharing with the seller's side. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Half the verified Maple Ridge condo roster is one 55+ village.** Rosewood Court, Emerald Manor, The Willows, Castlemount and Riverside are all presented as 55+ communities sharing one amenity building — Maple Ridge's established condo stock skews seniors-oriented, which shapes both the open-house traffic and the resale buyer pool for those buildings. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Four separate strata corporations share that amenity building.** The Fraserview Village buildings are distinct stratas marketed together, and the amenity building (indoor pool, hot tub, clubhouse, guest suite) is a village facility — a unit's fee and voting rights sit with its own strata, not the village. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **The feed is the schedule.** There is no separate official open-house calendar: the page's listings each carry the OPEN HOUSE label with the date and start/end times, and the page states the feed updates every 15 minutes — how those dates and times are entered on the brokerage side (board systems, ShowingTime, TouchBase) is not documented on an official public source and is UNVERIFIED. https://jaredgibbons.ca/maple-ridge-open-houses.html and (research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/maple-ridge-open-houses.md)
- **The rural open house is the rare event.** The rural sub-areas held thin pools in September 2026 (Websters Corners 8 detached listings, Thornhill 13, Whonnock 2, Northeast 2) against East Central's 82 detached and 120 attached — an open house east of Albion is a scarcer listing event than a central one, on inventory evidence only.
