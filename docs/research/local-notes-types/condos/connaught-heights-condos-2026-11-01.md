## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Connaught Heights is the City of New Westminster's west-end mainland neighbourhood, around 22nd Street Station, London Street and Marine Way; the City lists Connaught Heights among its Historic Neighbourhood Context Statements, so the City name and the board (GVR) name match. Precise OCP boundary roads: UNVERIFIED. Nearby areas: the West End and Moody Park to the east, Burnaby across the city's west boundary, and Queensborough across the Queensborough Bridge to the south-east. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- Where the condo stock concentrates: nowhere in Connaught Heights — the sub-area's entire listed stock in September 2026 was detached (see Section 2). New Westminster's condo stock concentrates in Downtown, Uptown and the Quay, east of this neighbourhood. The growth pressure here is prospective: the 22nd Street Station area is identified in the OCP as a Frequent Transit Development Area. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- Sub-area numbers, September 2026 (SnapStats GVR): detached inventory 15, solds 0; condos & townhomes inventory 0, solds 0. SnapStats publishes medians at community level only: New Westminster condos & townhomes median was $560,000 in September 2026 ($587/sqft, 95% sale-to-list, 27 days on market) and the detached median was $1,365,000. There is no Connaught Heights condo median — the segment did not exist locally that month. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- Transit anchors: 22nd Street Station (Expo Line) is at the area; TransLink routes from the station include the 100 (Marpole), 101 (Lougheed Station), 104 (Annacis Island) and 128 (Braid Station). Main roads: Marine Way / Stewardson Way and 20th Street / 22nd Street, linking west to Burnaby and south across the Queensborough Bridge via Highway 91A. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- Neighbourhood anchors: Connaught Heights Elementary at 2201 London Street, Connaught Heights Park (shared with the school), and Grimston Park, the community park at the west end of the city. The area has minimal retail services per the City's small-sites consultation record. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Form B and strata records:** on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate; the BC Real Estate Association advises reviewing the Form B for the most recent depreciation report, the strata's financial statements, the CRF balance and planned work to identify future special levies. Stated for completeness — there is no strata stock in Connaught Heights itself, so these rules bite on the Downtown/Uptown/Quay buildings a Connaught Heights condo searcher ends up considering instead, and on any future station-area strata. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports:** effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Minimum CRF contribution:** effective November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund in the annual budget. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/more-help-and-support/subscribing-for-updates
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void:** since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw; short-term rentals can still be restricted. Any future Connaught Heights strata will be born under this rule — a "no rentals" pitch on a future presale here would be old law. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ is the only legal age bylaw:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City zoning and plans:** houses sit in the Single Detached Residential Districts (RS series, s.310) of Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001 — RS-1 sets a minimum site area of 557.40 m² (6,000 sq. ft.) and a floor space ratio of 0.5; the RS districts allow secondary suites, and laneway/carriage houses are regulated under the same bylaw; townhouse districts RT-A and RT-B, where designated, require a minimum site area of 836.1 m² (9,000 sq. ft.) and minimum frontage of 20 m. The governing plan is Our City 2041, the City's Official Community Plan (Bylaw No. 7925, 2017), and the 22nd Street Station area is identified in it as a Frequent Transit Development Area — one of the areas where the City directs higher-density, transit-supportive growth. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- **Transit-Oriented Development Areas:** provincial Transit Oriented Development Areas introduced in 2024 enable density within 800 metres of the City's five SkyTrain stations, including 22nd Street, and the City is amending the OCP to implement TOD Area extensions — a February 17, 2026 City report addresses extending the "Residential – Limited Mid Rise" designation to properties on the outer edges of the provincial TOD areas. This is the mechanism that will eventually create condo stock here; today it changes land value, not the building list. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/2026_02_17_APC___Regional_Planning_and_Transit_Oriented_Development_Area_Extensions___2026_78.pdf
- **New Westminster property taxes:** 2026 taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on current taxes remaining unpaid on July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on any unpaid balance on September 3, 2026. New Westminster also operates its own electrical utility alongside City water/sewer services — a utility structure most Metro Vancouver buyers do not expect. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- **Floodplain:** the City's floodplain study zones only the city's low-lying edges — Zone 1 Queensborough, Zone 2 west-end Fraser River industrial land, Zone 3 the Quay waterfront and Zone 4 the Brunette River industrial area. No floodplain zone for Connaught Heights' residential streets was confirmed on a City source; lot-level status is UNVERIFIED and should be checked with the City. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- **GST on new condos:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — when station-area product is eventually built and sold by a developer, GST will apply to those first sales. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 (some secondary sources say March 12 — CRA governs). The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) is described in the same CRA publication. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less (maximum saving $8,000), partial $835,000–$860,000, none at $860,000+. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www.equitylawgroup.ca/news/closing-costs-bc-home-purchase and https://danmarusin.com/blog/bc-property-transfer-tax-2026/
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. No presale in the sub-area is verified today; the rule is the one to apply when the first station-area project markets. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60% (LTV up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), 4.50% at the top band with a non-traditional down payment, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

- **Q: Are there really no condos in Connaught Heights?** Correct, as of this research. SnapStats recorded zero condos & townhomes listings and zero solds in the sub-area in September 2026, the research file confirms no named condo or townhome complex on a developer or strata source, and the zoning base is RS single-detached districts. The neighbourhood's attached living is secondary suites and laneway/carriage houses inside houses — not strata product. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- **Q: Where do Connaught Heights downsizers buy condos instead?** The city's condo stock is in Downtown, Uptown and the Quay, east of the neighbourhood — the New Westminster community condos & townhomes median was $560,000 in September 2026 (SnapStats, community level). No source checked tracks where Connaught Heights sellers specifically land — that pattern is UNVERIFIED. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- **Q: What is the reGENERATE / Bold Vision plan for 22nd Street Station?** The City's 22nd Street Station Area Bold Vision (the reGENERATE Vision) was endorsed by Council in December 2024 after a vision process launched in spring 2023; the City describes the station area as a key growth area in the OCP, planned to become a high-density mixed-use core centred on the transit hub. It is a vision and planning framework — it has not yet produced a condo building in the sub-area. https://www.newwestcity.ca/planning-building-and-development/new-westminsters-official-community-plan/ocp-implementation-projects/22nd-street-station-area-bold-vision/sb_expander_articles/7273.php
- **Q: What does Bill 47 / the TOD Area mean for a house near the station?** Provincial TOD Areas enable density within 800 metres of SkyTrain stations, 22nd Street included, and the City is amending the OCP to implement TOD extensions (February 17, 2026 report). Marine Way listings already market assembled lots on that basis (one cites a minimum 20-storey tower at 5.0 FSR). What a specific lot may build is a City confirmation, not a listing claim — verify the designation and zoning before pricing in redevelopment value. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/2026_02_17_APC___Regional_Planning_and_Transit_Oriented_Development_Area_Extensions___2026_78.pdf and https://vancouvernashdom.com/new-westminster-connaught-heights-houses/SearchResults.form/?_pg=2

Those units are rental/family space inside a house purchase, not strata condos, and they do not come with a Form B or a strata. ## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The station got new elevators before the neighbourhood got a vision.** The City's Bold Vision page records that 22nd Street Station was included in TransLink's Expo Line Elevator Replacement Program and that, as of December 2025, two elevators had been upgraded, replacing 40-year-old infrastructure — the station's accessibility fix landed while the surrounding blocks are still detached houses. https://www.newwestcity.ca/planning-building-and-development/new-westminsters-official-community-plan/ocp-implementation-projects/22nd-street-station-area-bold-vision
- **Council endorsed a new identity for the station area in December 2024.** The reGENERATE Vision process ran 2023–2024 and the final Vision was endorsed by Council in December 2024, built on eight "Big Moves" — any Connaught Heights purchase near the station is a purchase inside an endorsed redesign area, even though no condo exists there yet. https://www.newwestcity.ca/planning-building-and-development/new-westminsters-official-community-plan/ocp-implementation-projects/22nd-street-station-area-bold-vision/sb_expander_articles/7273.php
- **One of the City's two small-sites lots is park-locked.** The consultation record for the City's affordable-housing small-sites initiative notes a Crown Land Grant on 2038 Ninth Avenue identifying the property for parks and recreation purposes — a buyer assuming both London Street / Ninth Avenue lots are future housing sites is only half right, and the City has also heard that Connaught Heights needs more parkland. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- **The elementary school park is not always public.** Connaught Heights Park is shared with Connaught Heights Elementary, and the City's small-sites consultation heard the shared park is not always accessible to the community during the school day; a recommended action in the City's Comprehensive Parks Plan is to acquire more parkland in Connaught Heights. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- **September 2026 was a zero month for the whole sub-area.** Connaught Heights listed 15 detached homes with 0 solds and had no attached stock at all — pricing evidence for any purchase here has to come from the wider New Westminster community figures and adjoining areas, not from in-area solds. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
- **The city's density is someone else's neighbourhood.** The 2021 Census counted 78,916 people in New Westminster at 5,052 people per sq. km — among the densest cities in Canada — yet this west-end pocket remains RS-zoned detached houses beside an Expo Line station, which is precisely why the province's TOD rules and the City's vision both target it. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/connaught-heights.md)
