## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. A GIS viewer supplies none of this — the Form B and title are the condo documents, the map is the parcel check behind them. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote; and since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. Neither figure appears on any GIS layer described in the sources for this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — any directory or bylaw line saying "rentals not allowed" is void for long-term rentals. Short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 allow only a 55-or-older strata residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. An age bylaw is a registered-bylaw fact, not a map layer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Zoning versus OCP — recorded as the page's own simplified explanation, not as bylaw-verified definitions:** "Zoning is the current rule — what you're allowed to build on the property today. OCP (Official Community Plan) is what the city wants the area to become over the next 10-30 years," with the page's worked example that a single-family-zoned property with a medium-density OCP designation "is a redevelopment opportunity." No municipal zoning bylaw or OCP was opened for this file to test those definitions, and they should be quoted only as the page's plain-language framing; the governing bylaw for any specific condo parcel is the municipality's own, cited in that area's file in this program. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **Flood plain checking, as the page describes the mechanics:** "Most municipal GIS maps have a flood plain layer you can toggle on," the named regional tools are Chilliwack MapOnline, Abbotsford CityMap and Delta's map, BC iMap holds provincial flood data, and a flood plain location "doesn't mean you can't buy it — but insurance, lending, and rebuilding rules change." Whether any named municipal tool in fact carries a toggleable flood layer under that name today, and the flood status of any specific property: UNVERIFIED in this file — no municipal GIS tool was opened and no property was looked up. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **Easements and rights-of-way, as the page states them:** GIS layers "often show registered easements and obvious utility right-of-ways, but not every encumbrance shows up on the map," and "for a complete picture, pull the title search — the title is the source of truth." No Land Title and Survey Authority source was opened in this pass to confirm the title-search mechanics behind that advice, so those mechanics are UNVERIFIED in this file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **Regulator context:** real estate trading services in BC are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), which states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and work for only the buyer or the seller in a transaction — the frame within which map findings become advice to a client. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **GST and rebates:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) — 100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements March 20, 2025–2031; GST New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** where a condo purchase is on a developer contract, REDMA s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **HBP / FHSA / CMHC:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What is GIS, in plain language?** As this site's own page puts it, Geographic Information Systems are "digital maps that carry data behind them" — click a property and, on the page's description, you can pull up zoning, lot dimensions, flood plain status, ALR boundaries and easements. The one tool reference this file verified is the Province's: iMapBC lets users view and analyze thousands of geographic datasets and upload, print or email their work. Which specific layers any one tool carries is UNVERIFIED in this file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/data/geographic-data-services/web-based-mapping (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **Q: Do I need GIS skills to buy a condo?** On the page's own answer for houses: no — for a standard townhome or detached home in an established neighbourhood GIS is rarely critical, and it matters most for land, acreage, property near water or in a flood zone, and anything you plan to build on, subdivide or significantly modify. For a condo, the building facts come from the Form B and title in any case; the map check is the parcel check behind the building. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **Q: What is the difference between zoning and an OCP?** In the page's plain-language framing: zoning is the current rule for what may be built on a property today, while the Official Community Plan is the municipality's long-term direction for the area over the next 10–30 years — so a property can be zoned for one use today and designated for denser use in the plan. Those are the page's simplified definitions, recorded as site copy, not as bylaw-verified definitions. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **Q: How do I find out if a property is in a flood plain?** The page's route: toggle the flood plain layer on a municipal GIS map — it names Chilliwack MapOnline, Abbotsford CityMap and Delta's map as the relevant regional tools — with BC iMap holding provincial flood data; and it warns that flood plain status affects insurance, lending and rebuilding rules. The current names, layers and mechanics of those municipal tools are UNVERIFIED in this file — none was opened in this pass. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **Q: Can a GIS map tell me about easements and rights-of-way?** Sometimes, on the page's own answer: GIS layers often show registered easements and obvious utility rights-of-way, but not every encumbrance appears on a map — the page's instruction is to use GIS as a first look and confirm with the title search, which it calls the source of truth. The title-search mechanics behind that advice are UNVERIFIED in this file; no Land Title and Survey Authority source was opened. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **Q: Can GIS tell me a condo's strata fees, levies or bylaws?** No source in this file states that any GIS tool carries strata fees, levies, bylaws or depreciation-report data — those facts are UNCONFIRMED as map data and are obtained from the Form B (Strata Property Act s. 59), which the strata must provide within 7 days for a maximum of $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Q: The page promises a walkthrough of the GIS tools — where is it?** It is not on the page. The intro promises "a quick walkthrough of the GIS tools I use most for BC properties, and how you can use them yourself," but the body that follows is a definition plus a four-question FAQ — no tool is walked through, no how-to steps for any tool appear, and no tool is linked. That gap is recorded in Section 5. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **Q: Which provincial tool was actually verified for this file?** iMapBC only: the Province's own page describes it as an online mapping tool for viewing and analyzing thousands of geographic datasets, with upload, print and email functions. The page's name for it, "BC iMap," is the site page's styling; the Province styles the product iMapBC. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/data/geographic-data-services/web-based-mapping (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
- **Q: Does a clean GIS screen mean a clean title?** No — on the page's own account, not every encumbrance shows up on a map, so an easement or right-of-way question is settled by the title search, not by the absence of a line on a map. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/geographic-information-systems-gis.md)
