## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **FTHB GST/HST Rebate — enactment (CENTRAL to this page):** amendments to Part IX of the Excise Tax Act in Bill C-4 introduced the FTHB GST/HST rebate, and Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 — secondary sources saying March 12 are superseded. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **FTHB rebate — amount, thresholds and timing:** together with the GST/HST new housing rebate, the FTHB rebate provides 100% of the GST (or the federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated single unit residential complex or residential condominium unit valued up to $1 million — a maximum of up to $50,000; between $1 million and $1.5 million the rebate is gradually reduced; and no FTHB rebate is available at $1.5 million or more. Eligibility turns on a builder agreement entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (for an owner-built home, construction beginning on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031), construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036, and the buyer being at least 18, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and a first-time buyer under the four-calendar-year look-back; claiming is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction, by builder credit at closing or directly to the CRA (Form GST190; Form GST191 for owner-built homes). Any straight-line formula for the reduction between $1 million and $1.5 million is UNVERIFIED — CRA says only "gradually reduced"; the live page's table wording ("full at $1M or less, reduced between $1M and $1.5M, none at $1.5M or more") matches the verified thresholds. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **GST New Housing Rebate (the pre-existing rebate):** generally 36% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) paid on a newly constructed or substantially renovated single unit residential complex or condominium unit, to a maximum rebate of $6,300; for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000 the rebate is gradually reduced, and no rebate is available at $450,000 or more. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC) — general rates:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million; the tax is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office. The live page's one-line "Over $3,000,000 — 5%" table entry compresses that structure and is recorded as a simplification, not adopted. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **PTT First-Time Home Buyers' Program (CENTRAL to this page):** a qualifying buyer (Canadian citizen or permanent resident; lived in BC at least a year immediately before registration or filed at least 2 income tax returns as a BC resident in the last 6 taxation years; never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world; never received the exemption or refund before) is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price where the property is used only as a principal residence, has a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), is 0.5 hectares or smaller and contains only residential improvements; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000; where only some purchasers qualify, only the qualifying purchasers' percentage of interest is eligible; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and occupy the home as a principal residence to the first anniversary of registration to keep the full exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Newly Built Home Exemption:** effective April 1, 2024, the fair market value threshold for a full exemption is $1,100,000, with a partial exemption phasing out over the $50,000 range above it and complete elimination at $1,150,000 for qualifying purchasers. Page-statement check: the page's FAQ figure of "up to $20,000" at the $1,100,000 threshold equals the general-rate PTT on $1,100,000 under the verified rates ($2,000 at 1% on the first $200,000 plus $18,000 at 2% on the remaining $900,000), so that figure is consistent with the verified frame. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **FHSA:** the CRA describes the First Home Savings Account as a registered plan letting a first-time home buyer save to buy or build a qualifying first home tax-free up to certain limits; FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000, and contributions are generally deductible. The standard program frame carries the FHSA at $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year — on this page specifically, the research file records the live page's $40,000 lifetime figure as UNVERIFIED page copy, because the lifetime limit was not stated on the CRA main page read in the sibling passes, which verifies only the $8,000 first-year room. The CRA also states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Home Buyers' Plan — figures UNVERIFIED on this page:** the standard program frame carries the HBP at $60,000 — on this page specifically, the live page's HBP figures (a $60,000 withdrawal limit, repayment starting the 2nd year after withdrawal and the 5th year for first withdrawals in 2022–2025, and a proposal to extend the 5th-year delay to withdrawals through December 31, 2028) are UNVERIFIED throughout, because the CRA's Home Buyers' Plan page returned HTTP 403 in the sibling passes and no HBP figure is adopted as verified for this page. https://jaredgibbons.ca/first-time-home-buyer-program.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Down payment and mortgage insurance (CMHC):** mortgage loan insurance is required when a down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more (where insurance is not available); CMHC's published premium range is 0.60%–4.50% of the total mortgage amount, and the premium can be paid in full or added to the mortgage. The live page's premium range (2.80%–4.00%, plus a 0.20% surcharge over 25 years) conflicts with that published range and is UNVERIFIED; the page's insured-mortgage price-cap date (under $1.5 million since December 15, 2024), its 30-year amortization availability, and its "CMHC Home Start" program name were likewise not re-opened on CMHC or Finance Canada pages: UNVERIFIED as stated. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **First-time home buyer's tax credit as the page states it:** a credit based on $10,000, worth up to $1,500, claimed on line 31270 — recorded as page copy only, because the amount was not re-read on a CRA page opened in the sibling passes: UNVERIFIED. https://jaredgibbons.ca/first-time-home-buyer-program.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Home owner grant context:** the grant reduces annual property tax on a principal residence and must be applied for each year; as published, the regular grant amount is $570 effective January 1, 2027 and the 2026 full-grant threshold is $2,075,000 assessed value — the live page's "2026 full-grant threshold: $2,075,000" and "up to $570 in the Lower Mainland" wording matches that frame. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. Resales carry no GST, so this page's rebates never apply to a Form B purchase — the Form B matters here only as the line between a resale condo (no GST, no rebate) and a new condo (GST, possibly rebated). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote; and since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — any directory or bylaw line saying "rentals not allowed" is void for long-term rentals; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 and Regulation 7.01 allow only a 55-or-older strata residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Presales (REDMA):** where a presale condo is the purchase, REDMA s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18 — a separate clock from the GST rebate's March 20, 2025 agreement-date test, and both must be checked on the same contract. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much property transfer tax does a first-time buyer in BC actually save?** A qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price where the property's fair market value is $835,000 or less, it is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the buyer's principal residence; above $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Q: How much GST can a first-time buyer get back on a new condo?** Up to $50,000: together with the new housing rebate, the FTHB GST/HST rebate returns 100% of the GST on a new or substantially renovated home (including a residential condominium unit) valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more, for builder agreements entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: What happened to the old GST new housing rebate?** It still exists on its own terms: generally 36% of the GST paid, to a maximum of $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and unavailable at $450,000 or more — the FTHB rebate is the separate top-up that takes a qualifying first-time buyer's combined recovery to 100%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?** Yes — the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal; FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000. The HBP dollar figures on the live page remain UNVERIFIED (CRA HBP page returned HTTP 403 in the sibling passes). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Q: Is a newly built home treated differently from a resale home?** Yes: the newly built home exemption has a full-exemption PTT threshold of $1,100,000 with a phase-out to $1,150,000, and a new home may also attract GST, which the federal FTHB rebate can offset by up to $50,000 for eligible buyers whose builder agreement dates from on or after March 20, 2025. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Q: What down payment do I actually need?** Under 20% down requires mortgage loan insurance: the minimum is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 up to $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more with no insurance available; CMHC's published premium range is 0.60%–4.50% of the total mortgage amount. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Q: The page's worked example shows a $26,200 premium on a $700,000 townhouse — can I rely on it?** Only as page content: the example's PTT legs ($4,000 with the exemption versus $12,000 without) follow the verified rates and exemption, but the premium leg uses the page's own 2.80%–4.00% range, which conflicts with CMHC's published 0.60%–4.50% range and is UNVERIFIED — price insurance from the CMHC range and a lender quote, not from the example. https://jaredgibbons.ca/first-time-home-buyer-program.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Q: How do I keep the PTT exemption after closing?** Move in within 92 days of registration and occupy the home as your principal residence to the first anniversary of registration, or part of the exemption is repayable. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Q: Does a resale condo qualify for any GST rebate?** No — resale homes are not subject to GST, so there is no GST to rebate; the rebates on this page are for newly built or substantially renovated homes only. https://jaredgibbons.ca/first-time-home-buyer-program.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
- **Q: Are the Home Buyers' Plan and tax-credit numbers on the page confirmed?** No — the page's HBP figures and its $10,000 / up-to-$1,500 tax-credit figures (line 31270) are UNVERIFIED site copy in the research file, because the CRA HBP page returned HTTP 403 in the sibling passes and the credit amount was not re-read on a CRA page opened in those passes. https://jaredgibbons.ca/first-time-home-buyer-program.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md)
