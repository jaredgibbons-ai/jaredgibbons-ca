# Boulevard — Condos: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named buildings themselves.

> **MUNICIPAL SPLIT — READ FIRST: "Boulevard" is the Grand Boulevard corridor, and it straddles two municipalities.** South of Highway 1 the corridor (Grand Boulevard and Grand Boulevard Park, between Keith Road and Highway 1) is in the **City** of North Vancouver; north of the highway it continues in the **District** of North Vancouver as Grand Boulevard East to Boulevard Crescent and Lynn Valley Road. The exact municipal split point on the corridor is UNVERIFIED. Every rule in Section 3 is therefore given twice — City and District — because taxes, zoning and permit areas follow the municipality, not the corridor name. ## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- The spine is Grand Boulevard itself: Grand Boulevard and Grand Boulevard Park between Keith Road in the south and Highway 1 in the north (City side), continuing as Grand Boulevard East to Boulevard Crescent and Lynn Valley Road (District side). Exact official boundary streets for the sub-area are UNVERIFIED. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- Where the condo stock concentrates: nowhere on the corridor — there is none (Section 2). The corridor's identity is its wide, park-like boulevard frontage with detached houses facing it, under City rules on the City side and District rules on the District side. Listing coverage of the area describes Grand Boulevard as primarily detached homes with larger lots, in contrast to Central Lonsdale's condos and townhomes, and the Boulevard (City) profile states the area is made up mostly of single-family homes. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md) and https://www.jimpilkington.com/north-vancouver/grand-boulevard/ and https://www.realestatevalley.ca/north-vancouver-real-estate.php?p=1
- Heritage frame: Grand Boulevard is a City heritage character area and forms part of the rectilinear system of boulevards and parks known as North Vancouver's Green Necklace, which also includes Victoria Park, Ottawa Gardens and Mahon Park. The Canadian Register of Historic Places describes a 105-metre-wide, 1.6-kilometre-long central garden boulevard, planned from 1906 by the North Vancouver Land and Improvement Company, with wide lots facing it. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md) and https://www.historicplaces.ca/fr/rep-reg/place-lieu.aspx?id=2425
- Nearby areas: Central Lonsdale (west, across the City core), Moodyville (east, City), Lynn Valley (north-east, District, along Lynn Valley Road), and Upper Lonsdale (west, District side). (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- Transit: TransLink route 240 (Lynn Valley / Downtown) runs via Lynn Valley Road, Boulevard Crescent, Grand Boulevard East, 15th Street, Jones Avenue, Keith Road, Marine Drive and the Lions Gate Bridge to downtown Vancouver — the corridor has a direct downtown bus on its own street. Route 230 runs on Lonsdale Avenue west of the corridor to Lonsdale Quay and the SeaBus, which crosses to Waterfront Station in 12 minutes, every 15 minutes during the day. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- Anchors beside the corridor: Queensbury Elementary (2020 Moody Avenue) and Ridgeway Elementary (420 East 8th Street) are the elementary schools beside the corridor, and Sutherland Secondary (1860 Sutherland Avenue) is the secondary school west of it; Lions Gate Hospital is at 231 East 15th Street, west of the corridor, and the North Vancouver City Library is at 120 West 14th Street. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- Market numbers: the City and District of North Vancouver are both outside SnapStats coverage in the September 2026 FVR + GVR reports (coverage is Surrey, S Surrey White Rock, Cloverdale, Langley, North Delta, Abbotsford, Mission, Burnaby, Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, New Westminster, Pitt Meadows and Maple Ridge only) — no median or inventory figure is quoted in this file. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

The provincial strata block below governs any strata purchase a Boulevard buyer makes elsewhere in North Vancouver; inside the corridor itself there is no strata stock for it to attach to. The municipal rules are given for both municipalities because the corridor straddles the line.

- **Form B and strata records:** on a resale strata purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate; the BC Real Estate Association advises reviewing the Form B for the most recent depreciation report, the strata's financial statements, the CRF balance and planned work to identify future special levies. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports:** effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Minimum CRF contribution:** effective November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund in the annual budget. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void:** since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw; short-term rentals can still be restricted. Any building-directory "rentals not allowed" line a buyer meets on a North Vancouver building is old bylaw text, not current law. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ is the only legal age bylaw:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City side — plans and zoning (south of Highway 1):** City of North Vancouver Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 8400 (2014, amended December 8, 2025) and Zoning Bylaw No. 6700. The Ground Oriented Zone, adopted December 8, 2025, allows up to 3 units on a lot of 280 m² or less, up to 4 units on a larger lot in the Low Rise Neighbourhood 1 designation, and up to 6 units in the Low Rise Neighbourhood 2 or Residential Level 4 designations; the adoption raised permitted units from 3 to 4 on nearly 4,300 properties. Grand Boulevard is also a City heritage character area — frontage and character rules, not just zoning, shape what can be built facing the boulevard. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **District side — plans and zoning (north of Highway 1):** District of North Vancouver Official Community Plan, Bylaw 7900, and Zoning Bylaw 3210. Section 525 (Bylaw 8799) sets R1 minimums of 4,000 m² / 30 m width / 34 m depth and R2 minimums of 660 m² / 18 m / 34 m, with modifiers R2(S1) 335 m² / 10 m, R2(S2) 1,100 m² / 24 m and R2(S3) 4,000 m² / 30 m; an R2 lot may hold a principal dwelling plus a secondary suite and a coach house, and since June 2024 three units total are permitted on single-family lots within the Urban Containment Boundary. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **Hazard permits differ by side:** on the City side the Streamside Protection Development Permit Area covers land within 15 metres of the top of bank of a watercourse or within 10 metres of the top of a ravine bank; on the District side OCP Bylaw 7900, Schedule B sets Development Permit Areas for Streamside Protection, Creek Hazard, Slope Hazard and Wildfire Hazard, any of which can apply on the District portion toward Lynn Valley. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **GST on new condos:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 (some secondary sources say March 12 — CRA governs). The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less (maximum saving $8,000), partial $835,000–$860,000, none at $860,000+. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www.equitylawgroup.ca/news/closing-costs-bc-home-purchase
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. No presale project on the corridor is confirmed, so this applies only to presales a buyer compares elsewhere in North Vancouver. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. (BC standard block, as used in batches C1–C4.)
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60% (LTV up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), 4.50% at the top band with a non-traditional down payment, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- **Tax timing — both municipalities, different rates:** City of North Vancouver 2026 taxes were due July 2, 2026; the 2026 total residential rate is 3.34028 per $1,000 of assessed value, plus a separate residential storm drainage rate of 0.10697 per $1,000. District of North Vancouver 2026 taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty after July 2, 2026 and a further 5% after September 1, 2026; the 2026 total residential rate is 3.28195 per $1,000, and single-family utility fees total $2,571.20 in 2026. Which bill a corridor address gets depends on which side of Highway 1 it sits. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can I buy a condo on Grand Boulevard itself?** No verified condo or townhome stock exists on the corridor — the confirmed housing form is detached houses facing the boulevard and its park, on both the City and District sides. The nearest real condo stock is west in Central Lonsdale, documented in the Central Lonsdale file. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **Q: Is a Boulevard address in the City or the District of North Vancouver?** Both exist on the same corridor: south of Highway 1 is the City, north of it (Grand Boulevard East, Boulevard Crescent) is the District. The exact split point is UNVERIFIED, so check the specific address — the City total residential tax rate is 3.34028 per $1,000 and the District's is 3.28195 per $1,000 for 2026, and the zoning rulebooks are different bylaws. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **Q: A search site shows "Grand Boulevard condos" — are they on the boulevard?** No. The results behind that label are Central Lonsdale buildings west of the corridor (the rental-aggregator results for this name point at 15th Street product). Check the street address, not the search label. https://www.zumper.com/apartments-for-rent/north-vancouver-bc/grand-boulevard
- **Q: What can a corridor house lot hold instead of a condo?** On the City side, the Ground Oriented Zone allows up to 3 units on a lot of 280 m² or less and up to 4 or 6 units on a larger lot depending on the OCP designation; on the District side, an R2 lot may hold a principal dwelling plus a secondary suite and a coach house. Those are one lot's units — they are not separate strata titles unless a future rezoning and stratification occur. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **Q: Does the heritage status change a rebuild on the boulevard?** Yes on the City side: Grand Boulevard is a City heritage character area in the Green Necklace system, so a boulevard-facing rebuild carries character expectations a side-street lot does not, on top of the zoning. The historic-place record notes early owners were bound by building and landscape restrictions registered by covenant, applicable to heirs and successors — pull the title for the specific lot. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md) and https://www.historicplaces.ca/fr/rep-reg/place-lieu.aspx?id=2425
- **Q: Which schools serve a corridor address?** School District 44 catchments are address-based and the locator result for a corridor address is UNVERIFIED — run the exact address with SD44. The schools beside the corridor are Queensbury Elementary (2020 Moody Avenue), Ridgeway Elementary (420 East 8th Street) and Sutherland Secondary (1860 Sutherland Avenue). Note the coming change: SD44 is opening a new elementary school in the Cloverley neighbourhood for 2026/27, and its catchment review potentially impacts the Ridgeway, Brooksbank, Queensbury and Queen Mary catchments east of Lonsdale Avenue. SD44 has elementary (K–7) and secondary (8–12) only, no middle tier. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **Q: How do I get downtown without a car?** Route 240 runs on the corridor's own streets — Boulevard Crescent and Grand Boulevard East — via 15th Street, Keith Road, Marine Drive and the Lions Gate Bridge directly to downtown Vancouver. Route 230 on Lonsdale Avenue connects to the SeaBus at Lonsdale Quay (12 minutes to Waterfront Station). (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **Q: If I buy a condo in Central Lonsdale instead, can the strata ban rentals?** Not for a long-term tenancy — rental-restriction bylaws have been unenforceable since November 24, 2022 anywhere in BC, whatever a building directory still shows. A short-term rental bylaw can still bind, and a 55+ age bylaw can still limit who may live in the unit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: What replaces Form B diligence for a corridor house purchase?** There is no strata, so there is no Form B, depreciation report or CRF to review. The equivalent checks are the municipality (City vs District) on title, the applicable zone and designation, any heritage-character or covenant terms on the boulevard frontage, and the hazard permit area that matches the side of the highway the lot sits on. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **Q: Are there creek or slope permit issues on the District-side streets?** Potentially: District OCP Schedule B sets Streamside Protection, Creek Hazard, Slope Hazard and Wildfire Hazard Development Permit Areas, and the research file notes any of them can apply on the District portion toward Lynn Valley. On the City side the equivalent check is the streamside area (15 m watercourse / 10 m ravine). Confirm the parcel on the applicable municipality's mapping before pricing a rebuild. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **One street name, two city halls.** The same "Boulevard" address can be City (GO Zone, OCP Bylaw No. 8400, total tax rate 3.34028) or District (R1/R2 zones under Bylaw 3210, OCP Bylaw 7900, total rate 3.28195) depending on which side of Highway 1 it sits — never quote one side's density or tax numbers for the other. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **The boulevard was a priced-to-exclude garden suburb.** The historic-place record states the 1906 development required residences to cost more than $4,000 to construct so the area would maintain a high status, with owners signing a covenant binding heirs and successors — the heritage character area on today's City books descends from that scheme. https://www.historicplaces.ca/fr/rep-reg/place-lieu.aspx?id=2425
- **The park median is 105 metres wide and 1.6 kilometres long.** That is the scale of the "boulevard" the houses face — a garden, not a traffic island — and it is part of the Green Necklace with Victoria Park, Ottawa Gardens and Mahon Park. https://www.historicplaces.ca/fr/rep-reg/place-lieu.aspx?id=2425 (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **The City rewrote the corridor's density on December 8, 2025.** The Ground Oriented Zone adopted that day raised permitted units from 3 to 4 on nearly 4,300 City properties and allows up to 6 units in the higher designations — a City-side side-street lot's value now turns on its OCP designation, while the District side still runs on principal + suite + coach house. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **The elementary catchment may move for 2026/27.** SD44's new Cloverley elementary school and its catchment review potentially impact the Queensbury and Ridgeway catchments — both schools sit beside this corridor — so a 2025 catchment statement for a corridor address should be re-run before a family writes. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
- **There are no SnapStats numbers for this corridor.** Both North Vancouver municipalities are outside SnapStats coverage in the reports used for this program, so any "Boulevard median" quoted from those reports does not exist; sub-area inventory, solds and medians are UNVERIFIED and no substitute source is used. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-5/boulevard.md)
