## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Form B — the condo seller's first timeline item:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days of request, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. For a seller working to a deadline, that 7-day statutory window is a real schedule item: a buyer cannot complete strata diligence until the certificate arrives, so a condo seller who orders the Form B and the strata documents at listing — rather than after an offer — removes a week of dead time that no pricing strategy can recover. The page itself does not mention the Form B or strata-document ordering at all: that the fast-sale process includes it is UNVERIFIED from the page — it is stated here as the provincial document rule a Langley condo seller's timeline runs on, not as a page claim. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/sell-my-house-fast-in-langley-bc.md)
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote; and since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. The current depreciation report is attached to the Form B — for a fast condo sale, the report is where a buyer prices the building's future levies, and an old or alarming report is a speed problem no launch schedule fixes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — a condo seller may market the unit's long-term rental potential even if the registered bylaws still carry an old rental-restriction line; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 allow only a 55-or-older strata residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. A seller in a 55+ building sells into a smaller buyer pool by law — a timeline fact for any deadline sale in the Langleys' 55+ stock, whatever a services page promises. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The regulator's framework for the service itself (BCFSA):** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority; a licensed real estate professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings — whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA; dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. When agency disclosure occurs in this page's fast-sale process: UNVERIFIED — the page does not state it. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-14/sell-my-house-fast-in-langley-bc.md)
