- **Privacy officer and policy duties (Province of BC, via search-result read):** every organization must designate at least one privacy officer to oversee privacy practices and ensure compliance with PIPA, with the privacy officer as the primary contact for privacy-related questions and complaints; every organization must have a privacy policy, available upon request, that explains how personal information is collected, used, disclosed, stored and disposed of; and an organization is responsible for all personal information it handles, including information shared with contractors. - **Oversight, safeguards and breaches (OIPC, via search-result read):** organizations in BC are subject to PIPA, which has rules for how and when organizations can collect, use and/or disclose personal information; organizations also need to make reasonable security arrangements to protect personal information; and the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner (OIPC) for BC oversees and enforces the administration of PIPA, including investigating privacy complaints and whether organizations have reasonable safeguards. When personal information is collected, used, disclosed or destroyed in contravention of PIPA it is called a privacy breach; the OIPC guidance sets out a four-step breach response (containment, risk evaluation, notification, prevention) and organizations can report breaches to the OIPC. - **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000.
