## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

For this topic the operative rules are the farm rules that replace strata law entirely, then the Chilliwack municipal layer, then the standard strata rules stated only for contrast.

- **The ALR stratification bar (the lead rule for this file):** ALR residences cannot be stratified. Depositing a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s. 6; Strata Property Act s. 239), subdivision of ALR land requires Agricultural Land Commission permission outside limited regulation exceptions (Agricultural Land Commission Act s. 21(1)), so ALR ground cannot be stratified without ALC permission — and no source checked shows a Chilliwack poultry-farm parcel with such a permission for residential strata use. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/chilliwack-poultry-farms-for-sale.md)
- **ALR housing frame:** on ALR land, one principal residence is permitted without an ALC application at a maximum total floor area of 500 m² (no more than 1,000 m² of fill), a secondary suite is permitted only when located within the principal residence, and at most one additional residence is permitted of up to 90 m² on properties 40 ha or less or up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha; local government bylaws may be more restrictive. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/chilliwack-poultry-farms-for-sale.md)
- **Supply management and quota (BC Chicken Marketing Board):** BC poultry production is supply-managed. For chicken, quota is the amount of chicken, expressed in kilograms of live weight, that the BC Chicken Marketing Board allots to a grower for production and marketing; the board's General Orders name two classes — mainstream quota (regular broilers, roasters, Cornish, certified organic) and specialty quota — and define a grower as a person engaged in the production of chicken to whom the board has allotted quota or issued a permit that has not been cancelled. The General Orders treat transfer of quota with registered premises and acquisition or disposition of quota without registered premises as different cases. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Board approval of transfers:** the board's role is to approve quota transactions, ensuring growers are in good standing and production is allocated appropriately for each growing cycle, and quota transfers are facilitated privately rather than through a board-run central platform described in the board's consultation paper. A BC Chicken Quota Exchange platform was stated by the board to be in development; whether it is operating is NOT CONFIRMED. No official source publishes quota transaction prices — UNVERIFIED. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Policy-Directions-Update-Sep-2026.pdf
- **The other boards:** the BC Broiler Hatching Egg Commission's regulated product is a broiler hatching egg, a saleable chick or a broiler breeder; mainstream quota there is hen-based (one unit = one placeable broiler breeder hen) and specialty is hatched-chick-based. Turkey quota under the BC Turkey Marketing Board is the number of pounds of turkey meat a producer is entitled to market in intraprovincial trade through normal channels, or to have marketed on the producer's behalf by the board, during a specified period. The BC Farm Industry Review Board supervises the regulated marketing system and hears appeals of a board's decision, order or determination, with appeals to be filed within 30 calendar days of the board's final decision. https://bcbhec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2022-Annual-Report.pdf and https://bcturkey.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/174_66.pdf and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/organizational-structure/ministries-organizations/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/npma-appeals
- **Quota position in this file's research, stated honestly:** the Chilliwack research file opened no marketing-board source, so quota inclusion/exclusion practice, transfer and relocation rules, board-approval, barn-audit, premises-certification and biosecurity requirements for a specific Chilliwack listing are UNVERIFIED in that file and are carried here only as the settled board rules above, from this program's province-wide poultry file. Get the quota position in the contract, in writing, and verify it with the applicable board directly. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/chilliwack-poultry-farms-for-sale.md) (Poultry-farms file: ~/workspace/local-notes/condos/poultry-farms-condos-2026-11-01.md)
- **Biosecurity and avian influenza (a closing-condition rule, not a strata rule):** BC Gov poultry guidance defines biosecurity as management practices that reduce disease and pests entering a farm and limit spread, using a Controlled Access Zone for the production area and a more restricted Restricted Access Zone where birds are housed. Highly pathogenic avian influenza is a reportable disease in Canada; the CFIA establishes primary control zones where it is detected, permits are required to move birds, their products and by-products through a control zone, and on a quarantined farm no birds, bird products such as eggs, or by-products such as manure may enter or leave without CFIA permission — which can affect bird placement, showings and sale timing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/ai_enhanced_biosecurity_for_small_scale_poultry_producers.pdf and https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected
- **BC Assessment farm classification:** farm classification is a voluntary program; the income thresholds are $2,500 if the total area of the farm operation is between 0.8 ha and 4 ha, $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if the operation is greater than 4 ha, and $10,000 if the operation is less than 0.8 ha; applications must be submitted to BC Assessment by October 31 for consideration for the next assessment and tax year, and a buyer who plans to continue farming a farm-classified property must submit a General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/chilliwack-poultry-farms-for-sale.md)

Chilliwack Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000 defines POULTRY as any domesticated fowl including chickens, turkeys, ducks, geese and swans, and lists General Agriculture as a permitted principal use in its AL, AU and AS zones. The City's SSMUH bylaw is Bylaw 5395 (June 18, 2024) — an urban-lot rule that does not create strata product on ALR farm land. https://chilliwack.com/ocp and https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/chilliwack-poultry-farms-for-sale.md)

- **The City's farm plan is a schedule, not the whole OCP.** Inside Chilliwack 2050 (Bylaw 2025, No. 5500), the Agricultural Area Plan is Schedule B1 and Development Permit Area D-4 Farm Home Plate governs farm home plates — the Sardis plan is a different schedule (B5), and citing one schedule for another area's rules misstates the plan. https://chilliwack.com/ocp (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/chilliwack-poultry-farms-for-sale.md)
- **Chilliwack's tax penalty is two dated steps, not one.** For City parcels the 2026 schedule is 5% on taxes outstanding after July 2, 2026 and a further 5% after August 4, 2026 — a closing that straddles either date carries a different adjustment than the seller's notice suggests. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2522 (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/chilliwack-poultry-farms-for-sale.md)

- **Biosecurity zones are part of the property, in practice:** BC Gov guidance structures a poultry farm around a Controlled Access Zone for the production area and a Restricted Access Zone where birds are housed, with steps such as allowing only essential visitors and restricting contact between poultry and wild birds — showings of an operating poultry farm are a biosecurity event, not a normal open house. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/ai_enhanced_biosecurity_for_small_scale_poultry_producers.pdf
