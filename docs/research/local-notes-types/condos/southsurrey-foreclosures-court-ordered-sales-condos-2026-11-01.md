## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

### The court layer (BC Laws sources, as stated in this program's Langley foreclosures file)

- **How a foreclosure starts:** a proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property, or for redemption, must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7(1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Order nisi and the redemption period:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption — Rule 21-7(5)(b). The Land Title Act refers to the court's order in a foreclosure as an "order nisi" (s. 271(1)). The Rules state no fixed length for the redemption period — it is the period the court fixes — and interest is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act, s. 19). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol33/consol33/96250_18 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Final order and applying for a sale:** in default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7(6); a party may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7(7). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5(3). The court's directions may appoint who has conduct, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of value — Rule 13-5(4). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Court approval of your purchase:** the manner of sale the court fixes may be sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5(4)(b); the person with conduct may apply, even before the redemption period expires, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7(9); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in Form 60 — Rule 13-5(6). The vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Competing bids:** the court's directions may define "the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids" — Rule 13-5(4)(d); the Supreme Court's practice direction PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings," governs practice in these proceedings. The timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66 were not retrieved from the court's website for the Langley file — UNVERIFIED beyond the rule text. The live page for this topic states sealed bids are collected in the courtroom by 9:45 a.m. under PD-66 — that account is the page's own statement, not adopted as verified here. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 and https://jaredgibbons.ca/southsurrey-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/southsurrey-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales.md)
- **Where it is filed and where to read it:** unless the court otherwise orders, a foreclosure proceeding is commenced at the Supreme Court registry in the municipality where the land is located, if there is one there, and otherwise at a registry in the judicial district in which the land is located (Law and Equity Act, s. 21(2)). Which registry serves South Surrey / White Rock as a stated mapping is UNVERIFIED — the research file opened no BC Courts locations source in its pass. Court Services Online eSearch is open to anyone with Internet access and shows the file number, registry, party names and filed documents for Supreme Court civil files, class "H - Foreclosure." https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01 and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/southsurrey-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales.md)

### The strata layer

- **Form B — the limit that matters most in a court sale:** in an ordinary resale, an owner, purchaser or authorized person may request a Form B Information Certificate and the strata corporation must provide it within 7 days (maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page), disclosing fees, amounts owed, approved levies, unfiled bylaw amendments, proceedings, parking/storage allocations and insurance and electrical-planning summaries. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Form B in a court-ordered sale — UNCONFIRMED as a general rule:** no official source read for this program states that a court-ordered sale will produce a full Form B and strata document package, or that it will not. The seller in a court sale is the person with conduct of sale under court directions (Rule 13-5(3)), not the strata-lot owner in the ordinary course, and the conditions of sale are the court-settled particulars (Rule 13-5(4)) — so a buyer must not assume the usual document package will be available, complete or current. What documents a specific court sale offers is a fact of that sale's conditions — read them, and treat any general claim either way as UNCONFIRMED. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years with no deferral vote, and since November 1, 2023 stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF annually. The older context buildings in Section 2 (Semiahmoo Estates 1990/1991, Suncliff Place 2002, The Capistrano 1980s per listings) are in the age band where those reports drive levy risk — and in a court sale, where the document package may be thin (above), the current depreciation report is the document to chase first. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rentals and age:** long-term rental-restriction bylaws are void since November 24, 2022; the directory/listing rental lines for Semiahmoo Estates and The Capistrano are void since that date and must not be read as current law. The only valid age bylaw is 55+ (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2), with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children — and 55+ is exactly the restriction Semiahmoo Estates, Suncliff Place and The Capistrano are published with, so in a court sale of a unit in those buildings the age bylaw, unlike the rental line, can still bind the buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Municipal layer — kept separate by city:** Surrey property answers to the **Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted June 15, 2026)** — Bylaw 18020 is the replaced predecessor only — and Surrey Zoning By-law No. 12000, parcel by parcel in COSMOS; Surrey taxes are due July 2 (5% penalty after July 2, further 5% after September 2), and Surrey short-term rentals are permitted for owners only from their primary residence with a $358-per-year business licence. White Rock property answers to **White Rock Zoning Bylaw 2024 No. 2506 and White Rock Official Community Plan 2017 No. 2220** — White Rock's pair only, never Surrey's bylaws. Neither city's bylaws change the court process — they matter only to what a buyer could later do with the property, and for a strata condo the strata bylaws matter more. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/official-community-plan (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/southsurrey-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales.md)

### Taxes on a court-ordered condo purchase

- **PTT and GST treatment of court-ordered sales specifically: UNCONFIRMED.** No official source read for this program states a special Property Transfer Tax or GST rule for a purchase through a court-ordered sale, and this file does not invent one. The general rules a buyer's lawyer and notary will apply are: BC PTT at 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million, with the First-Time Home Buyers' Program (full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000) and the Newly Built Home Exemption (full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000); GST at 5% on new construction only, not resales; the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 — 100% of GST to $1 million, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5 million, agreements March 20, 2025–2031); the GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out); Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.60%–4.50%; and, where a presale contract is assigned or a developer sale is involved, REDMA s. 21's 7-day rescission and s. 18 trust deposits. Whether a court vesting order changes any of that for a given purchase is a question for the buyer's lawyer on the specific file — UNCONFIRMED here. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is a court-ordered condo sale just a normal purchase with a discount?** No. The contract is conditional on court approval where the court has fixed sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" (Rule 13-5(4)(b)), the seller is the person with conduct of sale selling under court-settled conditions (Rule 13-5(3)–(4)), and there is no published statistic in any official source for a "typical discount" in South Surrey — any discount claim is UNCONFIRMED. Note the general market sold at 102% sale-to-list (detached) and 99% (attached) in this community in September 2026 — all-property figures, not foreclosure figures. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/southsurrey-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales.md)
- **Q: Can my accepted offer still lose?** Yes, two ways the rules themselves create: the person with conduct may apply to confirm a sale even before the redemption period expires (Rule 21-7(9)), but until the confirming order is made the sale is not final; and the court's directions may define rights to bid, make offers or meet bids (Rule 13-5(4)(d)), so a competing bid at the approval stage is a real possibility in this process. Get the specific file's bid instructions in writing — the live page's 9:45 a.m. sealed-bid account is the page's own statement, not verified process. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can the owner still save the condo after I offer?** The redemption right runs until the process ends it: the court fixes a redemption period in the order nisi (Rule 21-7(5)(b)), interest runs to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act, s. 19), and only in default of payment within that period may a final order of foreclosure be granted (Rule 21-7(6)). Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Q: Will I get the full strata documents — Form B, minutes, depreciation report?** UNCONFIRMED as a general promise. In an ordinary resale the strata must provide a Form B within 7 days of a proper request; no official source read for this program guarantees a court sale will produce a full package, and the conditions of sale are court-settled particulars that may differ from a standard contract. Price the information risk, and read the conditions of sale line by line with a lawyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: How do I check the court file myself?** Court Services Online eSearch is open to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry location, party names and the list of filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." Which registry serves a South Surrey file as a stated mapping is UNVERIFIED in the research file — the petition, the amount certified due and the orders made are the facts of the deal. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/southsurrey-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales.md)
- **Q: Is the title clean if I win?** That is what the confirming order does: on the Rule 21-7(9) application the court may order the sale confirmed, direct the disposition of the proceeds and vest title in the purchaser; the result of the sale is certified in Form 60 (Rule 13-5(6)). The vesting order, not the listing, is the title-cleaning step. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: The listings for Semiahmoo Estates and The Capistrano say rentals are banned and it's 55+. Which one binds me?** The rental line does not — rental-restriction bylaws have been void since November 24, 2022. The 55+ line can: a 55-or-older bylaw is the one age bylaw the Strata Property Act still permits (ss. 123.1/123.2). Semiahmoo Estates is directory-recorded with age restrictions, pets not allowed and rentals not allowed, and The Capistrano's listings present the same rental line with a 55+ community — read only the age and pet lines as potentially live, and confirm both on the registered bylaws. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/semiahmoo-estates-2239-152-street and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Do I pay PTT or GST differently because it's a court sale?** UNCONFIRMED — no official source read for this program states a court-sale-specific PTT or GST rule. Budget on the general rules in Section 3 and have a BC real-estate lawyer confirm the treatment on the specific file before the approval hearing, not after. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Is the unit vacant, and can I inspect inside?** Occupancy is a matter of the individual court file, not a published statistic — UNVERIFIED as a general statement. Inspection and access terms live in the court-settled conditions of sale for that file; if the conditions do not offer an inspection, do not assume one can be added. "As-is, where-is" / Schedule "A" conditions and the absence of a Property Disclosure Statement are likewise UNVERIFIED as stated process rules in the research file — one feed card states "SOLD AS IS, WHERE IS" in its own listing text only. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/southsurrey-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales.md)
- **Q: How long is the redemption period on the South Surrey file I'm looking at?** Whatever that file's order says. The Rules state no set length — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b) — so anyone quoting a standard period for a specific file without reading its order is guessing. Read the order on Court Services Online or through counsel. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
