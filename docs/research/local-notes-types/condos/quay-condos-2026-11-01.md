## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- The Quay is the City of New Westminster's Downtown waterfront neighbourhood along Quayside Drive on the Fraser River — the City's context-statement name for the same neighbourhood is "Quayside." Downtown sits directly above the waterfront, Uptown up the hill, Victoria Hill to the east, and Queensborough across the Fraser River to the south. Precise board boundary roads: UNVERIFIED. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- Where the condo stock concentrates: this sub-area is almost entirely condos. In September 2026 it recorded 59 condos & townhomes listings and 0 detached listings, with 16 sales (a 27% sales ratio — the strongest sub-area ratio of the New Westminster files in this batch). The stock lines Quayside Drive from Westminster Pier Park east along the Waterfront Esplanade: the 2019 Bosa towers at the west end (RiverSky 1 and 2), the 1989–1990s Bosa-era towers mid-drive (Anchor Pointe, Riviera, Tiffany Shores, The Promenade), the Laguna Court cluster (Laguna Landing, The Excelsior), and the Aragon buildings at the Renaissance Square / Rialto Court pocket (The Q, Murano Lofts), plus two 1980s wood-frame low-rises (Quay West, Quayside Terrace). Sources: the building entries in Section 2 below; market figures as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report via (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)

- Transit anchors: New Westminster Station and Columbia Station (Expo Line) at the top of the waterfront; TransLink route 103 is the Quayside / New Westminster Station route and route 102 serves Victoria Hill / New Westminster Station. Quayside Drive is the waterfront road; Columbia Street and 8th Street link the Quay to Downtown and the station, and Stewardson Way links west toward Marine Way and Burnaby. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Form B and strata records:** on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate; the BC Real Estate Association advises reviewing the Form B for the most recent depreciation report, the strata's financial statements, the CRF balance and planned work to identify future special levies. In the Quay's 1986–1997 stock (Quay West, Anchor Pointe, The Excelsior, Laguna Landing) this is the central diligence document — read it together with the building's flood and insurance position below. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports:** effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Minimum CRF contribution:** effective November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund in the annual budget. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/more-help-and-support/subscribing-for-updates
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void:** since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw; short-term rentals can still be restricted. Any Quay directory or listing line stating "rentals not allowed" reflects old bylaw text, not current law. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ is the only legal age bylaw:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. No Quay building in Section 2 is published as age-restricted — UNCONFIRMED either way until the registered bylaws are read on the Form B. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Floodplain — the Quay's defining rule:** the City's floodplain study places the Quay in Floodplain Zone 3, the Fraser River waterfront zone, where many newer buildings have floor space above the Flood Construction Level but egress and the waterfront path sit below it. The City publishes flood management information for its low-lying zones including the Quay; confirm the specific building's flood construction level compliance and the strata's insurance and deductible position lot by lot before subject removal. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- **City plans and zoning:** the Downtown Community Plan (Schedule C of the OCP) governs the downtown waterfront, under Our City 2041, the City of New Westminster Official Community Plan (Bylaw No. 7925, 2017); zoning is Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001, with multiple-unit residential and comprehensive development districts for the Quayside area. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- **New Westminster property taxes:** 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on current taxes remaining unpaid on July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on any unpaid balance on September 3, 2026. New Westminster operates its own electrical utility and City water/sewer services. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- **GST on new condos:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 (some secondary sources say March 12 — CRA governs). The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less (maximum saving $8,000), partial $835,000–$860,000, none at $860,000+. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000 — relevant only to any remaining developer sales (for example at Pier West), not to resales. https://www.equitylawgroup.ca/news/closing-costs-bc-home-purchase and https://danmarusin.com/blog/bc-property-transfer-tax-2026/
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18 — the frame for any developer-contract purchase at Pier West. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60% (LTV up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), 4.50% at the top band with a non-traditional down payment, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. Separately, a building's floodplain position can affect a lender's and insurer's view of a specific property — no source checked publishes a Quay building-by-building position, so treat any blanket claim as UNCONFIRMED. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is the Quay in the floodplain?** Yes — the City's floodplain study puts the Quay in Floodplain Zone 3, where many newer buildings have floor space above the Flood Construction Level but egress and the waterfront path sit below it. The practical questions are building-specific: flood construction level compliance, strata insurance cover and deductibles, and any flood-related claims history in the strata documents. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- **Q: Which buildings are the newest, and does warranty remain?** RiverSky 1 (908 Quayside Drive) and RiverSky 2 (988 Quayside Drive) were both built in 2019 by Bosa Properties — 33-storey concrete towers of 233 and 234 units. A current RiverSky 1 listing notes the building is still under 2-5-10 warranty; confirm the warranty position for the specific unit, because the 2-year and 5-year components expire before the 10-year structural component. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md) and https://www.estateblock.com/new-westminster/2705-908-quayside-dr-1173517
- **Q: Is there any low-rise or wood-frame stock, or is it all towers?** Mostly towers, but not all: Quay West (31 Reliance Court) is a 4-storey, 60-unit wood-frame low-rise built in 1986, Quayside Terrace (5 K de K Court) is a 4-storey building, and Murano Lofts (7 Rialto Court) is a 7-storey concrete mid-rise of 237 units built in 2005. Wood-frame versus concrete changes the insurance, depreciation-report and levy conversation — check which form the specific building is. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- **Q: Can I rent out a Quay condo long-term?** Yes — strata rental-restriction bylaws have been unenforceable since November 24, 2022 anywhere in BC, whatever older directory or bylaw text says. Short-term rentals are different: a strata bylaw can still restrict them, and the registered bylaws on the Form B govern that question. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: The Riviera's address and year don't match between sources — which is right?** UNCONFIRMED. The building-data source in the research file gives Riviera at 1245 Quayside Drive, 20 storeys, 307 units, built 1990; a current listing markets the Riviera at 1185 Quayside Drive, built 1995, with a pool/hot tub/gym amenity set. Treat them as an unresolved conflict and verify the strata plan number on the specific unit's title before relying on either set of figures. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md) and https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r3020203-1185-quayside-drive-1701-new-westminster-bc-v3m-6t8/brochure
- **Q: How is the commute, really?** Two Expo Line stations — New Westminster Station and Columbia Station — sit at the top of the waterfront, and route 103 runs between Quayside and New Westminster Station. Door-to-station walks lengthen toward the east-end buildings (Laguna Court, The Q); measure the specific building's walk rather than relying on "steps to SkyTrain" listing language. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- **Q: Which schools serve a Quayside Drive address?** School District 40 catchments are address-based. The verified locator run for 800 Carnarvon Street (the station address) returned École Qayqayt Elementary (K–5), Fraser River Middle (6–8) and New Westminster Secondary (9–12), with Early French Immersion shown at École Herbert Spencer Elementary for that address. One concrete published example: current Anchor Pointe listings list gas and hot water among the strata-fee amenity inclusions, with an exercise facility, sauna and club house on site. ## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The living space is above the flood level; the way out is not.** The City's floodplain study notes that in Zone 3 many newer buildings have floor space above the Flood Construction Level, but egress and the waterfront path sit below it — a distinction that shapes strata insurance and emergency planning, and one that listing copy never mentions. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- **One developer built most of the waterfront, in two waves.** Bosa built the first wave (Anchor Pointe 1989, Riviera 1990 per the building-data sources) and the current wave (RiverSky 1 and 2, both 2019; Pier West beside Pier Park) — cross-building comparisons of construction and warranty inside the Quay are unusually direct because so much stock shares one builder. Sources: Section 2 entries.
- **The Quay has two names and both are official in different places.** The board sub-area name is "Quay" (SnapStats GVR); the City's heritage context name for the same neighbourhood is "Quayside," and the street, park and bus route all use Quayside. Searches under only one name miss records filed under the other. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- **Tiffany Shores is four addresses, one strata.** 1210, 1220, 1230 and 1240 Quayside Drive sit under a single strata corporation, NWS2924, managed by Quay Pacific — document orders and depreciation reports run to the corporation, not to the individual street address. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- **The city's newest recreation asset is up the hill, not on the waterfront.** The təməsew̓txʷ Aquatic and Community Centre at 65 East Sixth Avenue fully opened in May 2024 — at 10,644 sq. m. the City's largest capital project to date, with an eight-lane 50-metre pool, leisure pool, fitness centre and gymnasia. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
- **There is no detached fallback in this sub-area.** September 2026 recorded 0 detached listings in the Quay against 59 condos & townhomes listings — a buyer set on this waterfront is choosing between buildings, not between housing forms, which is why the building-level diligence in Sections 2 and 3 carries the whole purchase. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-3/quay.md)
