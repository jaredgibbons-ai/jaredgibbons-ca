## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**Zero strata-condo stock in this topic, stated plainly at the top: a manufactured home in a Mission-area park is not a condo. There is no strata corporation, no Form B, no contingency reserve fund and no depreciation report — the buyer owns a manufactured home and rents the pad under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA). The parks below are documented as tenure traps — product a buyer can mistake for strata ownership — not as condo buildings, and none is counted as an apartment condo.**

### The core distinction — park rule vs strata bylaw

- In a strata, an age restriction is a registered bylaw: BC law allows only a 55-or-older residency bylaw (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2), it is disclosed on the Form B, and the May 1, 2023 exemptions for younger spouses/partners, live-in caregivers, legacy residents and defined children apply to it. In a manufactured home park, the 55+ rule is a **park rule** set by the park owner under the MHPTA tenancy — it is not an SPA bylaw, no Form B discloses it, and the SPA exemptions do not govern it; the written park rules, which the seller must hand to the proposed buyer before assignment is even requested, are the document that governs. Treat any "55+ strata" language in a park-home listing as a category error. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/mission-mobile-homes.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

Read this section as "rules a condo buyer expects, and what actually applies instead" — the Strata Property Act block is stated because buyers arrive with condo assumptions, and each item names the manufactured-home rule that replaces it.

- **Form B — there is none for a park home.** A Form B Information Certificate (Strata Property Act s. 59) exists only for strata lots: it states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached, provided within 7 days at a maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. The park-home equivalents are the written park rules and the relevant parts of the site tenancy agreement (which the seller must give the proposed buyer before asking the landlord to consent to assignment), the home's Manufactured Home Registry record, and its CSA label status. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports — not applicable to a park.** The rule that BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years (since July 1, 2024, no deferral vote) binds strata corporations only. A park has no depreciation report; the park's capital plans are the landlord's, and a buyer's protection is the MHPTA rent-increase regime below. **CRF minimum — not applicable:** the rule that stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund (since November 1, 2023) binds strata corporations only; a park home owner pays pad rent, with no CRF to inspect and no owner vote over the park's spending. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void — a strata rule, not a park rule.** Since November 24, 2022, no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw. That voiding is a Strata Property Act change; it does not rewrite a park's rules. Whether a park home may be rented out is governed by the park rules and the site tenancy agreement — read them, do not import the strata rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ — park rule vs SPA bylaw.** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw in a strata, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. A park's 55+ restriction (Mission Mobile Home Park) is a park rule under the MHPTA, set in the written park rules — the SPA exemption list does not govern it, and the park's own wording decides who may live in the home. Oasis Country Estates' "adult side" states no age number in any source checked: UNCONFIRMED as 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Pad tenancy law — the regime that actually governs.** Manufactured home park tenancies in BC are governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and its Regulation, administered through the Residential Tenancy Branch. On a sale where the home stays in the park, assignment of the site tenancy permanently transfers the pad tenancy to the buyer; under MHPTA s. 28 a tenant may assign only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the Regulation), an order of the director, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it; the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting (s. 28(3)); before requesting consent, the seller must provide the proposed buyer with written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; on assignment, the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase; an unreasonable refusal can be taken to RTB dispute resolution. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/mission-mobile-homes.md)
- **Pad rent increases — verified limits.** Landlords in manufactured home parks can increase rent only with 3 full months' notice, at most once every 12 months, and not above the yearly rent increase limit, plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park); further increases only by Residential Tenancy Branch order. The limits: **2026 = 2.3% plus the proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% plus the proportional amount.** https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s. 42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute the notice within 15 days, may in defined circumstances (home cannot be moved, no site within a reasonable distance, no tax owing on the home) apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes and (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/mission-mobile-homes.md)
- **Registration is what makes the sale effective.** In BC, the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered with the Manufactured Home Registry, which maintains the central register of manufactured home ownership and controls the movement of manufactured homes in BC. A home may not be sold without the original legible CSA decal attached (Z-240 for mobile homes, A-277 for modular homes); a lost label requires re-certification through a Technical Safety BC electrical inspection, whose approval label can stand in place of the CSA label for a sale. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/mission-mobile-homes.md)
- **Local zoning split:** inside the City of Mission, manufactured-home parks sit under City zoning and the City's Official Community Plan (Bylaw 6405-2026, adopted April 21, 2026); the Dewdney Deroche and Hatzic Island parks on this feed sit under FVRD electoral-area zoning instead. City/FVRD park-specific zoning, redevelopment or closure-notice rules for any named park: UNVERIFIED in the research pass — no park bylaw document was opened. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/mission-mobile-homes.md)
- **Property taxes, two systems behind one feed:** park homes inside the City of Mission sit in a city where 2026 property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a flat 10% penalty on current balances outstanding on July 3, 2026 (Community Charter ss. 233–234; Mission has no alternative-scheme bylaw). Parks in the FVRD electoral areas are rural — owners there receive the tax notice from the Province's Surveyor of Taxes Office and pay the Province, with rural taxes due July 2 and 5% + 5% penalties under the Taxation (Rural Area) Act, s. 11. How tax is split between a park owner and individual pad tenants in any specific park: UNVERIFIED — it is set by the park's tenancy agreements. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/rural-area and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01
- **GST and purchase taxes:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not ordinary resales; how GST and Property Transfer Tax apply to a specific manufactured-home purchase depends on whether land transfers with the home (a park unit transfers no land) — no source in the research file settles the treatment for a specific unit: UNCONFIRMED per unit; take conveyancing advice before writing. For qualifying new homes generally: the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (CRA News No. 122: Royal Assent March 13, 2026; secondary sources saying March 12 are not followed — CRA governs); the GST New Housing Rebate returns 36% to a $6,300 maximum across the $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. BC Property Transfer Tax generally runs 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million and 3% above, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million, with a First-Time Home Buyers' exemption (no PTT on the first $500,000 at ≤$835K fair market value, partial to $860K) and a Newly Built exemption (full $1.1M, partial to $1.15M) where land is acquired. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. Park-home resales are not REDMA presales — stated so a buyer does not assume a 7-day rescission exists on a park purchase; subject periods are contractual here. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60% to 4.50% — the published range is 0.6%–4.5% — plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. Park-home financing is lender-specific chattel-style lending in many cases — confirm financing with the lender and the park before subjects, never from the listing alone. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/ and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- **ALR, where a park or home sits on farm land:** ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6; SPA s. 239; ALCA s. 21(1)) — one more reason no ALR-ground park home is condo stock. Parcel-level ALR status for any park: UNVERIFIED. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is a park home a condo?** No. There is no strata corporation, no Form B, no CRF and no depreciation report. You own the manufactured home and rent the pad under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/mission-mobile-homes.md)
- **Q: Which Mission-area park is actually 55+, and is that the same 55+ as a condo?** Mission Mobile Home Park (34519 Lougheed Hwy, Hatzic) — its MLS records state "This has been 55+ mobile park" and record the park as "Adult Oriented." It is not the same 55+: in a strata, 55+ is a registered bylaw under SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 with statutory exemptions; in a park, 55+ is a park rule in the written park rules, and the SPA exemptions do not govern it. Get the park rules in writing before subjects. https://www.cotala.com/tours/85367/FullPublic.pdf?i=979581669 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Do I own the land under a Mission park home?** Generally no: the Province describes park tenancies as owning the manufactured home and renting the site it sits on. The one feed card claiming a home on its own lot (Triple Creek Estates) is the exception to test on title — if it holds, that purchase is land-plus-home, not a pad tenancy. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/mission-mobile-homes.md)
- **Q: Can the park raise my pad rent when I buy?** No — on assignment of the site tenancy the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, and the scheduled date for a subsequent rent increase does not change. Increases otherwise need 3 full months' notice, at most once every 12 months, within the yearly limit (2026: 2.3%; 2027: 2.2%) plus the proportional levy and utility amount. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: What does the park have to approve before I can buy?** The assignment itself: under MHPTA s. 28 the seller may assign the site tenancy only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, an order of the director, or authorization in the tenancy agreement), the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent, and the seller must first give you written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement. Build the consent timeline into subjects. A fresh Green Acres listing states the same gate in plain words: "Park approval is required." (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/mission-mobile-homes.md) and https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3065197-28-9267-shook-road-mission-british-columbia-v2v5m2-359900/brochure
- **Q: Is Oasis Country Estates a 55+ park?** Not as a whole, and not verifiably at 55+ at all: listings split it into an "adult side" and a "family section" and state no age number for the adult side. Treat any "55+" label for this park as UNCONFIRMED and get the park rules in writing. https://www.zolo.ca/mission-real-estate/41168-lougheed-highway/13
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold for redevelopment?** Under MHPTA s. 42 the landlord needs all permits in place first, then 12 months' notice and $20,000 compensation per tenancy, with defined routes to additional compensation where the home cannot be moved or no site is available within a reasonable distance — and a further payment (the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent) if the landlord does not proceed with the conversion within a reasonable period. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Are all the parks on this page in the City of Mission?** No — the feed includes parks in Dewdney Deroche and on Hatzic Island, which are FVRD Electoral Area G ground with rural taxes paid to the Province, alongside City parks. The MHPTA applies in both, but zoning and the tax penalty regime differ: City flat 10% after July 2, 2026, versus rural 5% after July 2 plus a further 5% after October 31. - **The feed's own parks pull in opposite age directions.** On one feed: a park whose records state 55+ outright (Mission Mobile Home Park, Hatzic), a park whose listing states "Family Friendly, no age restrictions" (Ruskin Place), a park with a playground and no age rule stated (Triple Creek Estates), a park under two names with no age rule stated (Nicomen Park / Nicomen Village), a park with park approval required and no age rule stated (Green Acres), and a gated park split into an adult side and a family section with no age number (Oasis Country Estates). - **Assignment consent is the hidden closing step in every park sale.** A house sale closes on title; a park-home sale also needs the landlord's consent to assign the site tenancy (MHPTA s. 28), with the park rules handed to the buyer before consent is even requested. Build that timeline into the contract. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/mission-mobile-homes.md)
- **A feed label once called a Ruskin Place pad home a "condo."** One feed variant carries the sub-type "Leasehold Condo/Strata" for a manufactured home in a pad-rent park with Ownership Type Other / Manufactured Home on its fuller record. Feed sub-type labels are marketing plumbing, not tenure — the title, the registry record and the park's tenancy documents settle what is being bought. https://www.estateblock.com/mission/19-9960-wilson-st-126341
- **The pad-rent cap has a second component.** On top of the percentage limit (2.3% in 2026, 2.2% in 2027) the landlord may add the proportional amount — the change in local government levies plus regulated park utility fees divided by the number of sites in the park — so two parks with the same percentage limit can post different lawful increases in the same year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
