## 2. PROVINCE-WIDE — NO SINGLE-AREA BUILDING LIST: WHAT A FIRST-TIME BUYER IS ACTUALLY CALCULATING

- **Stated plainly: there is no building list for this topic.** A province-wide calculator file does not name buildings for one area; building-by-building notes belong in the area condo files in this series. What this section documents instead is the calculation itself — the five numbers a first-time condo buyer is solving for, in the order the official rules require them.
- **1. The sticker stack: price + GST (new only).** Resale condos are generally exempt from GST; a new condo carries 5% GST on the price before rebates. The calculator's first fork is therefore resale vs newly built — it changes both the GST line and which PTT exemption is tested. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **2. Property transfer tax, gross then net.** Gross PTT is computed on the full fair market value at the general tiers (1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above, plus 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000), then the exemption the buyer actually qualifies for is subtracted — the first-time program (no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000) or, for a new build, the newly built home exemption (full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000). The two BC exemptions are mutually exclusive; a buyer who qualifies for both elects the larger. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **EXAMPLE 1 — resale condo (illustrative arithmetic only; inputs stated, not a market fact).** Inputs: purchase price $700,000; resale (no GST); buyer qualifies for the first-time program; property 0.5 ha or smaller, principal residence. Gross PTT: 1% × $200,000 = $2,000, plus 2% × $500,000 = $10,000, total $12,000. First-time exemption: PTT on the first $500,000 = $2,000 + (2% × $300,000 = $6,000) = $8,000 saved. Net PTT payable: $12,000 − $8,000 = **$4,000**.
- **EXAMPLE 2 — new condo (illustrative arithmetic only; inputs stated, not a market fact).** Inputs: purchase price $800,000; newly built (5% GST applies); buyer is an eligible first-time buyer under both federal and BC tests. GST: 5% × $800,000 = $40,000; First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate at this price (100% of GST up to $1M, maximum $50,000) = $40,000, net GST $0 if fully eligible and credited or claimed. Gross PTT: $2,000 + (2% × $600,000 = $12,000) = $14,000; Newly Built Home Exemption at $800,000 (full threshold $1,100,000) = $14,000, net PTT **$0** if all conditions are met. The example shows why the tool must test both BC programs: at $800,000 the newly built exemption ($14,000) is larger than the first-time exemption ($8,000).
- **3. The down payment and the insurance premium.** Minimum down payment: 5% on the first $500,000 and 10% on the portion above (homes $1.5M or more require 20% and are uninsurable). Under 20% down, CMHC mortgage insurance is required and the premium — 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount, by loan-to-value band — is usually added to the mortgage, which is why the calculator needs the down payment, not just the price. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **EXAMPLE 3 — insured purchase (illustrative arithmetic only; inputs stated, not a market fact).** Inputs: price $600,000; down payment 10% = $60,000; mortgage $540,000; loan-to-value 90%, in the 85.01%–90% band at a 3.10% premium on the schedule in Section 3. Premium: 3.10% × $540,000 = **$16,740**, typically added to the mortgage balance. Minimum down payment at this price would have been 5% × $500,000 + 10% × $100,000 = $35,000.
- **4. The savings room: FHSA and HBP.** FHSA participation room is $8,000 in the first year the account is opened (lifetime $40,000 in the standard block for this series — the research file could not re-read the lifetime limit on CRA's detailed page and marks it UNVERIFIED there; this file follows the series figure and flags the research file's caution). The HBP allows an RRSP withdrawal for a first home, and CRA confirms a buyer can use both FHSA and HBP for the same qualifying home when all conditions are met. The research file could not re-read the current HBP withdrawal limit on CRA's page and marks it UNVERIFIED; the series standard figure is $60,000, flagged here with the same caution. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **5. The condo-only lines a generic calculator misses.** Strata fees and any approved special levy are not purchase taxes, but for a condo they belong in the same affordability sheet: the Form B states the unit's monthly fee, any amount owed and any levy already approved, and the home owner grant is an annual item after purchase (regular grant $570 as published, effective January 1, 2027; 2026 threshold $2,075,000) that must never be netted against closing costs. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)

## 3. THE FULL RULES SET

### Strata Property Act — resale and ownership

- **Form B — Information Certificate (s.59).** On a resale, the strata corporation provides a Form B stating the unit's monthly strata fee, any amount owed, any special levy already approved, the CRF balance, parking and storage entitlements, any judgments/proceedings, and — since April 1, 2023 — a summary of the strata's insurance coverage. The BC Real Estate Association advises reviewing the Form B for the most recent depreciation report, the strata's financial statements, the CRF balance and planned work to identify future special levies. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports.** Effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Strata fees and the CRF.** Since November 1, 2023, strata corporations must contribute a minimum of 10% of the annual operating fund to the contingency reserve fund when approving budgets. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void.** Since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata corporation in BC may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit short-term rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws — 55+ only (ss.123.1 / 123.2).** The only permitted age-restriction bylaw is 55+, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner and defined children. The bylaw governs who may reside, not who may own. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

### Taxes and rebates — purchase

- **GST — resale vs new.** Resale condos are generally exempt from GST; new construction carries 5% GST in BC. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **FTHB GST Rebate (Bill C-4).** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 — some secondary sources say March 12; CRA governs) rebates 100% of the GST on a new home valued up to $1M (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5M, for agreements entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. The application window is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction; a builder can credit the rebate at transfer, or the buyer files Form GST190 (builder purchase) or GST191 (owner-built) with CRA. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST New Housing Rebate (the older rebate, still in place).** 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, phasing out for homes between $350,000 and $450,000.
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — general rates.** 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion to $2M, 3% above $2M, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3M. PTT is based on fair market value on the day the transfer is registered at the Land Title Office, and is not the same as annual property taxes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **PTT — first-time home buyers' program.** The buyer must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, have lived in BC for at least a year immediately before registration (or filed at least 2 BC income tax returns in the last 6 taxation years), have never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world, and never have received the exemption before. Full exemption conditions: principal residence only, fair market value $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), 0.5 hectares or smaller, only residential improvements — exempting PTT on the first $500,000. A partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000. The buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and occupy to the first anniversary, and claims go through the buyer's legal professional in the PTT return (or by refund application from the first anniversary to 18 months after registration). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **PTT — newly built home exemption.** Full exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), phasing out over the $50,000 range above it, eliminated at $1,150,000. A newly built home includes a unit in a newly built condo building not occupied since construction. Citizenship, BC, principal-residence, 0.5-hectare and occupancy conditions mirror the first-time program. The two exemptions are mutually exclusive — elect the larger. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Taxes deliberately out of scope.** BC's speculation and vacancy tax and the BC home flipping tax are not purchase-time costs a first-time-buyer calculator computes; neither is encoded here, and whether the live tool mentions them is UNVERIFIED. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **Additional PTT.** A foreign national, foreign corporation or taxable trustee must also pay additional PTT on the residential portion in a specified area; the rate is published on a separate provincial page not re-read for the research file, so the rate is UNVERIFIED here. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)

### Presales and assignments — REDMA

- **Disclosure and 7-day rescission.** A developer must give the buyer a disclosure statement filed with BCFSA before the contract binds; the buyer may cancel within 7 days after the later of signing the purchase agreement and receiving/acknowledging the disclosure statement, with the deposit refunded from trust. For a first-time buyer, a presale condo is where the calculator's new-build branch (GST + newly built exemption) usually applies. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/

### Financing supports

- **FHSA.** CRA describes the FHSA as a registered plan letting a first-time buyer save for a qualifying first home tax-free up to set limits; participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000 and contributions are generally deductible. The lifetime limit is the series figure of $40,000 — flagged: the research file could not retrieve CRA's detailed participation page and marks the lifetime limit UNVERIFIED there. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **HBP.** CRA describes the Home Buyers' Plan as allowing an RRSP withdrawal to buy or build a qualifying home, repayable within the published period, and confirms FHSA and HBP can be combined for the same home. The withdrawal limit is the series figure of $60,000 — flagged: the research file could not retrieve CRA's HBP page and marks the limit UNVERIFIED there. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **CMHC mortgage insurance.** Required when the down payment is under 20% on a home priced $1.5M or less; the insurance protects the lender, not the buyer. Minimum down payment is 5% on the first $500,000 and 10% on the portion above. Published premiums run 0.60% (LTV up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), 4.50% at the top band with a non-traditional down payment, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies, and the premium may be added to the loan. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much PTT does a first-time buyer in BC actually save?** A qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price — a maximum saving of $8,000 — provided the fair market value is $835,000 or less, the property is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the principal residence; above $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. See EXAMPLE 1 in Section 2 for the arithmetic at $700,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: What are the general PTT rates the calculator starts from?** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% on the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% on the portion over $2,000,000 — plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000 where that applies. The exemption reduces the bill; it does not change the tiers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Is a newly built condo treated differently from a resale condo?** Yes, twice over: the newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 (phase-out to $1,150,000) against the first-time program's $835,000, and the new home may attract 5% GST, which the federal first-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate can offset by up to $50,000. See EXAMPLE 2 in Section 2 — at $800,000 the newly built route nets both taxes to $0 on the stated inputs. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: Can I claim both BC exemptions — first-time and newly built?** No. A buyer who qualifies for both elects the larger one. At most first-condo prices the newly built exemption is larger, because its threshold ($1,100,000) and its base (the full PTT bill, not just the first $500,000) both exceed the first-time program's. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: How much can I put in an FHSA in the first year?** Participation room in the first year you open the account is $8,000, and contributions are generally tax deductible — which is why the account rewards being opened before the purchase year, not at offer time. The $40,000 lifetime figure is the series standard; the research file flags it as not re-read on CRA's detailed page. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same condo?** Yes — CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the HBP for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Q: How much down payment do I actually need?** The floor is 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any portion above $500,000 (so $35,000 on a $600,000 purchase — EXAMPLE 3 inputs), and 20% at $1.5M or more, where insurance is unavailable. Under 20% down adds the CMHC premium to the true cost. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: How much mortgage insurance will I pay with 10% down?** On the CMHC schedule, the 85.01%–90% loan-to-value band is 3.10% of the mortgage amount (the 90.01%–95% band is 4.00%, 4.50% with a non-traditional down payment source), within the overall 0.6%–4.5% range; amortizations over 25 years add 0.20%. EXAMPLE 3 in Section 2 works it at $540,000 of mortgage: $16,740. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- **Q: What does the calculator NOT tell me about a condo purchase?** The building. Strata fees, approved levies, CRF health and deductible exposure live in the Form B package, not in any tax calculator — and the live tool's own fields and formulas are NOT CONFIRMED in the research, so treat its output as an estimate to be re-run against the Form B and a legal professional's closing statement. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **Q: I bought, then had to move for work eight months later — do I lose the exemption?** Part of it can be repayable: the first-time and newly built exemptions both require moving in within 92 days of registration and occupying the home as a principal residence to the first anniversary of registration, and part may be kept if the buyer moves out early — the exact repayment is a PTT-return question for your legal professional, not a calculator output. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **April 1, 2024 is the date inside every threshold in this file.** The first-time program's $835,000 ceiling (partial to $860,000) and the newly built exemption's $1,100,000 threshold (out at $1,150,000) both took effect that day — a calculator, blog post or feature sheet using the old $500,000-era thresholds is pre-2024 material. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **March 13, 2026 — the FTHB GST rebate became law.** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 per CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122; some secondary coverage says March 12 — this file follows the CRA. The rebate reaches back to agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and runs before 2031. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **The federal and BC "first-time" tests are different tests.** BC's PTT program requires never having owned a principal-residence interest anywhere in the world plus a BC residence/tax-filing test; the federal GST rebate uses its own eligibility frame. Passing one does not prove the other — the calculator's single "first-time buyer?" toggle hides two separate determinations. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **The home owner grant is not closing money.** It is an annual reduction of property tax on a principal residence ($570 regular grant as published, effective January 1, 2027; 2026 full-grant threshold $2,075,000, $0 over $2,189,000), claimed each year after you own and occupy — a tool that nets it against purchase taxes overstates your closing position. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **Two figures in this file carry the research file's retrieval caution, stated openly.** The FHSA lifetime limit and the HBP withdrawal limit could not be re-read on CRA's detailed pages during the research (those pages could not be retrieved), so the series figures ($40,000 lifetime FHSA; $60,000 HBP) are used with that flag rather than presented as freshly verified — the same honesty the research file applies to the live tool's unconfirmed fields and formulas. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **The strata dates that changed a first condo's rule book:** rental bylaws void November 24, 2022; Form B insurance summary April 1, 2023; wider 55+ exemptions May 1, 2023; 10% CRF minimum November 1, 2023; depreciation reports every five years from July 1, 2024. A first-time buyer comparing a 2019 listing's bylaw summary against today's law is reading a superseded rule book. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
