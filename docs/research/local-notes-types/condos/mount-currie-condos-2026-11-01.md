The Village of Pemberton is the neighbouring municipality a few kilometres west (exact road distance on an official source: UNVERIFIED). The Nation states the community is home to approximately 1,495 of its more than 2,275 citizens, and is named for Mount Currie, the 2,591 m (8,501 ft) mountain at the centre of Líl̓wat Traditional Territory. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/ (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/mount-currie.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

**Why the standard condo frame does not apply here — read this before any other bullet.** A BC condo exists because provincial land law creates a strata corporation under the Strata Property Act on land held in fee simple (or a registrable leasehold). Mount Currie sits on federal reserve land: there is no fee-simple title to strata, no strata corporation, and no municipal zoning bylaw for the reserve lands. Land allocation runs through the Nation's Lands Department and Band Council processes. Whether the Nation has enacted a formal land code under the First Nations Land Management Act is UNVERIFIED. Every standard bullet below is therefore given in two parts: what the BC rule is, and why it does not attach here. Lot-specific questions go to the Nation's Lands Department. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/mount-currie.md)

- **How ownership actually works here (the operative frame):** the Nation's published "Five Steps to Home Ownership on Reserve" — (1) meet the Housing Manager; (2) a land encumbrance check from the Lands Department confirming a serviced lot is available and registered to the person; (3) a Band Council Resolution (the Band Council acts as guarantor on the loan); (4) possible housing subsidies from Indigenous Services Canada; (5) a Ministerial Loan Guarantee and bank financing. Because the Council acts as guarantor and a Ministerial Loan Guarantee backs the loan, standard mortgage and foreclosure mechanics do not apply the way they do off reserve. The Nation's own building-cost guidance: modest new construction at about $250–$300 per square foot including water, sewer and hydro connections, and CSA Z-240 modular/pre-manufactured homes at about $160–$180 per square foot installed and hooked up, with Mount Currie modulars required to meet zone 6 construction standards and a roof snow load rating of 136 lb per square foot. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Form B — does not exist here.** Off reserve, Strata Property Act s. 59 requires a strata to provide a Form B Information Certificate on a resale (fees, levies, CRF balance, insurance summary, depreciation report attached; within 7 days; max $35 + 25¢/page). There is no strata and no resale market in Mount Currie, so there is no Form B to request; the equivalent diligence for a citizen is the Lands Department land encumbrance check in the five-step process above. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF — provincial strata rules, no strata to apply them to.** Off reserve, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years (since July 1, 2024) and contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF (since November 1, 2023). Neither obligation creates or describes any housing right on reserve land, where capital planning runs through the Nation's Housing Plan (2018–2033) and its 12-units-per-year build goal. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental and age bylaws — provincial strata law, stated for comparison only.** Off reserve, strata rental-restriction bylaws are void since November 24, 2022, and Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw (Regulation 7.01 exemptions: legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and from May 1, 2023 younger spouses/partners and defined children). Mount Currie has no strata corporations, so no strata age bylaw creates a 55+ product here and no rental-bylaw rule creates a rental right here; rental housing in the community is Nation-administered band housing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Surrounding-area frame — SLRD Electoral Area C (context only, does NOT govern reserve parcels).** Off reserve, the Pemberton Valley around Mount Currie is SLRD Electoral Area C, a separate jurisdiction with its own bylaws; the Village of Pemberton is the neighbouring municipality with standard municipal taxes, zoning and MLS sales. Do not apply SLRD or Pemberton bylaws, taxes or market language to a reserve parcel, and do not file Pemberton buildings in this file. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/ (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/mount-currie.md)
- **Taxes and transfer taxes — do not apply as they do off reserve.** Standard municipal and regional district property taxes, and provincial property transfer tax, do not apply to reserve land the way they apply to fee-simple land off reserve; the Nation's own taxation laws or service fees for a specific lot are UNVERIFIED. For completeness, the off-reserve BC PTT rates are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2M, 3% above, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3M — they are stated here only so no page invents a Mount Currie application of them; they do not price a reserve-land home. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/mount-currie.md)
- **GST and buyer programs — off-reserve instruments, no application here.** Off reserve, 5% GST applies to new construction (with the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate — Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122; 100% of GST to $1M, max $50,000, phasing to $1.5M — the GST New Housing Rebate at 36% / max $6,300 / $350K–$450K, the First-Time Home Buyers' PTT Program and the Newly Built Home Exemption to $1.1M, REDMA's 7-day rescission (s. 21) and trust deposits (s. 18), the $60,000 Home Buyers' Plan, the FHSA ($40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year) and CMHC premiums of 0.60%–4.50%). None of these is a route into Mount Currie housing: there is no developer sale, no presale and no MLS purchase for them to attach to. Financing for citizens runs through the Band Council Resolution / Ministerial Loan Guarantee process above. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **ALR and flood:** reserve lands are not in the provincial Agricultural Land Reserve (a provincial designation over provincial/private land) — stated as UNVERIFIED pending an official citation in the research file, and any parcel question goes to the Nation's Lands Department. A community-specific floodplain map or flood construction rule for Mount Currie on an official source is likewise UNVERIFIED; the community sits in the Pemberton Valley near the Lillooet and Birkenhead rivers. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can anyone buy a condo — or any home — in Mount Currie?** No. Mount Currie is the main community of the Líl̓wat Nation on reserve land; housing is allocated and financed through the Nation's own process for its citizens, starting with the Nation's Housing Manager and Lands Department — there is no fee-simple open market and no condo product of any kind. Inquiries go to the Líl̓wat Nation (info@lilwat.ca, 604-894-6115). https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf and https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- **Q: Why doesn't the BC condo/strata system apply here?** Because the land is federal reserve land administered by the Nation, not provincial fee-simple land: there is no strata corporation, no Form B, no depreciation-report or CRF regime, and no municipal zoning bylaw for the reserve lands. Land allocation runs through the Nation's Lands Department and Band Council. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Q: How does home ownership work for Líl̓wat citizens?** Through the Nation's five steps: meet the Housing Manager; get a land encumbrance check from the Lands Department confirming a serviced lot registered to you; obtain a Band Council Resolution (the Council acts as guarantor); check Indigenous Services Canada housing subsidies and the Ministerial Loan Guarantee; and arrange bank financing. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
