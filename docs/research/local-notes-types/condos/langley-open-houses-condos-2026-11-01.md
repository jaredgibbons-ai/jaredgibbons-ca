## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3):** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, states that the disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Representation at an open house:** BCFSA states a licensed real estate professional in BC must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party, and must disclose at the outset whether they can represent the consumer as a client. Dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 — the host cannot simply "also represent" a buyer who walks in. The regulator is the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA). https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Identification or sign-in for open-house visitors:** NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED as a rule; a host may still ask, as a matter of the seller's instructions, but that practice is not in an official source. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/langley-open-houses.md)
- **How the dates and times get onto the page:** UNVERIFIED — no BC Gov or BCFSA source states how open-house dates and times are set and entered for publication on the brokerage/board side, and no official or board public source describing ShowingTime or TouchBase scheduling for the Langley market was found. This page's feed update interval is also UNVERIFIED — the page text read states none. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/langley-open-houses.md)
- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, CRF balance, insurance summary, with rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. This is the document step after the open house — the open house shows the unit, the Form B shows the building. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain one every five years, no deferral by vote. **Minimum CRF contribution:** at least 10% of the operating fund since November 1, 2023. In Willoughby's 2016–2024 stock this lands as a first-cycle question; in Langley City's older low-rises it is the levy story. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 — any directory line stating "rentals not allowed" is void for long-term rentals, and a directory line like Yorkson Creek Parkside 2's "rentals allowed" is now simply the default law; short-term rentals can still be restricted. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 — only 55+ is lawful; exemptions for younger spouse/partner, live-in caregiver, legacy residents and defined child cases (May 1, 2023 expansion). No building in Section 2 is published as age-restricted in its sources — UNCONFIRMED either way until the registered bylaws are read on the Form B. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Plans and zoning (two municipalities):** Willoughby Heights, Aldergrove and Walnut Grove are Township of Langley sub-areas — Township zoning is Bylaw No. 2500, and the Township's SSMUH bylaw is Bylaw 6020 (adopted November 18, 2024; no 6-plex tier exists in it). Langley City is a separate municipality with its own plan and zoning — a Langley City open house is not under Township rules. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/langley-open-houses.md)
- **GST on new condos:** 5% GST applies to new construction (any developer sales in Willoughby's newest projects), not resales. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026. The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial $835,000–$860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18 — the frame for any presentation-centre product on this feed, such as the Willoughby Heights townhome offered through a presentation centre on the first page. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60%–4.50%. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is the host at an open house the seller's agent?** The host is a licensee connected to the listing side; BCFSA requires a licensee to work for either the buyer or the seller, prohibits dual agency (2018) except in the rarest circumstances, and requires unrepresented consumers to be told the risks — assume anything non-factual you tell the host can reach the seller. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers

- **Q: Can I rent out a Langley condo long-term?** Yes — rental-restriction bylaws have been unenforceable since November 24, 2022 anywhere in BC; short-term rentals can still be restricted, and a 55+ age bylaw, where one exists, still limits who may live in the unit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: What should I ask for after I like a unit at an open house?** The strata package behind the Form B: the depreciation report (mandatory every five years for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024), financial statements, CRF balance and planned work — the items that identify future special levies. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
