## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- The City of Surrey's King George Corridor plan area runs along King George Boulevard from 8 Avenue in the south to the Nicomekl River in the north, with Highway 99 at the east — roughly 7 km and about 490 hectares (1,210 acres). It began as a 1995 concept plan; its designations have been superseded by the Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan update (current OCP: Bylaw No. 21900, adopted June 15, 2026), carried through Corporate Report R246, endorsed December 2, 2024. The City page states no named west boundary road, so the west edge is UNVERIFIED beyond the plan map itself. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/king-george-corridor-south (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/king-george-corridor.md)
- The south end of the corridor overlaps the Semiahmoo Town Centre Plan area (roughly 16 to 24 Avenues), which is why the newest buildings cluster around 17 Avenue and 152 Street. One listing feed card for this sub-area carried a White Rock civic address — the sub-area straddles the Surrey–White Rock line at its south end, and whether a specific unit is in Surrey or White Rock is an address-level check. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/king-george-corridor.md)
- SnapStats' September 2026 report counts the King George Corridor sub-area at 85 detached and 131 condos-and-townhomes active, with 8 and 27 sold — a 9% detached and 21% attached sales ratio. The 27 attached solds were the highest of any S Surrey White Rock sub-area that month, and the 131 attached listings were third-highest. SnapStats publishes no sub-area medians; S Surrey White Rock community medians for September 2026 were $1,628,000 detached and $700,000 attached. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/king-george-corridor.md)
- Transit on the corridor today is bus: the live page names routes 321 (King George Boulevard) and 345, with frequencies UNVERIFIED in this research. The planned King George Boulevard BRT would run about 19 km with 12 stations from Surrey City Centre to Semiahmoo Town Centre on dedicated lanes, every 5 minutes at peak, about 40 minutes end to end (roughly 40% faster than the current trip); Surrey Council has moved it to full design. Only the BRT's south portion crosses this plan area — the rest of the route is Whalley and Newton ground. https://www.surrey.ca/news-events/news/council-approves-next-stage-of-king-george-boulevard-brt https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/plans-and-projects/rapid-transit/bus-rapid-transit/brt-phase-3-engagement-summary.pdf
- The corridor's north edge is a river, not a road: the Nicomekl River bounds the plan area, Surrey's coastal floodplain mapping covers the Nicomekl/Serpentine lowlands, and the City is building Nicomekl Riverfront Park on the river's south bank. Flood construction levels are set parcel by parcel, so the flood status of any specific building site is UNVERIFIED without a parcel-level check. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/king-george-corridor.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- The Form B Information Certificate is the buyer's core document in every corridor strata: under s. 59 of the Strata Property Act the strata must provide it within 7 days of a request, for a fee of no more than $35 plus up to 25 cents per page for copies. In this corridor it does double duty — it is the only document that proves whether the 55+ and no-pets descriptions attached to Suncliff Place and The Cambridge are registered bylaws or just listing copy. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- Depreciation reports: since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more strata lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years — every complex in §2 qualifies, including the 1980s Cambridge buildings, where the report is the document that prices the next roof and envelope cycle. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/ https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- Minimum CRF contribution: since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the annual operating fund to the contingency reserve fund each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- Rentals: strata rental-restriction bylaws have been void since November 24, 2022 — any older directory or disclosure line in this corridor restricting rentals no longer has legal effect, though a strata may still ban or regulate short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- Age bylaws: the only age-restriction bylaw a BC strata may enforce is 55+, under ss. 123.1 and 123.2 of the Strata Property Act, with exemptions added May 1, 2023 (caregivers, and spouses/partners who met the requirement when they moved in, among them). Listings describe both Suncliff Place and The Cambridge as 55+; a 19+ or any other age line in an old document would be void, and the 55+ claim itself stands or falls on the registered bylaws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- Pets: pet limits and bans remain lawful strata bylaws. Published pointers in §2 range from "pets allowed with restrictions" (High Street) and "cats OK" headers (Ocean Bay Villas, High Street units) to "Pets: No" (a Cambridge II unit) and "no pets" listing copy (Suncliff Place, The Cambridge) — all pointers only; the registered bylaw set attached to the Form B governs. (Building entries in §2, each cited there.)
- Zoning: Surrey Zoning Bylaw No. 12000 governs the corridor, and the plan framework above it is now the Surrey 2050 OCP (Bylaw No. 21900, adopted June 15, 2026) — the 1995 corridor concept plan's designations were superseded through Corporate Report R246 (endorsed December 2, 2024), so an old disclosure citing the 1995 plan is citing history, not current zoning. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_Zoning_12000.pdf https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/king-george-corridor-south
- Small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH): Surrey permits 3 units on lots under 280 m² and 4 units on lots over 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of 281 m² or more within 400 m of a frequent bus stop — King George Boulevard's bus service makes that frequent-stop band relevant to the corridor's remaining house lots, though not to its strata complexes. One secondary suite per house is permitted under the BC Building Code framework; an unpermitted suite is not made legal by the SSMUH rules. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- Floodplain: the corridor's north end sits in the Nicomekl lowlands. Surrey's flood control pages cover the Serpentine–Nicomekl lowlands, and flood construction levels are parcel-specific — a buyer near the river end should confirm the building's flood construction level rather than rely on the sub-area map. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands
- Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty applied after July 2 and a further 5% after September 2. South-end addresses near the White Rock line need the municipality checked — a White Rock civic address pays White Rock taxes on White Rock's schedule, not Surrey's. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- GST on new builds is 5% — it applies to developer sales (relevant to remaining developer inventory at 2025 completions like King + Crescent and Halvo) and not to ordinary resales. The first-time home buyers' GST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026, per CRA Excise and GST/HST News No. 122) rebates 100% of the GST on a new home priced up to $1M (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5M, for agreements entered into from March 20, 2025 to 2031. The older GST New Housing Rebate covers homes the FTHB rebate does not: 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, phasing out between $350,000 and $450,000. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- BC Property Transfer Tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion up to $2M, 3% above $2M, plus a further 2% on the residential portion of a price above $3M. The First-Time Home Buyers' exemption is full up to $835,000 fair market value (on the first $500,000) and partial up to $860,000; the Newly Built Home Exemption is full up to $1.1M and partial up to $1.15M. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- Federal first-time buyer tools: the Home Buyers' Plan allows a $60,000 RRSP withdrawal; the First Home Savings Account allows $40,000 lifetime at $8,000 per year. CMHC mortgage insurance premiums run 0.6%–4.5%. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/ https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- Presales: REDMA gives a 7-day rescission period under s. 21 and requires purchase deposits to be held in trust under s. 18 — the framework for any assignment or remaining developer contract in the corridor's new buildings. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
