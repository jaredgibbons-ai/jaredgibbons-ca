### What a condo evaluation works from, instead of a building list

- The CMA method the page states: prices of properties similar to the seller's that have recently sold in the seller's area. For a condo, "similar" is settled building by building — the same building's recent sales first, then comparable buildings — because strata fees, levies and depreciation-report position differ by building even on the same street; that building-level diligence is what the Form B and depreciation report in Section 3 supply. The page does not state how many comparables a delivered CMA contains, what format it takes, or whether a home visit is part of it: UNCONFIRMED. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-11/home-evaluation.md)

- None are named on this page either. A townhouse seller uses the same form and the same CMA route; the evaluation distinction is the same one this program draws everywhere — a townhouse strata and an apartment-condo strata are different forms with different comparable sets, and neither is named or counted in this service file. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

**How to read this section for a seller.** The page states no condo-specific evaluation mechanics, so this section is framed as what a condo seller's evaluation must account for: a condo's market value is not read off comparable sale prices alone — the Form B position, the depreciation report and any approved levies are part of what a buyer will pay, and therefore part of what an evaluation has to reflect. Each rule below is the standard BC frame; anything about how Jared's own CMA treats them in a delivered report is UNCONFIRMED, because the page does not state it.

- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance, and a summary of the strata's insurance, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. A seller who orders the Form B before pricing has the same fee, levy and CRF figures a buyer's evaluation will use. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain one every five years, no deferral by vote. **Minimum CRF contribution:** at least 10% of the operating fund since November 1, 2023. An approved special levy still payable, and a depreciation report showing near-term envelope, roof or parkade work, are valuation facts for a condo — they move what a comparable sale in the same building actually proves. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 — no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw, whatever older directory or bylaw text says; short-term rentals can still be restricted. A condo evaluation that prices in an investor buyer pool is pricing under current law, not under a directory's stale rental line. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 — only 55+ is lawful; exemptions for younger spouse/partner, live-in caregiver, legacy residents and defined child cases (May 1, 2023 expansion). An age-restricted building's buyer pool is narrower by law, which is a comparable-selection fact for its evaluation — confirm the registered bylaw on the Form B. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **BCFSA outset-of-dealings context:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed real estate professional in BC must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings — whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA; dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. A CMA request through this page is, on the page's own text, a request for an evaluation — the page does not state when agency disclosure occurs in Jared's process: UNVERIFIED. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-11/home-evaluation.md)
- **Local plans and zoning (the seller's own municipality governs):** the seven areas on the page sit in different municipalities with different plans — for the Surrey and South Surrey / White Rock (Surrey side) areas, the governing plan is the Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 21900, with Zoning Bylaw No. 12000; White Rock is its own city, with Zoning Bylaw 2024 No. 2506 and OCP 2017 No. 2220. Which plan applies to a specific evaluated home is settled by its municipality, not by this page. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_Zoning_12000.pdf and (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-11/luxury-homes-for-sale-in-white-rock-south-surrey.md)
- **GST on new condos:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — a seller of a developer-purchased unit and a seller of a resale unit are in different tax frames, which an evaluation's comparable set must respect. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026. The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial $835,000–$860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. PTT is a buyer's cost, stated here because exemption eligibility affects the buyer pool an evaluation prices against. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. An assignment of a presale contract is not a resale of a finished condo and is not evaluated on resale comparables — a seller in that position is outside the resale CMA frame this page describes. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60%–4.50%, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
