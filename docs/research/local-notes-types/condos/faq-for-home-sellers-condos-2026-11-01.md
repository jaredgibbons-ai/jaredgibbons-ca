## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **The regulator frame (BCFSA) — the verified frame behind this page's answers:** a licensed real estate professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; the representation, duties and BCFSA complaint-route disclosures must happen at the outset of dealings. This page — like the sibling selling page — states no commission rate or amount, which BCFSA's framework places in the listing conversation. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Selling without a licence, verified at the regulator:** a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves — for example, by selling or renting out their own property — while a person must not provide real estate services to or on behalf of another, for or in expectation of remuneration, unless licensed or exempt. That supports the page's "not legally required to use a REALTOR®" answer. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Property transfer tax is the buyer's tax:** PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office — the page's repeated statement, "property transfer tax is paid by the buyer, not the seller," matches that frame. General rates: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Cooling-off / deposits (verified in the sibling pass from BCFSA, reused):** where a buyer rescinds an accepted offer under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining deposit balance to the buyer, with no separate release form; otherwise a deposit is held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder, and is not automatically returned if subjects are not removed. The live FAQ page does not itself raise the rescission period; the sibling selling page does. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. For a condo seller, the Form B package is what the buyer's diligence runs on. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote; and since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — any directory or bylaw line saying "rentals not allowed" is void for long-term rentals; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. A condo seller cannot market a unit on an old rental ban, and a buyer cannot be bound by one. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 and Regulation 7.01 allow only a 55-or-older strata residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **GST and rebates (seller-relevant edge):** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — a resale condo seller does not charge GST. First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) — 100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements March 20, 2025–2031; GST New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Presales (REDMA):** where the home being sold is an assignment of a developer contract, REDMA s. 21's 7-day rescission period and s. 18 trust-deposit rules frame the original purchase; assignment terms themselves are contract terms, UNCONFIRMED until the contract is read. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **HBP / FHSA / CMHC:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC published premium range 0.60%–4.50% — stated here as the standard program frame for a seller who is also buying; this page itself states none of these figures in the research file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Page claims recorded as page statements only — UNVERIFIED:** the seasonality pattern (November through January slowest, spring pick-up by March, softer August, September rebound); standard completion timelines of 45–60 days from accepted offer (and 2 weeks to 9 months in the structured FAQ) and the bridge-financing description (firm accepted offers on both sides required; setup fee and daily interest); the claim that a seller "may be required to accept" a cash offer at list price matching listed "cash" terms, with "set your terms as TBD" advice; listing contracts typically 4–6 months with a claimed 60-day minimum under the named board's rules; and the BC PST on real estate services point in the article linked from Costs & Logistics. No official, board or lender source for any of these was opened in the research pass. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What does this page actually answer?** Five themed groups — Pricing & Timing; Marketing & Showings; Offers & Negotiation; Preparing Your Home; Costs & Logistics — plus an eight-question structured FAQ covering why a home isn't selling, sell-before-buying, pricing high, whether a REALTOR® is needed, selling costs, renovations, best time to sell, and whether an offer must be accepted. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Q: Why isn't my home selling, per the page?** The page's answer, as a page statement: generally for one or more of three reasons — it is overpriced relative to comparable recent sales and current competition, it is not marketed well enough online and offline, or it does not show well; the fix starts with a home evaluation and an honest comparison against recent sales, active listings and expired or terminated listings. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Q: Do I need a REALTOR® to sell my home in BC?** No — BCFSA's licensing guidance confirms a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves, such as selling their own property; a licence (or an exemption) is required to provide real estate services to or on behalf of another person for remuneration. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Q: What are the costs of selling a home in BC, per the page?** The page lists real estate commission (split between the listing agent and the buyer's agent), cleaning, staging and preparation, legal fees and disbursements, and a possible mortgage penalty if the mortgage is broken early — and states no commission figure, which BCFSA's framework places in the listing conversation, where a licensee must give consumers information about commissions and fees. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md) https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Do I have to accept an offer on my home?** The page's answer, as a page statement: no — on receiving an offer you can accept it as-is, counter, or reject it altogether; a counter voids the previous offer. The page's further claim — that a seller "may be required to accept" a full-price cash offer matching listed terms — is UNVERIFIED as a legal-effect claim in the research file; read your own listing contract's stated terms and take that question to your agent or lawyer. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Q: Who pays the property transfer tax when I sell?** The buyer: PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office — when you buy your next home, you pay it as the buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Q: Should I price high and lower later, per the page?** The page's answer, as a page statement: no — pricing high and reducing later fails because the first two weeks on market are critical and a reduced listing reads as stale. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Q: Will renovations increase my sale value, per the page?** The page's answer, as a page statement: renovations rarely return 100% of cost. Its return ranges (kitchen or bathroom 75–100%, interior paint 50–100%, roof 50–80%, furnace/hot water tank, flooring, doors and windows 50–75%) are attributed on the page to experience and the Appraisal Institute of Canada, but no Appraisal Institute source was opened in the research pass, so those ranges as stated are UNVERIFIED. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Q: When is the best time to sell, per the page?** The page's answer, as a page statement: timing depends on home type, local and macro conditions, time of year and personal goals, with a REALTOR® tour and a sold/expired/active comparison as the recommended route; its specific seasonality pattern (November–January slowest, spring pick-up by March, softer August, September rebound) is UNVERIFIED — no board or SnapStats source for it was opened in the research pass. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
- **Q: What does the page say about showings and open houses?** As page statements: MLS exposure alone is not enough because buyers compare many homes; open houses are recommended the first weekend, with showings beginning there in competitive conditions; sellers should leave during showings, which typically take 15–20 minutes; and a lawn sign is not required but helps local exposure. The 15–20 minute figure is the page's own practice statement, not an official figure. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md)
