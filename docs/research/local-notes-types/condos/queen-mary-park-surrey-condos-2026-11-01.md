## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Queen Mary Park Surrey is a board sub-area in central Surrey, west of King George Boulevard and south of 96 Avenue, named for the City park of the same name. It has no City of Surrey neighbourhood or plan-area boundary — the City does not plan it as a named neighbourhood — so its formal boundaries are UNVERIFIED, and no boundary line for it should be published as fact. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/queen-mary-park-surrey.md)
- What can be said is observational: at the time the research file was read, the sub-area's live feed returned 167 results, and its first page spanned from Scott Road (120 Street) to 134 Street and from 81 Avenue to 96 Avenue. That is a listing observation from one reading, not a boundary. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/queen-mary-park-surrey.md)
- The sub-area sits where two City communities meet — Whalley/City Centre to the north and Newton to the south — and which City community a specific address falls in is address-specific and UNVERIFIED at the area level. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/queen-mary-park-surrey.md)

The City's park page does not list gardens, picnic areas or sports facilities for this park — wider descriptions circulating in site copy are not City facts. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- The Form B Information Certificate is the buyer's core document in every Queen Mary Park strata: under s. 59 of the Strata Property Act the strata must provide it within 7 days of a request, for a fee of no more than $35 plus up to 25 cents per page for copies. It carries the unit's fee, the CRF balance, parking and storage allocation, and the bylaws actually in force. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- Depreciation reports: since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more strata lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years — every building in §2 qualifies. In the 1990s southern cluster this report is the key document: it is where a claim like Sterling Court's "completely rainscreened, new building envelope" is either documented or not. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/ https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- Minimum CRF contribution: since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the annual operating fund to the contingency reserve fund each year. A listing's claim about a specific building's CRF balance (made for Tamaron in §2) is verified against the Form B, not repeated from the listing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- Rentals: strata rental-restriction bylaws have been void since November 24, 2022. Sterling Court's directory line "rentals allowed with restrictions" is therefore void in its restriction half, and Tamaron's "100% rental-friendly" listing description matches the legal position for every strata in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- Age bylaws: the only age-restriction bylaw a BC strata may enforce is 55+, under ss. 123.1 and 123.2 of the Strata Property Act, with exemptions added May 1, 2023. No building in §2 is published as 55+; any age line in an older document here other than 55+ would be void. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- Pets: pet limits remain lawful strata bylaws. Published pointers in §2 — "Cats OK" headers at The Crossing, Quinn, Galilea and Gabriel, "pets allowed with restrictions" at Sterling Court, "pet friendly" copy at Melrose Place and Tamaron — are pointers only; the registered bylaw set on the Form B governs. (Building entries in §2, each cited there.)
- Zoning and plan status: Surrey Zoning Bylaw No. 12000 governs, under the Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan (Bylaw No. 21900, adopted June 15, 2026). Queen Mary Park has no neighbourhood plan of its own, so there is no NCP designation sheet to quote for these buildings — any source presenting one is presenting something that does not exist. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_Zoning_12000.pdf (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/queen-mary-park-surrey.md)
- Small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH) and suites: Surrey permits 3 units on lots under 280 m² and 4 units on lots over 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of 281 m² or more within 400 m of a frequent bus stop — both RapidBus edges (R1 and R6) put much of this sub-area's house stock inside a frequent-stop band. One secondary suite per house is permitted under the BC Building Code framework, and an unpermitted suite is not made legal by the SSMUH rules. That matters here because the sub-area's house listings openly describe unauthorized suites; the legality of any specific suite is per-property and UNCONFIRMED until the permits are checked. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/queen-mary-park-surrey.md)
- Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty applied after July 2 and a further 5% after September 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- GST on new builds is 5%. It does not apply to ordinary resales — a Quinn resale listing says so for its own unit — but it does apply to developer sales, which is the live question at Gabriel (completed 2026) and any developer-held units at Galilea and Quinn. The first-time home buyers' GST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026, per CRA Excise and GST/HST News No. 122) rebates 100% of the GST on a new home priced up to $1M (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5M, for agreements entered into from March 20, 2025 to 2031. The older GST New Housing Rebate covers homes the FTHB rebate does not: 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, phasing out between $350,000 and $450,000. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- BC Property Transfer Tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion up to $2M, 3% above $2M, plus a further 2% on the residential portion of a price above $3M. The First-Time Home Buyers' exemption is full up to $835,000 fair market value (on the first $500,000) and partial up to $860,000; the Newly Built Home Exemption is full up to $1.1M and partial up to $1.15M — the exemption that matters for a developer purchase at Gabriel or Galilea. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- Federal first-time buyer tools: the Home Buyers' Plan allows a $60,000 RRSP withdrawal; the First Home Savings Account allows $40,000 lifetime at $8,000 per year. CMHC mortgage insurance premiums run 0.6%–4.5%. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/ https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- Presales and new completions: REDMA gives a 7-day rescission period under s. 21 and requires purchase deposits to be held in trust under s. 18 — the framework for developer contracts at Gabriel and any remaining developer inventory at Galilea and Quinn. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Where exactly is Queen Mary Park Surrey?** West of King George Boulevard and south of 96 Avenue, around the City park at 8972 Queen Mary Boulevard — but it has no official City boundary, because the City has no neighbourhood or plan area by this name. Anyone showing a precise boundary line for it is showing an invention; the board's own street-level line is UNVERIFIED in this research. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/queen-mary-park-surrey.md)
- **What condo stock is actually here?** Nine verified apartment buildings in two clusters: five 1990s–2000s low-rises on 120A/121A Streets by Scott Road (The Crossing, Melrose Place, Sterling Court, Eclipse, The Birches) plus Tamaron (2003) on 92A Avenue, and three 2024–2026 buildings on 134 Street by the hospital (Quinn, Galilea, Gabriel). (§2 above, each cited there.)
- **Why does Sterling Court show up under West Newton in some listings?** Because sources genuinely conflict: a current listing files it under West Newton while the building directory files it under Queen Mary Park Surrey. This file follows the directory and counts it here — flagged at the top so the choice is visible, not hidden. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/sterling-court-8142-120a-street https://www.livingfraservalley.com/listing/r3099082-8142-120a-street-203-surrey-bc-v3w-0n1/brochure
- **The new buildings are marketed as "Surrey City Centre" — is that where they are?** The marketing name is not the sub-area. "Surrey City Centre" is not a board sub-area name at all; MLS records file Quinn, Galilea and Gabriel under Queen Mary Park Surrey, on 134 Street south of 96 Avenue, by Surrey Memorial Hospital — walkable to the R1 RapidBus and King George Station's orbit, but not in the downtown tower district. (§2 above; research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/queen-mary-park-surrey.md)
- **Can I rent my unit out?** Yes — strata rental-restriction bylaws have been void since November 24, 2022, so even Sterling Court's directory line "rentals allowed with restrictions" no longer restricts a long-term rental. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Are pets allowed in the older buildings?** Published sources point that way — The Crossing, Melrose Place, Sterling Court, The Birches and Tamaron all carry pet-friendly or pets-with-restrictions descriptions, and the northern buildings carry "Cats OK" headers — but pet rules are bylaws, and the enforceable version is the registered set on the Form B. (§2 and §3 above.)
- **Is a suite in a Queen Mary Park house legal just because it's listed openly?** No. Listings in this sub-area openly describe unauthorized suites. Surrey permits one secondary suite per house within its size rules (a suite is capped at 40% of the principal unit's habitable floor area under the City's framework), and the SSMUH changes did not retroactively legalize unpermitted suites — legality is a per-property permit check, UNCONFIRMED for any specific house from listing copy alone. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/queen-mary-park-surrey.md)
- **Does a Gabriel or Galilea purchase carry GST?** If the seller is the developer, yes — 5% GST applies to new-build sales, and Gabriel was completed in 2026 with move-in-ready developer stock. A resale from an owner-occupier does not carry GST. Eligible first-time buyers may recover the GST under the Bill C-4 rebate (100% to a $1M price, maximum $50,000, phasing out to $1.5M), and the Newly Built Home Exemption can remove PTT up to $1.1M. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
