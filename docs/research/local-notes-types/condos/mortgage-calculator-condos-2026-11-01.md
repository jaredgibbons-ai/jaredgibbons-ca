## 2. NO SINGLE-AREA BUILDING LIST — THE CALCULATION FRAMEWORK FROM OFFICIAL SOURCES

- **Stated plainly: there is no building list for this topic. Verified named condo building count = 0 by design.** A province-wide calculator file does not name buildings for one area; building-by-building notes belong in the area condo files in this series. What this section documents instead is the calculation framework itself — the official rules any such tool must encode, as the research file re-read them on official pages on 2026-11-01.
- **Inputs the official rules require any such tool to ask for** — stated as research findings from the rules themselves, not as a description of the live tool: purchase price; down payment amount; contract interest rate; amortization period; whether the buyer is a first-time home buyer and whether the home is newly built (30-year insured amortization eligibility); payment frequency; property taxes, heating costs and strata/condo fees (debt-service ratios); gross household income and other debts; and whether the scenario is a purchase, a refinance, or a straight switch at renewal (whether the stress test applies). https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html and https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **Outputs a tool built on these rules would need to show** — again as research findings: the payment at the contract rate; the qualifying payment at the stress-test rate; the mortgage loan insurance premium where the down payment is under 20%; the resulting loan amount including any premium added to the mortgage; and the GDS/TDS position against the guideline ratios. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **How interest is calculated — semi-annual compounding.** Under the federal Interest Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. I-15, s.6, where principal and interest secured by a mortgage on real property are payable on a blended-payment plan, no interest is chargeable unless the mortgage contains a statement showing the principal and the rate of interest chargeable on it, "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" — the statutory basis for the Canadian convention that fixed mortgage rates are stated as nominal rates compounded semi-annually. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html
- The live page states the same convention (fixed-rate mortgages compounded semi-annually by law; variable-rate mortgages typically compounded monthly) — the fixed-rate half is confirmed by the Interest Act wording above; the variable-rate monthly-compounding practice is a lender-practice statement, not a statutory rule, and was not verified on a regulator page: UNVERIFIED as a rule. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/mortgage-calculator.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **Minimum down payment (CMHC).** For homes $500,000 or less, a minimum 5% down payment; for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require a minimum down payment of 20% and mortgage loan insurance is not available. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **EXAMPLE 1 — CMHC's own worked example (inputs stated by CMHC, not a market fact).** On a $750,000 home the required minimum is $25,000 (5% of $500,000) plus $25,000 (10% of $250,000) = $50,000; a buyer with $60,000 saved (8% down) still needs mortgage loan insurance because the down payment is under 20%. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **EXAMPLE 2 — CMHC's own premium example (inputs stated by CMHC).** A $690,000 mortgage (on the $750,000 purchase above, $60,000 down) at a 4% premium rate carries a $27,600 premium. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **The stress test rate.** OSFI's minimum qualifying rate (MQR) for uninsured mortgages is currently the greater of the mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25%; FCAC states federally regulated entities require the same test for insured and uninsured mortgages. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **EXAMPLE 3 — qualifying rate (illustrative arithmetic only; inputs stated, not a market fact).** Inputs: contract rate 4.50% (a user input, not a researched rate). Contract rate + 2% = 6.50%; floor 5.25%; qualifying rate = the greater = **6.50%**. The lender sizes the approval at 6.50%, while the buyer actually pays at 4.50%.
- **Affordability ratios (FCAC).** Gross Debt Service (GDS): total monthly housing costs should not be more than 39% of gross household income; monthly housing costs are the mortgage payments, property taxes, heating costs and 50% of condo fees (if applicable). Total Debt Service (TDS): total debt load should not be more than 44% of gross income. FCAC frames 39% and 44% as guidelines, not hard approval cut-offs. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **EXAMPLE 4 — GDS for a condo (illustrative arithmetic only; inputs stated, not a market fact).** Inputs: gross household income $10,000/month; mortgage payment at the qualifying rate $2,400; property taxes $250/month; heating $100/month; strata fees $400/month, of which 50% = $200 counts. Housing costs = $2,400 + $250 + $100 + $200 = $2,950. GDS = $2,950 ÷ $10,000 = **29.5%**, inside the 39% guideline on these inputs.
- **DISCREPANCY — flagged openly, as the research file flags it:** the live page states the CMHC premium "ranges from 2.80% to 4.00% of the loan amount (plus 0.20% for 30-year insured amortizations)." That range does not match CMHC's own published range of 0.6% to 4.5% re-read for the research, and the 0.20% surcharge was not confirmed on a CMHC or Finance page: the live page's range and the surcharge are UNVERIFIED here, and this file adopts CMHC's published range only. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/mortgage-calculator.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **What this tool does NOT decide:** a calculator computes payments and guideline ratios; it does not approve a mortgage. OSFI's framework leaves the underwriting decision with the federally regulated lender applying Guideline B-20, and the insurer sets its own eligibility on insured loans — the tool's output is an estimate to take to a lender or broker, not a qualification. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

### Strata Property Act — resale and ownership

- **Form B — Information Certificate (s.59).** On a resale, the strata corporation provides a Form B stating the unit's monthly strata fee, any amount owed, any special levy already approved, the CRF balance, parking and storage entitlements, any judgments/proceedings, and — since April 1, 2023 — a summary of the strata's insurance coverage. For a calculator user, the Form B is where the strata-fee input in the GDS ratio actually comes from. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Depreciation reports.** Effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Strata fees and the CRF.** Since November 1, 2023, strata corporations must contribute a minimum of 10% of the annual operating fund to the contingency reserve fund when approving budgets. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void.** Since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata corporation in BC may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit short-term rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws — 55+ only (ss.123.1 / 123.2).** The only permitted age-restriction bylaw is 55+, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner and defined children. The bylaw governs who may reside, not who may own. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

### Taxes and rebates — purchase (closing-cost cross-reference)

- **GST — resale vs new.** Resale condos are generally exempt from GST; new construction carries 5% GST in BC. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/mortgage-calculator.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **FTHB GST Rebate (Bill C-4).** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122) rebates 100% of the GST on a new home valued up to $1M (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5M, for agreements entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST New Housing Rebate (the older rebate, still in place).** 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, phasing out for homes between $350,000 and $450,000.
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — general rates.** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential property value greater than $3,000,000 where that applies. PTT is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office and is separate from annual property taxes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **PTT — first-time home buyers' program.** Exempting PTT on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), partial between $835,000 and $860,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **PTT — newly built home exemption.** Full exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), eliminated at $1,150,000. The two BC exemptions are mutually exclusive — elect the larger. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Closing-cost dollar amounts UNVERIFIED:** legal/notary fees, title insurance, home inspection and property-tax adjustments are real closing-cost categories named on the live page, but the live page's dollar range for legal/notary fees ($1,500–$2,500) is not an official figure and was not verified on any official source: UNVERIFIED as a figure. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/mortgage-calculator.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html

### Presales and assignments — REDMA

- **Disclosure and 7-day rescission.** A developer must give the buyer a disclosure statement filed with BCFSA before the contract binds; the buyer may cancel within 7 days after the later of signing the purchase agreement and receiving/acknowledging the disclosure statement, with the deposit refunded from trust. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers

### Financing supports and the topic-specific qualifying rules

- **The mortgage stress test — OSFI Guideline B-20 / minimum qualifying rate.** OSFI's MQR for uninsured mortgages is currently the greater of the mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25% (page date modified 2026-01-29). The buffer is currently 2% and the floor is currently 5.25%; OSFI reviews both at least annually, in consultation with the Department of Finance Canada and the Bank of Canada. OSFI obliges federally regulated lenders to apply this stress test, and Guideline B-20 sets the expectation that lenders apply the MQR to most newly underwritten residential mortgagors. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **Insured mortgages are tested the same way.** FCAC states federally regulated entities such as banks require borrowers to pass a stress test — proving they can afford payments at the higher of 5.25% or the negotiated contract rate plus 2% — and that "that's the case for insured and uninsured mortgages." https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **The renewal-switch exception.** OSFI does not expect lenders to apply the MQR to an uninsured straight switch at renewal — where a borrower switches their uninsured mortgage from one federally regulated lender to another with no increase to the amortization period and no increase to the loan amount. FCAC adds that a borrower with an existing mortgage must pass the stress test again if they refinance or take out a home equity line of credit. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Provincially regulated lenders UNVERIFIED in practice:** credit unions are not federally regulated; FCAC notes lenders that aren't federally regulated may also ask a borrower to pass a stress test — whether any given BC credit union applies it is lender-specific and UNVERIFIED here. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Mortgage loan insurance (CMHC).** Required when the down payment is under 20% on a home priced $1.5 million or less; the insurance protects the lender, not the buyer. Premiums as published by CMHC range from 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount, and the premium can be paid in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. The other two approved insurers (Sagen and Canada Guaranty) are named on the live page; their premium schedules were not read in the research and no figure from them is stated here. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **30-year insured amortization.** Eligibility for 30-year mortgage amortizations was extended to all first-time homebuyers and to all buyers of new builds, effective December 15, 2024, building on the August 1, 2024 measure for first-time homebuyers purchasing new builds. The exact general insured maximum (25 years) outside those two groups was NOT stated on a regulator page re-read in the research and is not asserted as verified here; the Department of Finance's detailed first-time-homebuyer criteria for the measure are likewise UNVERIFIED. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **FHSA.** $8,000 of participation room in the first year the account is opened, lifetime $40,000. **HBP.** Up to $60,000 withdrawn from an RRSP for a first home; CRA confirms FHSA and HBP can be combined for the same qualifying home. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What rate does the stress test use?** The greater of your mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25% — OSFI's minimum qualifying rate, applied by federally regulated lenders to insured and uninsured mortgages alike; OSFI reviews the 2% buffer and the 5.25% floor at least annually. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: What is the minimum down payment in Canada?** 5% on homes of $500,000 or less; on homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000; at $1,500,000 or more, a minimum of 20% — and mortgage loan insurance is not available at that level. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: When do I need mortgage loan insurance, and what does it cost?** Whenever your down payment is under 20% on a home priced at $1.5 million or less. CMHC publishes premium rates ranging from 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount, depending on down payment size and loan amount; the premium can be paid up front or added to the mortgage. The live page's narrower 2.80%–4.00% range is UNVERIFIED against CMHC — see Section 2. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: Can I get a 30-year amortization?** On an insured mortgage (under 20% down), 30-year amortizations have been available to all first-time homebuyers and to all buyers of new builds since December 15, 2024; on an uninsured mortgage (20% or more down) 30 years is a lender offering rather than an insured-program rule, so it is confirmed with the lender, not the calculator. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Q: How much of my income can go to housing and debt?** FCAC's guidelines: total monthly housing costs (mortgage payments, property taxes, heating, 50% of condo fees) should not exceed 39% of gross household income (GDS), and total debt load including all other debts should not exceed 44% (TDS); slightly higher ratios may still qualify but carry more risk. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: Why does only half my strata fee count in the GDS ratio?** Because that is FCAC's published composition: monthly housing costs for GDS are the mortgage payments, property taxes, heating costs and 50% of condo fees (if applicable). EXAMPLE 4 in Section 2 works it on stated inputs. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: Do I face the stress test again at renewal?** On a straight switch of an uninsured mortgage to another federally regulated lender, with no increase to the loan amount or amortization, OSFI does not expect the test to be applied — but a refinance or a new HELOC triggers the test again, so model the two paths separately. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: Does a credit union have to stress-test me?** UNVERIFIED for any specific lender: provincially regulated lenders are not federally regulated, and FCAC says only that non-federally-regulated lenders may also ask a borrower to pass a stress test. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: Does this calculator approve me for a mortgage?** No. A calculator computes payments and guideline ratios; OSFI's framework leaves the underwriting decision with the federally regulated lender applying Guideline B-20, and the insurer sets its own eligibility on insured loans. The live tool's own fields and formulas are NOT CONFIRMED in the research, so treat its output as an estimate to take to a lender or broker. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **Q: What closing costs sit outside the mortgage math?** BC property transfer tax alone runs 1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000 and 3% above that on fair market value before any exemption — cash needed at completion on top of the down payment. The live page defers PTT detail to the First-Time Buyer Calculator. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **December 15, 2024 — two branch points changed the same day.** The insured-mortgage price cap rose from $1 million to $1.5 million, and 30-year insured amortizations were extended to all first-time homebuyers and all buyers of new builds — a calculator built before that date branches wrong at both points. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **The three hard branch points in any correct calculator:** at $500,000 the down-payment tier changes; at $1,500,000 insurance disappears and 20% down becomes mandatory; at 20% down the insurance premium disappears and the amortization menu widens. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/mortgage-calculator.md) https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Canadian mortgage math is not divide-by-12.** The Interest Act's "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" wording is why a Canadian payment calculation converts the nominal rate using semi-annual compounding — a tool that simply divides the rate by 12 understates the payment. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html
- **The live page's premium range does not match its own regulator source — stated openly.** The page states 2.80%–4.00% plus 0.20% for 30-year insured amortizations; CMHC publishes 0.6%–4.5% and no surcharge figure could be confirmed on an official page in the research pass. This file uses CMHC's range and flags the page's as UNVERIFIED. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/mortgage-calculator.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **FCAC runs its own qualifier tool on the same ratios.** FCAC's Mortgage Qualifier Tool uses the same 39% GDS and 44% TDS as its guideline ratios — a free official cross-check for any number this page's tool produces. https://itools-ioutils.fcac-acfc.gc.ca/mq-hq/MQCalc-EAPHCalc-eng.aspx
