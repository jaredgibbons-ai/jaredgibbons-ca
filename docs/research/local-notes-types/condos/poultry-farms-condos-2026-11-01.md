## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**Zero condo stock: no apartment-condo building, strata condo complex or townhome complex is part of a poultry farm purchase in any source checked for this file. The "buildings" in this topic are poultry barns and a farm residence on freehold farm land — documented below so the zero is verified, not assumed.**

- What is actually being bought in this market, per the research file's farm profile: land (usually ALR), the barn package, the residence, and quota where the seller includes it — with the Abbotsford West listing behind the current pages expressly stating quota is not included, which the research file notes is a common structure. No strata lot appears anywhere in that package. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html (research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-2/poultry-farms.md)
- The barns are farm structures in law, not strata buildings: under the Agricultural Land Reserve Use Regulation, B.C. Reg. 30/2019, s. 5, the use of agricultural land to construct, maintain or operate a structure, other than a residential structure, that is necessary for a farm use is designated as a farm use, and under s. 4 it may not be prohibited by a local government enactment; s. 5 states for greater certainty that this includes a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation — the wording under which a poultry barn is treated as a farm building in the ALR. The ALC's own guidance says the same: such a structure may be regulated but not prohibited, and must be shown to be required for the farm use on the property and in line with the level of that farm use. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019 and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/763/2025/10/Necessary-Farm-Use-Structures-in-the-ALR-Guidelines.pdf
- Tenure confirms the zero: poultry operations sit on freehold farm land, almost always in the ALR in the Fraser Valley belt; the licence side is annual — hatcheries, growers, transporters and processors are licensed by the BC Chicken Marketing Board on an annual basis. A strata corporation, a Form B and a strata fee have no place in that structure. https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf (research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-2/poultry-farms.md)
- Why a condo building cannot simply appear on this land: a strata plan causes a subdivision (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s. 6), and subdivision of agricultural land is prohibited unless permitted by the Commission after an application or permitted under the ALR General Regulation (ALCA ss. 21(1), 18(5) and 19, as summarized in the bulletin). No source checked shows a poultry-farm parcel with such a permission for residential strata use. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

For this topic the operative rules are the farm rules that replace strata law entirely, then the Abbotsford municipal layer, then the standard strata rules stated only for contrast.

- **Supply management and quota (the rule set that actually governs):** BC poultry production is supply-managed. For chicken, quota is the amount of chicken, expressed in kilograms of live weight, that the BC Chicken Marketing Board allots to a grower for production and marketing; the board's General Orders name two classes — mainstream quota (regular broilers, roasters, Cornish, certified organic) and specialty quota — and define a grower as a person engaged in the production of chicken to whom the board has allotted quota or issued a permit that has not been cancelled. The General Orders treat transfer of quota with registered premises and acquisition or disposition of quota without registered premises as different cases. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Board approval of transfers:** the board's role is to approve quota transactions, ensuring growers are in good standing and production is allocated appropriately for each growing cycle, and quota transfers are facilitated privately rather than through a board-run central platform described in the board's consultation paper. A BC Chicken Quota Exchange platform was stated by the board to be in development; whether it is operating is NOT CONFIRMED. No official source publishes quota transaction prices — UNVERIFIED. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Policy-Directions-Update-Sep-2026.pdf
- **The other boards:** the BC Broiler Hatching Egg Commission's regulated product is a broiler hatching egg, a saleable chick or a broiler breeder; mainstream quota there is hen-based (one unit = one placeable broiler breeder hen) and specialty is hatched-chick-based. Turkey quota under the BC Turkey Marketing Board is the number of pounds of turkey meat a producer is entitled to market in intraprovincial trade through normal channels, or to have marketed on the producer's behalf by the board, during a specified period. The BC Farm Industry Review Board supervises the regulated marketing system and hears appeals of a board's decision, order or determination, with appeals to be filed within 30 calendar days of the board's final decision. https://bcbhec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2022-Annual-Report.pdf and https://bcturkey.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/174_66.pdf and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/organizational-structure/ministries-organizations/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/npma-appeals
- **ALR housing rules (the residence on the farm):** land in the ALR may generally have no more than one residence per parcel unless an additional residence qualifies; a principal residence built without an ALC application must be 500 m² or less in total floor area, and the ALC permits one additional residence without an application at 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller and 186 m² or less on parcels larger than 40 ha, subject to local bylaws. A secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence under the ALC wording in the Langley research — stated here as an ALC rule, with the parcel's own municipality to confirm its bylaw position. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ (research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-2/langley-farms.md)
- **Abbotsford municipal layer:** Abbotsford Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014 governs land in Abbotsford, under the Abbotsforward 2050 Official Community Plan adopted in December 2025 (the 2016 OCP is not current). In June 2024 Council adopted zoning amendments implementing provincial small-scale multi-unit housing and Transit-Oriented Areas legislation; a brief names those amendments as Bylaw 3560-2024, but that number is UNCONFIRMED on the City pages opened — the June 2024 adoption itself is City-confirmed. None of this creates condo product on a poultry farm; it is the municipal frame a farm parcel sits inside. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/making-progress-housing-abbotsford and https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/city-begins-implementing-updated-official-community-plan
- **Biosecurity and avian influenza (a closing-condition rule, not a strata rule):** BC Gov poultry guidance defines biosecurity as management practices that reduce disease and pests entering a farm and limit spread, using a Controlled Access Zone for the production area and a more restricted Restricted Access Zone where birds are housed. Highly pathogenic avian influenza is a reportable disease in Canada; the CFIA establishes primary control zones where it is detected, permits are required to move birds, their products and by-products through a control zone, and on a quarantined farm no birds, bird products such as eggs, or by-products such as manure may enter or leave without CFIA permission — which can affect bird placement, showings and sale timing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/ai_enhanced_biosecurity_for_small_scale_poultry_producers.pdf and https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected
- **Strata rules, for contrast only:** if a buyer cross-shops an Abbotsford strata condo instead of a farm, the standard rules are — Form B on request within 7 days (maximum $35 plus copying); depreciation reports every five years for stratas with five or more lots since July 1, 2024; a minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; long-term rental bylaws void since November 24, 2022 (any "rentals not allowed" directory line is void since that date); and 55+ as the only valid age bylaw (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2, Regulation 7.01). None of these applies to a freehold poultry operation. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Buyer-layer taxes:** a farm purchase can mix land, residence, barns and quota, and no source checked in this file states how BC Property Transfer Tax or GST applies across that mix for a specific operation — treatment for a specific deal is UNCONFIRMED here and is a lawyer/accountant question on the actual asset allocation. The general residential rules (PTT 1% / 2% / 3% / 5% bands; First-Time Home Buyers' Program to $835,000/$860,000; Newly Built Home Exemption to $1,100,000/$1,150,000; GST 5% on new housing; the Bill C-4 First-Time Home Buyers' GST rebate, 100% to $1 million, maximum $50,000, phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements March 20, 2025–2031, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122; GST New Housing Rebate 36%, maximum $6,300; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000/$8,000; CMHC premiums 0.60%–4.50%) are stated for the residence side only and must not be assumed to cover quota or barn assets. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is there any condo or strata component in a poultry farm purchase?** No verified one. The purchase package in the sources is land, barns, the residence and quota where included — on freehold farm land, usually ALR. There is no strata corporation, no Form B, no strata fee and no depreciation report in a poultry operation purchase. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019 (research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-2/poultry-farms.md)
- **Q: Does the quota automatically come with the farm?** No. Quota transfers require board approval, the General Orders deal separately with transfer with registered premises and acquisition/disposition without premises, and listings may state quota is not included — as the Abbotsford West listing behind the current pages does. Establish the quota package in writing: class, kilograms of live weight, and whether it transfers with the premises. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf and https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html
- **Q: Who regulates poultry farming in BC?** Each sector has its own marketing board — the BC Chicken Marketing Board (chicken), the BC Broiler Hatching Egg Commission (hatching eggs) and the BC Turkey Marketing Board (turkey) — supervised by the BC Farm Industry Review Board, which also hears appeals of board decisions within 30 calendar days. https://bcchicken.ca and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/organizational-structure/ministries-organizations/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/npma-appeals
- **Q: Can a municipality stop me building another poultry barn in the ALR?** It may regulate but not prohibit: under the ALR Use Regulation, B.C. Reg. 30/2019, a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation is a designated farm use that a local government enactment may not prohibit — but the ALC guidance adds it must be shown to be required for the farm use on the property and in line with the level of that farm use. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019 and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/763/2025/10/Necessary-Farm-Use-Structures-in-the-ALR-Guidelines.pdf
- **Q: Can I convert part of the farm to strata housing later?** Only through the ALC gate: a strata plan causes a subdivision in the ALR (Bulletin 09, s. 6), and subdivision requires Commission permission after an application or a permission in the ALR General Regulation. An approval for a specific parcel is UNCONFIRMED — do not price a farm on a strata-conversion assumption. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Q: How is quota priced?** UNVERIFIED from official sources: quota trades are facilitated privately, no official source publishes quota transaction prices, and the board's quota exchange was stated to be in development, not confirmed operating. Any per-kilogram figure a listing or a seller quotes is a private claim, not a published market price. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Policy-Directions-Update-Sep-2026.pdf
- **Q: What licences carry over to me as the new grower?** None automatically on the sources checked: hatcheries, growers, transporters and processors are licensed by the board on an annual basis, and a "grower" in the General Orders is a person the board has allotted quota or issued a permit — a buyer steps into that licensing and the board's quota-transfer approval as closing conditions. https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Q: What happens to a sale during an avian influenza outbreak?** HPAI is reportable; in a CFIA primary control zone, permits are required to move birds, eggs and by-products, and on a quarantined farm nothing bird-related enters or leaves without CFIA permission — which can delay placements, showings and completion. Confirm the property's status before fixing a completion date. https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected
- **Q: How big can the farmhouse be, and can I add a second home?** A principal residence built without an ALC application must be 500 m² or less; one additional residence is permitted without an application at 90 m² or less (parcels up to 40 ha) or 186 m² or less (over 40 ha), subject to local bylaws. Those are houses on one title — neither is a condo unit, and neither can be sold separately without the subdivision gate in Section 3. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: What due diligence replaces the strata document package here?** Board and land diligence: the quota package and the seller's good standing with the board (the board approves the transfer and checks standing and cycle allocation), the ALR status and barn position, and the CFIA/biosecurity status of the operation. The research file's selling tip is the mirror image: sellers should keep levies and compliance current before listing, because the board checks standing on the transfer. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Abbotsford adopted a new Official Community Plan in December 2025:** the Abbotsforward 2050 OCP is the current plan and the 2016 OCP is not current — any poultry-farm analysis in Abbotsford that cites the 2016 OCP as the governing plan is citing a superseded document. The City also confirmed in June 2024 that it adopted the provincial SSMUH and Transit-Oriented Areas zoning amendments (the bylaw number 3560-2024 attached to those amendments in one brief is UNCONFIRMED on City pages). https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/city-begins-implementing-updated-official-community-plan and https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/making-progress-housing-abbotsford
- **The topic pages' own listings prove the zero:** at research time, every listing behind the poultry pages was an Abbotsford farm or residential acreage property — Bradner (possible poultry use), Sumas Prairie (poultry/storage barn among its barns) and Abbotsford West (commercial poultry operation, quota not included). No condo or strata property fed the pages at all. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html (research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-2/poultry-farms.md)
- **Quota is measured three different ways in one topic:** broiler quota in kilograms of live weight per cycle, turkey quota in pounds of turkey meat per specified period, and hatching-egg quota in hen units (mainstream) or hatched chicks (specialty). A "quota included" line means nothing until the sector and the unit are named. https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf and https://bcbhec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2022-Annual-Report.pdf and https://bcturkey.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/174_66.pdf
- **Chilliwack's bylaw shows how municipal zoning treats poultry off the ALR point:** Chilliwack Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000 defines POULTRY as any domesticated fowl including chickens, turkeys, ducks, geese and swans, and lists General Agriculture as a permitted principal use in its AL, AU and AS zones — a reminder that the municipal layer is parcel-by-parcel across the valley, while the ALR barn protection in B.C. Reg. 30/2019 is province-wide. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- **Biosecurity zones are part of the property, in practice:** BC Gov guidance structures a poultry farm around a Controlled Access Zone for the production area and a Restricted Access Zone where birds are housed, with steps such as allowing only essential visitors and restricting contact between poultry and wild birds — showings of an operating poultry farm are a biosecurity event, not a normal open house. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/ai_enhanced_biosecurity_for_small_scale_poultry_producers.pdf
