## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **FTHB GST/HST Rebate — enactment (CENTRAL to this page):** amendments to Part IX of the Excise Tax Act in Bill C-4 introduced the FTHB GST/HST rebate, and Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 — the date the live page states; secondary sources saying March 12 are superseded. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)
- **FTHB rebate — amount and thresholds:** together with the GST/HST new housing rebate, the FTHB rebate provides 100% of the GST (or the federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated single unit residential complex or residential condominium unit valued up to $1 million; between $1 million and $1.5 million the rebate is gradually reduced; and no FTHB rebate is available at $1.5 million or more. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **FTHB rebate — maximum in dollars:** eligible individuals could get a rebate of up to $50,000 — consistent with 100% of the 5% GST on a $1 million home in BC, as the live page's savings table works it. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)
- **Worked examples — FTHB rebate, inputs stated (from the page's own table, arithmetic checked against the verified thresholds):** input price $500,000 at 5% GST = $25,000 GST, $25,000 rebate, $0 net GST; input $750,000 = $37,500 GST, $37,500 rebate, $0 net; input $1,000,000 = $50,000 GST, $50,000 rebate, $0 net; input $1,500,000 or above = $75,000+ GST, $0 rebate, full GST payable. https://jaredgibbons.ca/gst-rebate-in-bc.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Worked example — the page's $1.25 million row, flagged:** input $1,250,000 = $62,500 GST, about $25,000 rebate, about $37,500 net on the page's table — but that row depends on the page's "phases out on a straight-line basis" wording (a $1.25 million home qualifying for a 50% rebate, up to $25,000 back), and the straight-line formula itself was not stated on the CRA pages opened: CRA says only that the rebate is "gradually reduced" between $1 million and $1.5 million, so the mechanism is UNVERIFIED and the $1.25 million row is page copy, not a verified calculation. https://jaredgibbons.ca/gst-rebate-in-bc.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST New Housing Rebate (the pre-existing rebate):** generally 36% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) paid on a newly constructed or substantially renovated single unit residential complex or condominium unit, to a maximum rebate of $6,300; for homes with a purchase price or fair market value between $350,000 and $450,000 the rebate is gradually reduced, and no rebate is available at $450,000 or more. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Worked example — New Housing Rebate, inputs stated:** input price $300,000 (at or below the $350,000 phase-out start) at 5% GST = $15,000 GST; 36% of that GST is $5,400, which is under the $6,300 maximum, so the rebate on those inputs is $5,400 and the net GST is $9,600 — arithmetic from the verified 36% / $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 figures; inside the $350,000–$450,000 band the rebate is gradually reduced by a calculation this file does not state, because no official page opened states the reduction formula. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)
- **Do not double-count the old rebate:** the long-standing new housing rebate alone is worth at most $6,300 — the $50,000 figure exists only because the FTHB rebate tops the combined recovery up to 100% for qualifying first-time buyers. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)
- **First-time buyer definition:** the individual must be at least 18 years old, be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and not have lived in a home — in or outside Canada — that they or their spouse or common-law partner owned or jointly owned as a primary place of residence at any time in the calendar year of the purchase or in the preceding four calendar years. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property and timing conditions:** the buyer must meet the existing new housing rebate's eligibility criteria (or would meet them if that rebate's ceiling were $1.5 million instead of $450,000); must have entered a purchase and sale agreement with a builder on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (for an owner-built home, construction must begin on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031); construction must begin before 2031 and be substantially completed before 2036; the buyer must be the first to occupy the home as a place of residence after substantial completion; and the buyer must not have previously applied for an FTHB rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Claiming:** the application time limit is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction; a builder can credit the rebate against the amount payable when ownership transfers; where the builder does not credit it, the buyer applies directly to the CRA through a CRA account or by mailing Form GST190 (houses purchased from a builder), and an owner-builder who started construction on or after March 20, 2025 applies directly using Form GST191. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)
- **Two live-page limitation details recorded as page statements only:** the bar where a spouse or common-law partner has already claimed the rebate, and the assignment-sales rule that the original purchase agreement must date from on or after March 20, 2025 — neither was stated on the CRA pages opened, so both are UNVERIFIED in this file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/gst-rebate-in-bc.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million; the tax is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000 (the newly built thresholds as verified in the program's batch-6 pass and not re-opened for this page, stated plainly as the research file does). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)
- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. Resales carry no GST, so this page's rebates never apply to a Form B purchase — the Form B matters here only as the line between a resale condo (no GST, no rebate) and a new condo (GST, possibly rebated). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote; and since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — any directory or bylaw line saying "rentals not allowed" is void for long-term rentals; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 and Regulation 7.01 allow only a 55-or-older strata residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Presales (REDMA):** where a presale condo is the purchase, REDMA s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18 — a separate clock from the rebate's March 20, 2025 agreement-date test, and both must be checked on the same contract. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much GST can a first-time buyer in BC get back on a new condo?** Up to $50,000: together with the new housing rebate, the FTHB GST/HST rebate returns 100% of the GST on a new or substantially renovated home (including a residential condominium unit) valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Who counts as a first-time home buyer for this rebate?** An individual who is at least 18, is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and has not lived in a home that they or their spouse or common-law partner owned or jointly owned as a primary place of residence in the calendar year of purchase or in the preceding four calendar years. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: My presale was signed before March 20, 2025 — do I still qualify?** On the verified conditions, no: eligibility turns on a builder agreement entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, with construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036 — the signing date is checked before the price. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)
- **Q: What happened to the old GST new housing rebate?** It still exists on its own terms: generally 36% of the GST paid, to a maximum of $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and unavailable at $450,000 or more — the FTHB rebate is the separate top-up that takes a qualifying first-time buyer's combined recovery to 100%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Show me the math on a $750,000 new condo.** Inputs: price $750,000, GST rate 5% — GST payable $37,500; the home is valued at or under $1 million, so the combined FTHB + new housing rebate returns 100% of the GST, $37,500, for $0 net GST, assuming every buyer and timing condition is met. https://jaredgibbons.ca/gst-rebate-in-bc.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: And on a $1.25 million new home — is the rebate exactly $25,000?** That figure cannot be confirmed from the official pages opened: the page's table works it as about $25,000 back on $62,500 of GST using a straight-line phase-out, but CRA News No. 122 says only that the rebate is "gradually reduced" between $1 million and $1.5 million, so the $25,000 row is UNVERIFIED as a calculation even though the endpoints (100% at $1M, none at $1.5M) are verified. https://jaredgibbons.ca/gst-rebate-in-bc.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: How and when do I claim the FTHB rebate?** Usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction: a builder can credit the rebate at closing and submits the jointly completed Form GST190, or you apply directly to the CRA through a CRA account or by mailing Form GST190 — and Form GST191 for an owner-built home. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
- **Q: Can I combine the GST rebate with BC's property transfer tax exemption?** Yes — they are separate federal and provincial programs: the BC first-time program exempts PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price where the fair market value is $835,000 or less, with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000, and nothing in either program's conditions as read bars claiming both. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)
- **Q: Does a resale condo qualify for any GST rebate?** No — resale homes are not subject to GST, as the page itself notes, so there is no GST to rebate; the rebates on this page are for newly built or substantially renovated homes only. https://jaredgibbons.ca/gst-rebate-in-bc.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)
- **Q: Are the Home Buyers' Plan and tax-credit numbers on the page confirmed?** No — the page's HBP figures and its $10,000 / up-to-$1,500 tax-credit figures are UNVERIFIED site copy in the research file, because the CRA HBP page failed to load in the research pass (HTTP 403) and the credit amount was not re-read on a CRA page opened in that pass; the research file carries both as questions for Jared. https://jaredgibbons.ca/gst-rebate-in-bc.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/gst-rebate-in-bc.md)

- **A co-op share can qualify for this rebate even though it is not a condo.** CRA News No. 122 states the FTHB rebate is also generally available on a share in a co-operative housing corporation entitling the buyer to occupy a new unit, and on a new home bought on land leased from the builder — two tenure forms a buyer could easily assume are excluded. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **The buyer must be the first person to live in the home.** Among the verified conditions: the buyer must be the first to occupy the home as a place of residence after substantial completion, and must not have previously applied for an FTHB rebate — a new home that someone else occupied first is outside the rebate on the conditions as read. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
