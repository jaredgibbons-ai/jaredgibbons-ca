## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. For a condo seller, this is the document package the buyer's side will price from — a selling page that never mentions it still leads to a sale governed by it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote; and since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — any directory or bylaw line saying "rentals not allowed" is void for long-term rentals; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 and Regulation 7.01 allow only a 55-or-older strata residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. No building on this page is recorded as 55+ — the page names no building. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **GST and rebates:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — most sales this page describes are resales and carry no GST, apart from GST the page itself lists on legal fees in its costs section. First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) — 100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements March 20, 2025–2031; GST New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/selling.md)
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. PTT is a buyer's tax, stated in this standard block for completeness; the seller-side taxes this page actually raises are the BC home flipping tax and GST on legal fees, below and in Section 5. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** where a seller is assigning or a developer-contract purchase is in the chain, REDMA s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18 — distinct from the BC Home Buyer Rescission Period below, which is the cooling-off rule this page actually describes. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **HBP / FHSA / CMHC:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC published premium range 0.60%–4.50% — buy-side programs, stated in this standard block; none is raised on the selling page itself. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **The regulator's framework (BCFSA, opened in the research pass):** in BC a licensed real estate professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; disclosure of whether the professional can represent the consumer, the duties owed, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA must happen at the outset of dealings, and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/selling.md)
- **Commission on this page:** the page states no commission rate, amount or structure anywhere, so what a seller pays Jared's brokerage is UNVERIFIED from this page — under the BCFSA framework above, commission and fee information is given to the consumer in the listing conversation, off-page. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/selling.md)
- **Cooling-off / rescission, verified at the regulator:** if a buyer rescinds an accepted offer under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining balance of the deposit to the buyer, with no separate release form signed by either party; the page's own statement matches — a cancelling buyer must tell the seller in writing and pay 0.25% of the offer price. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/selling.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html
- **Deposits generally (BCFSA):** a buyer's deposit is usually held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder, a neutral party; where subject clauses are not removed, the deposit is not automatically returned and both parties must sign a release, except where the buyer exercises the right of rescission. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/selling.md)
- **BC home flipping tax, verified on the Province page the selling page links to:** the tax applies to profit from selling a BC property (including presale contracts) owned for less than 730 days, under the Residential Property (Short-Term Holding) Profit Tax Act effective January 1, 2025; the rate is 20% of net taxable income for a property disposed of within 365 days and gradually decreases over the next 365 days, with no tax at 730 days; a return must be filed within 90 days of the sale where the seller is subject to the tax or an exemption applies only after filing. The page's shorter wording — "20% of the profit," "drops to zero by day 730," "a return may be due within 90 days" — is a simplification of "net taxable income" and the filing conditions as the Province states them. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/bc-home-flipping-tax (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/selling.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html
