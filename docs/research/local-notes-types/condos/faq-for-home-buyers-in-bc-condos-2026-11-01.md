## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

This section is the full verified buyer frame for this page: the strata block first (the diligence a condo buyer actually runs), then the tax and program frame exactly as the research file verifies it, with every page-only figure flagged in-entry.

- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. The page's subject list — financing, inspection, property disclosure statement, title search, strata documents including the depreciation report — is the checklist to clear before subject removal, because after removal the deal is firm and the deposit is at risk. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md)
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote; and since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — any directory or older bylaw line saying "rentals not allowed" is void for long-term rentals and must be flagged void wherever it appears; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 and Regulation 7.01 allow only a 55-or-older strata residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City zoning / OCP:** no single city zoning or OCP bylaw applies to a province-wide FAQ page, and none is stated — the zoning and OCP bylaw for a specific condo is the one named on that area's own file. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md)
- **GST — FTHB GST/HST Rebate (verified, CRA):** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 (secondary sources saying March 12 are superseded; CRA governs); together with the GST/HST new housing rebate it provides 100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more — a maximum of up to $50,000, for agreements March 20, 2025–2031. The page's program bullet ("save up to $50,000 in GST on new homes up to $1M") matches this verified position. 5% GST applies to newly built and presale homes and not to resale homes, as the page states. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md)
- **GST New Housing Rebate (verified, CRA):** generally 36% of the GST paid, to a maximum rebate of $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and not available at $450,000 or more. The live FAQ page does not state the old rebate's own numbers; they are recorded here so the rebuild can add them accurately. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md)
- **Property Transfer Tax (verified, Province):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million; the tax is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office, and PTT cannot be blended into the mortgage, as the page correctly notes. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: a qualifying buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 where the property is used only as a principal residence, has a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), is 0.5 hectares or smaller and contains only residential improvements; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption (standard block): full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md)
- **Down payment and mortgage insurance (verified, CMHC):** mortgage loan insurance is required when a down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more (where insurance is not available); CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount. The page's down-payment tiers and its $750,000 worked example ($25,000 + $25,000 = $50,000 minimum) match the verified tiers. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md)
- **HBP / FHSA — standard-block figures with the research position attached:** the standard block for this program is Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 and FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. For THIS page the research file holds both page figures UNCONFIRMED: the CRA Home Buyers' Plan page returned HTTP 403 in this program and no HBP figure is adopted as verified, and the CRA FHSA main page read in the sibling passes verifies only the $8,000 first-year participation room, not a lifetime limit. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What is a subject in a BC purchase contract?** A condition that must be met before the deal becomes firm — the live page lists financing, inspection, property disclosure statement, title search and strata document review as the common subjects, completed during a negotiable subject-removal period; the page's "typically 7 days" norm is practitioner copy and is UNCONFIRMED as a stated norm. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html
- **Q: How much property transfer tax will I pay, and what does a first-time buyer save?** At the general rates: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. A qualifying first-time buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 of the price at a fair market value of $835,000 or less, with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000. Test a target price against $835,000 first. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md)
- **Q: Do I pay GST on a home purchase in BC?** On a new build or presale, yes — 5% at completion; resale homes are generally exempt. A qualifying first-time buyer can recover up to $50,000 through the FTHB GST/HST rebate (100% of the GST to $1 million, gradually reduced to $1.5 million, none at $1.5 million or more), and the pre-existing new housing rebate alone returns 36% of the GST to a maximum of $6,300, phasing out between $350,000 and $450,000. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md)
- **Q: What is the minimum down payment, and when is mortgage insurance required?** 5% on homes of $500,000 or less; 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999; and 20% at $1,500,000 or more. Below 20% down, mortgage loan insurance is required, and CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md)
- **Q: Can I combine the Home Buyers' Plan and the FHSA, and how much can I take out?** The CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. The amounts are a different matter in this file: the page's HBP figure ($60,000) and FHSA figure ($40,000) are UNCONFIRMED — the CRA HBP page returned 403 in this program and the CRA FHSA main page verifies only the $8,000 first-year room — so confirm both limits with the CRA or a lender before planning around them. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html
- **Q: What is the difference between the deposit and the down payment?** As the page's structured FAQ frames it, the deposit is the money that secures the accepted offer and is at risk once subjects are removed, while the down payment is the buyer's total equity contribution at completion, of which the deposit forms part — the page's "typically around 5%" deposit figure and its timing norm are practitioner copy, UNCONFIRMED as stated norms; the verified down-payment tiers are the CMHC tiers in Section 3. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html
- **Q: Buying a resale condo — what strata documents matter most?** The Form B package: strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and insurance summary, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached — provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. Since July 1, 2024 a 5+-lot strata's depreciation report must be refreshed every five years, so check its date, not just its existence. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Q: The bylaws say rentals are not allowed — can I rent the condo out?** Long-term, yes — rental-restriction bylaws have been void province-wide since November 24, 2022, whatever the older bylaw text says. Short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw, and a 55+ age bylaw (SPA ss.123.1/123.2, Reg 7.01) can still bind who resides in the unit. Confirm the current registered bylaws on the Form B package. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: I am writing subject-free — does the rescission period protect me?** Do not rely on the page's summary alone: the page states a 3-business-day rescission period with a 0.25% fee, but that statement is UNCONFIRMED in this file — no BCFSA source was opened — so confirm the current rescission rules with a REALTOR® or lawyer before writing without subjects. A presale developer contract is a different clock again: REDMA s. 21 gives 7 days. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md) https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html
- **Q: What closing costs should I budget beyond the down payment?** The page's rule of thumb is closing costs of 1.5%–4% of the purchase price — practitioner copy, UNCONFIRMED as a stated norm. What is verified is the largest single item's math: PTT at the general rates is $2,000 on the first $200,000 plus 2% on the portion to $2,000,000, before any exemption, and PTT cash must be kept separate because it cannot be blended into the mortgage. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-18/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.md)
