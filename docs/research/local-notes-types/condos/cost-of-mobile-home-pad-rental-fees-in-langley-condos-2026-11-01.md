## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**Zero strata-condo stock in this topic, stated plainly at the top: a manufactured home in a Langley park is not a condo. There is no strata corporation, no Form B, no contingency reserve fund and no depreciation report for a pad-rental home — the buyer owns a manufactured home and rents the pad under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA). The parks below are documented as tenure traps — product a buyer can mistake for strata ownership — not as condo buildings, and none is counted as an apartment condo. Count line: 3 verified 55+ pad-rental parks named; 1 co-operative excluded with its reason; 2 parks held UNCONFIRMED and never counted; verified pad-rent dollar figures published in this file: 0.**

### The core distinction — park rule vs strata bylaw

- In a strata, an age restriction is a registered bylaw: BC law allows only a 55-or-older residency bylaw (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2), it is disclosed on the Form B, and the May 1, 2023 exemptions for younger spouses/partners and defined children apply to it. In a manufactured home park, the 55+ rule is a **park rule** set by the park owner under the MHPTA tenancy — it is not an SPA bylaw, no Form B discloses it, and the SPA exemptions do not govern it; the written park rules, which the seller must hand to the proposed buyer before assignment is even requested, are the document that governs. The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Regulation (B.C. Reg. 481/2003) expressly recognises a park reserved for tenants who have reached 55 and allows a landlord to refuse an assignment where the purchaser does not meet the park's age requirement. Treat any "55+ strata" language in a park-home listing as a category error. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

Read this section as "rules a condo buyer expects, and what actually applies instead" — the Strata Property Act block is stated because buyers arrive with condo assumptions, and each item names the manufactured-home rule that replaces it. The MHPTA regime is the substance of this file.

- **Pad rent increases — the verified limits (the core rule of this page).** Landlords in manufactured home parks can increase rent only with 3 full months' notice, at most once every 12 months, and not above the yearly rent increase limit, plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park); the landlord may not round up, may not carry forward an unused portion of a prior year's limit, and if proper notice was not given the increase takes effect on the earliest date that would comply. Further increases only by Residential Tenancy Branch order. The limits: **2026 = 2.3% plus the proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% plus the proportional amount.** Freshly re-verified against the Province's page in this pass. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Worked example (illustrative inputs, stated — NOT a Langley park's rent):** a pad rent of $800 per month, with an increase taking effect in 2026. The percentage component is 2.3% × $800 = $18.40, so the rent may rise to $818.40 per month **plus** the proportional amount — the park's own change in local government levies plus regulated park utility fees, divided by that park's site count. The proportional amount cannot be computed from any published Langley figure, because no official source publishes a park's levy/utility change or site count for this calculation; it is the figure to ask the specific park for. The $800 input is an arithmetic illustration only — no official source states it as any park's rent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Above-limit increases need an order, not a notice.** A landlord may apply to the Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB Form 52) for an additional increase for financial loss from increased operating or financing costs, or for substantial and essential repairs or renovations that are reasonable, necessary and not expected to recur. Tenants do not have to pay an increase above the legal amount and may deduct an overpayment from future rent or apply for dispute resolution. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment on a sale — what happens to the pad rent when a Langley park home is bought.** Assignment of the site tenancy permanently transfers the pad tenancy to the buyer; under MHPTA s. 28 a tenant may assign only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it; before asking, the seller must give the proposed buyer written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent; and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. Landlords and tenants in a manufactured home park must have a written tenancy agreement for the site. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s. 42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute the notice within 15 days, may in defined circumstances apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Registration is what makes the sale effective, and the CSA label is a sale gate.** In BC, the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered with the Manufactured Home Registry, through a lawyer or notary filing the original signed and witnessed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership forms. Manufactured homes include modular and mobile homes; the CSA label states the standard — modular homes under A-277, mobile homes under Z-240. A home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal; a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification. A permit to move a home is issued by the Registry, a tax certificate for transport purposes is required (with exceptions including a move to a different pad within the same park), and the transport permit expires 30 days after issue. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Taxes on a home in a park.** The Province states a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value will not include the value of the land if the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/cost-of-mobile-home-pad-rental-fees-in-langley.md)
- **Form B — there is none.** A Form B Information Certificate (Strata Property Act s. 59) exists only for strata lots. The park-home equivalents are: the written park rules and the relevant parts of the site tenancy agreement (which the seller must give the proposed buyer before asking the landlord to consent to assignment), the home's Manufactured Home Registry record, and its CSA label status. For contrast, the strata regime this topic does NOT carry: depreciation reports every five years for stratas with five or more lots since July 1, 2024, and a minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023 — both bind strata corporations only; a park has no depreciation report and no CRF to inspect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void — a strata rule, not a park rule.** Since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw. That voiding is a Strata Property Act change; it does not rewrite a park's rules. Fernridge Park's listing-stated "no rentals" position, for example, is a park-rule question governed by the park rules and the site tenancy agreement — read them, do not import the strata rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ — park rule vs SPA bylaw.** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw in a strata, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. A park's 55+ restriction (Cedar Creek Estates, Fernridge Park, Pineridge) is a park rule under the MHPTA, set in the written park rules — the SPA exemption list does not govern it, and the park's own wording decides who may live in the home. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **What pad rent includes is park-by-park: UNCONFIRMED as a general Langley statement.** Inclusions (utilities, garbage, water/sewer, amenities) are set in the specific park's written site tenancy agreement; the live page's "usually includes / usually does not include" lists, and its claim that 55+ parks "often cost less," are site copy, not an official source. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/cost-of-mobile-home-pad-rental-fees-in-langley.md)
- **GST and purchase taxes.** 5% GST applies to new construction, not ordinary resales; how GST and Property Transfer Tax apply to a specific manufactured-home purchase depends on whether land transfers with the home (a park unit transfers no land) — no source in the research file settles the treatment for a specific unit: UNCONFIRMED per unit; take conveyancing advice before writing. For qualifying new homes generally: the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (CRA News No. 122: Royal Assent March 13, 2026); the GST New Housing Rebate returns 36% to a $6,300 maximum across $350,000–$450,000. BC Property Transfer Tax generally runs 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million and 3% above, with a First-Time Home Buyers' exemption (full to $835K FMV, partial to $860K) and a Newly Built exemption (full $1.1M, gone $1.15M) where land is acquired. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. Park-home resales are not REDMA presales — stated so a buyer does not assume a 7-day rescission exists on a park purchase; subject periods are contractual here. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60%–4.50% of the total mortgage amount. Park-home financing is lender-specific chattel-style lending in many cases — confirm financing with the lender and the park before subjects, never from the listing alone. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much is pad rent in Langley?** No official source publishes a Langley pad-rent figure, average or range, so no figure is stated in this file — and the $650–$950 range in the live page's copy is UNVERIFIED site copy, not an official statistic. A pad rent is set park by park in the written Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement; the figure that matters is the specific park's current rent, obtained from the park and the tenancy agreement before an offer. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/cost-of-mobile-home-pad-rental-fees-in-langley.md)
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up each year?** Up to the yearly limit set by the Residential Tenancy Branch: for manufactured home site tenancies the 2026 limit is 2.3% plus a proportional amount, and the 2027 limit is 2.2% plus a proportional amount. The proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of sites in the park. Increases require 3 full months' notice and can occur only once every 12 months; anything above the limit requires an RTB order. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does the park raise my pad rent when I buy the home?** No. On assignment of the site tenancy under s. 28 of the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same and the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase does not change; the landlord's prior written consent is required (or deemed given under the regulations) and the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Q: Which Langley parks are actually 55+ pad-rental parks?** Three verified, all in Brookswood in the Township: Cedar Creek Estates (3031 200 St), Fernridge Park (20071 24 Ave) and Pineridge (2270 196 St). Pioneer Park in Aldergrove is 55+ but is a co-operative, not a pad-rental park. Cedar Lane Park and Deer Creek Estates are held UNCONFIRMED in this file — a directory groups both with its 55+ parks, but neither is in the verified roster and no park-operator source was obtained for either. Sources: Section 2 above.

That is a park rule under tenancy law — the strata exemptions for younger spouses, caregivers and legacy residents do not automatically apply, so ask the park for its rule in writing. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Q: What does pad rent include in a Langley park?** UNCONFIRMED as a general statement — inclusions are set park by park in the written site tenancy agreement. Do not budget from a generic "usually includes" list; read the specific agreement for utilities, garbage, water/sewer and amenity charges before writing. ## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **There is no Langley pad-rent average to look up — and a page that prints one has not sourced it.** Neither the Province, the Township, the City nor SnapStats publishes a park-level or average pad rent for Langley; SnapStats publishes no manufactured-home category and no rent figure of any kind. The only official "number" in this topic is the increase formula. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/cost-of-mobile-home-pad-rental-fees-in-langley.md)
- **The proportional amount makes two parks' increases differ in the same year.** It is the park's own levy plus regulated-utility change divided by that park's own site count — so the same 2.3% headline limit for 2026 produces a different dollar increase at Cedar Creek Estates than at Fernridge Park or Pineridge. Ask the specific park for its last proportional-amount calculation. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **All of Langley's verified 55+ pad-rental parks are in one Township pocket.** Cedar Creek Estates, Fernridge Park and Pineridge are all in Brookswood; no verified 55+ pad-rental park in Langley City, Walnut Grove, Willoughby or Fort Langley appears in the roster. A buyer fixed on a 55+ park in Langley is, in practice, shopping Brookswood — or the Aldergrove co-op. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **A transfer does not trigger a market re-pricing of the pad.** Unlike a strata purchase, where fees are whatever the current budget says, an MHPTA assignment carries the existing pad rent and the existing next-increase date to the new tenant — the seller's rent history is part of what a buyer is acquiring, and it is documented in the tenancy agreement the seller must share before consent is sought. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **The CSA label is a hard gate, not a detail.** A manufactured home without its original legible CSA decal (Z-240 mobile, A-277 modular) cannot be sold until Technical Safety BC re-certification — label status is a price and timeline fact on every Langley park purchase, and it is the dividing line between renovated, readily saleable park homes and original-condition fixer product. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **"55+" in a Langley park and "55+" in a Langley strata are different legal objects.** The park rule sits in tenancy regulation and park rules and can support refusing an assignment; the strata rule sits in a registered bylaw under ss. 123.1/123.2 with Regulation 7.01 exemptions. A couple who qualify to live in a Parkway Estates strata unit on a spousal exemption have no automatic equivalent right in a Brookswood park. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Two Brookswood parks carry directory 55+ labels this program could not verify.** Cedar Lane Park (2305 200 St) and Deer Creek Estates (2315 198 St) both appear in a directory's 55+ grouping, and both are held UNCONFIRMED here — Cedar Lane for want of a park-operator source despite supportive listings, Deer Creek because its listing evidence says only "adult oriented" and a co-op association name raises a tenure question. A directory label is not a park rule. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Park names drift between sources; addresses do not.** The 200 Street park appears as Cedar Creek Estates and Cedar Creek Manufactured Home Park, and Cedar Lane appears as Cedar Lane Park and Cedar Lane Estates in listing sheets. Search and verify by street address. (Roster source: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/langley-mobile-home-communities-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md)
