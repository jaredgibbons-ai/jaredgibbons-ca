- Terminology this file uses exactly: in BC a "court-ordered sale" is a sale conducted under a court order inside a foreclosure proceeding, not a separate process, and almost every BC foreclosure sale is "subject to court approval" because the sale contract is conditional on the court approving it. "Bank foreclosure" is a misnomer in BC — sales run through the court, not the bank. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- The governing rules are provincial: the Supreme Court Civil Rules (notably Rule 21-7 on foreclosure proceedings and Rule 13-5 on court-ordered sales), the Law and Equity Act, and the Land Title Act's reference to the order nisi. The process facts in this file are the BC facts; the Langley layer is the condo stock in Section 2 and the court-file check in Section 4. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

### The court layer (from the research file's official sources)

- **How a foreclosure starts:** a proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property, or for redemption, must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7(1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Order nisi and the redemption period:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption — Rule 21-7(5)(b). The Land Title Act refers to the court's order in a foreclosure as an "order nisi" (s. 271(1)). The Rules state no fixed length for the redemption period — it is the period the court fixes — and interest is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act, s. 19). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol33/consol33/96250_18 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Final order and applying for a sale:** in default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7(6); a party may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7(7). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5(3). The court's directions may appoint who has conduct, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of value — Rule 13-5(4). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Court approval of your purchase:** the manner of sale the court fixes may be sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5(4)(b); the person with conduct may apply, even before the redemption period expires, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7(9); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in Form 60 — Rule 13-5(6). The vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Competing bids:** the court's directions may define "the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids" — Rule 13-5(4)(d); the Supreme Court's practice direction PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings," governs practice in these proceedings. The timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66 were not retrieved from the court's website for the research file — UNVERIFIED beyond the rule text. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Where it is filed and where to read it:** unless the court otherwise orders, a foreclosure proceeding is commenced at the Supreme Court registry in the municipality where the land is located, if there is one there, and otherwise at a registry in the judicial district in which the land is located (Law and Equity Act, s. 21(2)). Which registry serves Langley as a stated mapping is UNVERIFIED. Court Services Online eSearch is open to anyone with Internet access and shows the file number, registry, party names and filed documents for Supreme Court civil files, class "H - Foreclosure." https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01 and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do

### The strata layer

- **Form B — the limit that matters most in a court sale:** in an ordinary resale, an owner, purchaser or authorized person may request a Form B Information Certificate and the strata corporation must provide it within 7 days (maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page), disclosing fees, amounts owed, approved levies, unfiled bylaw amendments, proceedings, parking/storage allocations and insurance and electrical-planning summaries. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Form B in a court-ordered sale — UNCONFIRMED as a general rule:** no official source read for this file states that a court-ordered sale will produce a full Form B and strata document package, or that it will not. The seller in a court sale is the person with conduct of sale under court directions (Rule 13-5(3)), not the strata-lot owner in the ordinary course, and the conditions of sale are the court-settled particulars (Rule 13-5(4)) — so a buyer must not assume the usual document package will be available, complete or current. What documents a specific court sale offers is a fact of that sale's conditions — read them, and treat any general claim either way as UNCONFIRMED. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Form F is a separate, litigated point:** published case commentary on BC court-ordered strata sales turns on the Form F Certificate of Payment (Strata Property Act s. 115) — whether a strata must deliver a Form F where fines are outstanding was decided differently on different facts in Toronto Dominion Bank v. Madson (2001) and Canada Trustco Mortgage Co. v. Gies (2001), turning on the terms of the sale order and whether the strata appeared and objected. That is secondary legal commentary, not a rule a buyer can rely on for a specific file — the sale order's own terms govern. https://www.cwilson.com/fine-and-form-fs/
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years with no deferral vote, and since November 1, 2023 stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF annually. All three verified Langley City apartment buildings (1988–1990) are in the age band where those reports drive levy risk — and in a court sale, where the document package may be thin (above), the current depreciation report is the document to chase first. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rentals and age:** long-term rental-restriction bylaws are void since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44); both Parkway buildings carry directory lines saying "rentals not allowed," and those lines are void since that date and must not be read as current law. The only valid age bylaw is 55+ (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2, Regulation 7.01) — and 55+ is exactly the restriction the Parkway buildings, Glenmont Park and Huntsfield Green are published with, so in a court sale of a unit in those buildings the age bylaw, unlike the rental line, can still bind the buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Municipal layer:** Township of Langley Zoning Bylaw No. 2500 and its SSMUH Bylaw 6020 (adopted November 18, 2024: 3 units on lots up to 280 m², 4 units on lots under 4,050 m², lots over 4,050 m² ineligible, no 6-unit tier) govern Township property; Langley City Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300 governs City property and defines a secondary suite as the lesser of 100 m² and 40% of the home. Neither bylaw changes the court process — they matter only to what a buyer could later do with the lot, and for a strata condo lot the strata bylaws matter more. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/small-scale-multi-unit-housing.aspx (City suite definition as named in this file's brief; not re-read from the bylaw text — UNCONFIRMED beyond the brief.)

### Taxes on a court-ordered condo purchase

- **PTT and GST treatment of court-ordered sales specifically: UNCONFIRMED.** No official source read for this file states a special Property Transfer Tax or GST rule for a purchase through a court-ordered sale, and this file does not invent one. The general rules a buyer's lawyer and notary will apply are: BC PTT at 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million, 3% to $3 million, 5% above, with the First-Time Home Buyers' Program (no PTT on the first $500,000 at $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000) and the Newly Built Home Exemption (full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000); GST at 5% on new housing and not on resales; the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4; 100% of GST to $1 million, maximum $50,000, phasing out to $1.5 million; agreements March 20, 2025–2031; Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA Excise and GST/HST News No. 122); the GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000); Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.60%–4.50%. Whether a court vesting order changes any of that for a given purchase is a question for the buyer's lawyer on the specific file — UNCONFIRMED here. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/bc-property-transfer-tax-exemptions-guide/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is a court-ordered condo sale just a normal purchase with a discount?** No. The contract is conditional on court approval where the court has fixed sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" (Rule 13-5(4)(b)), the seller is the person with conduct of sale selling under court-settled conditions (Rule 13-5(3)–(4)), and there is no published statistic in any official source for a "typical discount" in Langley — any discount claim is UNCONFIRMED. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: Can my accepted offer still lose?** Yes, two ways the rules themselves create: the person with conduct may apply to confirm a sale even before the redemption period expires (Rule 21-7(9)), but until the confirming order is made the sale is not final; and the court's directions may define rights to bid, make offers or meet bids (Rule 13-5(4)(d)), so a competing bid at the approval stage is a real possibility in this process. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can the owner still save the condo after I offer?** The redemption right runs until the process ends it: the court fixes a redemption period in the order nisi (Rule 21-7(5)(b)), interest runs to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act, s. 19), and only in default of payment within that period may a final order of foreclosure be granted (Rule 21-7(6)). Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Q: Will I get the full strata documents — Form B, minutes, depreciation report?** UNCONFIRMED as a general promise. In an ordinary resale the strata must provide a Form B within 7 days of a proper request; no official source read for this file guarantees a court sale will produce a full package, and the conditions of sale are court-settled particulars that may differ from a standard contract. Price the information risk, and read the conditions of sale line by line with a lawyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: How do I check the court file myself?** Court Services Online eSearch is open to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry location, party names and the list of filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." The petition, the amount certified due and the orders made are the facts of the deal. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do
- **Q: Is the title clean if I win?** That is what the confirming order does: on the Rule 21-7(9) application the court may order the sale confirmed, direct the disposition of the proceeds and vest title in the purchaser; the result of the sale is certified in Form 60 (Rule 13-5(6)). The vesting order, not the listing, is the title-cleaning step. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: The Parkway directory says rentals are banned and it's 55+. Which one binds me?** The rental line does not — rental-restriction bylaws have been void since November 24, 2022. The 55+ line can: a 55-or-older bylaw is the one age bylaw the Strata Property Act still permits (ss. 123.1/123.2). Both Parkway Estates and Parkway II are directory-recorded with age restrictions, pets not allowed and rentals not allowed; read only the age and pet lines as potentially live, and confirm both on the registered bylaws. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/parkway-estates-5360-205-street (building directory, secondary source) and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Do I pay PTT or GST differently because it's a court sale?** UNCONFIRMED — no official source read for this file states a court-sale-specific PTT or GST rule. Budget on the general rules in Section 3 and have a BC real-estate lawyer confirm the treatment on the specific file before the approval hearing, not after. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Is the unit vacant, and can I inspect inside?** Occupancy is a matter of the individual court file, not a published statistic — the research file marks any general statement UNVERIFIED. Inspection and access terms live in the court-settled conditions of sale for that file; if the conditions do not offer an inspection, do not assume one can be added. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 (research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-2/langley-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales.md)
- **Q: How long is the redemption period on the Langley file I'm looking at?** Whatever that file's order says. The Rules state no set length — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b) — so anyone quoting a standard period for a specific Langley file without reading its order is guessing. Read the order on Court Services Online or through counsel. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
