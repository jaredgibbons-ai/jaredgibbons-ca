## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

### Strata Property Act — resale and ownership

- **Form B — Information Certificate (s.59).** On a resale, the strata corporation provides a Form B stating the unit's monthly strata fee, any amount owed, any special levy already approved, the CRF balance, parking and storage entitlements, any judgments/proceedings, and — since April 1, 2023 — a summary of the strata's insurance coverage. A condo card on this page is a summary; the Form B is the disclosure behind it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Depreciation reports.** Effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Strata fees and the CRF.** Since November 1, 2023, strata corporations must contribute a minimum of 10% of the annual operating fund to the contingency reserve fund when approving budgets. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void.** Since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata corporation in BC may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit short-term rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws — 55+ only (ss.123.1 / 123.2).** The only permitted age-restriction bylaw is 55+, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner and defined children. The bylaw governs who may reside, not who may own. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

### Taxes and rebates — purchase

- **GST — resale vs new.** Resale condos are generally exempt from GST; new construction carries 5% GST in BC.
- **FTHB GST Rebate (Bill C-4).** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122) rebates 100% of the GST on a new home valued up to $1M (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5M, for agreements entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST New Housing Rebate (the older rebate, still in place).** 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, phasing out for homes between $350,000 and $450,000.
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — general rates.** 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion to $2M, 3% above $2M, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3M. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **PTT — first-time home buyers' program.** Exempting PTT on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), partial between $835,000 and $860,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **PTT — newly built home exemption.** Full exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), eliminated at $1,150,000. The two BC exemptions are mutually exclusive — elect the larger. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption

### Presales and assignments — REDMA

- **Disclosure and 7-day rescission.** A developer must give the buyer a disclosure statement filed with BCFSA before the contract binds; the buyer may cancel within 7 days after the later of signing the purchase agreement and receiving/acknowledging the disclosure statement, with the deposit refunded from trust. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers

### Financing supports

- **FHSA.** $8,000 of participation room in the first year the account is opened, lifetime $40,000. **HBP.** Up to $60,000 withdrawn from an RRSP for a first home; CRA confirms FHSA and HBP can be combined for the same home. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **CMHC mortgage insurance.** Required when the down payment is under 20% on a home priced $1.5M or less; published premiums run 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained

### Topic-specific: the display/consent framework for listings

- **Who owns the system and who may use it.** At the national level, the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA), a trade association, owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service® ("MLS®") and REALTOR®, and comprises over 100,000 real estate brokers and agents across 100 local real estate boards and associations across Canada. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Membership is the access condition.** Agents and brokers must become members of a local real estate board and CREA in order to access the MLS® system — described by the Competition Bureau as a comprehensive database of current and historical listing information crucial to residential real estate brokerage services — and must adhere to both CREA's rules, such as the REALTOR® Code, and the rules of their respective local real estate boards. In order to use the MLS® system to facilitate real estate transactions, agents must be a member of CREA and agree to comply with CREA's rules. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Display of a seller's information has been a governed, litigated question.** The October 2010 consent agreement between the Commissioner of Competition and CREA — registered with the Competition Tribunal to remain in force for a 10-year period — required CREA not to adopt, maintain or enforce rules denying agents the ability to provide mere postings or discriminating against agents who offer them, and it also encompassed areas such as negotiations regarding the cooperating seller's commission and where a seller's information can be displayed. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Careful reading:** that agreement is cited as the documented federal milestone on MLS® display governance, not as a current BC display rule; its 10-year term as described in the Bureau's submission has run, and what followed it is UNVERIFIED in this file. The operative display rules for this page today are the boards' own MLS®/Reciprocity rules, which are not public documents. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/our-listings.md) https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **The BC regulator's rules that apply to every listing on this page.** In British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed real estate professional in BC must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings:** whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA; dual agency — representing two or more clients in the same transaction whose interests conflict — was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Practical consequence for this page:** a buyer who clicks "Schedule viewing / Email" on one of Jared's listings and is unrepresented is, in BCFSA's framework, a non-client dealing with the seller's professional until a client relationship is formed — the page's contact tools start that disclosure conversation, they do not replace it. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
