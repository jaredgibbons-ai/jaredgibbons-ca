## 1. SCOPE — WHAT THIS TOPIC COVERS

- This is a province-wide topic, not a place: how buying a strata condominium works in British Columbia — the documents, the taxes, the rebates, the strata rules — for any city or sub-area in BC. It replaces a single-area guide the way the underlying research file replaced a place guide with a how-it-works guide. (Research file §1)
- What "condo" means in BC law: a strata lot in a strata plan registered under the Strata Property Act — most often an apartment unit in a low-rise or tower, but the same legal form covers townhouses and bare-land strata lots. The buyer's ownership is the strata lot plus a share of the common property, governed by the strata corporation's bylaws and rules. The Province's strata hub is the starting point for buyers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing
- The two purchase streams this topic covers: (1) **resale** — an existing strata lot bought from its owner, with disclosure through the Form B; and (2) **presale / new construction** — a lot bought from a developer before or during construction, governed by the Real Estate Development Marketing Act (REDMA) disclosure regime instead of the Form B at the contract stage. The rules in Section 3 mark which stream each rule belongs to.
- What this topic does not cover: a specific building's fees, bylaws or condition (those are per-building and live in the area condo notes), freehold houses, co-ops and leasehold tenure except where a rule expressly touches them, and manufactured homes — despite this file's slug, that product is governed by a different Act and registry, as the research file sets out. (Research file §§1, 4)

  - New-home warranty: presale and new strata homes in BC carry the 2-5-10 new home warranty framework referenced in presale guidance. https://www.presalesbc.com/resources/faq/

## 3. THE FULL PROVINCIAL CONDO RULES SET

### Strata Property Act — resale and ownership

- **Form B — Information Certificate (s.59).** On a resale, the strata corporation provides a Form B stating the unit's monthly strata fee, any amount owed, any special levy already approved, the CRF balance, parking and storage entitlements, any judgments/proceedings, and — since April 1, 2023 — a summary of the strata's insurance coverage. The current budget, the bylaws/rules and the most recent depreciation report accompany it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Strata fees and the CRF.** Fees fund the operating fund and the contingency reserve fund. Since November 1, 2023, strata corporations must contribute a minimum of 10% of the annual operating fund to the CRF when approving budgets at the AGM; developers' initial CRF contributions for new developments also rose from 5% to 10% of estimated operating expenses. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Depreciation reports (s.94).** Required for stratas with 5+ lots, renewed at least every five years (see Section 2 for the regional catch-up deadlines). From July 1, 2027, owner-developers of new strata plans must help fund the first depreciation report (lesser of $5,000 + $200 per strata lot, or $30,000), payable by the first AGM. https://pushormitchell.com/2024/05/16/updates-to-strata-requirements-depreciation-reports/
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void.** Since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44, Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022), no strata corporation in BC may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — existing ones are invalid and no amendment is needed. Stratas may still ban or limit **short-term** rentals by bylaw. The developer Rental Disclosure Statement (Form J) regime was eliminated at the same time. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws — 55+ only (ss.123.1 / 123.2).** Since November 24, 2022 the only permitted age-restriction bylaw is 55+. Exemptions under the Strata Property Regulation cover legacy residents and live-in caregivers, and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the resident cared for before they turned 19. The bylaw governs who may **reside**, not who may own: under-55s can own a lot in a 55+ strata but may not be able to live in it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Insurance.** The strata insures the common property and the buildings as originally built; the Form B insurance summary (April 1, 2023 onward) discloses the policy and its deductibles, including water-damage deductibles that can be assessed back to an owner — the reason unit-owner (strata) insurance is standard advice. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals

### Taxes and rebates — purchase

- **GST — resale vs new.** Resale condos are generally exempt from GST; new construction (and substantially renovated homes) carries 5% GST in BC. https://northam-law.com/pre-sale-condo-purchases-in-british-columbia-key-legal-protections-for-buyers/
- **FTHB GST Rebate (Bill C-4).** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate, enacted by Bill C-4 (Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122), works together with the GST/HST new housing rebate to rebate 100% of the GST on a new single-unit residential complex or residential condominium unit valued up to $1M; between $1M and $1.5M the rebate is gradually reduced, and none is available at $1.5M or more (maximum $50,000). Agreements must be entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, with construction beginning before 2031 and substantially complete before 2036 (CRA guidance summarized in secondary sources). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST New Housing Rebate (the older rebate, still in place).** 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, phasing out for homes between $350,000 and $450,000 (task brief figure; the FTHB rebate tops this rebate up so the combined refund reaches the percentages above).
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — general rates.** 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion to $2M, 3% above $2M, plus an additional 2% on the residential portion above $3M. https://getwealthy.blog/land-transfer-tax-rebates-canada-2026/
- **PTT — first-time buyer exemption.** Qualifying first-time buyers pay no PTT on the first $500,000 of a home worth up to $835,000 (maximum saving $8,000), with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000 and none at $860,000 or more; eligibility requires Canadian citizenship or permanent residence, a BC residency/tax-filing test, and never having owned an interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/bc-property-transfer-tax-exemptions-guide/
- **PTT — newly built home exemption.** A separate exemption, not limited to first-time buyers: full PTT relief on a newly built home up to $1,100,000, phasing out between $1,100,000 and $1,150,000; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and keep it as a principal residence for the first year. The first-time buyer and newly built exemptions are mutually exclusive — a buyer who qualifies for both elects the larger. https://www.bronsonjob.com/guides/property-transfer-tax-bc

### Presales and assignments — REDMA

- **Disclosure statement first.** A developer must give the buyer a disclosure statement filed with the BC Financial Services Authority setting out all material facts (layouts, estimated strata fees, developer background, common property) before the contract binds; a contract signed without the required disclosure may not be enforceable. https://northam-law.com/pre-sale-condo-purchases-in-british-columbia-key-legal-protections-for-buyers/
- **7-day rescission.** The buyer may cancel within 7 days after the later of signing the purchase agreement and receiving/acknowledging the disclosure statement, with the deposit refunded. Deposits are held in trust by a law firm, notary or brokerage, not paid to the developer directly. Material changes after signing require an amended disclosure statement. https://northam-law.com/pre-sale-condo-purchases-in-british-columbia-key-legal-protections-for-buyers/ and https://meridianlawgroup.ca/pre-sale-properties-and-a-developers-requirements-to-disclose-building-permits-financing-commitments-and-buyers-rights-to-terminate-agreements/
- **Assignments.** Selling a presale contract before completion is an assignment of paper, not of property. Developers typically restrict it — consent usually required, fees commonly 1%–3% of the original price, and some contracts prohibit assignment until sell-out or prohibit public marketing. Read the assignment clause before signing; the BC flipping tax is a separate consideration on short holds. https://yvr-realestate.com/blog/how-to-buy-a-presale-condo-in-bc-and-win/

### Financing supports

- **Home Buyers' Plan (HBP).** Up to $60,000 per person withdrawn from an RRSP for a first home, repayable over the HBP period. **First Home Savings Account (FHSA).** $8,000 of contribution room per year to a $40,000 lifetime limit, deductible going in and tax-free coming out for a qualifying first home. The two can be combined for the same purchase. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/
- **CMHC mortgage insurance.** Required when the down payment is under 20% on a home priced $1.5M or less; CMHC states premium rates range from 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount (smaller down payment = higher premium), and the premium may be added to the loan. Minimum down payment is 5% on the first $500,000 and 10% on the portion above. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- **Municipal layer — zoning, OCP and SSMUH vary by city.** There is no province-wide multi-family zoning; each municipality's zoning bylaw and OCP govern where condos can be built, while the Province's Bill 44 SSMUH legislation required every local government to allow small-scale multi-unit housing (a suite/ADU, 3–4 units, or 6 units near frequent bus stops, depending on lot and location) in formerly single-detached zones by June 30, 2024, subject to granted extensions. Worked local examples are in this series' area files: Abbotsford (Zoning Bylaw 2400 (2014); OCP Bylaw 2600-2016; SSMUH Bylaw 3560-2024, adopted June 25, 2024) and Chilliwack (OCP Bylaw 5500; SSMUH Bylaw 5395, adopted June 18, 2024). https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2024hous0178-001534

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What documents should I read before removing subjects on a resale condo?** The Form B with its attachments — current budget, bylaws and rules, and the most recent depreciation report — plus at least two years of strata council and AGM minutes. The Form B is a prescribed certificate under s.59 of the Strata Property Act and must include the insurance summary since April 1, 2023. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: What do strata fees actually cover, and what is the CRF?** Fees split between the operating fund (day-to-day common expenses) and the contingency reserve fund (major repairs that occur less than yearly). Since November 1, 2023 at least 10% of the operating fund must go to the CRF each year. A low fee with a thin CRF and an old depreciation report is how special levies happen. https://pushormitchell.com/2024/05/16/updates-to-strata-requirements-depreciation-reports/
- **Q: Can the strata stop me renting out my condo?** Not long-term — rental-restriction bylaws have been void since November 24, 2022. It can still restrict short-term rentals, and an age bylaw (55+) can restrict who may live there, tenant or owner. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Can I buy in a 55+ building if I'm 48 and my partner is 57?** Ownership is not age-restricted, and residence is permitted where a resident meets the age requirement — with the May 1, 2023 exemptions expressly covering a younger spouse or partner of a qualifying resident. Check the specific bylaw wording. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Are pets and parking guaranteed by the listing?** No. Pet bylaws are building-specific and survive in the filed bylaws; parking and storage entitlements for the specific lot are stated on the Form B (stall/locker numbers or "none"), not in listing copy. Directory and listing pet lines are leads, not proof.
- **Q: What insurance do I need beyond the strata's policy?** A unit-owner policy covering your contents, improvements, liability and — critically — assessment of the strata's deductible (the Form B insurance summary discloses the deductibles, including water damage). CMHC makes the same distinction for its own product: mortgage default insurance protects the lender, not the borrower. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: How much extra tax do I pay on a new condo vs a resale?** A new condo carries 5% GST (before rebates); a resale generally does not. Both can attract PTT unless an exemption applies — first-time buyer (to $835,000 / partial to $860,000) or newly built (full to $1,100,000 / out at $1,150,000), but not both at once. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/bc-property-transfer-tax-exemptions-guide/
- **Q: I'm a first-time buyer of a new condo — how do the rebates stack?** Potentially: FTHB GST rebate (100% of GST to $1M, max $50,000, from Bill C-4), the PTT newly built exemption (usually larger than the first-time buyer exemption at condo prices), the FHSA ($40,000 lifetime) and the HBP ($60,000). Each has its own eligibility test and clock — the federal GST test uses a four-year look-back, while BC's PTT first-time test is never-owned-anywhere. Confirm eligibility per program before counting any of them. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/
- **Q: I signed a presale last week and I'm having second thoughts.** You have the REDMA 7-day rescission window, counted from the later of signing and receiving the disclosure statement; give written notice inside the window and the deposit is refunded. After the window, exit is an assignment question, and assignments exist only on the contract's terms. https://meridianlawgroup.ca/pre-sale-properties-and-a-developers-requirements-to-disclose-building-permits-financing-commitments-and-buyers-rights-to-terminate-agreements/
- **Q: How much mortgage insurance will I pay with 10% down?** CMHC's schedule puts a 90.01%–95% loan-to-value premium at 4.00% (4.50% with a non-traditional down payment source) and the 85.01%–90% band at 3.10%, within the overall 0.6%–4.5% range; amortizations over 25 years add 0.20%. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home

## 5. RECENT PROVINCE-WIDE CHANGES, WITH DATES

- **November 24, 2022 — rental and age bylaws reset.** Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) voided all residential rental-restriction bylaws, ended the Form J Rental Disclosure Statement regime, and confined age bylaws to 55+ (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **April 1, 2023 — Form B insurance summary.** Strata corporations must include a summary of insurance coverage on the Form B. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **May 1, 2023 — wider 55+ exemptions.** Younger spouses/partners, children under 19 in the resident's care, and adult children previously cared for by the resident were added to the exemption list. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **November 1, 2023 — 10% CRF minimum.** AGM budgets must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF; developer initial CRF contributions for new stratas rose to 10% as well. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **April 1, 2024 — PTT thresholds raised.** The first-time buyer exemption ceiling moved to $835,000 (partial to $860,000) and the newly built home exemption to $1,100,000 (phasing out at $1,150,000), the thresholds still in effect in the sources checked. https://www.bronsonjob.com/guides/property-transfer-tax-bc
- **June 30, 2024 — SSMUH deadline.** Local governments had to have zoning updated for small-scale multi-unit housing; by September 16, 2024 the Province reported 172 of 188 local governments had adopted SSMUH bylaws for areas without an extension, with 21 governments granted extensions (Chilliwack partially, to December 31, 2030, for the Proper/Hazel Park area). https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2024hous0178-001534
- **July 1, 2024 — depreciation reports tightened.** Five-year renewal became mandatory; stratas formed before that date with no report face the July 1, 2026 (CRD/FVRD/Metro Vancouver) and July 1, 2027 (rest of BC) catch-up deadlines. https://pushormitchell.com/2024/05/16/updates-to-strata-requirements-depreciation-reports/
- **October 27, 2025 — more depreciation-report professionals.** Additional qualified professionals (including professional licensees in engineering, architectural technologists and certified technicians) may provide depreciation reports and electrical planning reports, per OIC 187-25. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **March 13, 2026 — FTHB GST rebate becomes law.** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent (CRA, Excise and GST/HST News No. 122, April 2026), bringing the 100%-to-$1M GST rebate for first-time buyers of new homes into force for agreements on/after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. Note: some secondary coverage dates Royal Assent March 12, 2026; the CRA publication used here states March 13, 2026, and this file follows the CRA. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **December 15, 2024 (financing rule, still current) — insured-mortgage cap $1.5M.** CMHC insurance requires the home price to be $1.5M or less and applies the 5%-on-first-$500,000 / 10%-on-the-rest minimum down payment structure. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
