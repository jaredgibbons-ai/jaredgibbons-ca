## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Form B and strata records:** on a resale strata purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate; the BC Real Estate Association advises reviewing the Form B for the most recent depreciation report, the strata's financial statements, the CRF balance and planned work to identify future special levies. All four Princess Crescent complexes date from 1993–1995, so the depreciation report is the first document to read in this sub-area. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports:** effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Minimum CRF contribution:** effective November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund in the annual budget. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void:** since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw; short-term rentals can still be restricted. Any building-directory "rentals not allowed" line a buyer meets on a Princess Crescent complex is old bylaw text, not current law — none of the four complexes' current rental bylaws were confirmed on a citable source for this file in any event (UNCONFIRMED, Section 2). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ is the only legal age bylaw:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. This is the rule Princess Gate's published "55+" description operates under — confirm the filed bylaw and its exemption wording on the Form B. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City plans and zoning:** Canyon Springs sits under the City of Coquitlam Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996 — the surrounding detached stock is in the R-1, R-2 and R-3 zones, with subdivision minimums of 650 m² and 18.5 m frontage (R-1, R-3) and 465 m² and 12.5 m (R-2). The City adopted an Interim Official Community Plan on April 27, 2026 (Bylaws 5511, 2026 and 5512, 2026), superseding the Citywide OCP No. 3479, 2001. Coquitlam's SSMUH rules allow up to 3 units on lots of 280 m² or less and up to 4 units on lots over 280 m² and under 4,050 m², including secondary suites and carriage/laneway homes; lots not on municipal water and sewer are outside the SSMUH provisions. Riparian and flood rules also apply near the area's creeks: a Watercourse Protection Development Permit Area within 30 m of the top of a watercourse or ravine bank, Zoning Bylaw s. 519 flood construction setbacks and s. 523 riparian setbacks. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1436/Official-Community-Plan-Review (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/canyon-springs.md)
- **GST on new condos:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — the Princess Crescent stock is 1993–1995 resale stock, so GST does not apply to it. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 (some secondary sources say March 12 — CRA governs). The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less (maximum saving $8,000), partial $835,000–$860,000, none at $860,000+. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000. At Canyon Springs' resale price points the First-Time Home Buyers' Program thresholds are the live ones — check the exact price against the $835,000 / $860,000 cut-offs. https://www.equitylawgroup.ca/news/closing-costs-bc-home-purchase
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. No presale project inside Canyon Springs is confirmed — this applies only to presales a buyer compares in North Coquitlam or Burke Mountain. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year. (BC standard block, as used in batches C1–C3.)
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60% (LTV up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), 4.50% at the top band with a non-traditional down payment, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- **Coquitlam tax timing:** City of Coquitlam property taxes run January 1 to December 31 and were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on any unpaid balance after July 2, 2026 and an additional 5% penalty after September 15, 2026, applied also to unclaimed Home Owner Grants. https://coquitlam.ca/544/Property-Taxes (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/canyon-springs.md)

- **Q: Princess Gate is 55+. Can a younger buyer purchase there?** The published listings describe Princess Gate as a 55+ adult-oriented community across 2985 and 2995 Princess Crescent. A 55+ bylaw is the only age bylaw valid under Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and, from May 1, 2023, younger spouses/partners and defined children of a 55+ resident. Confirm the filed bylaw on the Form B before assuming who may live in the unit. https://www.vancouverhomesearch.com/listing/r3094963-2985-princess-crescent-318-coquitlam-canyon-springs-homes-for-sale-499900/brochure and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can a strata here ban me from renting my unit out long-term?** No — rental-restriction bylaws have been unenforceable anywhere in BC since November 24, 2022. A short-term rental bylaw can still bind. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: What should I check first in a 1993–1995 wood-frame complex?** The Form B package: the latest depreciation report, financials, CRF balance and planned work or levies. Every strata with 5+ lots must have a depreciation report every five years with no deferral vote (since July 1, 2024), and the CRF must receive at least 10% of the operating fund (since November 1, 2023) — on 30-year-old wood-frame buildings, envelope and roof timing in that report is the purchase decision. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The whole sub-area's condo stock is one street.** All four verified complexes — The Jefferson, The Madison, The Montclaire and Princess Gate — stand on Princess Crescent, a single crescent off the Pinetree Way corridor, built within about two years of each other (1993–1995). A "Canyon Springs condo search" is, in practice, a Princess Crescent search. https://www.zolo.ca/coquitlam-real-estate/2990-princess-crescent/310
- **The name belongs to two places at once.** Coquitlam's Canyon Springs is a board sub-area; North Vancouver's Canyon Springs is a single Polygon building at 2665 Mountain Highway in Lynn Valley, built 2015. Search results, showing requests and even listing remarks mix the two — verify the municipality on every Canyon Springs hit. https://lynnvalleylife.com/news-events/polygon-ceo-likes-seeing-north-van-kids-come-home (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/canyon-springs.md)
- **Coquitlam replaced its OCP in April 2026.** The City adopted an Interim Official Community Plan on April 27, 2026 (Bylaws 5511, 2026 and 5512, 2026), superseding the Citywide OCP No. 3479, 2001 — any Canyon Springs density or land-use claim sourced to the old OCP is out of date. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1436/Official-Community-Plan-Review
- **The sub-area has market stats, but no sub-area median — by design.** SnapStats' September 2026 report gives Canyon Springs 21 condos & townhomes listed, 5 sold and a 24% sales ratio, while the $625,000 Condos & Townhomes median is Coquitlam-wide. Any source quoting a "Canyon Springs median" is inventing a figure the publisher does not produce. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/canyon-springs.md)
- **The school answer depends on three tiers, not two.** SD43 runs elementary, middle and secondary schools, and the one locator result in the research file (Pinetree Way Elementary → Summit Middle → Pinetree Secondary, for a Pinetree Way sample address) is a corridor answer, not a confirmed Princess Crescent catchment — the middle-school step is the one buyers from two-tier districts forget to check. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/canyon-springs.md)
- **Creek rules reach further than buyers expect.** Coquitlam's Watercourse Protection Development Permit Area applies within 30 m of the top of a watercourse or ravine bank, and Zoning Bylaw ss. 519 and 523 add flood construction and riparian setbacks — relevant to the detached lots around the Princess Crescent cluster and to any strata common-property work near a creek. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/canyon-springs.md)
