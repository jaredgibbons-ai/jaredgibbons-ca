## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- The Village of Harrison Hot Springs is an incorporated resort village at the south end of Harrison Lake, in the Fraser Valley Regional District, surrounded by — but separate from — the District of Kent. Esplanade Avenue is the waterfront street facing the lake, and it is where the condo stock concentrates (below); Hot Springs Road is the main road in and out, running south to Agassiz. https://www.kentbc.ca/media/0enenmdy/doc_636900753607636141.pdf (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)
- Where condo stock concentrates: the Esplanade Avenue strip. Every counted apartment-condo building in Section 2 stands on Esplanade Avenue (120, 328, 378, 450 and 470 Esplanade), with the one documented townhouse complex on Hot Springs Road (520). The 2021 Census dwelling mix underlines how small the apartment shelf is: of 885 occupied private dwellings, apartments in buildings of fewer than five storeys were 135 (15.3%), row houses 100 (11.3%), single-detached houses 575 (65.0%), and apartments in buildings of five or more storeys 0 (0.0%). https://www.harrisonhotsprings.ca/sites/default/files/2025-02/2025.02.19---VHHS-Housing-Needs-Report.pdf (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)
- Market figures: **Harrison Hot Springs is NOT covered by SnapStats.** No Harrison Hot Springs row exists in the SnapStats FVR September 2026 report (its communities stop at Mission), CADREB publishes board-wide statistics only, and no parent community row applies — Harrison is its own municipality. All market figures — inventory, solds, sales ratio, median price, days on market — are UNVERIFIED and nothing is substituted. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)
- The village is small and old for its size: 2021 Census population 1,905 (up from 1,468 in 2016), 885 occupied private dwellings, 715 owners (80.8%) and 165 renters (18.6%); people 65+ were 32% of the 2021 population. The Village's 2025 Interim Housing Needs Report puts need at 185 units over 5 years and 637 over 20 years. https://www.harrisonhotsprings.ca/sites/default/files/2025-02/2025.02.19---VHHS-Housing-Needs-Report.pdf and https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/as-sa/fogs-spg/page.cfm?topic=7&dguid=2021A00055909027&lang=E (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)
- The hot springs are the village's reason for being: two spring types — Potash at 40°C and Sulphur at 62°C — are pumped and cooled to supply the public mineral pool and the resort pools. The public indoor pool, the Harrison Mineral Baths at Hot Springs Road and Esplanade Avenue, is owned and operated by the Harrison Hot Springs Resort; there is no natural outdoor hot spring open to the public. https://tourismharrison.com/explore/harrison-river-valley/our-region/our-hot-springs/ (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)
- Schools and services: the village school is Harrison Hot Springs Elementary (K–6) at 501 Hot Springs Road (~100 students, SD78 Fraser-Cascade); grades 7–12 go to Agassiz Elementary-Secondary School in Agassiz. Grocery and everyday shopping, the arena/recreation complex and the secondary school are all in Agassiz (District of Kent), roughly a 10-minute drive south. Transit: BC Transit Route 71 (Agassiz–Harrison) runs from downtown Chilliwack through Agassiz to Harrison Hot Springs, terminus at St. Alice St/Esplanade Ave, administered by the FVRD — service is limited, check schedules per trip. https://sd78.bc.ca/harrison/ and https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/transit.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

- **Form B:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. In Harrison, the Form B package is also where a building's short-term rental bylaw actually gets settled — marketing claims about Airbnb permission conflict between sources for at least one counted building (Oasis, Section 2). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote; since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. The 1990s Esplanade buildings (Laguna Beach 1993, Echo Beach 1996, Harrison On The Lake 1979) are where the depreciation report and CRF balance matter most in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since November 24, 2022 for strata property anywhere in BC — the "rentals allowed w/restrictions" element of Aqua Shores' directory line is VOID as a long-term-rental restriction. That does NOT settle short-term rentals: a strata may still ban or limit short-term rentals by bylaw, and in Harrison Village zoning and licensing sit on top (below). **Age bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children under Strata Property Regulation 7.01. No Harrison building in this file is verified to hold a 55+ bylaw — the 55+ program found 0 in every 55+ category in the village. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Village plans and zoning (current — 1184 governs, not 864):** the current consolidated Official Community Plan is **OCP Bylaw No. 1184, 2022** (consolidated to November 3, 2025), which repealed OCP Bylaw No. 864, 2007 — the research file's 864 citations (Village Centre / Lakeshore designations, the 2017 "Resort Residential" provisions, the Pine Avenue and Echo Avenue/Eagle Street neighbourhood plans) are **864-era material, labelled as such**, and are superseded as the plan of record. Day-to-day use sits in **Zoning Bylaw No. 1115, 2017**, under which "Tourist Accommodation" is a commercial-zone use. The Village began an OCP and Zoning Bylaw update process in 2025, so confirm the current text with the Village before relying on a zone. https://www.harrisonhotsprings.ca/sites/default/files/2026-02/Bylaw-1184---Official-Community-Plan-%28Consolidated-to-Nov-3%2C-2025%29.pdf (55+ file: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/harrison-hot-springs-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md; research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)
- **Short-term rentals — three layers, all local:** the Village is **exempt from the provincial principal-residence rule** as one of BC's designated resort regions, so nightly rental runs on Village rules instead: (1) Tourist Accommodation zoning under Bylaw No. 1115, 2017; (2) since December 15, 2025, Business Licence Bylaw No. 1128 (amended by Bylaw No. 1231, 2025) requires at s. 2.1(h) a **separate business licence for each dwelling unit** so operated; (3) the strata's own short-term rental bylaw, building by building. Accommodation in the designated area (Village + District of Kent + FVRD Electoral Area C) also carries the **3% MRDT**, whose current cycle runs to a repeal/renewal date of January 1, 2027 — confirm renewal before underwriting 2027+ accommodation income. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2023hous0060-001598 and https://www.harrisonhotsprings.ca/sites/default/files/2026-01/Bylaw-1128---Business-Licence-and-Regulation-%28Consolidated-to-December-15%2C-2025%29.pdf and https://tourismharrison.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-Tourism-Harrison-One-Year-Tactical-Plan-FINAL.pdf (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

Industry sources record Harrison On The Lake and Oasis as permitting them and Echo Beach as allowing a 7-day minimum, while the four large Esplanade buildings are recorded as disallowing them — and for Oasis the same publisher's two pages conflict, so settle it from the registered bylaws, not a listing. - **Q: Is the village in a flood zone?** On the lakeshore and Miami River side, yes. The flood construction level is 14.55 m (Bylaw No. 1179, 2022); the existing dike crest (13.9 m) sits below the 1894 flood of record (14.1 m) and the required 15.1 m crest, and a combined fixed + deployable dike project is in detailed design for Zones 3–6. Check a building's floor elevation against the FCL before writing. https://www.harrisonhotsprings.ca/sites/default/files/2025-05/2025.04.22---Regular-Council-Meeting-Minutes.pdf
- **Q: Can I rent out a Harrison condo long-term?** Yes — strata rental-restriction bylaws have been void since November 24, 2022 anywhere in BC, including the restriction element in Aqua Shores' directory line. Short-term rental is the separate, layered question in the first answer above. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Where do Harrison kids go to school, and where do we shop?** Harrison Hot Springs Elementary (K–6) at 501 Hot Springs Road in the village, then Agassiz Elementary-Secondary School (7–12) in Agassiz. Grocery, everyday services, the arena and the secondary school are all in Agassiz, about a 10-minute drive south — the village itself has a small resort strip on Esplanade Avenue and Hot Springs Road. https://sd78.bc.ca/harrison/ (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)
- **Q: What PTT should I budget?** On the Section 3 worked example — inputs: $500,000 fair market value (illustration only; no Harrison median is published), fully residential, no exemption — the bill is $8,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Are there manufactured homes in the village, and is either park 55+?** Yes, as rental pads, not ownership product: Glencoe Motel and Trailer Park (259 Hot Springs Rd; 27 pads/mobile homes in the FVRD inventory) and Harrison Holiday Park (973 Hot Springs Rd; 12 pads). Neither source states any age restriction — do not call either park 55+. Pad rent increases are capped under the MHPTA (2026: 2.3% + proportional; 2027: 2.2% + proportional). https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/About~the~FVRD/Documents/Homelessness/2020%20Kent-HHS%20Housing%20Inventory.pdf
- **Q: Is there public transit?** Yes, limited — BC Transit Route 71 (Agassiz–Harrison) links Harrison Hot Springs with Agassiz and downtown Chilliwack, terminus at St. Alice St/Esplanade Ave. Check schedules per trip. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/transit.html

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The OCP on the Village's website changed in 2025.** OCP Bylaw No. 1184, 2022 (consolidated to November 3, 2025) is the current plan and repealed the 2007 Bylaw 864 plan that the research file and older pages still cite; the Village also began an OCP/Zoning update process in June 2025. Any page copy citing "OCP Bylaw 864" as current is out of date. https://www.harrisonhotsprings.ca/sites/default/files/2026-02/Bylaw-1184---Official-Community-Plan-%28Consolidated-to-Nov-3%2C-2025%29.pdf (55+ file: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/harrison-hot-springs-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md)
- **The seniors housing project stalled on provincial budget cuts (Feb 2026).** The proposed 60-unit, four-storey affordable seniors project (Agassiz-Harrison Community Services) had a July 2025 council letter of intent and letters of support — but BC Housing's February 17, 2026 notice killed the 2025 Community Housing Fund round, so the application will not be advanced. The Village's own project page had staged design/permits for summer/fall 2026 only if funding were awarded. Status: unfunded proposal, no timeline. https://pagesarchive.blackpress.ca/BC/Processed/AHO/260227/AHON260227_A02.pdf (55+ file: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/harrison-hot-springs-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md)
- **Short-term rental permission in Harrison is building-by-building — and the public record contradicts itself.** One industry publisher's two pages disagree about Oasis (permitted on one, prohibited on the other), listings market Oasis units as operating Airbnbs, and Echo Beach is recorded with a 7-day minimum. The only document that settles a building's position is its registered bylaws, read with the Village's per-unit business licence rule. https://www.liftylife.ca/blog/vacation-rental-management-in-harrison-hot-springs/ and https://www.liftylife.ca/blog/buying-property-in-harrison-hot-springs/
- **A new elementary school is being explored — and the first MOU already failed.** The current Harrison Hot Springs Elementary building is 75+ years old and over capacity. The Village and SD78 are exploring a shared "Civic Campus," but the June 15, 2026 council motion to forward a revised MOU to SD78 failed; as of Aug 10, 2026 the stage is a new exploratory MOU — conceptual and unfunded, no timeline. Present it as a possibility, never a promise. https://www.harrisonhotsprings.ca/village-office/public-notices/press-release-civic-campus and https://getintoitharrison.ca/civic-campus-building-tomorrow-together (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)
- **The dike project is mid-design, and the numbers are stark.** Crest 13.9 m, 1894 flood of record 14.1 m, flood construction level 14.55 m, required crest 15.1 m — and the village floods from the south only if Kent's Dikes A–C fail. In September 2025 Council directed detailed design for a deployable waterfront dike for Zones 3–6 (pump station to Rendall Park). Lakeshore buyers should ask about timing and any cost-sharing before writing. https://www.harrisonhotsprings.ca/village-office/public-notices/council-meeting-highlights-september-8-2025 (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/harrison-hot-springs.md)
