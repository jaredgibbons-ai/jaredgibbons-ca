## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT CONDOS

The strata rules below are the standard BC block, stated for completeness — they govern any strata purchase in Maple Ridge, including the rare condo that might surface in this band. The tax rules after them are the ones that actually bite at this band, and they bite on the detached side.

- **Form B and strata records:** on a resale purchase the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate; the BC Real Estate Association advises reviewing the Form B for the most recent depreciation report, the strata's financial statements, the CRF balance and planned work to identify future special levies. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Depreciation reports:** effective July 1, 2024, all BC stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, with no deferral vote; stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Minimum CRF contribution:** effective November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund in the annual budget. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/more-help-and-support/subscribing-for-updates
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void:** since November 24, 2022 (Bill 44), no strata may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw; short-term rentals can still be restricted. Any Maple Ridge directory line stating "rentals not allowed" reflects old bylaw text, not current law. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ is the only legal age bylaw:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1 and 123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **City zoning and plans:** Maple Ridge Zoning Bylaw No. 7600-2019 governs. The City's Official Community Plan citation is CONTESTED in earlier program research (Bylaw 7060-2014 vs Bylaw 7998-2024) — flagged as contested, never asserted as one number; the research file for this topic records that the City's OCP updates and area plans for the Town Centre, Hammond, Silver Valley and Albion were adopted in fall 2025. https://www.mapleridge.ca/your-government/plans-strategies/official-community-plan-area-plans/official-community-plan
- **PTT at this band — the rule that bites hardest:** general rates are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000 — every purchase in this band reaches into the 2% tier almost entirely, and purchases over $2,000,000 reach the 3% tier on the top portion — plus a further 2% on the residential property value greater than $3,000,000. On a mixed-class property the further 2% applies only to the residential portion, and where land is classed as farm only because it is used for an owner's or farmer's dwelling, up to 0.5 hectares is treated as residential property — directly relevant to the farm-classed acreages that appear in this band. PTT is based on the fair market value on the day the property is registered at the Land Title Office. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Neither BC purchase exemption reaches this band:** the First-Time Home Buyers' Program full exemption caps at a fair market value of $835,000 (exempting the first $500,000, partial to $860,000) and the Newly Built Home Exemption is fully eliminated at $1,150,000 — a buyer here budgets full PTT. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Home owner grant phase-out at this band:** the grant threshold for 2026 is $2,075,000 — a full regular grant at or below that assessed (or partitioned) value, reduced by $5 for each $1,000 over it, and $0 for the regular grant over $2,189,000 — so many homes in this band receive a reduced grant or none, even as principal residences. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **GST on new condos:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4) rebates 100% of GST up to a $1 million price (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 — note the rebate itself phases out below this band's floor, so it does not offset GST on an over-$1.8M new purchase. CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122 states Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 (some secondary sources say March 12 — CRA governs). The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Presales (REDMA):** the Real Estate Development Marketing Act s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year — recorded for completeness; neither changes the tax math at this band. https://homehappy.ca/bc-first-time-buyer-programs/
- **Mortgage default insurance (CMHC):** published premiums run 0.60% (LTV up to 65%) to 4.00% (90.01%–95% LTV), 4.50% at the top band with a non-traditional down payment, plus 0.20% where the over-25-year amortization surcharge applies. CMHC insurance is a sub-20%-down product with a $1.5M price ceiling in the sources checked — a purchase in this band is conventionally financed, so the premium table is background, not a closing line. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- **Floodplain, escarpment and ALR — for the detached side of the band:** properties along the Fraser River frontage sit in a floodplain where the flood construction level is 6.7 m geodetic (including freeboard), a covenant on title identifies construction in the floodplain, and no habitable floor space may be located below that level on affected lots; in the Fraser River Escarpment policy area, properties within 300 m of the crest require a geotechnical report attached to a Section 219 covenant before a building permit, and within 100 m a detailed report addressing potential seismic slope failure; ALR status is parcel-specific through the Agricultural Land Commission's mapping tools. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/floodplain-construction-0 and http://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/fraser-river-escarpment-information-package and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/

Model it before offering, not at closing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: What parcel checks matter most on a Maple Ridge acreage over $1.8M?** Three, all parcel-specific: ALR status, through the Agricultural Land Commission's maps and online mapping tools; floodplain status on Fraser River frontage (flood construction level 6.7 m geodetic, covenant on title, no habitable floor space below that level); and escarpment status on the south-western slopes (geotechnical report within 300 m of the crest, detailed report within 100 m). Kanaka Creek adds a fourth on the Cottonwood / Albion side: it is a salmon-bearing watercourse protected by Kanaka Creek Regional Park, and creek-adjacent acreages carry watercourse and development-permit questions that are parcel-specific. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/ and https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/floodplain-construction-0 and https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/kanaka-creek-regional-park
- **Q: Can I rent out a strata unit long-term if one does surface in this band?** Yes — strata rental-restriction bylaws have been unenforceable since November 24, 2022 anywhere in BC. What can still bind you is a short-term rental bylaw in the registered bylaws, and a 55+ age bylaw limiting who may live in the unit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
