## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

### The settled court-ordered-sale treatment (BC Supreme Court)

- **Step 1 — the petition:** a foreclosure proceeding must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7(1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). Rule 21-7 as read on 2026-11-01 sets out no demand step before the petition; whether the standard mortgage terms prescribed under the Land Title Act give the lender a post-default option to demand payment is UNVERIFIED in this pass. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Step 2 — the order and the redemption period:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption — Rule 21-7(5)(b); the court may determine or certify the amount due and pronounce judgment for it — Rule 21-7(5)(c) and (e). The Land Title Act refers to this order as an "order nisi," with a later final order of foreclosure — Land Title Act s. 271(1), carried on the settled Langley treatment; the Mission research file did not re-verify that wording in its own pass. The redemption period has NO fixed length in the Rules: it is the period the court fixes. The Law and Equity Act interest-to-redemption wording (s. 19) was not re-verified in this pass and is UNVERIFIED here. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Final order / applying for a sale:** in default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7(6); a party of record may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7(7). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5(3); the court's directions may appoint who has conduct, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of value — Rule 13-5(4). Carried on the settled Langley treatment; the Mission research file could not re-load the Rule 13-5 page on 2026-11-01 (browser rate limit) and marks every Rule 13-5 mechanic UNVERIFIED in its own pass. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Court approval of the sale:** the manner of sale may be fixed as sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5(4)(b); the person having conduct may apply to the court, even though the time for redemption has not expired, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7(9) (verified live in this pass); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in **Form 60** by the person having conduct of the sale — Rule 13-5(6), on the settled Langley treatment only. That vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Bids and practice direction PD-66:** the court's directions may define "the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids" — Rule 13-5(4)(d), on the settled Langley treatment; the Supreme Court's civil practice direction **PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings,"** governs practice in these proceedings. Neither the Rule 13-5 text nor the text of PD-66 was retrieved from an official source on 2026-11-01 for Mission, so the timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66 are UNVERIFIED in detail in this file. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm
- **Where the proceeding is filed:** the filing-registry rule (Law and Equity Act s. 21(2) — municipality / judicial district) was not re-verified in this pass and is UNVERIFIED here. What is confirmed is only the locations-list position in Section 1: Mission has no registry of its own, and no stated mapping assigns Mission files to Abbotsford or Chilliwack. https://www.bccourts.ca/supreme_court/locations.htm?location=kamloops
- **Court records:** Court Services Online is BC's electronic court registry; eSearch is available to anyone with Internet access, Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry location, party names and filed documents, and its file classes include "H - Foreclosure." It publishes no area statistics. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period on a Mission townhouse?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — it is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What does "subject to court approval" mean for my offer?** The person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7(9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title in you — and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires — so until that order, the sale is not final. Do not book movers on acceptance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** UNVERIFIED as a stated process rule in this file — the bid-rights wording (Rule 13-5(4)(d)) and the PD-66 bid timing were not retrieved from an official source on 2026-11-01. Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for, and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing before spending on inspections.
