## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**The strata block (applies when the Langley home being sold fast is a strata townhome).**

- **Form B diligence.** SPA s. 59: Form B within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢ per page — fees, amounts owing, special levies, CRF balance, insurance, rules, budget, Rental Disclosure Statement and depreciation report attached. For a seller, that package is the buyer's diligence: ordering it early is the strata-side work a deadline sale cannot skip (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate).
- **Depreciation reports.** Stratas with 5+ lots must obtain a depreciation report every 5 years since July 1, 2024; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER the threshold. **CRF minimum:** 10% of the operating budget since November 1, 2023 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing).
- **Rentals.** Rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 — a directory or listing line saying "rentals not allowed" for a Langley strata is void for long-term rentals and is flagged void wherever met; rentals are allowed. Short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation).
- **Age.** Only 55+ age bylaws are valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01; spouse/caregiver/legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion; a 45+ bylaw is void and does NOT convert to 55+). A valid 55+ bylaw (§2.4) narrows the resale buyer pool — a timeline fact a fast-sale seller must price in, not a defect (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws).
- **Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhome or detached Langley home carries NO strata, NO Form B, NO strata fees — title, municipal rules (Township or City, depending on the side of the boundary) and registered covenants/HOA only. That is a genuinely shorter document chain on a deadline, and the only strata-versus-fee-simple statement this topic file makes.

**The regulator frame and municipal frame — per this file's research.**

- **BCFSA frame:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings — whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA; dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. When agency disclosure occurs in Jared's fast-sale process is UNVERIFIED — the page does not state it (research file §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers).
- **Costs omission — documented for this page, asserted only as this file's research documents it:** the page states **no fee, no commission, and no statement of who pays for preparation, photography or advertising** anywhere in its text (research file §4). That omission sits against the BCFSA duty above to give consumers information about commissions and fees — the cost conversation belongs before signing, and this file invents no figure for it.
- **Municipal rules:** this slug's research documents no Township or City of Langley zoning, SSMUH, suite or tax rule for this service page, and none is imported here from a sub-area file — the municipal rule set for a specific Langley home is the one in that home's sub-area file (for Willoughby product, for example, the willoughby-homes-for-sale townhomes file in this batch). **Purchase taxes, rebates and financing (buyer-side figures a fast-sale seller's buyers will run).**

- **FTHB GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122 (March 13 governs; no sibling for this slug carries March 12 as its own date): 100% of GST to $1,000,000, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1,500,000, agreements March 20, 2025–2031 (CRA: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html). GST applies to new construction, not resales.
- **New Housing Rebate:** 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out (CRA: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html).
- **PTT:** 1% / 2% / 3% tiers plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — an "Over $3M — 5%" row is flagged as a compression of the 3% + further 2% structure wherever met. **Worked examples, inputs stated, no exemption:** $700,000 → $2,000 (1% × $200,000) + $10,000 (2% × $500,000) = **$12,000**; $1,000,000 → $2,000 + $16,000 = **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → $2,000 + $20,000 = **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → $2,000 + $26,000 = **$28,000**. Exemptions: first-time buyers full on first $500,000 at ≤$835,000, phasing to $860,000; newly built full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/understand).
- **HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5%** (CMHC homeowner premium schedule). **Q: How fast can a Langley home actually sell?**
A: UNVERIFIED as a general claim — the page's FAQ says a well-prepped, well-priced home in a desirable neighbourhood can have offers within seven to fourteen days and that closing usually adds 30 to 60 days, but those are the page's editorial figures with no official source, and no market timeline is attached to this file (§1, §5). **Q: Will selling fast cost me money?**
A: The page's own framing is a trade-off: it claims pricing 3–5% below comparables usually nets close to fair market through competing offers, while a seven-day cash-investor sale usually costs 15–25% — both figures are UNVERIFIED site copy, not sourced data (§5). **Q: Do I have to fix everything up before selling?**
A: On the page's editorial advice: for an MLS® listing, basic prep (declutter, deep clean, minor repairs, professional photos) is presented as worthwhile and major renovations as rarely paying back; for a cash sale, no preparation, with the trade-off in price. Those are the page's claims, stated as site copy in §5 — what prep pays on a specific home is property-by-property (research file §7).

**Q: Does requesting this service make me Jared's client?**
A: The page does not say when client status begins — UNVERIFIED in this file. Under the BCFSA framework, disclosure about whether the professional can represent you as a client must happen at the outset of dealings — ask for it in the first conversation (§3; research file §7).

**Q: My Langley home is a strata townhouse — what slows a fast sale?**
A: The strata document chain, not the showing schedule: the buyer's Form B package (fees, levies, CRF balance, depreciation report, registered bylaws) is the diligence, and a valid 55+ bylaw on the complex narrows the buyer pool by law. A fee-simple home has no Form B at all — the contrast is in §3.

**Q: Can I rent my townhouse out instead of selling fast?**
A: Long-term, a strata rental-restriction bylaw is void since November 24, 2022 anywhere in BC, so renting is legally open in a strata — whether it fits your deadline is a numbers conversation, not a page claim. Short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw (§3).

**Q: Is this a "we buy houses" cash offer?**
A: No — on the page as read, there is no cash-offer program, no named investor buyer and no purchase guarantee. The cash-investor route appears only as one end of a trade-off described in the page's FAQ, carried here as UNVERIFIED site copy (§1, §5).

**Q: What PTT will my buyer pay on a Langley townhouse?**
A: On the worked examples in §3 (inputs stated, no exemption): $12,000 at $700,000, $18,000 at $1,000,000, $22,000 at $1,200,000 and $28,000 at $1,500,000 — the 1% / 2% tiers only, at those prices. A qualifying first-time buyer or newly-built exemption changes the buyer's bill, not the seller's price (§3).
