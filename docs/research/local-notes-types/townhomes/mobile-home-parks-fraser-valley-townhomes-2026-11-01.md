## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**The strata block — stated for contrast, because it does NOT apply to a pad-rental park site.**

- A strata townhome purchase carries: **Form B** under SPA s.59 within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢ per page; **depreciation reports** on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 (a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold); a **CRF minimum** of 10% of the operating budget since November 1, 2023; **rental-restriction bylaws VOID** since November 24, 2022; and **only 55+ age bylaws valid** since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01; May 1, 2023 exemptions; a 45+ bylaw is void and does not convert) (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing). None of that machinery applies to a pad-rental site: the MHPTA frame below governs instead, and a buyer who expects a Form B for a park home will not get one.
- **Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhome carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — municipal rules and registered covenants/HOA only. A park home is a third case again: chattel home plus a site tenancy.

**MHPTA frame (the rules that actually govern this page's stock).**

- **Governing law:** BC's Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act covers most manufactured home tenancies; manufactured homes are built off-site and transported to a final location for installation (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes).
- **Pad-rent increases:** only once every 12 months, only with 3 full months' written notice, only up to the yearly limit; no rounding up and no carry-forward of an unused portion. **Limits: 2026 = 2.3% plus a proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% plus a proportional amount** — the proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of sites in the park (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes).
- **NO pad-rent dollar figure is ever published in this file.** This page states no pad rent for any park, and no official source publishes a figure, average or range. The ranges on sibling pages of the site ($650–$950 Langley, $550–$950 Chilliwack, $600–$900 on a further Langley page) are UNVERIFIED site copy wherever met and are never adopted (research file §5).
- **Assignment (MHPTA s.28):** assignment permanently transfers the site tenancy; the tenant may assign only with the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it — the Act's word is "director," never "arbitrator"; the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting, and on assignment the pad rent and the scheduled date of a subsequent increase stay the same (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf).
- **Park closure / change of use (MHPTA s.42):** the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the effective date; a tenant may end early on at least 10 days' written notice, the dispute window is 15 days, and additional compensation (assessed value minus the $20,000, and a further $5,000 or 12 months' pad rent in the non-proceeding case) may be ordered on the conditions the Province describes (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes).

**Purchase taxes, rebates and financing.**

- **FTHB GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122: 100% of GST to $1,000,000, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1,500,000, agreements March 20, 2025–2031. GST treatment of a manufactured-home purchase turns on whether the home is new and on the site tenure — confirm with a lawyer/notary rather than assuming either rebate applies (CRA: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html).
- **New Housing Rebate:** 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out (CRA, same source).
- **PTT:** 1% / 2% / 3% tiers plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — an "Over $3M — 5%" row is a compression, flagged wherever met. Exemptions: first-time buyers full on first $500,000 at ≤$835,000, phasing to $860,000; newly built full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000. Whether PTT applies at all to a given park-home purchase turns on whether a registrable land interest transfers — a pad-rental purchase generally transfers no land interest; confirm per transaction (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/understand).

A: No figure is stated in this file, ever. The figure that matters is the specific park's current rent in its written site tenancy agreement (§3).

**Q: How much can a park raise pad rent each year?**
A: Up to the manufactured-home site limit: 2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027 — once every 12 months, on 3 full months' notice, with no rounding up and no carry-forward (§3).

**Q: Is a park home a townhome for counting or strata purposes?**
A: No. Parks are never townhouse stock, even when a listing form field describes a home's shape as townhouse-style. There is no strata, no Form B and no depreciation report for a pad-rental site; the MHPTA governs (§§2, 3).

**Q: What transfers when I buy a park home?**
A: The home, plus assignment of the site tenancy under MHPTA s.28 — which needs the park owner's prior written consent (or a director's order), carries no consent fee, and keeps the pad rent and next increase date unchanged (§3).

**Q: What happens if the park is sold or converted?**
A: Under MHPTA s.42 the landlord must first have all required permits and approvals, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 on or before the effective date; the tenant may leave early on 10 days' notice, has 15 days to dispute, and may be eligible for additional compensation through dispute resolution (§3).

**Q: Is Cottonwood Village a 55+ townhouse complex I can count on?**
A: It is a pad-park in Chilliwack (7610 Evans Road), counted nowhere as townhouse stock. Its placement (West Chilliwack vs Sardis West Vedder) is UNRESOLVED and its lease terms conflict across sources (2076 vs November 30, 2039 vs ~16 years) — neither conflict is settled in this file (§2.5).
