## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

Read this section as (a) the rules that decide whether land can ever become townhouse stock, and (b) the standard strata block a buyer meets the moment a strata townhouse is the comparison purchase instead.

**The ALR stratification bar — the core townhouse rule for this topic.**

- ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6: https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf); a strata plan deposit is itself a subdivision (Strata Property Act s. 239: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_01), and subdivision of ALR land needs Agricultural Land Commission permission (Agricultural Land Commission Act s. 21(1)); the ALC bulletin adds that a lease of part of an ALR parcel for more than 3 years is itself a subdivision requiring the Commission's permission. On ALR parcels the housing frame is one principal residence at a maximum of 500 m², a secondary suite only if entirely contained within the principal residence and local bylaws permit suites, plus at most one additional residence of 90 m² (parcels ≤40 ha) or 186 m² (parcels >40 ha) (https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/, via the research file).
- BC Assessment farm classification, where a Mission-fringe parcel is farmed, runs in the full three-tier form: $2,500 of farm income for 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the land's value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by October 31 — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR wherever it appears (https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification).

**The strata block (applies to the cross-referenced strata stock in §2.1, not to a vacant lot).**

- **Form B diligence.** A buyer of a strata townhouse is entitled to an Information Certificate (Form B) under s. 59 of the Strata Property Act, provided within seven days of request; the fee is capped at $35 plus up to 25 cents per page for paper copies. The Form B states the monthly strata fee, money the owner owes the strata, approved special levies not yet due, the CRF balance, the strata's insurance, its rules, its current budget and financial statement, any Rental Disclosure Statement, and the current depreciation report attached (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/strata-information-buyers-sellers; Strata Property Act s. 59: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_04). A vacant lot has no Form B; the document matters here when a buyer compares a lot purchase against a resale in an existing Mission strata.
- **Depreciation reports.** Since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more strata lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years; stratas with fewer than five lots sit UNDER that threshold and are exempt (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-regulations/depreciation-reports). A developer creating a new strata on Mission land creates this obligation with the strata.
- **Contingency reserve fund.** Since November 1, 2023, a strata must contribute a minimum of 10% of its annual operating budget to the CRF (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/finances).
- **Rentals.** Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID province-wide since November 24, 2022 (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-regulations/changes-to-legislation) — a future strata on this land could not lawfully carry one, and the directory "rentals not allowed" lines carried for Cherry Lane in the sibling files are void in-entry wherever they appear. Short-term rentals are a separate matter a bylaw can still restrict.
- **Age.** Since November 24, 2022, the only age-restriction bylaw a BC strata may enforce is 55+ (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1–123.2 and Regulation 7.01: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_08), with exemptions for spouses/partners, caregivers and legacy residents, including the May 1, 2023 expansion. A 45+ bylaw is void and does NOT convert to 55+.

**Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple (non-strata) townhouse carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — municipal rules plus any registered covenants or a homeowners' association only. Most cards on this feed are fee-simple land, which is precisely why no strata expectation should be imported onto them.

**Property taxes — two systems, never mixed.**

**Purchase taxes, rebates and financing (BC/Canada-wide).**

- **First-time home buyers' GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on **March 13, 2026** (CRA News Release No. 122 — March 13 governs; any sibling carrying March 12 is superseded on this point). 100% of GST on a new home up to $1,000,000, phasing out to $1,500,000, maximum rebate $50,000, for qualifying purchases from March 20, 2025 to 2031 (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html). How GST applies to a specific vacant-lot purchase depends on the seller's and the parcel's status — take conveyancing advice per parcel; the rebate frame matters for a qualifying new home built on the lot.
- **New Housing Rebate (GST/HST).** 36% of the GST on new or substantially renovated housing, maximum $6,300, phasing out between $350,000 and $450,000 (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/new-housing-rebate.html).
- **Property Transfer Tax (PTT).** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% on the portion above $2,000,000, PLUS a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. A summary reading "Over $3M — 5%" compresses the 3% + 2% structure and is flagged wherever it appears — the tax is not a flat 5% (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/understand). Exemptions: first-time buyers — full exemption on the first $500,000 where fair market value is $835,000 or less, phasing out to $860,000; newly built homes — full exemption to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions).
- **Home Buyers' Plan / First Home Savings Account.** HBP $60,000 from RRSPs (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsp-related-plans/what-home-buyers-plan.html); FHSA $40,000 lifetime at $8,000 per year (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html).
- **Mortgage insurance.** CMHC premiums run 0.6%–4.5% of the mortgage amount (https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/industry-innovation-and-leadership/industry-expertise/resources/mortgage-loan-insurance-and-securitization/mortgage-insurance-premiums). No page defect is asserted here: this slug's research file documents no CMHC premium error on a Mission lots & land page, and land/construction financing terms differ from a completed-home mortgage in any case.
- **Presales (REDMA).** Where a developer sells homes to be built on assembled Mission land, the buyer must receive the disclosure statement with deposits held in trust under s. 18 and has a 7-day right of rescission under s. 21 of the Real Estate Development Marketing Act (https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/04066_01). A raw-lot contract carries no such rescission right.

## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: Can I buy an ALR lot and build strata townhomes on it?**
A: No. ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6), a strata plan deposit is a subdivision (Strata Property Act s. 239), and ALR subdivision needs Commission permission (Agricultural Land Commission Act s. 21(1)). The ALR housing frame is one principal residence to 500 m² plus at most one additional residence of 90 m² or 186 m² by parcel size — a house-and-second-residence frame, not a multi-unit strata frame (https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/).

**Q: Who do I pay property tax to on a vacant lot, and what is the penalty?**

Confirm the tenure line and exactly what transfers before valuing a card as vacant land.

**Q: Can I subdivide a Mission-area acreage?**
A: It depends on the governing authority and on ALR status: City parcels follow the City's process, FVRD parcels the regional district's, and ALR subdivision needs ALC approval — with even a lease of part of an ALR parcel for more than 3 years counting as a subdivision requiring the Commission's permission (https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf). "Subdividable" in listing text clears neither gate.

**Q: Can I put a secondary suite in a townhouse I build on a Mission lot?**
A: No — in the City of Mission suites are not permitted in townhouses or rowhomes at all; a suite must be a self-contained living space inside a single family home, registered with the City, through the building permit process (https://www.mission.ca/business-building/building-development/land-development/secondary-suites-program). Feed claims for suite or coach-home potential (UC372, RR7s) are listing text, UNVERIFIED against the bylaw.

**Q: How do I price the servicing gap between lots?**
A: Feed cards range from fully serviced city lots to raw acreage described with a drilled well — all listing text for that parcel only. Parcel-level servicing status, connection costs and DCCs are UNVERIFIED in the research file; confirm water, septic approval, connection costs and DCCs for the specific PID in writing before comparing a raw parcel's price to a serviced lot's price (research file, as above).

**Q: If I build a new home on the lot, what first-buyer help exists?**
A: The first-time buyer GST rebate (100% of GST to $1,000,000, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1,500,000, Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026), the BC newly built home PTT exemption (full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000), the Home Buyers' Plan ($60,000) and the FHSA ($40,000 lifetime) — each with its own eligibility rules; confirm the build contract's structure qualifies before counting any of them (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions).
