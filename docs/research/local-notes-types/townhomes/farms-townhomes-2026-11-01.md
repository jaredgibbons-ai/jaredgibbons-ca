## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**The strata block — stated for the contrast, because on this topic its main work is prohibitory.**

- **Form B diligence.** SPA s. 59: Form B within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢ per page — fees, amounts owing, special levies, CRF balance, insurance, rules, budget, Rental Disclosure Statement and depreciation report attached. **No Form B exists in a farm purchase** — there is no strata corporation; the diligence is title, ALC status, BC Assessment classification and municipal zoning (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate).
- **Depreciation reports / CRF (strata only).** Stratas with 5+ lots must obtain a depreciation report every 5 years since July 1, 2024 (a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER the threshold); CRF minimum 10% of the operating budget since November 1, 2023. Neither applies to a freehold farm — the farm's capital reserve is the owner's own barns, shops and equipment (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing).
- **Rentals / age (strata law).** Strata rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022, and only 55+ strata age bylaws are valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01; spouse/caregiver/legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion; a 45+ bylaw is void and does NOT convert to 55+). Neither rule reaches a freehold farm parcel — tenancy of a farm residence is a Residential Tenancy Act / farm-worker housing question, not a bylaw question (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation).
- **Fee-simple contrast — this topic IS the fee-simple case.** A farm purchase carries NO strata, NO Form B, NO strata fees — title, the applicable municipality's rules, the ALC frame where the parcel is in the ALR, and any registered covenants only.

**The ALR and farm-classification frame — the rules that actually govern this topic.**

- **ALR housing frame (province-wide, ALC):** on ALR land, one principal residence is permitted without an ALC application at a maximum total floor area of 500 m² (no more than 1,000 m² of fill); a secondary suite is permitted only when located within the principal residence; and at most one additional residence is permitted of up to 90 m² on properties 40 ha or less or up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha. Local government or First Nation government bylaws may further regulate or restrict residences, and may not be more permissive than the ALC's requirements. **ALR residences cannot be stratified** (ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6; SPA s. 239; ALCA s. 21(1)) (research file §4; https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/).
- **BC Assessment farm classification (province-wide, full three-tier form):** farm classification is a voluntary, property-by-property program. The income thresholds are **$2,500 if the total area of the farm operation is between 0.8 ha (1.98 acres) and 4 ha (10 acres); $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if the operation is greater than 4 ha; and $10,000 if the operation is less than 0.8 ha** — a flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR and is never used. Applications must be submitted to BC Assessment by October 31 for the next assessment and tax year (late applications are not accepted), and a buyer who plans to continue farming a farm-classified property must submit a General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase (research file §4; https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification).

The live page's South Surrey A-1/A-2 reference is a link to a separate page's scope, recorded as site copy only (research file §4).

## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: Are market statistics published for farms in BC?**
A: No figure is stated in this file: SnapStats has no farm category, and no official source opened publishes farm sale counts, per-acre prices or farm days on market — each is UNVERIFIED and a price opinion stands on the specific property (research file §7).

**Q: Is every property on the farms portal a working farm?**
A: No — as read, its describable cards include two unbuilt agricultural Land parcels, a hobby-farm acreage that is not in the ALR, a 0.63-acre residential lot with farm-adjacent zoning permissions, and a rancher whose card text connects it to farming only by backing onto a farm. The feed is generic; no card is a confirmed sector operation. Read each card on its own text (§1, §2; research file §7).

**Q: Can a farm residence be turned into strata townhomes?**
A: Not on ALR land within the ALC frame: ALR residences cannot be stratified — a strata plan deposit is a subdivision (SPA s. 239) and subdivision of agricultural land is prohibited unless the Commission permits it after an application or the ALR General Regulation permits it (ALCA s. 21(1); ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6). That is why this townhomes file counts zero (§2.1, §3).

**Q: What residence can an ALR farm parcel have?**
A: Under the ALC frame: one principal residence of 500 m² or less (no more than 1,000 m² of fill), a secondary suite only within the principal residence, and at most one additional residence of up to 90 m² on properties 40 ha or less or up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha, with local government rules possibly more restrictive — and only where the parcel is actually in the ALR (§3; research file §7).

**Q: What income must a farm show to keep farm classification?**
A: BC Assessment's thresholds, in full: $2,500 for an operation of 0.8 ha to 4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha for a larger operation; and $10,000 for an operation under 0.8 ha. A flat "$2,500+" is an error. Applications are due by October 31 for the next tax year (§3; research file §7).

**Q: Does buying farm land automatically keep its farm classification?**
A: No — classification is a voluntary, property-by-property BC Assessment program with income thresholds by operation size, and a buyer who plans to continue farming must submit a General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase or the classification can be removed (§3; research file §7).

**Q: How is PTT calculated on a farm purchase?**
A: The general tiers are 1% / 2% / 3% plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — worked examples: $700,000 → $12,000; $1,000,000 → $18,000; $1,200,000 → $22,000 (inputs and tier math in §3). But the apportionment of PTT and GST between land, buildings and residence on a specific farm is UNVERIFIED in this file — model it with agricultural counsel before offering (§3).

**Q: A farm listing shows two homes already — is the second one legal?**
A: Not to be assumed. On ALR land the permitted frame is one principal residence (≤500 m²), a suite only inside it, and at most one additional residence (90 m² / 186 m² by parcel size) without an ALC application — a property already carrying two dwellings needs its approvals history checked, parcel by parcel (§3).

**Q: Where does a retiring farmer actually buy a 55+ home?**
A: In the farm-community cities, on the verified rosters — Abbotsford, Chilliwack and Langley City hold the program's verified 55+ ownership stock; the farm sub-areas themselves hold none in any source checked (§2.4; 55-plus sibling).

**Q: What should a farm seller document before listing?**
A: The farm income history supporting classification and any water licences or ALC approvals in place — those are the items a farm buyer's lender and lawyer ask for first (research file §6).
