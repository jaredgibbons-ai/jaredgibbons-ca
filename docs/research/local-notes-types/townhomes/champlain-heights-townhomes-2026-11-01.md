## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **Tenure first (the local rule that overrides everything else here):** Champlain Heights mixes freehold detached houses, strata townhouses and leasehold complexes from its planned build-out; lead every purchase with a tenure check and confirm tenure and lease terms (expiry, rent, prepaid vs monthly) on title before pricing, because lease terms change both financing and value. A strata Form B exists only for a strata purchase — it is not the document set for a co-op or a leasehold interest. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-4/champlain-heights.md)
- **Form B diligence (the three strata complexes in Section 2):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. Listing-level rental/pet lines in Section 2 are listing data, not bylaws — the Form B and registered bylaws govern. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote. **CRF minimum:** since Nov 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. Marine Woods' listing copy calls its strata "proactive" with a contingency fund — the report and the Form B figures, not the adjective, govern. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since Nov 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. Highgate's directory "rentals not allowed" line is flagged VOID in-entry in Section 2 for exactly this reason. The provincial rule does not convert a leasehold or co-op interest into a strata rental — the underlying tenure documents govern those products. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, exemptions expanded May 1, 2023). No Champlain Heights townhouse complex is published as 55+ in the sources checked — age bylaws: UNCONFIRMED per complex; check the Form B and registered bylaws (sibling 55-plus file). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple homes (the detached streets):** NO strata, NO Form B, NO strata fees — municipal rules (Zoning and Development By-law No. 3575, R1-1 schedule) and any registered covenants only, stated plainly. No fee-simple townhouse complex is verified in the area (Section 2).
- **City zoning:** Vancouver's zoning is the Zoning and Development By-law **No. 3575**. The house streets sit under the R1-1 District Schedule — minimum site area 306 m² for a single detached house or duplex, 464 m² for a multiple dwelling with 5 units, 557 m² for 6 to 8 units; a single detached house may include a secondary suite and a laneway house, and a multiplex may have up to 6 units or up to 8 rental units. Champlain Heights South is also the subject of a site-specific CD-1 district — CD-1 (139), Champlain Heights South, By-law No. 5381 (amending By-law No. 3575), whose permitted uses include townhouse, including stacked townhouses, and apartments. ## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The leases were a deliberate 1970s plan, and renewals are live now.** Champlain Heights was planned in the early 1970s as a showcase community of curved roads and cul-de-sacs mixing housing types and incomes, and developed in the early 1980s on a large tract of City-owned land — which is why so much of it sits on prepaid 99-year City leases expiring in the 2080s. CHF BC records that the City developed the area from 1974 to 1986 leasing sites to non-market housing sponsors instead of selling them, and its lease-renewal update records new long-term lease approvals for co-ops including Cedar Mill (July 2025) — a buyer of any leasehold or co-op interest here is buying into an active lease-renewal landscape, not a settled one. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champlain_Heights and https://www.chf.bc.ca/vancouver-lease-renewals-backgrounder-and-update/
- **Everett Crowley Park was a landfill.** The park at 8200 Kerr Street adjoining the neighbourhood (40 hectares per City web copy; 38.08 hectares per the Park Board dataset — quote either with its source) is a former city landfill and the area's green core, with walking trails and an off-leash area for dogs — relevant context for homes on its edge. - **The mall's zoning is older than the neighbourhood.** The Champlain Mall CD-1 (By-law No. 4550) was approved at a public hearing April 20, 1970, before the planned community was built out; the mall parcel at East 54th Avenue and Kerr Street (3200 East 54th Avenue) has carried a public library inside it since that era, and the standalone Champlain Heights Branch library is now at 7110 Kerr Street. https://council.vancouver.ca/990727/ub1a.htm
- **Zoning already contemplates apartments here — tenure is the constraint, not the bylaw.** CD-1 (139), Champlain Heights South (By-law No. 5381, amending By-law No. 3575), lists townhouse including stacked townhouses and apartments among its permitted uses — the absence of apartment condos reflects the leasehold / co-op build-out that actually happened, not a zoning prohibition. https://cd1-bylaws.vancouver.ca/cd-1(139).PDF
- **The neighbourhood has its own library branch but no community centre of its own name.** The Champlain Heights Branch library is at 7110 Kerr Street inside the neighbourhood, while pool, rink and fitness services are at Killarney Community Centre, 6260 Killarney Street — and the library serving the wider Killarney file carries this neighbourhood's name, a small sign of how completely the two names interleave on the ground even though the board files them as separate sub-areas. https://vancouver.ca/parks-recreation-culture/killarney-community-centre.aspx
- **Health care is a community centre, not a hospital, on this side.** The South Community Health Centre, 6405 Knight Street, provides public and community health services for South Vancouver, west of Champlain Heights; the nearest hospital confirmed on a health-authority page for this sub-area: UNVERIFIED in the sources checked — do not quote one without checking. https://www.vch.ca/en/location-service/home-health-south-community-health-centre
