## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **Form B diligence (the counted strata and the cross-referenced one):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. At Shaughnessy Park, the Form B package is also where this file's two open items settle: the complex's strata plan/total (listings don't state the total; the 32-home figure is the siblings' city-wide research figure) and the unit's exact position under the bylaws (pets with restrictions, per the directory record). At Citadel Pointe, the assembly file's LMS3766 / 1999 facts come from a listing MLS sheet — confirm against the Form B there too. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold. Shaughnessy Park (32 homes) and Citadel Pointe are both above the threshold on their published figures. **CRF minimum:** since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since Nov 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. **Flagged in-entry, as required:** the published bylaw lines for both Shaughnessy Park (§2, "rentals allowed with restrictions") and Citadel Pointe (§2, "rentals allowed w/restrictions") each contain a rental-restriction element that is VOID — a buyer may rent the unit long-term regardless of what the directory line says; only the non-rental elements of those bylaw summaries (pets, number limits) stand as published. The same applies to Portica West's published line in the apartment section. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, exemptions expanded May 1, 2023 for a younger spouse/partner, live-in caregivers, legacy residents and defined children). In PoCo that frame has exactly one ownership application city-wide — Mayfair Terrace, apartment form (§2) — and no application to either townhouse complex in this file, neither of which carries any age restriction in any source. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple reality — stated plainly.** No fee-simple townhouse complex verifies in Glenwood PQ (§2) — and the one fresh name that might have been fee-simple (Westmount Park) is queued precisely because its tenure could not be stated. A fee-simple townhome, where one exists elsewhere, carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — municipal rules plus registered covenants/HOA only. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How many townhouse complexes are in Glenwood PQ?** One is counted in this file — Shaughnessy Park (3655 Shaughnessy Street, strata, 32 homes) — and one more, Citadel Pointe (678/688 Citadel Dr, freehold strata LMS3766, 1999), is counted in the PoCo assembly townhomes file and cross-referenced in §2. Three more named complexes (Lexford, The Summit, Citadel Mews) sit in the program's queue, counted nowhere, and a fourth fresh name (Westmount Park, 2139 Prairie Ave, built 2016) is queued because its tenure could not be verified — §2 names all of them so nothing is hidden and nothing is double-counted. (§2 above)
- **Q: Why isn't Westmount Park counted? It looks like a normal 2016 townhouse complex.** Because no source opened states whether it is strata or fee-simple, or how many units it has — and this program counts a complex only when STRATA vs fee-simple can be stated expressly. It is documented in §2 as a queue item with exactly what is missing; a title or strata-source pull settles it and it enters the count then. (§2 above)
- **Q: Shaughnessy Park's directory line says "rentals allowed with restrictions." Can I actually rent my unit out?** Yes, long-term — that restriction element has been void province-wide since November 24, 2022. The pets element of the same published line (pets allowed with restrictions) stands as published. Short-term rentals are a separate bylaw question — confirm on the Form B. (§3 above)
- **Q: Is Shaughnessy Park from 1982 or 1983?** UNCONFIRMED — one current listing records 1982; a building directory and other listings record 1983. The conflict is carried openly in §2 rather than settled by picking the convenient figure; the strata plan and title settle it for a specific purchase. (§2 above)
- **Q: Is there a 55+ townhouse in Port Coquitlam?** No verified one anywhere in the city. PoCo's only verified 55+ ownership complex is Mayfair Terrace (2266 Atkins Ave) — an apartment-form strata with a mandatory Aspira service plan ($715/mo first resident / $400 second, listing-stated) — and its other 55+ name, Kew Lifestyles at Montrose, is rental. Neither counted townhouse complex here carries any age restriction. (§2 above)
- **Q: The Lexford name shows up as both an apartment and a townhouse. Which is it?** Both references exist and they are kept apart: the apartment building at 2178 Prairie Avenue (built 2026, Polygon) is counted as an apartment condo in the condos sibling; the townhouse reference survives only in listing text with no verified address, tenure or unit count, and sits in the queue, counted nowhere. Do not let a listing merge them. (§2 above)
- **Q: Will the SkyTrain come to Port Coquitlam and lift Glenwood values?** There is nothing to price in: a Port Coquitlam SkyTrain extension is at the exploratory business-case stage in the Mayors' Council February 19, 2026 work plan and TransLink's Transport 2050 priorities — no funding, no route, no dates. The rail service that exists is the West Coast Express from Port Coquitlam Station, five trains each way on weekdays. (§1 above)
- **Q: What do the SnapStats numbers actually say about Glenwood townhouses?** They don't split townhouses out. September 2026, as the report states it: Glenwood Condos & Townhomes combined — 41 active, 7 sold, 17% sales ratio; the only attached median published is the Port Coquitlam community median of $660,000. Anyone quoting a "Glenwood townhouse median" is inventing it. (§1 above)
- **Q: Which schools serve a Shaughnessy Street address?** UNVERIFIED per address. The one completed SD43 locator run on record in this sub-area (3064 Glen Drive, run 2026-10-23) resolved to Glen Elementary → École Maple Creek Middle → Pinetree Secondary — a result for that address, not a blanket catchment. Confirm the exact address with SD43. (§1 above)
- **Q: Is Glenwood in the floodplain?** Much of Port Coquitlam is Fraser/Pitt floodplain behind dykes under the City's Hazard Lands DPA — but Glenwood lot-level flood status is UNVERIFIED, and no Glenwood-specific figure should be quoted from a listing. Check the specific lot against the City's mapping and the Form B/title for any flood covenant. (§1 above)
- **Q: What are PoCo's tax dates for budgeting?** 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026 — 5% penalty after July 2 and a further 5% after September 15, 2026 — and flat annual utility charges were due March 31, 2026, billed separately from taxes. (§3 above)

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **"Glenwood" is a stats name, not a city neighbourhood.** The City OCP (Bylaw No. 3838) never designates "Glenwood" as a neighbourhood, and the City's own profile boundaries are not stated in its profile text — the name lives in the board/SnapStats sub-area system. Practical consequence: zoning, permits and catchment questions are answered lot-by-lot from Bylaw 3630 and SD43, never from the sub-area label. (§1 above)
- **The sub-area's stats hide the townhouses inside a combined bucket.** SnapStats' Glenwood line merges condos and townhomes (41 active / 7 sold / 17% in Sep 2026) and publishes no sub-area median at all — so the two counted townhouse complexes in this file compete, statistically, invisibly inside a number dominated by apartment buildings like The Arden and The Sapphire. The community attached median ($660,000) is the only honest attached figure to quote. (§1 above)
- **Two of this sub-area's complexes answer to the same developer name in different programs.** Lexford is simultaneously a completed 2026 apartment building counted in condos (2178 Prairie Ave) and an unverified townhouse reference in the queue — the program keeps the name in both places with the split stated, because collapsing them either invents a townhouse complex or erases an apartment building. (§2 above)
- **The queue is the honest part of the PoCo townhouse map.** Of the six named townhouse products touching this sub-area in the sources, exactly two are counted (one here, one in the assembly file) and four are queued with the missing fact named for each — tenure for Westmount Park and Citadel Mews, verification standard for The Summit, everything beyond listing text for Lexford. A buyer told "PoCo has lots of townhouse complexes" is being quoted the queue as if it were stock. (§2 above)
- **PoCo's entire 55+ ownership market is one apartment building with a service contract.** Mayfair Terrace's mandatory Aspira plan ($715/mo first resident / $400 second, listing-stated) sits *on top of* strata fees — a carrying-cost structure no townhouse in this file has — and it is the only 55+ ownership option in the city; the rental alternative (Kew Lifestyles at Montrose) is the only other 55+ door. (§2 above)
- **The West Coast Express is a weekday-only, peak-only service.** Five trains each direction, no weekend or holiday service — a Glenwood townhouse bought for a downtown commute works on that schedule or not at all, and the SkyTrain business case changes nothing on any published timeline. (§1 above)
- **Shaughnessy Park's age is genuinely unsettled in the public record.** 1982 in one current listing, 1983 in the directory and other listings — a one-year gap that matters for depreciation-report reading and insurance, carried as a conflict in §2 rather than averaged away. (§2 above)
