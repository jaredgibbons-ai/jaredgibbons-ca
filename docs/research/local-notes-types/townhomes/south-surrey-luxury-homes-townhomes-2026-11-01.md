## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

A topic file carries no complexes of its own, so this section states the standard block a buyer needs when a search on this page leads them to a real strata townhouse on South Surrey ground (counted in the files named in §2.3), plus the two-city municipal frame this feed crosses.

**The strata block (BC standard — applies to every strata townhouse cross-referenced in §2.3).**

- **Form B diligence.** A buyer of a strata townhouse is entitled to an Information Certificate (Form B) under s. 59 of the Strata Property Act, provided within seven days of request; the fee is capped at $35 plus up to 25 cents per page for paper copies. The Form B states the monthly strata fee, money the owner owes the strata, any special levies approved but not yet due, the contingency reserve fund (CRF) balance, the strata's insurance, its rules, its current budget and financial statement, any Rental Disclosure Statement, and the current depreciation report attached (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/strata-information-buyers-sellers; Strata Property Act, s. 59: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_04).
- **Depreciation reports.** Since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more strata lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years; stratas with fewer than five lots sit UNDER that threshold and are exempt (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-regulations/depreciation-reports).
- **Contingency reserve fund.** Since November 1, 2023, a strata must contribute a minimum of 10% of its annual operating budget to the CRF (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/finances).
- **Rentals.** Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID province-wide since November 24, 2022 — any directory line for a cross-referenced complex reading "rentals not allowed" is void and is flagged as such in the file that counts that complex (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-regulations/changes-to-legislation). Short-term rentals are a separate, municipal matter (below).
- **Age.** Since November 24, 2022, the only age-restriction bylaw a BC strata may enforce is 55+ (Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1–123.2: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_08; Regulation 7.01), with exemptions for spouses/partners, caregivers and legacy residents, expanded May 1, 2023. A 45+ bylaw is void and does NOT convert to 55+. The South Surrey presales in §2.3 (Harlowe, Calais, Rockford) are explicitly NOT 55+.

**Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple (non-strata) townhouse would have NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — only municipal rules plus any registered covenants or a homeowners' association. The detached houses that fill this page's feed are fee-simple purchases of exactly that kind (land plus house, municipal rules and title covenants only) — which is precisely why they are not townhouse-complex stock.

**Purchase taxes, rebates and financing (BC/Canada-wide).**

- **First-time home buyers' GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on **March 13, 2026** (CRA News Release No. 122 — March 13 governs; any sibling file stating March 12 is superseded on this point). 100% of GST on a new home up to $1,000,000, phasing out to $1,500,000, maximum rebate $50,000, for qualifying purchases from March 20, 2025 to 2031 (Canada Revenue Agency: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculate/gst-hst-new-housing-rebate.html).
- **New Housing Rebate.** 36% of GST on new or substantially renovated housing, maximum $6,300, phasing out between $350,000 and $450,000 (Canada Revenue Agency: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/new-housing-rebate.html).
- **Property Transfer Tax.** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% on the portion above $2,000,000, PLUS a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. A summary row reading "Over $3M — 5%" compresses the 3% + 2% structure and is flagged wherever it appears — the tax is not a flat 5% (BC Government, PTT: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/understand). At this page's price points the upper tiers are the rule, not the exception: a $2,600,000 purchase with no exemption pays $2,000 + $36,000 + $18,000 = $56,000 before the further 2% residential-portion levy is considered (that levy applies only above $3,000,000). Exemptions: first-time buyers — full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, phasing out to $860,000; newly built homes — full exemption to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 (BC Government, PTT exemptions: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions) — both thresholds sit far below this feed's visible prices.
- **Home Buyers' Plan / First Home Savings Account.** HBP limit $60,000 (Canada Revenue Agency: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsp-related-plans/what-home-buyers-plan.html); FHSA $40,000 at $8,000 per year (Canada Revenue Agency: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html).
- **Mortgage insurance.** CMHC premiums run 0.6%–4.5% of the mortgage amount by loan-to-value (CMHC: https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/industry-innovation-and-leadership/industry-expertise/resources/mortgage-loan-insurance-and-securitization/mortgage-insurance-premiums). No page defect is asserted in this file: this slug's research documents no CMHC premium error on this page — the known 2.80%–4.00% page error pattern is NOT documented for this slug and is therefore not asserted here.
- **Presales.** A presale buyer has a 7-day right of rescission under s. 21 of the Real Estate Development Marketing Act, and developers must hold deposits in trust under s. 18 (BC Laws, REDMA: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/04066_01) — the frame for the South Surrey presales in §2.3.
