### The strata form that does apply to land — bare-land strata (documented as its own form, NOT counted)

- **Bare-land strata:** the Strata Property Act defines a "bare land strata plan" as a strata plan on which the boundaries of the strata lots are defined on a horizontal plane by reference to survey markers and not by reference to the floors, walls or ceilings of a building. In a bare-land strata, the strata lot is essentially a plot of land: the plan is filed before any homes are constructed, the lot owner builds (or contracts to build) the home on the lot, and the common property can include roads, streetlights, a septic system, a clubhouse, playground and forested areas. You cannot tell by looking whether detached homes sit on a bare-land strata — the words "bare land strata" appear on the first page of the strata plan. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol27/consol27/98043_01 and https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/
- **What that means for a Langley lot buyer:** if a lot is a bare-land strata lot, there IS a strata corporation — with bylaws, strata fees for the common property, and the Section 3 strata rules — even though there is no building. If the lot is freehold, none of that applies. The title and, where one exists, the strata plan decide which purchase you are in. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **The townhome-relevant frame on land is buildability, stated plainly:** a buyer who wants townhouse-form product on a Langley lot is buying zoning, servicing and approvals — Township Zoning Bylaw No. 2500 (including its townhouse/rowhouse zones and the R-CL compact-lot rules below) in the Township, and City Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300 (R2 Townhouse Residential) in the City — not an existing complex. No strata, no Form B and no strata fees attach to a freehold lot itself. https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/zoning-bylaw.aspx and https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/zoning-bylaw
- **Bare-land strata exception — Form B diligence:** where a lot IS a bare-land strata lot, a resale purchase still runs through the strata: Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. For a freehold lot there is no Form B at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports (bare-land stratas included):** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote; a strata with four or fewer lots sits UNDER that threshold. A bare-land strata's report covers the common property (roads, systems, amenities), not the houses on the lots. **CRF minimum:** since Nov 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental and age bylaws (bare-land stratas):** rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since Nov 24, 2022 — any "rentals not allowed" line is void for long-term rentals; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. Only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2), with Regulation 7.01 exemptions expanded May 1, 2023. Both rules apply to a bare-land strata exactly as to a building strata. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Insurance difference a bare-land buyer must know:** the Strata Property Act requires the strata corporation to insure common property, common assets, and buildings shown on the strata plan — in a bare-land strata the house sits entirely inside the strata lot and is not shown as a building on the plan, so the lot owner insures the house; the strata does not. https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/
- **Fee-simple lots — stated plainly:** NO strata, NO Form B, NO strata fees — diligence is title, municipal rules (the applicable zoning bylaw below) and any registered covenants or HOA documents only. Basis: Section 2 above.
- **Township SSMUH — Bylaw 6020 (adopted November 18, 2024):** allows 3 units on lots up to 280 m² and 4 units on lots under 4,050 m²; lots over 4,050 m² are ineligible, and **NO 6-plex tier exists in Bylaw 6020**. Secondary suite cap: 120 m². Added-unit housing eligibility requires a property to be connected to both municipal water and sewer; the Township's June 10, 2024 council report estimates approximately 55% of the Township's single-family and duplex lots (~15,000 properties) will qualify for 3 to 4 units, varying by servicing (Brookswood ~15% and Fort Langley ~22% eligible; Walnut Grove ~99% and Murrayville ~98%). https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/small-scale-multi-unit-housing.aspx and https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/resources/social-sustainability/housing/TOL-Council-Report---Provincial-Housing-Legislation,-June-10,-2024.pdf
- **Coach houses and suites — Township:** a coach house is a fee-simple second unit on a detached lot under Zoning Bylaw 2500 s. 102 — never strata, never a townhouse complex. Willoughby's compact-lot pattern is the Residential Compact Lot Zone R-CL (s. 403): lots run from a minimum lot area of 220 m² to a maximum of 464.5 m². https://www.tol.ca/en/services/resources/bylaw-services/bylaws/Zoning-Bylaws/Section-400-Residential-(R,-MH,-FH-and-CRE)-Zones.pdf
- **Willoughby plan status — reading stage flagged, never upgraded:** the Willoughby Community Plan was Bylaw 1836 (1979), now Schedule 6 to Bylaw 6200 (adopted Dec 1, 2025). The Willoughby Plan Update Bylaws 6201/6202/6203 were at first/second reading May 25, 2026 ONLY, with a Public Hearing directed for June 15, 2026 — never present them as adopted. Land in Willoughby (a first-page sub-area on this feed) prices off the adopted documents, not the reading-stage drafts. https://ic10.esolg.ca/11207577_TownshipofLangley/en/connect/resources/get-involved/engage-tol/2026/willoughby-community-plan-update/6201-Schedule-1.pdf
- **Zoning — the two Langleys, with the City adoption-date flag:** Township: Zoning Bylaw 1987 No. 2500 "applies to the entirety of the Township." City: Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300 "applies to the entire area within the boundaries of the City of Langley" and repeals Zoning Bylaw 1996 No. 2100 (the City's media library still serves a consolidated Bylaw 2100 file — Bylaw 3300 is the current source). **The adoption date of Bylaw 3300 is conflicted in sources and is UNVERIFIED in this file; the bylaw number (2025 No. 3300) is cited, not a date.** Minimum lot sizes (Bylaw 2500, s. 110.1, as reproduced in a Township council report): SR-1 — 3,716 m²; SR-2 — 8,094 m²; SR-3 — 1,765 m²; RU-1 — 1.7 ha; RU-2 — 8.0 ha; RU-3 — 8.0 ha; urban R-1B/R-1C/R-2 — 464.5 m²; RM-1 — 3,716 m²; RM-2/RM-3/RM-3A/RM-4 — 1,672 m². City (Bylaw 3300): R1 Suburban Residential — 557 m² minimum lot size / 16 m frontage; R2 Townhouse Residential — 1,000 m² (except lots created by subdivision with a registered party wall agreement: maximum 280 m², minimum frontage 4.5 m); R3 Low Rise Residential — 1,000 m² / 26.0 m. https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/zoning-bylaw.aspx and https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/zoning-bylaw and https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/resources/social-sustainability/housing/TOL-Council-Report---Provincial-Housing-Legislation,-June-10,-2024.pdf
- **Subdivision and servicing:** all Township subdivisions must be approved by the Approving Officer under the Land Title Act, through application, review, decision letter, conditions, final approval and registration in the Land Title Office; City Bylaw 3300 adds that meeting the minimum lot standards does not bind the Approving Officer to approve. Development cost charges: Township DCC Bylaw No. 5897; City DCC Bylaw 2024 No. 3256. Servicing upgrades are determined under the Township's Subdivision and Development Servicing Bylaw 2019 No. 5382; the Township's forms include Septic Sewer Certification and Well Testing. https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/subdivision.aspx (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/langley-lots-land.md)
- **ALR — residences cannot be stratified, and strata plans are subdivisions:** ALC Information Bulletin 09 (with Agricultural Land Commission Act s. 21(1) and Strata Property Act s. 239 in the governing frame) states that a person must not subdivide agricultural land unless permitted by the Commission after an application, or permitted under the ALR General Regulation, and that an approving officer (including under the Strata Property Act) is prohibited from approving a subdivision of ALR land unless so permitted (ALCA s. 18(5)); the bulletin devotes a section to "Strata Plans Cause Subdivision" — creating a bare-land strata on ALR land is itself a subdivision needing the same ALC permission. With 235 km² of the Township in the ALR, this is the rule underneath most rural Langley lot questions. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf and https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/about-the-township.aspx
- **ALR housing rules and the farm thresholds, in full three-tier form:** a principal residence built without an ALC application must have a total floor area of 500 m² or less, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill; a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence; and one additional detached residence is permitted up to 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or smaller or 186 m² on larger parcels, where Township bylaws also permit it. A flat "$2,500+" farm figure, where a page states one, is an ERROR against this tiered structure and is not used in this file. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Environment and servicing on lots:** Township development permit types include Streamside Protection and Enhancement (Schedule 3 of the Township OCP, for watercourses not in the ALR); a permit is required to cut or remove a protected tree under Tree Protection Bylaw 2019, No. 5478. A home not connected to a community sewer must use an onsite sewage system complying with the Sewerage System Regulation, designed and installed by an Authorized Person (Fraser Health); the provincial GWELLS registry holds recorded wells (older wells may not appear), domestic groundwater use needs no licence, and non-domestic use requires a water licence under the Water Sustainability Act. https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/development-activity.aspx and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/health-topics-a-to-z/onsite-sewerage-systems/regulatory-requirements-for-onsite-sewerage-systems
- **Taxes and rebates:** PTT general rates are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — a page row reading "Over $3M — 5%" is a compression of that 3% + 2% structure, not a single rate; PTT is payable on a land purchase registered at the Land Title Office unless an exemption applies. First-time buyers' exemption: full on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000 (a vacant lot's eligibility is a PTT-return question: UNCONFIRMED here for bare land). Newly built home exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000, for a qualifying newly built home. GST on a vacant-lot purchase depends on the seller and the lot's history — no single rate is stated here: UNCONFIRMED for any specific parcel. For a newly built home on the lot: 5% GST applies; FTHB GST rebate — Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 (100% to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, max $50,000, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031); New Housing Rebate 36% / max $6,300 / $350K–$450K. HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year (whether a bare lot alone qualifies is a program-conditions question — do not assume it); CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5% for insured home purchases (vacant-land financing is lender-specific and that schedule is not a land-loan rule). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Presales (REDMA):** where a developer markets strata lots (including bare-land strata lots), the buyer must receive the disclosure statement (REDMA s. 18, deposits in trust) and has a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing or acknowledging it (REDMA s. 21). https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Are there any townhouse complexes in this file?** None — count 0, stated plainly. This is a lots-and-land topic; a townhouse is a built home, not a lot. Langley townhouse complexes live in the Langley area townhomes files in this folder. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/langley-lots-land.md)
- **Q: What is a bare-land strata lot, in plain terms?** A plot of land that is a strata lot: the Strata Property Act defines a bare land strata plan by lot boundaries drawn on a horizontal plane by survey markers, not by a building's floors, walls or ceilings. You own the lot, you build the house, and a strata corporation owns the common property (roads, systems, amenities) and charges strata fees. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol27/consol27/98043_01
- **Q: How do I tell if a Langley lot is bare-land strata or freehold?** Not by looking — detached homes on a bare-land strata look like any detached homes. The words "bare land strata" appear on the first page of the strata plan at the Land Title Office; if the homes are drawn on the plan, it is a building strata instead. https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/
- **Q: Who insures the house on a bare-land strata lot?** You do. The strata corporation insures common property, common assets and buildings shown on the strata plan — a house entirely inside a bare-land strata lot is not shown on the plan, so it is the lot owner's policy, not the strata's. https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/
- **Q: Can I turn ALR land into a bare-land strata, or stratify an ALR residence?** No — ALR residences cannot be stratified, and ALC Information Bulletin 09 treats strata plans as plans that cause subdivision: subdivision of ALR land requires Commission permission after an application unless the ALR General Regulation permits it, and an approving officer (including under the Strata Property Act) cannot approve it otherwise. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Q: How much of Langley is in the ALR?** The Township of Langley states 235 square kilometres of the Township is within the Agricultural Land Reserve; check a specific parcel on the Agricultural Land Commission's maps. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/about-the-township.aspx and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Q: What is the smallest rural lot I can create in the Township?** It depends on the zone under Zoning Bylaw No. 2500, s. 110.1: RU-1 requires 1.7 ha for a new lot, RU-2 and RU-3 require 8.0 ha, SR-2 requires 8,094 m² and SR-1 requires 3,716 m² — and ALR land also needs Agricultural Land Commission permission to subdivide. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/resources/social-sustainability/housing/TOL-Council-Report---Provincial-Housing-Legislation,-June-10,-2024.pdf
- **Q: Does SSMUH let me put four homes — or six — on a Township lot?** Bylaw 6020 (adopted November 18, 2024) permits 3 units on lots up to 280 m² and 4 units on lots under 4,050 m², with eligibility turning on municipal water and sewer connection (about 55% of Township lots, ~15,000 properties, are estimated to qualify; Brookswood ~15%, Fort Langley ~22%, Walnut Grove ~99%, Murrayville ~98%). **There is no 6-plex tier in Bylaw 6020** — any source claiming one for the Township is wrong. Secondary suite cap is 120 m², and a coach house is a fee-simple second unit under s. 102, never strata. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/small-scale-multi-unit-housing.aspx
- **Q: Can I build a second home on a rural Langley lot?** On ALR land, one additional detached residence is permitted without an ALC application where the conditions are met — 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller, 186 m² or less on larger parcels — and Township bylaws must also permit it; the principal residence is capped at 500 m², and a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Are the Langley market numbers in this file land prices?** No. SnapStats publishes no land category; the Section 1 figures are Langley residential context only (detached median $1,345,000 and "Condos & Townhomes" combined median $611,000 in September 2026), and land-specific statistics are UNVERIFIED. ## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Two Langleys, two current zoning bylaws — and a stale one still online.** Township land sits under Zoning Bylaw 1987 No. 2500; City land under Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300, which repealed Bylaw 1996 No. 2100 — yet the City's media library still serves a consolidated Bylaw 2100 file. Quote Bylaw 3300 for the City; its adoption date is conflicted in sources and is UNVERIFIED here. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/zoning-bylaw
- **A strata plan is a subdivision in the ALR — even with no building.** ALC Information Bulletin 09 (October 18, 2022) devotes a section to "Strata Plans Cause Subdivision": filing a bare-land strata plan on reserve land triggers the same ALC permission requirement as a conventional subdivision.
