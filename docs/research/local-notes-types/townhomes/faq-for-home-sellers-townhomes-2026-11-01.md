## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

- **Regulator — the frame a seller-FAQ reader is actually hiring under:** BCFSA states a licensed real estate professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; the representation, duties and BCFSA complaint-route disclosures must happen at the outset of dealings (research file §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers).
- **Selling without a licence, verified at the regulator:** a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves — for example, by selling or renting out their own property — while a person must not provide real estate services to or on behalf of another, for or in expectation of remuneration, unless licensed or exempt. That supports the page's "not legally required" answer, carried as a page statement in §2.1 (research file §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information, read via search result 2026-11-01).
- **Property transfer tax is the buyer's tax:** PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office. The page's repeated statement — "property transfer tax is paid by the buyer, not the seller" — matches that frame (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html).
- **Cooling-off, for the offers answers:** where a buyer rescinds an accepted offer under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining deposit balance to the buyer, with no separate release form; otherwise a deposit is held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder, and is not automatically returned if subjects are not removed. The live FAQ page does not itself raise the rescission period; the sibling selling page does (research file §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits).
- **Form B diligence (for the strata townhome sale a reader of this page may go on to make).** SPA s. 59: Form B within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢ per page — fees, amounts owing, special levies, CRF balance, insurance, rules, budget, Rental Disclosure Statement and depreciation report attached. The FAQ page itself contains no strata information about any complex (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate).
- **Depreciation reports.** Stratas with 5+ lots must obtain a depreciation report every 5 years since July 1, 2024; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold. **CRF minimum:** 10% of the operating budget since November 1, 2023 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing).
- **Rentals.** Rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 for long-term tenancies. **Age.** Only 55+ age bylaws are valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01; May 1, 2023 exemptions) (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation).
- **Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhome carries NO strata, NO Form B, NO strata fees — municipal rules and registered covenants/HOA only, stated plainly.
- **City rules.** No single municipality governs this page — its scope spans Surrey (Surrey 2050 OCP **Bylaw No. 21900**, adopted June 15, 2026 — NEVER 18020 as current; Zoning Bylaw No. 12000; suite cap 40% of principal habitable area, one per unit, not a separate strata lot; STRs permitted owner-only with the licence CONFLICT $358/yr carried / $350/yr + fees disclosed) and the wider Fraser Valley and Greater Vancouver. Rules for any specific sale are that property's own municipality's bylaws — UNCONFIRMED from this page.
- **FTHB GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122: 100% of GST to $1,000,000, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1,500,000, agreements March 20, 2025–2031; CRA's wording for the phase-out is "gradually reduced." **New Housing Rebate:** 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out (CRA: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html).
- **PTT:** 1% / 2% / 3% tiers plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — an "Over $3M — 5%" row is a compression of the 3% + further 2% structure and is flagged wherever met; no such row is documented in this file's research. Worked anchors: $700K → $12,000; $1.0M → $18,000; $1.2M → $22,000; $1.5M → $28,000. Exemptions: first-time buyers full on first $500,000 at ≤$835,000, phasing to $860,000; newly built full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/understand).

## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: What is the faq-for-home-sellers.html page?**
A: Jared Gibbons' seller FAQ — five themed answer sections (Pricing & Timing; Marketing & Showings; Offers & Negotiation; Preparing Your Home; Costs & Logistics), an eight-question structured FAQ that repeats the themed answers in short form, and a home-evaluation call to action. It has no listings, no market data and no roster (§1; research file §7).

**Q: Why isn't my home selling?**
A: The page's answer, carried as a page statement: generally for one or more of three reasons — it is overpriced relative to comparable recent sales and current competition, it is not marketed well enough online and offline, or it does not show well; the fix starts with a home evaluation and an honest comparison against recent sales, active listings and expired or terminated listings (§2.1; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html).

**Q: Do I need a REALTOR® to sell my home in BC?**
A: No — BCFSA's licensing guidance confirms a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves, such as selling their own property; a licence (or an exemption) is required to provide real estate services to or on behalf of another person for remuneration (§3; https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information, read via search result 2026-11-01).

**Q: What are the costs of selling a home in BC?**
A: The page lists real estate commission (split between the listing agent and the buyer's agent), cleaning, staging and preparation, legal fees and disbursements, and a possible mortgage penalty if the mortgage is broken early — and states no commission figure, which BCFSA's framework places in the listing conversation, where a licensee must give consumers information about commissions and fees (§2.1, §3; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers).

**Q: Do I have to accept an offer on my home?**
A: The page's answer, carried as a page statement: no — on receiving an offer you can accept it as-is, counter, or reject it altogether; a counter voids the previous offer. The page's further "may be required to accept" a full-price cash offer matching listed terms is UNVERIFIED as a legal-effect claim in this file's research — read your own listing contract's stated terms and take that question to your agent or lawyer before relying on any general statement (§§2.1, 3; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html).

**Q: Who pays the property transfer tax when I sell?**
A: The buyer: PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office — when you buy your next home, you pay it as the buyer (§3; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax).

**Q: Should I price high and lower later?**
A: The page's answer, carried as a page statement: no — pricing high and reducing later fails because the first two weeks on market are critical and a reduced listing reads as stale. Price from sold evidence at launch (§2.1; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html).

**Q: Will renovations increase my sale price by what they cost?**
A: The page's position, carried as a page statement: renovations rarely return 100% of cost. Its specific return ranges (kitchen/bathroom 75–100% and the rest in §2.5) are attributed on the page to the Appraisal Institute of Canada but are UNVERIFIED in this file — no Institute source was opened (§2.5).

**Q: When is the best time to sell?**
A: The page's answer turns on home type, local and macro conditions, time of year and personal goals, with a REALTOR® tour and a sold/expired/active comparison as the recommended route — carried as a page statement. Its specific seasonality pattern (November–January slowest, spring pick-up by March, softer August, September rebound) is UNVERIFIED in this file — no board or SnapStats source was opened for it (§§2.1, 3).

**Q: How long is a listing contract?**
A: The page states Fraser Valley and Greater Vancouver listing contracts are typically 4–6 months, with a claimed 60-day minimum under the named board's rules — both UNVERIFIED in this file (no board rule page was opened), and the board name on the page ("Real Estate Board of Greater Vancouver") is dated wording; the current name in the site's own footer notices is Greater Vancouver REALTORS® (GVR). What governs any seller is the agreed expiry date in their own signed listing contract (§§1, 3).
