## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

For this topic file, this section carries the open-house frame first (verbatim, as the sibling topic files carry it), then the townhouse-purchase frame a buyer needs after the open house — because the open house is where a Maple Ridge townhouse purchase starts, and the strata documents are where it is actually diligenced.

### The open-house frame

- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3):** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, states that the disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Representation at an open house:** a licensed real estate professional in BC must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party to the transaction. The regulator is the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and the Province's Buying and selling a home page directs prospective purchasers to BCFSA for information on purchasing property in British Columbia. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home
- **What that means in practice:** chatting factually with the host at an open house does not by itself make you their client — but once you start sharing your motivation, budget or needs with a licensee who represents the seller, the disclosure rules engage. Use the townhouse open house for facts (fees, bylaws, parking, the depreciation report's date); save your story for your own agent. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Identification or sign-in for open-house visitors:** NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED as a rule; a host may still ask, as a matter of the seller's instructions, but that practice is not in an official source. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/maple-ridge-open-houses.md)

### The townhouse-purchase frame (after the open house)

- **Strata diligence — the Form B regime (Maple Ridge's confirmed townhouse stock is strata):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. The open house shows the unit; the Form B shows the complex. For the tenure-UNCONFIRMED complexes cross-referenced in Section 2 (Alouette Estates and the six Cottonwood names), whether a Form B regime even applies is itself unconfirmed until title is pulled. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote. **CRF minimum:** since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. A sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER the depreciation-report threshold — none of the cross-referenced complexes with published counts is that small (Gold Creek Estates 10, Carriage Lane 36, The Meadows at Verigin's Ridge 71), but confirm the lot count on the Form B for any complex whose count is UNCONFIRMED. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since November 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. An open-house host or feature sheet repeating a directory's "rentals not allowed" line for a Maple Ridge townhouse complex (Carriage Lane's directory line included) is repeating unenforceable text. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1, 123.2), with Regulation 7.01 exemptions expanded May 1, 2023. Maple Ridge's position is sharper than most cities': **Maple Ridge has ZERO verified 55+ strata townhouses** — Fraser Glen and Dogwood Lane are marketed 55+ in listings only, Fraserview Village's 55+ buildings are apartments, Haney Pioneer Village is a 60+ co-op, and St. George's Village is a co-op. Confirm on each Form B whether any registered bylaw is actually a 55+ bylaw before assuming who may live in a unit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple contrast:** where a Maple Ridge townhouse is genuinely fee-simple, it carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — municipal rules plus any registered covenants/HOA only. No fee-simple complex is counted in this topic file (it counts none at all), and no fee-simple townhouse complex is verified in any Maple Ridge area townhomes file on disk (East Central 0, Cottonwood 0, Southwest 0) — a "no-strata townhouse" on the feed needs its tenure proved from title, not from the card. Basis: Section 2 above.
