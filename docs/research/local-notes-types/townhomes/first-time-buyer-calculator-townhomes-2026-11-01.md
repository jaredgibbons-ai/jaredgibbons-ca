## 2. COMPLEXES

**This topic file carries NO complex roster of its own — verified townhouse complexes counted here: 0 (strata 0, fee-simple 0).** A province-wide calculator does not name complexes for one area; complex-by-complex notes belong in the area townhomes files in this series. What this section documents instead is the calculation itself — the numbers a first-time townhome buyer is solving for, in the order the official rules require them.

- **1. The sticker stack: price + GST (new only).** Resale townhomes are generally exempt from GST; a new townhome carries 5% GST on the price before rebates. The calculator's first fork is therefore resale vs newly built — it changes both the GST line and which PTT exemption is tested. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **2. Property transfer tax, gross then net.** Gross PTT is computed on the full fair market value at the general tiers (1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000), then the exemption the buyer actually qualifies for is subtracted — the first-time program (no PTT on the first $500,000 at values of $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000) or, for a new build, the newly built home exemption (full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **EXAMPLE 1 — resale townhome (illustrative arithmetic only; inputs stated, not a market fact).** Inputs: purchase price $700,000; resale (no GST); strata townhome; buyer qualifies for the first-time program; property 0.5 ha or smaller, principal residence. Gross PTT: 1% × $200,000 = $2,000, plus 2% × $500,000 = $10,000, total $12,000. First-time exemption: PTT on the first $500,000 = $2,000 + (2% × $300,000 = $6,000) = $8,000 saved. Net PTT payable: $12,000 − $8,000 = **$4,000**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **EXAMPLE 2 — new townhome (illustrative arithmetic only; inputs stated, not a market fact).** Inputs: purchase price $800,000; newly built (5% GST applies); buyer is an eligible first-time buyer under both federal and BC tests. GST: 5% × $800,000 = $40,000; First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate at this price (100% of GST up to $1M, maximum $50,000) = $40,000, net GST $0 if fully eligible and credited or claimed. Gross PTT: $2,000 + (2% × $600,000 = $12,000) = $14,000; Newly Built Home Exemption at $800,000 (full threshold $1,100,000) = $14,000, net PTT **$0** if all conditions are met. The example shows why the tool must test both BC programs: at $800,000 the newly built exemption ($14,000) is larger than the first-time exemption ($8,000). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **3. The down payment and the insurance premium.** Minimum down payment: 5% on the first $500,000 and 10% on the portion above $500,000 (homes $1,500,000 or more require 20% and are uninsurable). Under 20% down, CMHC mortgage insurance is required and the premium — 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount, by loan-to-value band — is usually added to the mortgage, which is why the calculator needs the down payment, not just the price. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **EXAMPLE 3 — insured purchase (illustrative arithmetic only; inputs stated, not a market fact).** Inputs: price $600,000; down payment 10% = $60,000; mortgage $540,000; loan-to-value 90%, in the 85.01%–90% band at a 3.10% premium on the CMHC schedule carried in the sibling condos file. Premium: 3.10% × $540,000 = **$16,740**, typically added to the mortgage balance. Minimum down payment at this price would have been 5% × $500,000 + 10% × $100,000 = $35,000. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **4. The savings room: FHSA and HBP.** FHSA participation room is $8,000 in the first year the account is opened, $40,000 lifetime in the standard block for this series — the research file could not re-read the lifetime limit on CRA's detailed page and marks it UNVERIFIED there; this file follows the series figure and flags that caution. The HBP allows an RRSP withdrawal for a first home, and CRA confirms a buyer can use both FHSA and HBP for the same qualifying home when all conditions are met; the research file could not re-read the current HBP limit and marks it UNVERIFIED there; the series standard figure is $60,000, flagged with the same caution. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **5. The townhome-only lines a generic calculator misses.** For a strata townhome, strata fees and any approved special levy are not purchase taxes, but they belong in the same affordability sheet: the Form B states the unit's monthly fee, any amount owed and any levy already approved. For a fee-simple townhome there is NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees. The home owner grant is an annual item after purchase (regular grant $570 as published, effective January 1, 2027; 2026 threshold $2,075,000) that must never be netted against closing costs. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

### The verified frame the calculator must encode

- **CMHC flag (top-flag):** CMHC publishes premium rates ranging from 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount, the premium is based on the size of the down payment and the loan amount, and it can be paid in full at purchase or added to the mortgage; mortgage loan insurance is required when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20%, it protects the lender rather than the buyer, and it allows qualification with as little as 5% down. A calculator page stating 2.80%–4.00% understates both ends of that published range and is WRONG. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **PTT flag:** the general tiers are 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000, plus — if a property has residential property worth over $3,000,000 — a further 2% tax on the residential property value greater than $3,000,000 (only the residential portion on a mixed-class property). A single row reading "Over $3M — 5%" compresses the 3% tier and the further 2% into one rate and misstates how each portion is taxed — flag it. PTT is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office and is not annual property tax. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **PTT — first-time home buyers' program:** the buyer must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, have lived in BC for at least a year immediately before registration (or filed at least 2 BC income tax returns in the last 6 taxation years), have never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world, and never have received the exemption before; full exemption requires principal-residence use only, fair market value $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), 0.5 hectares or smaller and only residential improvements, exempting PTT on the first $500,000; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and occupy to the first anniversary, and claims go through the buyer's legal professional in the PTT return (or by refund application from the first anniversary to 18 months after registration). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **PTT — newly built home exemption:** full exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), phasing out over the $50,000 range above it, eliminated at $1,150,000; a newly built home includes a unit in a newly built building not occupied since construction; citizenship, BC, principal-residence, 0.5-hectare and occupancy conditions mirror the first-time program. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **FTHB GST rebate:** Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 — 100% of GST to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, maximum $50,000, agreements March 20, 2025–2031; the application time limit is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction, a builder can credit the rebate at transfer, or the buyer files Form GST190 (builder purchase) or GST191 (owner-built). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
- **New Housing Rebate:** 36% of GST, maximum $6,300, phasing out for homes between $350,000 and $450,000. (Series standard block; Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **FHSA / HBP:** FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year (lifetime figure flagged with the research file's retrieval caution in Section 2); HBP $60,000 (same flag). CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the HBP for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Down payment:** for homes $500,000 or less, minimum 5%; for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require minimum 20% and mortgage loan insurance is not available. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Additional PTT:** a foreign national, foreign corporation or taxable trustee must also pay additional PTT on the residential portion in a specified area; the rate is published on a separate provincial page not re-read for the research file, so the rate is UNVERIFIED here. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **Taxes deliberately out of scope:** BC's speculation and vacancy tax and the BC home flipping tax are not purchase-time costs this calculator computes; whether the live tool mentions them is UNVERIFIED. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)

### The strata frame behind a townhome purchase

- **Form B (Strata Property Act s. 59):** the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote. **CRF minimum:** since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since November 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1, 123.2), with Regulation 7.01 exemptions expanded May 1, 2023. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Presales (REDMA):** where the townhome priced into the calculator is a presale, the buyer must receive the developer's disclosure statement (REDMA s. 18, deposits in trust) and has a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing or acknowledging it (REDMA s. 21). https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much PTT does a first-time buyer in BC actually save?** A qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price — a maximum saving of $8,000 on the EXAMPLE arithmetic in Section 2 — provided the fair market value is $835,000 or less, the property is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the principal residence; above $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: What are the general PTT rates the calculator starts from?** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% on the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% on the portion over $2,000,000 — plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000 where that applies. It is not a single "5% over $3M" rate. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Is a newly built townhome treated differently from a resale townhome?** Yes, twice over: the newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 (phase-out to $1,150,000) against the first-time program's $835,000, and the new home may attract 5% GST, which the federal first-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate can offset by up to $50,000. See EXAMPLE 2 in Section 2. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: How much can I put in an FHSA in the first year?** Participation room in the first year you open the account is $8,000, and contributions are generally tax deductible — which is why the account rewards being opened before the purchase year, not at offer time. The $40,000 lifetime figure is the series standard; the research file flags it as not re-read on CRA's detailed page. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same townhome?** Yes — CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. The HBP figure carried in this series is $60,000, flagged with the research file's retrieval caution. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Q: How much down payment do I actually need?** The floor is 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any portion above $500,000 (so $35,000 minimum on a $600,000 purchase — EXAMPLE 3 inputs), and 20% at $1,500,000 or more, where insurance is unavailable. Under 20% down adds the CMHC premium to the true cost. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: How much mortgage insurance will I pay — is it 2.80%–4.00%?** No — that range is WRONG against CMHC's published 0.6%–4.5% of the total mortgage amount. On the schedule carried in the sibling files, the 85.01%–90% loan-to-value band is 3.10%; EXAMPLE 3 in Section 2 works it at $540,000 of mortgage: $16,740. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: What does the calculator NOT tell me about a townhome purchase?** The complex. Strata fees, approved levies, CRF health and deductible exposure live in the Form B package, not in any tax calculator — and the live tool's own fields and formulas are NOT CONFIRMED in the research, so treat its output as an estimate to be re-run against the Form B and a legal professional's closing statement. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
- **Q: I bought, then had to move for work eight months later — do I lose the exemption?** Part of it can be repayable: the first-time and newly built exemptions both require moving in within 92 days of registration and occupying the home as a principal residence to the first anniversary of registration, and part may be kept if the buyer moves out early — the exact repayment is a PTT-return question for your legal professional, not a calculator output. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Should I count the home owner grant as closing money?** No — it is an annual reduction of property tax on a principal residence ($570 regular grant as published, effective January 1, 2027), claimed each year after you own and occupy; a calculator should not net it against purchase taxes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **April 1, 2024 is the date inside every threshold in this file.** The first-time program's $835,000 ceiling (partial to $860,000) and the newly built exemption's $1,100,000 threshold (out at $1,150,000) both took effect that day — a calculator, blog post or feature sheet using older thresholds is pre-2024 material. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **March 13, 2026 — the FTHB GST rebate became law.** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 per CRA's Excise and GST/HST News No. 122. The rebate reaches back to agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and runs before 2031. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **The federal and BC "first-time" tests are different tests.** BC's PTT program requires never having owned a principal-residence interest anywhere in the world plus a BC residence/tax-filing test; the federal GST rebate uses its own eligibility frame. Passing one does not prove the other — a single "first-time buyer?" toggle hides two separate determinations. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/first-time-buyer-calculator.md)
