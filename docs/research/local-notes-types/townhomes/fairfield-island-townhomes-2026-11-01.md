## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **Why this section still matters with zero stock:** if a strata purchase does occur here (a bare-land strata lot, as in §2), BC strata housing is governed by the Strata Property Act — and the standard block below is the rule set that would apply. Pure fee-simple detached purchases have no Form B; diligence runs on title, permits and the flood/ALR checks below instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing
- **Form B diligence:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale purchase in a strata (here, realistically a bare-land strata lot), the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. A listing line claiming "no other restrictions" for a bare-land strata (§2) is a listing statement, not the registered bylaws — read them on the Form B. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold. The §2 Williams Road example is described as a 6-unit bare-land strata — over the threshold on its stated count; its report covers the common property, not the houses on the lots. **CRF minimum:** since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since Nov 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. No counted entry exists in §2 to flag in-entry; the rule is stated so any directory "rentals not allowed" line surfacing for a Fairfield Island strata lot is read as void on exactly this rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, exemptions expanded May 1, 2023 for a younger spouse/partner, live-in caregivers, legacy residents and defined children). No Fairfield Island complex is verified as 55+ — there is no named complex at all (§2). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple contrast — stated plainly.** A fee-simple townhouse (and a fee-simple detached house, the dominant form here) carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees; municipal rules (Chilliwack Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000) plus any registered covenants or homeowners' association govern instead.
- **City plan and zoning:** the current plan is the Chilliwack 2050 OCP, Bylaw No. 5500, including the Schedule B2 Chilliwack Proper and Fairfield Island Neighbourhoods Plan and the Schedule D development permit areas (D-1 Drinking Water Supply, D-2 Hillside Environment, D-3 Riparian Area, D-4 Farm Home Plate, D-5 Hillside Development, D-6 Downtown, D-7 Mixed-Use Areas, D-8 Residential, D-9 Commercial, D-10 Industrial). Which DPAs apply to a given Fairfield Island parcel is a per-parcel check: UNVERIFIED for any specific parcel. Zoning is Zoning Bylaw No. 5000 (its §6 sets the farm zones). https://chilliwack.com/ocp
- **SSMUH and suites:** Chilliwack's SSMUH amendment is Zoning Bylaw Amendment Bylaw 2024, No. 5395, adopted June 18, 2024, allowing 3 to 4 dwelling units on parcels within the urban growth boundary zoned for single-detached and duplex housing. A secondary suite is capped at the lesser of 90 m² and 40% of the principal dwelling, one accessory dwelling unit is permitted per lot, garden suites are capped at 100 m², and parking rules sit at s. 5.04 (Zoning Bylaw No. 5000, ss. 2, 4.06, 5.04). Application to any specific parcel remains a per-parcel check. (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/rules-gaps-2026-11-01.md, items 6 and 7f)

- **ALR:** agriculture accounts for 67% of the City's total land base and 29% of economic activity on the City's own statement. Properties in the ALR are subject to the Agricultural Land Commission Act and regulations, ALC approval may be required before the City can approve related development, and the City directs owners to check ALR status on the City Webmap; an ALC application may be required for a new house, coach house or suite, for placing fill or removing soil, for non-farm uses, and for subdivision or a boundary adjustment. The OCP includes Development Permit Area 4 – Farm Home Plate, and a Development Permit is required when the Zoning Bylaw's Farm Home Plate regulations are not met. Farm-class thresholds, where farm class is in issue, run in the full three-tier form ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR wherever met. Exemptions: first-time buyers — exempt on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000; newly built home exemption — full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000. Worked example, inputs stated: a $600,000 resale detached house (the dominant form here) with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $400,000 ($8,000) = **$10,000**. FTHB GST rebate — Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122 (100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031); New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350K–$450K; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5%. 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. No CMHC defect is asserted here — this file's own research documents no such defect for this slug. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

- **The plan pairing surprises people:** Fairfield Island is not planned on its own — its neighbourhood plan is the joint Schedule B2 "Chilliwack Proper and Fairfield Island Neighbourhoods Plan," so plan searches run under only "Fairfield Island" miss documents filed under Chilliwack Proper. https://chilliwack.com/ocp
- **The plan expects infill, not a townhouse project.** The Schedule B-2 framework addresses infill development opportunities — redevelopment of existing large lots into smaller-lot single detached, duplex or row housing, some convenience commercial, and new attached housing along Hope River Road — and notes there are limited commercial services within the neighbourhood at present. No townhouse complex for Fairfield Island appears in any source checked. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=5211
- **Fairfield Island's housing era is stated in the City's own plan.** The Schedule B-2 plan characterises the current building stock as predominantly one- and two-storey 1970s–1980s detached homes — a buyer here is buying a house of that era, with a house inspection to match, not a strata purchase with a depreciation report. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=5211
