## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**The verified program frame — CRA / Province / CMHC figures govern every statement in this section. Where the live page or this slug's research differs from a standing correction, the correction (the primary-source position) governs and the file says so.**

- **FTHB GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122 — March 13 governs; secondary sources' March 12 is REJECTED, CRA governs. Together with the GST/HST new housing rebate it provides 100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated home valued up to $1,000,000, is **gradually reduced** between $1,000,000 and $1,500,000 (CRA's wording — a page's $1.25M straight-line phase-out row is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism), and is not available at $1,500,000 or more — maximum $50,000. Eligibility turns on a builder agreement entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (owner-built construction beginning on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031), construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036, and the buyer being at least 18, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and a first-time buyer under the four-calendar-year look-back (CRA: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html).
- **New Housing Rebate:** generally 36% of the GST paid, maximum $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and not available at $450,000 or more (CRA, same News No. 122 source).
- **HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year — the standing verified CRA figures, which GOVERN here.** The HBP figure carries the 2026–2028 repayment-relief deferral. Note on method, stated plainly: this slug's research file itself held the HBP figures and the FHSA lifetime figure as UNVERIFIED (the CRA HBP page returned 403 in the sibling passes, and the CRA FHSA main page read stated only the $8,000 first-year room) — the standing corrections supply the verified CRA figures ($60,000; $40,000 lifetime), and under this program's rule the SOURCE governs, so those figures are stated as verified here and the research file's UNVERIFIED hold is superseded on those two points. The page's further HBP detail (repayment starting the 2nd year after withdrawal, 5th year for first withdrawals in 2022–2025, and a proposal to extend the 5th-year delay to withdrawals through December 31, 2028) and its first-time home buyer's tax credit figures (a credit based on $10,000, worth up to $1,500, claimed on line 31270) remain UNVERIFIED page statements beyond the governed figures (research file §4; https://jaredgibbons.ca/first-time-home-buyer-program.html; CRA: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html).
- **FHSA + HBP together:** the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000, and contributions are generally deductible (CRA: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html).
- **PTT:** general rates 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — the page's "Over $3,000,000 — 5%" row is flagged as a compression of that 3% + further 2% structure. Worked anchors: $700K → $12,000; $1.0M → $18,000; $1.2M → $22,000; $1.5M → $28,000. First-time buyers' program: a qualifying buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 where the property is used only as a principal residence, has a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), is 0.5 hectares or smaller and contains only residential improvements; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and occupy the home as a principal residence to the first anniversary of registration to keep the full exemption. Newly built home exemption: full threshold $1,100,000, phasing out over the $50,000 range above it and eliminated at $1,150,000 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption).
- **Down payment and mortgage insurance (CMHC):** mortgage loan insurance is required when a down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more (where insurance is not available); **CMHC's published premium range is 0.6%–4.5%** of the total mortgage amount, and the premium can be paid in full or added to the mortgage. The live page's premium range (2.80%–4.00%, plus a 0.20% surcharge over 25 years) conflicts with that published range and is UNVERIFIED — documented in THIS slug's own research (§§1, 2.5). The page's insured-mortgage price-cap date (under $1.5 million since December 15, 2024), its 30-year amortization availability, and its "CMHC Home Start" program name were likewise not re-opened on CMHC or Finance Canada pages: UNVERIFIED as stated (CMHC: https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained; research file §4).
- **Home owner grant context:** the grant reduces annual property tax on a principal residence and must be applied for each year; as published, the regular grant amount is $570 effective January 1, 2027 and the 2026 full-grant threshold is $2,075,000 assessed value — the live page's wording on those two points matches that frame (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant; https://jaredgibbons.ca/first-time-home-buyer-program.html).
- **Form B diligence (for the strata townhome purchase a reader of this page may go on to make).** SPA s. 59: Form B within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢ per page — fees, amounts owing, special levies, CRF balance, insurance, rules, budget, Rental Disclosure Statement and depreciation report attached. The program page itself contains no strata information about any complex (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate).
- **Depreciation reports.** Stratas with 5+ lots must obtain a depreciation report every 5 years since July 1, 2024; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold. **CRF minimum:** 10% of the operating budget since November 1, 2023 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing).
- **Rentals.** Rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 for long-term tenancies. **Age.** Only 55+ age bylaws are valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01; May 1, 2023 exemptions) (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation).
- **Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhome carries NO strata, NO Form B, NO strata fees — municipal rules and registered covenants/HOA only, stated plainly.

## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: What is the first-time-home-buyer-program.html page?**
A: Jared Gibbons' BC first-time-buyer program explainer — three cost buckets, a worked $700,000 example, a nine-program table, PTT detail, a "who counts as a first-time buyer" section and seven FAQ answers, linking onward to the first-time buyer calculator. It has no listings and no roster (§1; research file §7).

**Q: How much property transfer tax does a first-time buyer in BC actually save?**
A: A qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price where the property's fair market value is $835,000 or less, it is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the buyer's principal residence; above $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies (§3; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers).

**Q: How much PTT would I pay without the exemption?**
A: Use the anchors: $700K → $12,000; $1.0M → $18,000; $1.2M → $22,000; $1.5M → $28,000, under the 1% / 2% / 3% tiers plus the further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. The page's worked $700,000 example nets to $4,000 with the exemption ($12,000 gross less the $8,000 first-time saving) (§§2.1, 3).

**Q: How much GST can a first-time buyer get back on a new home in BC?**
A: Up to $50,000: the FTHB GST/HST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026) returns 100% of the GST on a new or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more, for builder agreements entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (§3; CRA News No. 122).

**Q: What happened to the old GST new housing rebate?**
A: It still exists on its own terms: generally 36% of the GST paid, to a maximum of $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and unavailable at $450,000 or more (§3; CRA News No. 122).

**Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?**
A: Yes — the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. The governed figures are HBP $60,000 and FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year (§3; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html).

**Q: Is a newly built home treated differently from a resale home?**
A: Yes: the newly built home exemption has a full-exemption PTT threshold of $1,100,000 with a phase-out to $1,150,000, and a new home may also attract GST, which the federal FTHB rebate can offset by up to $50,000 for eligible buyers whose builder agreement dates from on or after March 20, 2025 (§3; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption).

**Q: What down payment do I actually need, and what does the insurance cost?**
A: Under 20% down requires mortgage loan insurance: the minimum is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 up to $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more with no insurance available. Price the premium from CMHC's published 0.6%–4.5% range — NOT from the page's 2.80%–4.00% range, which conflicts with CMHC and is UNVERIFIED (§§2.5, 3; https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained).

**Q: The page's worked example shows a $26,200 premium on a $700,000 townhouse — can I rely on it?**
A: Only as page content: the example's PTT legs ($4,000 with the exemption versus $12,000 without) follow the verified rates and exemption, but the premium leg uses the page's own 2.80%–4.00% range, which conflicts with CMHC's published 0.6%–4.5% range and is UNVERIFIED — price insurance from the CMHC range and a lender quote, not from the example (§2.1; research file §7).

**Q: I bought before — do I still count as a first-time buyer?**
A: It depends on which program's test applies: the BC PTT program requires that the buyer never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world and never received the exemption before, while the federal GST rebate uses a four-calendar-year look-back. Test the specific purchase against each program's own conditions in §3 before assuming either answer (research file §4; §3).

## 5. Local details a buyer would not know

- **The one number on this page that conflicts with its own regulator source is the insurance premium.** The page states 2.80%–4.00%; CMHC publishes 0.6%–4.5%. That conflict is documented in THIS slug's own research — it is not imported from the calculator slug, whose research documents no such error for the calculator tool, and no calculator defect is asserted anywhere in this file (§§1, 2.5, 3).
- **Two different "first-time buyer" tests sit on the same page.** BC's PTT program is a never-owned test; the federal GST rebate is a four-calendar-year look-back test. A buyer can pass one and fail the other — the page's single "who counts" section has to be read program by program (§§3, 4).
- **At $800,000 new, the newly built exemption beats the first-time exemption.** The newly built home exemption reaches to a $1,100,000 full threshold, past the first-time program's $835,000 ceiling — a new-build buyer should test both provincial programs, not just the one the page is named for (§3).
- **The phase-out has no published formula in the source read.** CRA says the GST rebate is "gradually reduced" between $1M and $1.5M — any straight-line row (including a $1.25M midpoint calculation) is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism, however tidy it looks in a table (§3).
- **The exemption has homework after closing.** Move in within 92 days of registration and stay as your principal residence to the first anniversary, or part of the PTT exemption is repayable — the saving is conditional for a full year after the purchase (§3).
- **A one-day signing difference decides $50,000.** FTHB GST rebate eligibility turns on a builder agreement dated on or after March 20, 2025 — a presale signed March 19, 2025 is outside the window the rebate was built for (§3).
