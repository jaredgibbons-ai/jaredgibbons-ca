## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **Form B diligence (any strata purchase on this ground, including the queued complexes if a unit is bought):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. Listing-level rental/pet lines are listing data, not bylaws — the Form B and registered bylaws govern. In the core's mixed-use towers, read the depreciation report and the commercial-component cost sharing early. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote; a strata with four or fewer lots sits UNDER that threshold. **CRF minimum:** since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since Nov 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. Flagged in-entry in §2: Camberley Green's building-page "rentals not allowed" line is void for long-term rentals on exactly this rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, exemptions expanded May 1, 2023 for a younger spouse/partner, live-in caregivers, legacy residents and defined children). **A 45+ bylaw is void and does NOT convert to 55+** — directly relevant to the Glendale Village queue entry in §2, where a 2008 advertisement's "45 plus restriction" line carries no legal force today. No complex counted or queued here carries a verified current 55+ bylaw for the core. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple contrast — stated plainly.** A fee-simple townhouse carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees; municipal rules (Surrey Zoning Bylaw No. 12000) plus any registered covenants or homeowners' association govern instead. No verified fee-simple townhouse complex exists in the downtown core (§2).
- **City plan:** Surrey's current Official Community Plan is the Surrey 2050 OCP, **Bylaw No. 21900, adopted June 15, 2026** — the former OCP, Bylaw No. 18020, is replaced and is NEVER cited as current in this file. The downtown's own layer is the City Centre Plan (2017, update underway adding a Central Business District and an Entertainment District). Zoning is Surrey Zoning Bylaw No. 12000. Density charges changed mid-2026: Community Amenity Contributions were superseded by the City's Amenity Cost Charge and bonus density provisions as of June 15, 2026, with the CAC page kept as a historic reference for eligible in-stream applications — exact per-file amounts are application-specific and UNVERIFIED as general figures. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/community-amenity-contributions (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/surrey-city-centre.md)
- **Zoning and SSMUH:** Surrey Zoning Bylaw No. 12000 governs; its SSMUH framework (in force since July 8, 2024) allows a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop, on eligible lots under 4,050 m² within the Urban Containment Boundary — relevant to the detached fringe blocks around the core, not to the tower parcels. ## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Your search has to be filed under "Whalley."** Board reporting and MLS sub-area data for downtown Surrey sit under Whalley — a literal "City Centre" sub-area search returns nothing, because no such board sub-area exists, even though the City officially calls the downtown "City Centre (Whalley)." https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- **One project site holds six residential towers with two different tenures.** King George Hub's four condo towers (Hub One, Hub Two, Plaza One, Plaza Two) stand beside two purpose-built rental towers (The Line, Tether). A listing that says only "King George Hub" has not told you whether the unit can be bought at all. https://pci-group.com/projects/king-george-hub/ (~/workspace/local-notes/condos/surrey-city-centre-condos-2026-11-01.md)
- **The inventory depth cuts both ways.** Whalley's 635 attached listings in September 2026 were the deepest of any Surrey sub-area — downtown sellers compete with new-build launches, and downtown buyers have more comparable sales to price against than almost anywhere else in the city. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/surrey-city-centre.md)
- **The core's only verified edge townhouse complex is in a queue, not a count.** Glendale Village (13858 102 Avenue, built 1985) verifies cleanly on fresh listings as a gated Whalley townhouse complex, yet counts nowhere in this program yet — because this slug's research file never named it. Its strata plan number and unit count exist only on a source this program never cites, and a "45 plus restriction" line in a 2008 advertisement describes a bylaw class that has been void since 2022. (§2 above)
- **The CAC era ended mid-2026.** Density charges on new City Centre projects switched from Community Amenity Contributions to the Amenity Cost Charge regime on June 15, 2026 — the same date the Surrey 2050 OCP (Bylaw No. 21900) was adopted — relevant to presale pricing comparisons between in-stream older projects and new launches, and a date older Surrey guides get wrong. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/community-amenity-contributions (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/surrey-city-centre.md)
- **The plan is changing under the towers.** The City Centre Plan update adds a Central Business District and an Entertainment District (Council endorsed the Entertainment District in October 2025), and large multi-phase applications such as Onni's Regency Gardens redevelopment (10520 132 St / 105–105A Ave area, Application No. 7924-0084-00) are in process — construction disruption and amenity upside both follow the plan, street by street. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan and https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7924-0084-00 (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/surrey-city-centre.md)
- **Stale restriction lines are everywhere in Whalley building data.** Because rental-restriction bylaws died province-wide on Nov 24, 2022 without any bylaw amendment being required, older building pages still showing "rentals not allowed" (Camberley Green's page among them) are describing a void bylaw — the Form B, not the aggregator page, is the document to trust. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
