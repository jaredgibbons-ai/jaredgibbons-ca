## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- There are no streets or boundaries for this file: the research file's scope is a buyer's guide to purchasing a manufactured home anywhere in British Columbia — "there is no MLS sub-area for this guide and no single municipality — the rules below are the provincial framework, with municipal zoning varying by city." https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes (Research file §1)
- The two ownership forms the topic is built around: (1) you own the home and rent the pad (site) it sits on in a manufactured home park — the land is the park owner's; (2) you own both the home and the land it sits on. https://jaredgibbons.ca/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-guide.html (Research file §1)
- Which law applies to which form: the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land you own is dealt with through the land title system instead. Neither form is a townhouse strata — a townhouse purchase is governed by the Strata Property Act, a different statute with a different document set (Form B, depreciation report, filed bylaws). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing
- Terminology: "manufactured home" is the legal term; "mobile home" is the everyday term. A manufactured home is factory-built to the CSA standard and registered in the Manufactured Home Registry — it is not the same product class as a site-built townhouse, and the registry/CSA-label rules in Section 3 are what separate a registrable manufactured home from an unregistered unit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry (Research file §1)
- Market numbers for manufactured homes: UNVERIFIED, by design — the SnapStats FVR September 2026 report (this program's only stats source) reports detached and attached (condos & townhomes) housing by community and sub-area and does not report manufactured homes as a property type. No townhome-only SnapStats figure exists anywhere in this program either; attached figures always combine condos & townhomes. (Research file §2)
- Where the topic touches real townhouse stock: only by cross-reference, in Section 2 — the area townhomes files in this series (for example Aldergrove, Poplar and Sumas Mountain in batch T1) carry the verified rosters. This topic file names no townhouse complex as its own.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

For this topic file, this section carries the manufactured-home frame the research file supports — because that frame is what a buyer comparing a "townhouse-style" park home against a real townhouse needs — followed by the townhouse-side contrast in one block.

### The manufactured-home frame (research-file sourced)

- **Pad tenure and assignment — the core of a park purchase.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 (Research file §4)
- **Park owner consent and the 10-day rule.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director), requested in writing on RTB Form 10; the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, silence by the end of the 10th day means consent is deemed given, and the park owner must not charge a fee for giving consent. On assignment the purchaser becomes the tenant, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000 (Research file §4)
- **Park rules** are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only with 3 full months' notice, and a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy (Research file §4)
- **Pad rent increases — VERIFIED figures, formula only, NEVER a dollar figure.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped at the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). The verified inflation components for this program are: **2026 = 2.3% + proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional amount.** No official source publishes a pad-rent DOLLAR figure for any park, and none is published here — any page stating a specific park's pad rent in dollars is stating a listing figure, not an official one. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes (Research file §4)
- **Park closure or redevelopment.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes (Research file §4)
- **Ownership registry.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. There is no Form B in this system — the registry record and the tenancy agreement are the disclosure set. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry (Research file §4)
- **Property tax.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant (Research file §4)
- **PTT position — as the research file states it, with a flag.** The research file documents one PTT point only: the Province's newly built home exemption expressly includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) — relevant to a new manufactured home on the buyer's own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. **Flag:** any flat claim that "PTT does not apply on a pad" is UNVERIFIED on this research file — the file documents no such blanket rule, and the PTT position for a specific park purchase must be confirmed for that transaction, not asserted from the topic page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption (Research file §4)
- **Age-restricted parks.** In a designated 55-plus park, at least one tenant on the site must be 55 years of age or older (the Province's general rule). That is a park rule under tenancy law — not a Strata Property Act s.123.1/123.2 bylaw — so the strata exemptions for younger spouses/partners, caregivers and defined children do not automatically apply inside a pad-rental park; the park rules govern. No official source names a specific age-restricted park for this topic file: park names UNVERIFIED at topic level. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes (Research file §4)
- **Zoning for parks is municipal.** There is no province-wide park zoning — for example, Surrey's Zoning Bylaw No. 12000 has a Manufactured Home Residential Zone (RM-M), and Maple Ridge's Zoning Bylaw 7600-2019 has an RMH Manufactured Home Park Residential zone. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7915-0417-00.pdf (Research file §4)
- **Financing:** lender and mortgage-insurer rules for manufactured homes (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in the research pass: UNVERIFIED — confirm financing terms for the specific home and tenure before removing subjects. (Research file §4)

### The townhouse-side contrast (one block)

- A real townhouse purchase is a strata purchase (or, where verified, a fee-simple purchase — no fee-simple townhouse complex is verified in this batch's area files): the document set is the Form B (fees, levies, CRF balance, parking/storage, insurance summary since April 1, 2023), the depreciation report (mandatory on a five-year cycle for 5+ lot stratas since July 1, 2024), a CRF funded at a minimum 10% of the operating fund since November 1, 2023, rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022, and 55+ as the only valid age bylaw (SPA ss.123.1/123.2, May 1, 2023 exemptions). Buyer frame shared with any new home: FTHB GST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 — follow March 13 over any sibling file stating March 12; 100% to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements March 20, 2025–2031); New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350K–$450K; PTT exemptions as stated in the area files; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 / $8,000 per year; CMHC 0.6%–4.5%; REDMA s.21 rescission and s.18 trust deposits where a townhouse is a presale. None of that machinery exists in a pad assignment — which is the point of the contrast. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: The listing calls this park home a "townhouse." Is it one?** No. If you own the home and rent the pad, it is a manufactured home under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, whatever the form field says — the Cottonwood Village precedent in Section 2 is this program's standing example. A townhouse is a strata lot (or a fee-simple row home) under land-title law. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: If I buy a mobile home in a park, do I own the land?** No. You own the home and rent the site under a pad tenancy agreement; the Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on. The exceptions prove the rule: in a bare-land strata such as Sea Ridge Bays you own the lot as a strata lot, and in a co-operative such as Pioneer Park the land is held through the co-op. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes (Research file Q1)
- **Q: Can the park owner refuse to let me buy a home in the park?** The sale needs the park owner's prior written consent to assign the tenancy — but the owner must respond within 10 days of the written request (RTB Form 10), silence counts as consent given, and no fee can be charged for consent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf (Research file Q2)
- **Q: How much can my pad rent go up each year?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the inflation rate plus the park's proportional levy/utility amount — verified at 2.3% + proportional for 2026 and 2.2% + proportional for 2027. There is no official pad-rent dollar figure for any park; anyone quoting one is quoting a listing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes (Research file Q3)
- **Q: What replaces the Form B and depreciation report when I buy in a park?** Nothing equivalent — the disclosure set is the written tenancy agreement and park rules (which the seller must give you before consent is even requested), the Manufactured Home Registry record, and the home's CSA certification label, which must be attached at the point of sale. That thinner disclosure is a real difference from a townhouse strata purchase, not a technicality. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold or redeveloped?** For a conversion of all or a significant part of the park, the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay you $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date. A strata townhouse owner has no equivalent exposure — the strata cannot be closed out from under an owner on a landlord's notice. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes (Research file Q4)
- **Q: How is the purchase actually registered?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered. Your lawyer or notary files the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership. A townhouse, by contrast, transfers in the Land Title Office as a strata lot. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry (Research file Q5)
- **Q: I'm 52 and my partner is 58 — can we live in a 55+ park? In a 55+ townhouse strata?** Different answers under different law. In a designated 55-plus park, the provincial rule is that at least one tenant on the site must be 55 or older, and the park's own rules govern the rest. In a 55+ strata, the Strata Property Act exemptions (expanded May 1, 2023) expressly cover a younger spouse or partner of a qualifying resident. Do not carry the strata answer into a park, or the park answer into a strata. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Do I pay property transfer tax on a park home?** The research file documents only the newly built home exemption point (a new manufactured home placed and affixed on vacant land you own). A flat "PTT does not apply on a pad" claim is UNVERIFIED on this file — confirm the position for your specific transaction with your lawyer or notary before removing subjects. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: So when IS a manufactured home also strata?** When the park itself is a strata — a bare-land strata like Sea Ridge Bays, where you own the lot, pay a strata fee instead of pad rent, and get the strata document set. It is still not a townhouse: the home on the lot is a manufactured home, documented as its own form in Section 2. https://realtylink.org/en/print/property/263120958

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The consent clock is the park equivalent of a subject period, and most buyers have never heard of it:** the park owner has 10 days to respond to an assignment request on RTB Form 10, and silence is consent — a deal structured without that window in its dates is structured wrong. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf (Research file §6)
- **Pad rent rises by formula, not by negotiation — and the formula has two moving parts:** the BC inflation component (2.3% for 2026, 2.2% for 2027, as verified for this program) plus a proportional amount that depends on the individual park's levy and regulated-utility changes divided by its site count. Two parks in the same city can post different lawful increases in the same year, and neither increase can be checked against a published dollar figure, because none exists on an official source. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **A missing CSA label is a financing and resale problem, not a cosmetic one:** the transfer only takes effect when registered, the filing goes through a lawyer or notary, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry (Research file §6)
- **The tax assessment quietly tells you the tenure:** in a park, the assessed value does not include the land, because the home owner does not own it — the cleanest one-line test of pad tenure on a tax notice, and the reason a park home and a townhouse at the same sticker figure are not the same asset. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **Park rules can change on 3 months' notice; strata bylaws cannot change that way:** a landlord may change park rules with 3 full months' notice (subject to the fairness/clarity/material-term limits), while a strata bylaw change needs an owner vote and Land Title filing. The daily-life rules of a park home are less locked-in than a townhouse buyer's. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy
