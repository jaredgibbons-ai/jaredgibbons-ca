## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

### The settled court-ordered-sale treatment (BC Supreme Court) — as carried in the Langley townhomes file

- **Step 1 — the petition.** A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property, or for redemption, must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7(1). All persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished, and all persons against whom relief is sought, must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner may also join a claim arising out of the mortgage and a person liable to pay the mortgage debt — Rule 21-7(3). If the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation, a person who subsequently registers an interest need not be served, is bound by an order made in the proceeding, and may file a response — Rule 21-7(4). A petitioner who serves the petition must include the informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, **Form 125** — Rule 21-7(4.1) (research file §4, confirmed verbatim in this program: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03).
- **Step 2 — the court's powers and the redemption period.** The court may make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7(5)(a); order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a **redemption period the court fixes**, with foreclosure of the equity of redemption in default — Rule 21-7(5)(b); and determine summarily, or order an account be taken and certified, the amount due — Rule 21-7(5)(c). The Rule text states **no fixed redemption-period length** — the court fixes it in its order (research file §4).
- **Step 3 — conduct of sale, approval and vesting (Rule 13-5).** Under the settled treatment, a sale inside the proceeding is conducted under the court's directions (Rule 13-5): the court may give conduct of sale to a party, direct the particulars and conditions of sale, set or approve pricing directions, and a sale by contract is conditional on court approval; on approval the court confirms the sale, directs the proceeds and vests title in the purchaser, with the registrar's certificate in **Form 60** evidencing the vesting. Competing offers are dealt with at the approval hearing under the court's directions and **Practice Direction PD-66** (the live page's account — sealed bids collected in the courtroom by 9:45 a.m. on the hearing day — is the page's own statement of that practice). **Research-file caution, carried openly:** this slug's research file marks Rule 13-5 mechanics, PD-66 and Form 60 UNVERIFIED program-wide under its batch-10 standing position, and does not adopt the live page's 9:45 a.m. account (research file §4). ## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?**
A: By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition (§3).

**Q: How long is the redemption period?**
A: There is no fixed length in the Rule text — the court fixes the period in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b). Until that period expires (or the court orders otherwise), the respondent can pay what is due and end the proceeding (§3).

**Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer at the approval hearing?**
A: Plan for the possibility: under the settled treatment, a sale by contract is conditional on court approval and competing offers are dealt with at the approval hearing under the court's directions and PD-66 (the live page describes sealed bids collected in the courtroom by 9:45 a.m.). This slug's research file marks those mechanics UNVERIFIED program-wide — either way, get the specific file's bid instructions in writing from the seller's counsel before spending money (§3).

**Q: Do I get a Form B if the townhouse is strata?**

**Q: The feed shows mostly houses and acreages — where are the townhouses?**
A: Any mortgaged property can be the subject of a Rule 21-7 proceeding, with no property-type limit; the feed at reading happened to be detached-led (Morgan Creek, Sunnyside Park, White Rock, Hazelmere, King George Corridor cards). A townhouse's complex context lives in its sub-area townhomes file — except Morgan Creek, which has no townhomes file and no verified roster (§1/§2.1).

**Q: Can the owner still save the property after I offer?**
A: The redemption structure is the risk: the court may order payment of what is due within the court-fixed redemption period, with foreclosure in default — Rule 21-7(5)(b) — and the court may also make a final order of foreclosure under Rule 21-7(5)(a). Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections (§3).
