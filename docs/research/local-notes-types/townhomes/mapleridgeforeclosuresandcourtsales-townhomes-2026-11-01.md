## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

### The settled court-ordered-sale treatment (BC Supreme Court)

- **Step 1 — the petition:** a foreclosure proceeding must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7(1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). Rule 21-7 sets out no demand step before the petition. Verified in this topic's own research opens. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Step 2 — the order and the redemption period:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption — Rule 21-7(5)(b); the court may determine or certify the amount due and pronounce judgment for it — Rule 21-7(5)(c) and (e). The Land Title Act refers to this order as an "order nisi," with a later final order of foreclosure — Land Title Act s. 271(1). The redemption period has NO fixed length in the Rules: it is the period the court fixes, and interest is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem — Law and Equity Act s. 19. Steps verified in this topic's own opens through Rule 21-7(5); the order-nisi and interest wording is carried from the settled Langley sibling treatment. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol33/consol33/96250_18 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Final order / applying for a sale:** in default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7(6); a party may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7(7). Carried from the settled sibling treatment: this topic's own research pass was stopped by a browser-service 429 before re-reading the Rule 21-7 continuation text, and that limit is stated here rather than hidden. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5(3); the court's directions may appoint who has conduct, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of value — Rule 13-5(4). Carried from the settled sibling treatment: Rule 13-5 was not opened in this topic's own research pass. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Court approval of the sale:** the manner of sale may be fixed as sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5(4)(b); the person having conduct may apply to the court, even though the time for redemption has not expired, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7(9); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in **Form 60** by the person having conduct of the sale — Rule 13-5(6). That vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. Carried from the settled sibling treatment. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Bids and practice direction PD-66:** the court's directions may define "the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids" — Rule 13-5(4)(d); the Supreme Court's civil practice direction **PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings,"** governs practice in these proceedings and replaced the earlier sealed-bid practice direction PD-62, per the Court of Appeal. The text of PD-66 was not retrieved in either research pass, so the timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66 are UNVERIFIED in detail — the live page's sealed, unconditional competing-bid description stays the page's own statement. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm
- **Where the proceeding is filed:** unless the court otherwise orders, a foreclosure proceeding is commenced at the Supreme Court registry in the municipality where the land is located, if there is one there, and otherwise at a registry in the judicial district in which the land is located, and applications are heard at that registry — Law and Equity Act s. 21(2). Which registry serves Maple Ridge as a stated mapping: UNVERIFIED (the court's locations page states no service areas, and this topic's research did not open s. 21(2)) — the live page's New Westminster-or-Vancouver hearing statement is recorded in Section 1 as the page's own statement, not as a verified mapping. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Court records:** Court Services Online is BC's electronic court registry; eSearch is available to anyone with Internet access, Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry location, party names and filed documents, and its file classes include "H - Foreclosure." It publishes no area statistics. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. Rule 21-7 sets out no demand step before the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b), within which the respondent must pay what is due and for costs or be foreclosed of the equity of redemption. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Is an accepted offer on a Maple Ridge court-ordered townhouse final?** No — treat it as not final until the court confirms it. The manner of sale may be fixed as sale by contract conditional on court approval (Rule 13-5(4)(b)), and confirmation, proceeds and vesting run under Rule 21-7(9) with the result certified in Form 60. Get the specific file's approval process from the conduct-of-sale lawyer before booking movers or spending on inspections. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** Plan for the possibility: the court's directions may define bid, offer and meet-bid rights (Rule 13-5(4)(d)), and PD-66 governs foreclosure practice — but the timing and manner for competing bids under PD-66 are UNVERIFIED in detail in this program, and the live page's sealed, unconditional competing-bid description is the page's own statement. Get the seller's counsel's bid instructions for the specific file in writing. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm
- **Q: Will I get a Form B for a court-ordered strata townhouse?** UNCONFIRMED — no source states that the ordinary Form B package is available on the same terms when the seller side is a conduct-of-sale party. Do not price the unit on a promised Form B, depreciation report or CRF figure until the specific file's listing documents confirm what is actually produced. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Q: Do PTT and GST work the same on a court sale?** UNCONFIRMED — no special treatment is stated in any source in the research file, and none is asserted here. Have your lawyer cost both taxes for the specific court transaction before you set your ceiling. (Section 3.)
- **Q: Where can I check the court file?** Court Services Online (https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do) offers eSearch to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: Which registry will the Maple Ridge hearing be at?** UNVERIFIED as a stated mapping: the filing rule is Law and Equity Act s. 21(2) (the municipality's registry if there is one there, otherwise the judicial district's), and the live page's New Westminster-or-Vancouver statement is the page's own, put to Jared in the research's questions, not verified here. Confirm from the court file itself on Court Services Online. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: Are court-ordered townhouses automatically cheap?** No published source supports a standard foreclosure discount. The general Maple Ridge market already sold at 97% sale-to-list (detached) and 94% (attached, combined) in September 2026, and the live page's own FAQ makes the same point in its words — discounts reflect condition, as-is risk or occupancy rather than automatic below-market pricing. https://jaredgibbons.ca/mapleridgeforeclosuresandcourtsales.html
- **Q: Can the owner stop the sale by paying?** The redemption period the court fixes under Rule 21-7(5)(b) is exactly that window — pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within it, or be foreclosed of the equity of redemption. Which stage a file is at decides whether that window is still open; ask before spending money. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
