## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **Form B diligence (for any strata townhouse a subscriber later buys off a newsletter listing):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. A newsletter item is a pointer; the Form B is the disclosure behind any townhouse it features. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote; a strata with four or fewer lots sits UNDER that threshold. **CRF minimum:** since Nov 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since Nov 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. Any directory or listing line stating "rentals not allowed" for a strata townhouse is void for long-term rentals and is flagged as such wherever it appears in this program. **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2), with Regulation 7.01 exemptions expanded May 1, 2023. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple contrast:** a fee-simple townhouse carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — diligence is title, municipal rules and any registered covenants or HOA documents only, stated plainly. A newsletter headline never states tenure; title does. Basis: the standard tenure rule applied across this program.
- **Taxes and rebates:** PTT general rates are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — a page row reading "Over $3M — 5%" is a compression of that 3% + 2% structure, not a single rate. First-time buyers' exemption: full on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000; newly built home exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000. FTHB GST rebate — Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 (100% to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, max $50,000, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031); New Housing Rebate 36% / max $6,300 / $350K–$450K; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5%. 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Presales (REDMA):** where a newsletter item markets a presale townhouse, the buyer must receive the developer's disclosure statement (REDMA s. 18, deposits in trust) and has a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing or acknowledging it (REDMA s. 21). https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **CASL's three requirements, restated for the sender:** obtain prior consent (express or implied), provide identification and contact information, and include a working unsubscribe mechanism — with monetary penalties for violations, per the CRTC. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti/reg.htm
- **Onus of proof sits with the sender:** express consent can be obtained in writing or orally, and in either case the onus is on the person sending the message to prove they obtained consent — the signup flow behind this page needs to create a durable consent record, because the promise on the page is only half the obligation. Unsubscribe timelines are the yardstick: the link must remain valid for at least 60 days after the CEM is sent, and a request must be processed without delay, and no later than 10 business days after receiving it. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **The BC privacy wrinkle, from the OPC itself:** British Columbia (with Alberta and Quebec) has its own private-sector privacy law that has been deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA, and organizations subject to a substantially similar provincial law are generally exempt from PIPEDA with respect to collection, use or disclosure that occurs within that province — while businesses handling personal information that crosses provincial or national borders remain subject to PIPEDA regardless. A BC REALTOR®'s subscriber list therefore sits primarily under BC's provincial regime for in-province handling, with PIPEDA behind it for cross-border handling; this file states the framework only and does not give legal advice on which statute governs a specific list. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/?wbdisable=false
