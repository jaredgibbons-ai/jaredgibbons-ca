## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**The MHPTA frame governs this topic — stated first, because it replaces the strata frame rather than sitting beside it.**

- **What the buyer actually holds.** In a manufactured home park, the tenant typically owns the manufactured home and rents the site (the pad) it sits on under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and its Regulation, administered through the Residential Tenancy Branch (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes, via the research file). There is no strata corporation, no Form B, no CRF and no depreciation report.
- **Pad-rent increases.** A landlord may increase pad rent only once every 12 months, on 3 full months' notice, and by no more than the yearly limit — **2.3% for 2026 and 2.2% for 2027, plus a proportional amount** for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees (the change in levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). A landlord may apply to the RTB for an additional increase above the limit for increased operating or financing costs or for substantial and essential repairs or renovations (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes, via the research file). **No pad-rent dollar figure is published in this file** — the research file's per-card pad figures are listing text only, UNVERIFIED against any park source, and are not restated here.
- **Selling a home that stays in the park — assignment (MHPTA s. 28).** A tenant may assign the site tenancy agreement to the buyer only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed obtained under the Regulation), an order of the director, or authorization in the tenancy agreement itself; the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting (s. 28(3)); before requesting consent the seller must give the proposed buyer written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; on assignment the pad rent and the scheduled date for a subsequent increase carry over unchanged; an unreasonable refusal can be taken to RTB dispute resolution (RTB assignment information sheet, via the research file: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf).
- **Park rules.** A landlord must give tenants a written copy of the park rules (if any) before signing the tenancy agreement. The rules, pet and age provisions, and approval requirements of any specific park on this feed (feed text variously claims "no age restrictions," "Park Approval Is Needed" and pet limits): UNVERIFIED — listing text only; obtain the park's own rule package before writing (research file, as above).
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s. 42).** Where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to another use, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute the notice within 15 days, may in defined circumstances apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes, via the research file).
- **Park rule vs strata bylaw — the distinction that decides age claims.** In a strata, an age restriction is a registered bylaw: since November 24, 2022 only a 55+ bylaw is lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2, Regulation 7.01), disclosed on the Form B, with spouse/caregiver/legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion; a 45+ bylaw is void and does NOT convert to 55+. In a park, a 55+ rule is a park rule under the MHPTA — not an SPA bylaw, not disclosed on any Form B, and not governed by the SPA exemptions. Treat any "55+ strata" language in a park-home listing as a category error (basis: the condos sibling, as above).

**The strata block (for the cross-referenced strata stock in §2.1 — the comparison purchase, not this topic).**

- **Form B diligence.** Strata Property Act s. 59: provided within seven days of request, fee capped at $35 plus up to 25 cents per page; states the monthly fee, amounts owing, approved special levies, the CRF balance, insurance, rules, budget and financial statement, any Rental Disclosure Statement, and the depreciation report attached (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/strata-information-buyers-sellers; https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_04).
- **Depreciation reports / CRF.** Since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years; sub-five-lot stratas sit UNDER that threshold (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-regulations/depreciation-reports). Since November 1, 2023, the minimum CRF contribution is 10% of the annual operating budget (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/finances). Neither obligation exists in a park — that absence is part of what the lower park-home price is buying.
- **Rentals.** Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID province-wide since November 24, 2022 (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-regulations/changes-to-legislation) — the directory "rentals not allowed" lines carried for strata stock elsewhere in Mission (Cherry Lane is the roster example) are void in-entry. Park tenancies are not strata rentals and are not governed by this rule; assignment consent (§3, MHPTA s. 28) is the park-side control instead.

**Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhouse carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — municipal rules plus registered covenants/HOA only. A manufactured home on its own owned lot (the Triple Creek claim in §1) is fee-simple land with a manufactured home on it — still not a townhouse complex, and its tenure must be confirmed on title before it is priced as either product.

## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: What am I actually buying with a Mission manufactured home in a park?**
A: The manufactured home itself, plus a site tenancy — you rent the pad it sits on under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act. That is why the pad-rent limit, the park rules and the assignment process matter as much as the home's price — and why the purchase is not a townhouse, strata or fee-simple purchase on any reading (RTB assignment information sheet, via the research file: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf).

**Q: How much can my pad rent go up?**
A: Once every 12 months at most, on 3 full months' notice, and by no more than the yearly limit — 2.3% for 2026 and 2.2% for 2027 — plus a proportional amount covering the park's change in local government levies and regulated utility fees divided across its sites; anything above that requires an RTB order (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes). This file publishes no pad-rent dollar figure for any park — the current pad rent for a specific home is a document check against the tenancy agreement, not a figure to take from a listing card.

**Q: Can the park refuse my buyer when I sell?**
A: The sale works by assigning your site tenancy agreement, which under MHPTA s. 28 requires the landlord's prior written consent (or deemed consent, or an order of the director, or authorization in the agreement itself); the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent; you must give the proposed buyer the park rules and written tenancy terms before requesting consent; the pad rent carries over unchanged on assignment; and an unreasonable refusal can be taken to RTB dispute resolution (RTB information sheet, as above).

**Q: Is there a Form B or a depreciation report for a park home?**
A: No. Those are Strata Property Act documents (Form B under s. 59, within 7 days, $35 + 25¢/page; depreciation reports every five years for stratas of 5+ lots since July 1, 2024) and a park has no strata corporation to issue either. The park-side diligence set is the tenancy agreement, the park rules and the assignment record instead (§3).

**Q: What is the median price of a manufactured home in Mission?**
A: SnapStats publishes no manufactured-home median — its Mission splits are Detached (median $814,000, September 2026) and "Condos & Townhomes" combined ($430,000), covering all dwellings. The live feed's first page is a listing snapshot, not a median, and no manufactured-home median is stated in this file (research file: `~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/mission-mobile-homes.md`).

**Q: Are all the parks on this page in the City of Mission?**
A: No — the first page includes parks in Dewdney Deroche and on Hatzic Island, which are unincorporated FVRD Electoral Area G ground with rural taxes paid to the Province, alongside City of Mission parks. The MHPTA applies in both; zoning and the property-tax penalty regime differ — flat 10% after July 2 in the City, 5% + 5% in the rural regime (research file, as above).

**Q: Is Mission Mobile Home Park a 55+ strata I can buy into?**
A: It is neither a strata nor a purchase of land: it is the program's one listing-verified 55+ manufactured-home park in this market, and its 55+ rule is a park rule under the MHPTA set by the park owner — not a Strata Property Act bylaw, with no Form B and no SPA exemptions (basis: `~/workspace/local-notes/condos/mission-mobile-homes-condos-2026-11-01.md`). Oasis Country Estates' "adult side," by contrast, states no exact age in any source and is UNCONFIRMED as 55+.

**Q: A card says a Triple Creek home is "on its own lot" — is that a townhouse or fee-simple purchase?**
A: Neither label is verified. The feed mixes pad tenancies with at least one own-lot claim; a pad home, an owned-lot manufactured home and a strata lot finance, insure and resell differently, so verify which tenure is actually being sold on title before comparing prices (research file, as above). An owned-lot manufactured home is still not a townhouse complex.

**Q: What happens if the park closes or converts?**
A: Under MHPTA s. 42 the landlord must first have all necessary permits and approvals, then give 12 months' notice and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the effective date, with defined additional-compensation routes where the home cannot be moved or no site is available within a reasonable distance (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes). Confirm the park's ownership and any redevelopment signals as part of due diligence.

**Q: Can I rent out a park home, or a Mission strata townhouse instead?**
A: Different statutes, different answers. In a strata, long-term rental-restriction bylaws are void since November 24, 2022, so a Cherry Lane or Luther Place townhouse can be rented long-term whatever an old directory line says. In a park, occupancy and any subletting or assignment are governed by the tenancy agreement and park rules under the MHPTA — read the park's own documents; the strata rule does not carry across (§3).
