## 2. Complexes — no roster, by design

**This file counts 0 townhouse complexes, 0 strata and 0 fee-simple — it is a topic file and carries no roster of its own.** What follows documents what a townhome evaluation works from, which is the townhome-specific content a seller needs:

- **Comparables come from the same complex first.** A CMA "evaluates the prices of similar properties recently sold in your area" (research file §1: https://www.jaredgibbons.ca/home-evaluation.html). For a townhouse, the most similar properties are units in the seller's own complex — same strata plan, same fee structure, same age and construction — and a competent townhome CMA starts there before widening to competing complexes. Each complex's verified identity (name, street, year, tenure) lives in its own area file in this program; nothing is re-counted here.
- **The strata position moves the number.** Two units with identical floor plans in the same complex can support different values once the strata's position is read: the monthly strata fee, any approved special levies, the contingency reserve fund balance and the depreciation report's renewal schedule all change what a buyer will pay and what a lender will finance. Those figures come from the Form B package (§3), which is why an evaluation of a strata townhouse that ignores the strata documents is incomplete.
- **Strata versus fee-simple changes the evidence.** A strata townhouse evaluation works from Form B data (fees, levies, CRF, depreciation report). A fee-simple townhouse has NO Form B, NO strata fees and NO depreciation report — its evaluation works from municipal records, registered covenants and any homeowners' association documents instead. Mixing the two evidence sets is the most common townhome valuation error, and it is why every complex entry in this program states STRATA versus FEE-SIMPLE explicitly.
- **Market context is combined-category only.** SnapStats reports Surrey and South Surrey White Rock as Detached versus "Condos & Townhomes" combined — there is no townhome-only statistic, and sub-area medians are NOT published (community medians only). An evaluation that quotes a "townhome median" for a Surrey sub-area is quoting a figure that does not exist in the source (SnapStats monthly Fraser Valley and Greater Vancouver reports, as carried in this program's area research files).
- **Presale evaluations are a different exercise.** A presale townhouse has no resale comparables of itself; its evaluation turns on the developer's pricing, the disclosure statement and completion timing, and the buyer's 7-day rescission right under REDMA s. 21 runs from the original contract, not from any later valuation (§3). No complex is named or counted here for presale purposes.

## 3. Rules that affect a townhome evaluation

**The regulator's frame (BC Financial Services Authority).**

- A licensee must make disclosure at the outset of dealings with a consumer, and must give the consumer information about the commission or fee the licensee will earn, confirmation that the licensee will act for the buyer or the seller only, an explanation of the licensee's duties to clients and non-clients, and the risks to an unrepresented consumer of going unrepresented (BCFSA, disclosure requirements for real estate licensees: https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professionals). WHEN that disclosure occurs inside Jared's evaluation process is UNVERIFIED — the page does not say, and this file does not assume it.
- Dual agency has been prohibited in BC since 2018 except in the rarest circumstances — an evaluation conversation is with a seller's prospective licensee, and the same licensee generally cannot also act for the buyer of that home (BCFSA: https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professionals).

**The strata block (what a strata townhouse's evaluation must account for).**

- **Form B diligence.** A buyer of a strata townhouse is entitled to an Information Certificate (Form B) under s. 59 of the Strata Property Act, to be provided within seven days of request; the fee is capped at $35 plus up to 25 cents per page for paper copies. The Form B states the monthly strata fee, money the owner owes the strata, any special levies approved but not yet due, the contingency reserve fund (CRF) balance, the strata's insurance, its rules, its current budget and financial statement, any Rental Disclosure Statement, and the current depreciation report attached (BC Government, strata Forms information: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/strata-information-buyers-sellers; Strata Property Act, s. 59: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_04). A seller who knows their Form B position before listing prices into the market buyers will actually see.
- **Depreciation reports.** Since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more strata lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years; stratas with fewer than five lots are UNDER the threshold and exempt (BC Government, depreciation reports: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-regulations/depreciation-reports). A report showing a major renewal (roofs, siding, plumbing) inside the buyer's holding period is a valuation input, not fine print.
- **Contingency reserve fund.** Since November 1, 2023, a strata must contribute a minimum of 10% of its annual operating budget to the CRF (BC Government, strata finances: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/finances). A thin CRF against an aging complex is the classic source of post-listing price reductions via special levies.
- **Rentals.** Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID province-wide since November 24, 2022 — a strata can no longer restrict rentals, so an evaluation must not discount a townhouse for a "no rentals" bylaw, and any directory line saying "rentals not allowed" is legally void (BC Government, strata rental rules: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-regulations/changes-to-legislation).
- **Age.** Since November 24, 2022, the only age-restriction bylaw a BC strata may enforce is 55+ (Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1–123.2: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_08), with exemptions added May 1, 2023 for caregivers and for spouses/partners and specified family members (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-regulations/changes-to-legislation). A 55+ complex's buyer pool is narrower by design — an evaluation in a 55+ complex prices against comparables inside that same restricted pool.

**Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhouse evaluation has NO strata layer at all: no Form B, no strata fees, no CRF, no depreciation report — the value case rests on the property itself, municipal rules, and any registered covenants or homeowners' association charges (Strata Property Act scope: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_01).

**Surrey rules that surface in townhouse valuations** (the page's listed areas are Surrey-centred; the other listed areas' city rules live in their own files).

- Surrey's current Official Community Plan is the Surrey OCP, Bylaw No. 21900, adopted June 15, 2026; the former OCP, Bylaw No. 18020, is NOT current and is never cited as such in this file (City of Surrey, OCP: https://www.surrey.ca/planning-development/plans-strategies/surrey-official-community-plan). Zoning is under Surrey Zoning Bylaw No. 12000 (https://www.surrey.ca/bylaws-services/bylaws).
- Secondary suites in Surrey are capped at 40% of the principal dwelling's habitable floor area (City of Surrey, secondary suites: https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/permits-licences/secondary-suites) — a townhouse with a lawful suite carries an income fact an evaluation should capture; inside a strata, the bylaws govern whether the suite may exist at all.
- SSMUH: Surrey implemented the provincial small-scale multi-unit housing rules on July 8, 2024 — up to 3 units on lots under 280 m², up to 4 units on lots over 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop (City of Surrey, SSMUH: https://www.surrey.ca/planning-development/housing/small-scale-multi-unit-housing). SSMUH applies to eligible single-family lots, NOT to lots inside an existing strata townhouse complex — no SSMUH uplift belongs in a strata townhouse's evaluation.
- Short-term rentals: Surrey permits them only in the operator's own principal residence, with a City business licence at $358/year as stated on the City's licence page. DISCLOSED CONFLICT: a City of Surrey 2024 corporate report proposed $350/year plus fees; this file carries the licence page's $358 figure and directs the reader to the licence page for the current amount (City of Surrey, short-term rentals: https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/permits-licences/short-term-rentals). Investor-value claims in an evaluation should not assume short-term-rental income a licence and the strata's bylaws may not allow.
- Property tax timing: Surrey property taxes are due July 2; a 5% penalty applies to amounts unpaid after July 2 and a further 5% after September 2 (City of Surrey, property taxes: https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/taxes-utilities/property-taxes) — a seller completing across the deadline should see the adjustment in their net figures.

**Purchase taxes, rebates and financing (these shape the buyer pool an evaluation prices into).**

- **First-time home buyers' GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on **March 13, 2026** (CRA News Release No. 122 — March 13 governs; any sibling file stating March 12 is superseded on this point). The rebate is 100% of the GST on a new home up to $1,000,000, phasing out to $1,500,000, to a maximum rebate of $50,000, for qualifying purchases from March 20, 2025 to 2031 (Canada Revenue Agency: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculate/gst-hst-new-housing-rebate.html). Resale townhouses do not attract GST.
- **New Housing Rebate (GST/HST).** Separate from the first-time buyer rebate: a 36% rebate of the GST on new or substantially renovated housing, to a maximum of $6,300, phasing out between $350,000 and $450,000 of value (Canada Revenue Agency: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/new-housing-rebate.html).
- **Property Transfer Tax (PTT).** General rates: 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion from $200,000 to $2,000,000, and 3% on the portion above $2,000,000, PLUS a further 2% on the residential portion of a property valued above $3,000,000. A summary row reading "Over $3M — 5%" compresses the 3% + 2% structure and is flagged wherever it appears — the tax is not a flat 5% (BC Government, PTT: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/understand). Worked example: an $800,000 resale townhouse with no exemption pays $14,000 PTT (1% × $200,000 + 2% × $600,000) — buyer-side cost that an evaluation's list-price strategy should remember is sitting on top of the price.
- **PTT exemptions.** First-time home buyers: full exemption on the first $500,000 of value where the fair market value is $835,000 or less, phasing out to $860,000. Newly built homes: full exemption up to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 (BC Government, PTT exemptions: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions). The $835,000 threshold matters to townhouse pricing: a list price just under it keeps the full first-time-buyer exemption in play for the buyer pool.
- **Home Buyers' Plan / First Home Savings Account.** HBP withdrawal limit is $60,000 from RRSPs (Canada Revenue Agency: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsp-related-plans/what-home-buyers-plan.html); the FHSA allows $40,000 of contributions at $8,000 per year (Canada Revenue Agency: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html).
- **Mortgage insurance.** CMHC mortgage default insurance premiums for high-ratio purchases run from 0.6% to 4.5% of the mortgage amount depending on the loan-to-value ratio (CMHC: https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/industry-innovation-and-leadership/industry-expertise/resources/mortgage-loan-insurance-and-securitization/mortgage-insurance-premiums). No page defect is asserted in this file: this slug's research file documents no CMHC premium error on the home-evaluation page (that defect is documented against the mortgage-calculator page in its own file, and is not imported here).
- **Presales.** Where a seller holds a presale townhouse contract rather than a completed home, the REDMA frame applies to the original purchase: a 7-day right of rescission under s. 21 of the Real Estate Development Marketing Act, and deposits held in trust under s. 18 (BC Laws, REDMA: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/04066_01). Assignments of presale contracts carry their own disclosure and consent rules — an evaluation of an assignment is not a standard CMA.

**Q: Which areas does the evaluation cover?**
A: The page lists South Surrey, White Rock, Langley, Abbotsford, Surrey, Tsawwassen and Ladner, and directs anyone unsure to call or text 604-928-1361 (research file §1: https://www.jaredgibbons.ca/home-evaluation.html).

**Q: How does a townhome evaluation differ from a house evaluation?**
A: The comparables start inside your own complex, and the strata's position — monthly fee, approved levies, CRF balance, depreciation report — is part of the value case, all of it readable from the Form B package (Strata Property Act, s. 59: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_04). A fee-simple townhouse has no Form B at all; its evaluation works from municipal records and covenants instead (§2).

**Q: My complex has a special levy coming. Will that change my evaluation?**
A: Yes — approved-but-undue levies are stated on the Form B, and buyers price them in (BC Government, strata Forms information: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/strata-information-buyers-sellers). The same is true of a depreciation report showing a major renewal ahead: since July 1, 2024 every strata with five or more lots must refresh its report every five years (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-regulations/depreciation-reports).

**Q: Can I use a "townhome median price" for my area to check the evaluation?**
A: No such published figure exists. SnapStats reports Detached versus "Condos & Townhomes" combined, and publishes community medians only — no townhome-only figure and no sub-area median (SnapStats monthly reports, as carried in this program's area research files). A CMA built on actual sold comparables is the better instrument for a single complex.

**Q: Does the evaluation involve a visit to my home?**
A: UNVERIFIED — the page does not say whether a home visit is involved, what the CMA contains, or what happens to the information submitted (research file §1–§2). Those are fair questions for the follow-up contact the page promises.

**Q: What disclosures should I expect when I deal with a licensee?**
A: At the outset of dealings, a licensee must give you disclosure covering the commission or fee they will earn, that they will act for one side only, their duties to clients and non-clients, and the risks of being unrepresented (BCFSA: https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professionals). Dual agency has been prohibited since 2018 except in the rarest circumstances. When that disclosure happens inside the evaluation process specifically is UNVERIFIED (§3).

**Q: Will selling costs change what my townhouse is "worth"?**
A: The CMA speaks to market value; your NET is a different number. Your buyer also pays property transfer tax on top of the price — $14,000 on an $800,000 resale with no exemption (BC Government, PTT: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/understand) — which is part of the affordability the list price is competing for.

**Q: My townhouse is in a 55+ complex. Does that change the evaluation?**
A: Yes — a valid 55+ bylaw (the only age restriction BC law still enforces, since November 24, 2022; Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1–123.2: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98043_08) narrows the buyer pool by design, so the evaluation should be built on comparables inside that same restricted pool rather than the open market.
