## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **PTT at this band — the rule that bites hardest:** general rates are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000 — every purchase in this band reaches the 3% tier on its top portion — plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 (only the residential portion on a mixed-class property; where land is classed as farm only because it is used for an owner's or farmer's dwelling, up to 0.5 hectares is treated as residential property). It is not a single "5% over $3M" rate. PTT is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office and is not annual property tax. **Worked example (inputs stated, illustrative arithmetic only):** at a $2,500,000 fair market value, all residential — $2,000 (1% × $200,000) + $36,000 (2% × $1,800,000) + $15,000 (3% × $500,000) = $53,000, with no further 2% because the value does not pass $3,000,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Neither BC purchase exemption reaches this band:** the First-Time Home Buyers' Program full exemption caps at a fair market value of $835,000 (partial to $860,000, exempting PTT on the first $500,000) and the Newly Built Home Exemption is full to $1,100,000 and fully eliminated at $1,150,000 — both far below this page's floor, so a buyer in this band budgets full PTT. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Home owner grant phase-out at this band:** the grant threshold for 2026 is $2,075,000 — a full regular grant at or below that assessed (or partitioned) value, reduced by $5 for each $1,000 over it, and $0 for the regular grant if the residential or partitioned value is over $2,189,000 — so many homes in this band receive a reduced grant or none, even as principal residences. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **GST and the federal rebates at this band:** 5% GST applies to new construction only, not resales. The FTHB GST rebate — Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 (100% of GST to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, maximum $50,000, agreements March 20, 2025–2031) — phases out below this band's floor, so it does not offset GST on an over-$1.8M new purchase. The New Housing Rebate (36% / maximum $6,300 / $350K–$450K) likewise does not reach this band. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Form B diligence (for any strata townhome that does appear in the band):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote. **CRF minimum:** since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since November 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1, 123.2), with Regulation 7.01 exemptions expanded May 1, 2023. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple contrast:** a fee-simple townhome in this band carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — municipal rules plus any registered covenants/HOA only; tenure must be proved from title, not from the feed card. Basis: Section 2 above.
- **City plan:** Surrey's current Official Community Plan is the Surrey 2050 OCP, Bylaw No. 21900 — Bylaw No. 18020 is the replaced predecessor and is never cited as current in this file. Surrey Zoning Bylaw, 1993, No. 12000 governs. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/surrey-homes-over-1-8m.md)
- **Floodplain and ALR — for the detached and large-lot side of the band:** the City states Surrey's coastal floodplain stretches from Boundary Bay and Mud Bay along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers towards Cloverdale and Newton, includes the Little Campbell River area near White Rock, makes up about 20% of Surrey's entire land area, and contains over 60 sq. km of agriculture land and 10% of the Agricultural Land Reserve in Metro Vancouver; most of the floodplain is protected by dykes, with sea dams, ditches, flood boxes, pumps and spillways completing the system. ALR status is parcel-specific through the Agricultural Land Commission's maps and online mapping tools. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/
- **Presales (REDMA):** a presale townhome is never counted as resale band stock in this file; where a presale is in scope, the buyer must receive the developer's disclosure statement (REDMA s. 18, deposits in trust) and has a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing or acknowledging it (REDMA s. 21). https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Buyer frame, for completeness:** HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5% — recorded for completeness; CMHC insurance is a sub-20%-down product and a purchase in this band is conventionally financed in the sources checked, so the premium table is background, not a closing line. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained

- **Q: Is there extra property transfer tax on a home over $3 million?** Yes. The Province applies a further 2% tax to the residential property value greater than $3,000,000, on top of the general tiers (1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000); on a mixed-class property the further 2% applies only to the residential portion. See the worked example in Section 3. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Does the first-time buyer exemption or the new-home exemption help at $1.8M?** No. The first-time program's full exemption caps at a fair market value of $835,000, and the newly built home exemption is fully eliminated at $1,150,000 — both far below this band, so a buyer here budgets full PTT. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Why is the townhome side of a $1.8M band so thin in Surrey?** On the sourced figures: Surrey's "Condos & Townhomes" combined median was $512,944 in September 2026 — the band floor is well above the median attached price — while the detached median was $1,215,000. The band is the upper tail of the detached market first; no townhome type split for the band is published. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/surrey-homes-over-1-8m.md)
- **Q: What should I check on a large Surrey property near farmland or the coast?** Two parcel-level checks: ALR status, through the Agricultural Land Commission's maps and online mapping tools; and floodplain status, since the City puts about 20% of Surrey's land area in its coastal floodplain, names Crescent Beach and Panorama/Gray Creek among its residential areas, and notes most of the floodplain is dyke-protected. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/ and https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- **Q: What does the home owner grant do at this level?** Less than buyers expect: the 2026 threshold is $2,075,000 of assessed value, the regular grant reduces by $5 per $1,000 over it, and it is $0 over $2,189,000 — two otherwise similar band homes straddling the threshold carry a real annual-cost difference. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **Q: Can I rent out a strata townhome in this band long-term?** Yes — strata rental-restriction bylaws have been unenforceable since November 24, 2022 anywhere in BC. What can still bind you is a short-term rental bylaw in the registered bylaws, and a 55+ age bylaw limiting who may live in the unit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Do schools matter at this price point?** The same way as at any price: Surrey Schools (SD36) provides a School Locator that identifies the catchment school based on the home address, so catchments are confirmed address by address, not by sub-area name or price. https://www.surreyschools.ca/cougarcreek/_ci/p/174781
