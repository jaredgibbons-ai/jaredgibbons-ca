## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**The verified program frame — CRA / Province / CMHC figures govern. Where this slug's research held a figure UNVERIFIED because a CRA page failed in that pass, the gap is stated openly and the program's verified core governs, per the gst-rebate townhomes precedent.**

- **FTHB GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122: together with the new housing rebate it provides 100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated home valued up to $1,000,000, is gradually reduced between $1,000,000 and $1,500,000, and is not available at $1,500,000 or more — maximum $50,000; purchase agreements March 20, 2025–2031. The live page's bullet ("save up to $50,000 in GST on new homes up to $1M") and its Taxes-section answer match this verified position (research file §4; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html).
- **New Housing Rebate:** generally 36% of the GST paid, maximum $6,300, gradually reduced between $350,000 and $450,000, and not available at $450,000 or more. The live FAQ page does not state the old rebate's own numbers; they are recorded here so a rebuild can add them accurately (research file §4; CRA News No. 122, as above).
- **GST itself:** the live page states GST of 5% applies to newly built and presale homes and not to resale homes — consistent with the rebate frame above, under which only new or substantially renovated housing carries GST (research file §4; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html).
- **PTT:** general rates 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — an "Over $3M — 5%" row is a compression of the 3% + further 2% structure and is flagged wherever met; no such row is documented in this file's research. The tax is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office, and PTT cannot be blended into the mortgage. Worked anchors, tier math shown: $700K → 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**; $1.0M → $2,000 + 2% × $800,000 ($16,000) = **$18,000**; $1.2M → **$22,000**; $1.5M → **$28,000** (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax).
- **PTT first-time buyers' program:** a qualifying buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 of the price where the property is used only as a principal residence, has a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), is 0.5 hectares or smaller and contains only residential improvements; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000. The live page's wording — full exemption on homes up to $500,000, the exemption applying to the first $500,000 (up to $8,000) for qualifying homes above $500,000 up to $835,000, phasing out completely at $860,000 — matches this verified position in substance (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers).
- **Down payment and mortgage insurance (CMHC, verified):** mortgage loan insurance is required when a down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more (where insurance is not available); **CMHC's published premium range is 0.6%–4.5%** of the total mortgage amount. Worked example, inputs stated and tier math shown: a $750,000 home → 5% × $500,000 ($25,000) + 10% × $250,000 ($25,000) = **$50,000 minimum down payment** — the live page's tiers and its $750,000 example match the verified tiers (research file §4; https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html). **No CMHC page error is asserted for this slug** — this file's research does NOT document the 2.80%–4.00% error for this page, so it is NOT documented for this slug and NOT asserted here (see §1 FILE STATUS).

## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: What is a subject in a BC purchase contract?**
A: A condition that must be met before the deal becomes firm — the live page lists financing, inspection, property disclosure statement, title search and strata document review as the common subjects, completed during a negotiable subject-removal period; the page's "typically 7 days" norm is practitioner copy and is UNVERIFIED in this file as a stated norm (§§2.5, 3; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html).

**Q: How much property transfer tax will I pay, and what does a first-time buyer save?**
A: At the general rates: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — $12,000 on $700,000 with the tier math shown in §3. A qualifying first-time buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 of the price at a fair market value of $835,000 or less, with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000 (§3; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers).

**Q: Do I pay GST on a home purchase in BC?**
A: On a new build or presale, yes — 5%; resale homes are generally exempt. A qualifying first-time buyer can recover up to $50,000 through the FTHB GST/HST rebate (100% of the GST to $1 million, gradually reduced to $1.5 million, none at $1.5 million or more), and the pre-existing new housing rebate alone returns 36% of the GST to a maximum of $6,300, phasing out between $350,000 and $450,000 (§3; CRA News No. 122).

**Q: What is the minimum down payment, and when is mortgage insurance required?**
A: 5% on homes of $500,000 or less; 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999 — $50,000 on a $750,000 home, tier math in §3; and 20% at $1,500,000 or more. Below 20% down, mortgage loan insurance is required, and CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount (§3; https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained).

**Q: Can I combine the Home Buyers' Plan and the FHSA, and how much can I take out?**
A: The CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal — verified in the sibling passes. The amounts are a different matter in this file: the live page's HBP figure ($60,000) and FHSA figure ($40,000) are UNVERIFIED in this slug's research — the CRA HBP page returned 403 in this program and the CRA FHSA main page verifies only the $8,000 first-year room — so §3 carries both limits on the program's verified core only, with that gap stated openly (§3; research file §7).

**Q: What happens to my deposit if I rescind or collapse the deal?**
A: Read the page's answers as page statements: its rescission summary (3 business days, 0.25% fee) is UNVERIFIED in this file — no BCFSA page was opened in this pass — and its deposit norm (typically around 5%, due upon or within 24 hours of subject removal) is practitioner copy with no official source. What governs any buyer is the signed contract and the current rescission rules; confirm both before writing subject-free (§§2.5, 3).

**Q: Deposit versus down payment — are they the same money?**
A: No — the page's structured FAQ treats them as separate questions for a reason: the deposit is the good-faith money paid under the contract (timing and amount per the contract; the page's "around 5%" norm is UNVERIFIED here), while the down payment is the total equity the lender's tiers in §3 require at completion, of which the deposit forms part. Price both from the contract and the CMHC tiers, not from a rule of thumb (§§2.5, 3).

**Q: How much should I budget for closing costs on top of the down payment?**
A: The page's rule of thumb — 1.5%–4% of the purchase price, with an inspection at $400–$700 — is UNVERIFIED practitioner copy in this file. The verified cash lines are PTT (tier math in §3, payable in cash and not blendable into the mortgage), GST on a new home net of any rebate, and legal/registration costs quoted for the specific purchase (§§2.5, 3).

**Q: Does getting pre-approved hold my rate, and for how long?**
A: The page states pre-approval rate holds of 90–120 days and a 1–2 week pre-approval process — both UNVERIFIED in this file as stated norms, with no lender source opened. Treat a hold as a lender-specific term to confirm in writing with your lender or broker (§2.5).
