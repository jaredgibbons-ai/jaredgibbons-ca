## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **Form B diligence (any strata townhouse in this band — Glenwood Village, the Kings Landing townhomes, and every cross-referenced strata):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. A detached purchase in the same band carries none of this — which is why the heading defect in §2 matters in diligence, not just in labelling. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold — none of the cross-referenced complexes is such a strata on its published scale. **CRF minimum:** since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since Nov 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw (and Surrey's STR regime below applies on top). Glenwood Village's recorded "Rentals Not Allowed" line is flagged VOID in-entry in §2 on exactly this rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, exemptions expanded May 1, 2023 for a younger spouse/partner, live-in caregivers, legacy residents and defined children). Directly relevant in this band: Glenwood Village is recorded 55+; its same-street namesake Heights is recorded with no age restriction. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple contrast — stated plainly.** A fee-simple house (most of this band's detached cards) carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees; municipal rules (Surrey Zoning Bylaw No. 12000) plus any registered covenants or homeowners' association govern instead. A half-duplex or townhome card in the same feed can carry the opposite treatment — read the Title to Land field card by card.
- **City plan:** Surrey's current Official Community Plan is the Surrey 2050 OCP, **Bylaw No. 21900, adopted June 15, 2026** — the former OCP, Bylaw No. 18020, is the replaced predecessor and is NEVER cited as current in this file. Zoning is Bylaw No. 12000. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **SSMUH (in force for Surrey since July 8, 2024):** a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop. The July 8, 2024 changes renamed the former RF-12 / RF-12C zones to the R4 Small Lot Residential Zone and RF-SD to R6 Semi-Detached — older material using RF-12 / RF-SD names is using pre-2024 zone names. The City states the SSMUH changes do NOT retroactively legalize a secondary suite or coach house built without permits. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **Secondary suites (Surrey):** one secondary suite may be permitted per single family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit, capped at **40% of the principal dwelling unit's habitable floor area** under Zoning Bylaw No. 12000; a suite is not permitted within a coach house or garden suite, and no 90 m² cap was found in the current consolidated bylaw text in the research pass. A coach house or garden suite must be at least 35 m² and at most 75 m², rising to 120 m² for lots zoned RA, R2, R2-O and R3 where the existing house or duplex is below maximum FAR, with one extra parking space required. Multiple detached cards in this feed describe basement suites in listing text (a walkout suite at 14683 61 Avenue; two suites described at 8708 143a Street; a 2-bedroom suite at 6131 147b Street) — listing text only, evidence that suite product sits inside this band, not a verified count. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/12000 and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/residential-building/residential-building-permits/coach-houses-garden-suites
- **Short-term rentals (Surrey):** STRs are permitted, owner-only — the host must be the property owner living in the home as their primary residence; tenants cannot operate them; a short-term rental is the renting of a dwelling unit by its owner for under 90 consecutive days per patron. The City's Short-Term Rentals page states the business licence fee is **$358 per year** — that figure is carried here. **DISCLOSED CONFLICT:** the City's 2024 corporate report on the policy proposed $350 per year plus fees — confirm the current figure on the City's licence page before quoting it to a client. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/short-term-rentals
- **Surrey property taxes:** due July 2, with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2 — city-wide figures, not band-specific. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- **PTT and financing tiers — worked examples, inputs stated, tier math shown.** PTT rates: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million; a page row "Over $3M — 5%" is a compression of 3% + 2% — the tier math governs, and no purchase in this band reaches the $2M tier. *Example A (the feed's townhome card price):* a $599,999 resale townhouse, no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $399,999 ($7,999.98) = **$9,999.98**. *Example B (near the ceiling):* a $1,785,000 detached purchase, no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $1,585,000 ($31,700) = **$33,700**. First-time buyers — exempt on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000, so only the lower-priced part of this band can sit inside the full-exemption value range on price alone; newly built home exemption — full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000. **Down-payment / insurance tiers (CMHC, as the research states them):** homes $500,000 or less require a minimum 5% down; homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999 require 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes at $1,500,000 or more require a minimum 20% down with insurance not available — this band straddles all three tiers.
