## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**The "rules" for this utility page are the BC private-sector privacy framework the rebuild must be written against, stated only from the official sources the research read (labelled search-result reads, because direct opens of those hosts returned a fetch-service 429 in that pass and that line was stopped). The strata and purchase blocks below are the standard frame for any townhome transaction a reader of this page may go on to make — the privacy page itself contains no strata or purchase information about any complex.**

- **Governing framework (Province of BC):** to collect, use or disclose personal information, private sector organizations in BC must follow the personal information and privacy rules set out in the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), which are based on the 10 principles of privacy protection, and the rules generally apply to all private sector organizations including corporations, partnerships, unincorporated associations and not-for-profit organizations (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/managing-a-business/running-a-business/protect-personal-information, read via search result 2026-11-01).
- **Privacy officer and policy duties (Province of BC):** every organization must designate at least one privacy officer to oversee privacy practices and ensure compliance with PIPA, with the privacy officer as the primary contact for privacy-related questions and complaints; every organization must have a privacy policy, available upon request, that explains how personal information is collected, used, disclosed, stored and disposed of; and an organization is responsible for all personal information it handles, including information shared with contractors (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/managing-a-business/running-a-business/protect-personal-information, read via search result 2026-11-01).
- **Oversight and safeguards (OIPC):** organizations in BC are subject to PIPA, which has rules for how and when organizations can collect, use and/or disclose personal information (information about identifiable individuals such as name, account numbers and photographs, including information about customers); organizations also need to make reasonable security arrangements to protect personal information; and the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner (OIPC) for BC oversees and enforces the administration of PIPA, including investigating privacy complaints and whether organizations have reasonable safeguards (research file §4; https://www.oipc.bc.ca/documents/guidance-documents/3134, read via search result 2026-11-01).
- **Breaches (OIPC):** when personal information is collected, used, disclosed or destroyed in contravention of PIPA it is called a privacy breach; the OIPC guidance sets out a four-step breach response (containment, risk evaluation, notification, prevention) and organizations can report breaches to the OIPC (research file §4; https://www.oipc.bc.ca/documents/guidance-documents/3134, read via search result 2026-11-01).
