## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**The strata block (applies when the new home bought is a strata townhome or condo).**

- **Form B diligence.** SPA s.59: Form B within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢ per page — fees, amounts owing, special levies, CRF balance, insurance, rules, budget, Rental Disclosure Statement and depreciation report attached (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate).
- **Depreciation reports.** Stratas with 5+ lots must obtain a depreciation report every 5 years since July 1, 2024; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold. **CRF minimum:** 10% of the operating budget since November 1, 2023 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing).
- **Rentals.** Rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022. Short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation).
- **Age.** Only 55+ age bylaws are valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01; exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion). A 45+ bylaw is void and does not convert to 55+ (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws).
- **Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhome carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — municipal rules and registered covenants/HOA only.

**Purchase taxes, rebates and financing — the verified core of this topic.**

- **FTHB GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122: together with the new housing rebate it provides 100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated single unit residential complex or residential condominium unit valued up to $1,000,000; between $1,000,000 and $1,500,000 the rebate is gradually reduced; no rebate at $1,500,000 or more; maximum $50,000; purchase agreements March 20, 2025–2031, construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036 (research file §4; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html).

## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: How much GST can a first-time buyer in BC get back on a new home?**
A: Up to $50,000: 100% of the GST up to $1 million, gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and nothing at $1.5 million or more (§3; CRA News No. 122).

**Q: When did the rebate become law?**
A: Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 — not March 12, as some secondary sources say. Eligibility turns on agreements entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (§3).

**Q: Who counts as a first-time buyer for this rebate?**
A: At least 18, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, with no home owned and lived in as a primary residence by the buyer or their spouse or common-law partner in the purchase year or the preceding four calendar years (§3).

**Q: Is the phase-out a straight line — do I get 50% back at $1.25 million?**
A: UNVERIFIED as a mechanism. The live page works that example, but CRA's pages opened say only "gradually reduced" between $1 million and $1.5 million — do not rely on the linear formula until CRA's calculation is captured (§3).

**Q: What happened to the old new housing rebate?**
A: It still exists on its own terms: 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, phasing out between $350,000 and $450,000. The FTHB rebate is the top-up that takes a qualifying first-time buyer's combined recovery to 100% (§3).

**Q: How and when do I claim?**
A: Usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction: a builder can credit the rebate at closing with Form GST190, or you apply directly to CRA; owner-builders use Form GST191 (§3).

**Q: Can I stack the GST rebate with BC's PTT exemption?**
A: Yes — separate federal and provincial programs. BC's first-time program exempts PTT on the first $500,000 where fair market value is $835,000 or less, with a partial exemption to $860,000 (§3).

**Q: Does a resale townhome qualify?**
A: No. The rebate is for new or substantially renovated housing only; resale homes are not charged GST in the first place (§§1, 2.5).

**Q: Are the page's Home Buyers' Plan and tax-credit figures verified?**
A: No — the HBP figures and the $10,000 / $1,500 tax-credit figures on the live page are UNVERIFIED site copy in the research file, because the CRA pages for them did not load or were not re-read in that pass (§3).

**Q: Does this file count any complexes?**
A: No — it is a topic file with no roster of its own and counts 0 (§2).

## 5. Local details a buyer would not know

- **The signing date matters more than the completion date.** A presale signed before March 20, 2025 is outside the rebate no matter the price or completion year; construction must still begin before 2031 and be substantially completed before 2036 (§3).
- **The $50,000 headline is really two rebates stacked.** The long-standing new housing rebate alone is worth at most $6,300; the FTHB rebate tops the combined recovery up to 100% only for qualifying first-time buyers (§3).
- **The page's worked table outruns its source at one row.** The $500,000, $750,000, $1,000,000 and $1,500,000 rows follow directly from the verified thresholds; the $1,250,000 row depends on the page's straight-line mechanism, which CRA's opened pages do not state (§3; research file §5).
- **March 12 versus March 13 is a real fork in secondary coverage.** CRA News No. 122 governs: Royal Assent was March 13, 2026 (§1).
