## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

### The settled court-ordered-sale treatment (BC Supreme Court)

- **Step 1 — the petition:** a foreclosure proceeding must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7(1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner may also join a claim arising out of the mortgage and a person liable to pay the mortgage debt — Rule 21-7(3); if the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation, a person who subsequently registers an interest need not be served, is bound by an order in the proceeding, and may file a response — Rule 21-7(4); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). Rule 21-7 sets out no demand step before the petition. This subrule range WAS read on an official source in this slug's own research pass. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/chilliwackforeclosuresandcourtsales.md)
- **Step 2 — the order and the redemption period:** the court may make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7(5)(a); order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption — Rule 21-7(5)(b); and determine or certify the amount due — Rule 21-7(5)(c). The redemption period has NO fixed length in the Rules: it is the period the court fixes. The Land Title Act refers to this order as an "order nisi," with a later final order of foreclosure — Land Title Act s. 271(1), and interest is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem — Law and Equity Act s. 19. The Rule 21-7(5)(a)–(c) wording WAS read in this slug's pass; the Law and Equity Act and Land Title Act wordings are carried on the settled Abbotsford treatment, UNVERIFIED in this slug's own pass (429). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Final order / applying for a sale:** in default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7(6); a party may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7(7). Carried on the settled Abbotsford treatment; this slug's research file could not read Rule 21-7 beyond (5)(c) in its own pass (429) and marks these subrules UNVERIFIED in its own pass. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5(3); the court's directions may appoint who has conduct, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of value — Rule 13-5(4). Carried on the settled Abbotsford/Langley treatment; the Rule 13-5 page could not be opened in this slug's own pass (429) and those mechanics are UNVERIFIED in that pass. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Court approval of the sale:** the manner of sale may be fixed as sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5(4)(b); the person having conduct may apply to the court, even though the time for redemption has not expired, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7(9); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in **Form 60** by the person having conduct of the sale — Rule 13-5(6). That vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. Carried on the settled treatment, with the same 429 basis note as above. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Bids and practice direction PD-66:** the court's directions may define "the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids" — Rule 13-5(4)(d); the Supreme Court's civil practice direction **PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings,"** governs practice in these proceedings and replaced the earlier sealed-bid practice direction PD-62, per the Court of Appeal. The text of PD-66 was not retrieved in either research pass, so the timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66 are UNVERIFIED in detail; the live page's account of sealed, subject-free backup offers submitted on the morning of a hearing is the page's own account and is not adopted here. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm and https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwackforeclosuresandcourtsales.html
- **Where the proceeding is filed:** unless the court otherwise orders, a foreclosure proceeding is commenced at the Supreme Court registry in the municipality where the land is located, if there is one there, and otherwise at a registry in the judicial district in which the land is located, and applications are heard at that registry — Law and Equity Act s. 21(2), carried on the settled Abbotsford treatment; this slug's research file did not verify that wording or any registry address in its own pass (429) — see the UNVERIFIED live-page registry claim in §1. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Court records:** Court Services Online is BC's electronic court registry; eSearch is available to anyone with Internet access, Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry location, party names and filed documents, and its file classes include "H - Foreclosure." It publishes no area statistics. Carried on the settled Abbotsford treatment; the Court Services Online pages could not be opened in this slug's own pass (429). https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period on a Chilliwack townhouse?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — it is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What can the court order at the first stage?** Under Rule 21-7(5) the court may make a final order of foreclosure, order payment of what is due within a court-fixed redemption period with foreclosure in default, or determine or have certified the amount due. Do not assume every file is at an early, reversible stage. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What does "subject to court approval" mean for my offer?** Where the court has directed sale by contract conditional on its approval (Rule 13-5(4)(b), settled treatment), the person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7(9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title in you — until that order, the sale is not final. Do not book movers on acceptance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** UNVERIFIED as a stated process rule in this slug's own research pass — the bid-rights wording in Rule 13-5(4)(d) and the PD-66 practice direction could not be opened in that pass (429), though the settled treatment carries the bid-rights direction power in §3. Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for, and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing before spending on inspections. (§3 above)
- **Q: Will I get a Form B and depreciation report for a court-ordered townhouse?** UNCONFIRMED — that is the honest answer at topic level. In an ordinary resale you would; in a court sale, what documents are produced is a per-file fact stated in the listing/file documents, and no source in this research states a general rule. Budget your diligence around what the file actually produces. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Q: Do I pay PTT and GST the normal way on a court-ordered sale?** UNCONFIRMED at topic level — no source in this slug's research file states the PTT/GST treatment of court sales. Confirm both for your specific transaction before removing subjects; do not rely on a blog summary of "foreclosure taxes." (§3 above)
- **Q: Where can I check the court file myself?** Court Services Online eSearch — Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure" (settled treatment; UNVERIFIED in this slug's own pass). For Chilliwack, confirm the file's registry location there — the live page's Chilliwack Law Courts claim is UNVERIFIED in this file. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: Can the owner still save the property after I offer?** The owner may redeem by paying what is due within the redemption period the court fixes, and the person with conduct of sale may apply to confirm a sale even before the redemption period expires (settled treatment) — ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Is a court-ordered townhouse cheaper than market?** No official source publishes Chilliwack foreclosure counts, discounts or pricing statistics, CADREB publishes board-wide only, and SnapStats has no foreclosure category and does not cover Chilliwack — any "typical discount" figure is UNVERIFIED. https://www.cadreb.com/market/
- **Q: I'm the owner in difficulty — what is my window?** The redemption period the court fixes under Rule 21-7(5)(b) is the window to pay what is due or sell before a final order of foreclosure can be granted. Earlier advice is worth more than late advice. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
