## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**The strata block (applies when the home being sold is a strata townhome — the seller-side diligence a buyer will run).**

- **Form B diligence.** SPA s. 59: Form B within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢ per page — fees, amounts owing, special levies, CRF balance, insurance, rules, budget, Rental Disclosure Statement and depreciation report attached. A townhome seller should expect the buyer's diligence to run through exactly this package (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate).
- **Depreciation reports.** Stratas with 5+ lots must obtain a depreciation report every 5 years since July 1, 2024; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold. **CRF minimum:** 10% of the operating budget since November 1, 2023 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing).
- **Rentals.** Rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 — a seller cannot market a strata townhome on a "rentals not allowed" restriction, and a buyer cannot be bound by one for long-term rentals. Short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation).
- **Age.** Only 55+ age bylaws are valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01; exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion; a 45+ bylaw is void and does NOT convert to 55+). A 55+ townhome's buyer pool is the bylaw's pool — the selling page does not state this, and it is carried here as the governing law, not as page content (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws).
- **Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhome sale carries NO strata, NO Form B, NO strata fees — title, municipal rules and registered covenants/HOA only. The page's eight steps apply to both tenures; the document package does not.

**The selling framework this page operates under (regulator facts opened in the research pass).**

- **BCFSA consumer framework:** a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, warn unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; representation, duties and the BCFSA complaint route must be disclosed at the outset, and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances (research file §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers).
- **Cooling-off / rescission:** if a buyer rescinds under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the balance of the deposit to the buyer, with no separate release form (research file §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits). The page's own statement matches (research file §4).
- **Deposits:** usually held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder; where subjects are not removed the deposit is not automatically returned and both parties must sign a release, except on rescission (research file §4; same source).
- **BC home flipping tax:** applies to profit from selling a BC property (including presale contracts) owned less than 730 days, under the Residential Property (Short-Term Holding) Profit Tax Act effective January 1, 2025 — 20% of net taxable income within 365 days, grading to no tax at 730 days, with a return due within 90 days where the tax or a filing-based exemption applies. The page's shorter wording ("20% of the profit," "drops to zero by day 730") is a simplification of "net taxable income" and the filing conditions as the Province states them (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/bc-home-flipping-tax).

**Purchase taxes, rebates and financing (buyer-side, stated because sellers price against them).**

- **FTHB GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122: 100% of GST to $1,000,000, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1,500,000, agreements March 20, 2025–2031 (CRA: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html).
- **New Housing Rebate:** 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out (CRA, same source).
- **PTT:** 1% / 2% / 3% tiers plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — an "Over $3M — 5%" row is flagged as a compression of the 3% + further 2% structure wherever met. Exemptions: first-time buyers full on first $500,000 at ≤$835,000, phasing to $860,000; newly built full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/understand).
- **HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5%** (CMHC homeowner premium schedule). **No CMHC page error is asserted for this slug** — this file's research does not document the 2.80%–4.00% error for the selling page, so it is NOT documented for this slug and NOT asserted here. ## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: What are the steps to sell a home, according to the page?**
A: Eight steps: a free first chat; pricing from recent nearby sales and current listings; getting ready with cleaning, small repairs and decluttering; professional photos, video, floor plans and drone photos, then MLS® listing; showings with feedback after each one; offers walked through on price, deposit, conditions and dates; conditions and the cooling-off period; and closing through a lawyer or notary, with keys on possession day (research file §4; https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html).

**Q: What does it cost to sell a home in BC?**
A: The page lists legal/notary fees, mortgage-discharge legal fees, GST on legal fees, a possible prepayment penalty and the seller's property-tax share — and states no commission figure at all. That omission is this slug's documented defect: BCFSA requires commission and fee information be given to the consumer in the listing conversation, so the full figure is UNVERIFIED from the page (§3).

**Q: Is the "Top 1% and Top 10" line an approved claim?**
A: No — not in that form. The approved claim is "Top 1%." The page's line adds "and Top 10 out of all Realtors in the Fraser Valley," expanding the approved claim; it is carried here as a flagged page statement, UNVERIFIED against board numbers (§3).

**Q: Can a buyer back out after I accept their offer?**
A: For most homes, yes, inside the Home Buyer Rescission Period: within three business days after acceptance, in writing, and the brokerage then pays you the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the balance of the deposit to the buyer, with no release form required (research file §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits).

**Q: Do I pay tax if I sell soon after buying?**
A: Possibly the BC home flipping tax: profit on a BC property owned less than 730 days, at 20% of net taxable income within 365 days, grading to zero at day 730, with a return due within 90 days where the tax or a filing-based exemption applies (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/bc-home-flipping-tax).

**Q: What disclosures apply when I hire a BC realtor?**
A: BCFSA states a licensee must give consumers commission and fee information, explain the duties owed to clients and non-clients, warn unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, work for only the buyer or the seller, and make the representation, duties and complaint-route disclosures at the outset (research file §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers).

**Q: Does selling a townhome work differently from selling a house on this page?**
A: The page's eight steps are the same. The difference is the document package: a strata townhome sale runs through the Form B regime (fees, levies, CRF, depreciation report), while a fee-simple townhome carries no strata package at all (§3).

**Q: How long will my home take to sell?**
A: UNVERIFIED — the page's own answer is that timing "depends on your home, price and the market at the time," and no days-on-market figure is stated on the page or in this file (research file §5).

**Q: Does every listing really get staging, video, floor plans and drone photos?**
A: The page promises all four "with every listing," twice. Whether every listing in fact receives all four is UNVERIFIED — it cannot be confirmed from the page itself, and the site's marketing-plan page names none of video, drone or floor plans (research file §5).
