## 2. COMPLEXES

**This land topic file carries NO townhouse roster of its own — verified townhouse complexes counted here: 0 (strata 0, fee-simple 0).** A vacant lot has no townhouse on it; townhouse complexes in Chilliwack are counted once in their sub-area townhomes files and are never re-counted here.

- **Bare-land strata — documented as its own form, NOT counted as townhomes:** the Strata Property Act defines a "bare land strata plan" as a strata plan on which the boundaries of the strata lots are defined on a horizontal plane by reference to survey markers and not by reference to the floors, walls or ceilings of a building. The lot owner builds (or contracts to build) the home on the lot, and the common property can include roads, streetlights, a septic system, a clubhouse, playground and forested areas. You cannot tell by looking whether detached homes sit on a bare-land strata — the words "bare land strata" appear on the first page of the strata plan. Tenure in the feed's framing: land parcels are overwhelmingly freehold, and bare-land strata lots appear in newer subdivisions (a card shown in the research pass is a unit-numbered land listing on Chilliwack Mountain), but no Chilliwack-wide freehold/strata split is published on a source opened: UNVERIFIED, and no named bare-land strata complex is published for this topic — any named complex here would be UNCONFIRMED, and none is listed. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol27/consol27/98043_01 and https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/ (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/chilliwack-lots-land.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

### The land frame — zoning, ALR and subdivision as the research documents them

- **SSMUH:** Chilliwack's small-scale multi-unit housing amendment is Zoning Bylaw Amendment Bylaw 2024, No. 5395, which amended Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000 and was adopted June 18, 2024. The City's signed staff report for Bylaw 5395 describes it as allowing 3 to 4 dwelling units on parcels within the urban growth boundary zoned for single-detached and duplex housing — directly relevant to what a serviced urban lot in this feed can carry; eligibility is parcel-specific. - **Suites and ADUs:** a secondary suite is capped at the lesser of 90 m² and 40% of the principal dwelling, one accessory dwelling unit is permitted per lot, garden suites are capped at 100 m², and parking rules sit at s. 5.04 (Zoning Bylaw No. 5000, ss. 2, 4.06, 5.04). (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/rules-gaps-2026-11-01.md, item 7f)
- **ALR:** properties in the ALR are subject to the Agricultural Land Commission Act and ALR regulations; ALC approval may be required before the City can approve related development applications, and the City directs owners to check ALR status on the City Webmap; an ALC application may be required for a new house, coach house or suite, for placing fill or removing soil, for non-farm uses, and for subdivision or a boundary adjustment, with ALC applications referred to the City for review. ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6; Strata Property Act s. 239; ALCA s. 21(1)) — creating a bare-land strata on ALR land is itself a subdivision and needs the same ALC permission. The ALC caps a principal residence at 500 m² total floor area, permits a secondary suite only within the principal residence, and limits an additional residence to up to 90 m² (parcels ≤40 ha) or 186 m² (parcels >40 ha). Farm-class thresholds run in the full three-tier form ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR wherever met. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009 and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

### The strata block — bare-land strata lots only

- **Form B (bare-land strata lots):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — where a lot is a bare-land strata lot, a resale purchase still runs through the strata: the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fee, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. For a freehold lot there is NO Form B at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports and CRF (bare-land strata):** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, no deferral by vote — this applies to a bare-land strata corporation as it does to a building strata, and its report covers the common property (roads, systems, amenities), not the houses on the lots; a sub-5-lot bare-land strata sits UNDER that threshold. Minimum CRF contribution: at least 10% of the operating fund since November 1, 2023. Insurance difference: the strata corporation insures common property, common assets and buildings shown on the strata plan — a house entirely inside a bare-land strata lot is not shown on the plan, so the lot owner insures the house. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements and https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/
- **Rental and age bylaws (bare-land strata):** rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since Nov 24, 2022 — no strata, including a bare-land strata, may enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw; short-term rentals can still be restricted, and any directory "rentals not allowed" line is flagged void in-entry. Only 55+ age bylaws are lawful (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, exemptions expanded May 1, 2023). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Fee-simple contrast — stated plainly.** A fee-simple lot (and a fee-simple townhouse built on one) carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees; municipal rules (Zoning Bylaw No. 5000) plus any registered covenants or homeowners' association govern instead.
- **Chilliwack property taxes (city ground only):** notices are mailed at the end of May and taxes are due the first working day after July 1; a 5% penalty applies to taxes outstanding after July 2, 2026 and an additional 5% to taxes outstanding after August 4, 2026. FVRD electoral areas use the separate rural regime (Taxation (Rural Area) Act s. 11, 5% after July 2 + 5% after Oct 31); never mix the two. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2522
- **Taxes and rebates:** PTT is 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million; a page row reading "Over $3M — 5%" is a compression of the 3% tier plus that further 2% — flagged; the tier math governs. Worked example, inputs stated: a $400,000 vacant lot with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $200,000 ($4,000) = **$6,000** in general PTT — the general tiers only; GST treatment of a vacant-lot purchase and any first-time-buyer treatment for a lot-plus-build are UNVERIFIED in the research pass, so no GST figure is stated here and the live page's GST and down-payment summaries are not adopted. Exemption thresholds for reference: first-time buyers — exempt on the first $500,000 at $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000; newly built home exemption — full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 (application to a lot-plus-build: UNVERIFIED, confirm with a lawyer). FTHB GST rebate — Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122 (100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031); New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350K–$450K; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5%. Land-financing/down-payment terms: UNVERIFIED — no lender source was opened. No CMHC defect is asserted here — this file's own research documents no such defect for this slug. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Are there any townhouses in this file?** None — count 0 by design. This is a lots-and-land topic; the only strata form a lot can take is a bare-land strata lot (§2). Chilliwack townhouses live in the Chilliwack sub-area townhomes files in this series, counted once there. (§2 above)
- **Q: What is a bare-land strata lot, in plain terms?** A plot of land that is a strata lot: lot boundaries drawn on a horizontal plane by survey markers, not by a building's floors, walls or ceilings. You own the lot, you build the house, and a strata corporation owns the common property (roads, systems, amenities) and charges strata fees. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol27/consol27/98043_01
- **Q: How do I tell if a Chilliwack lot is bare-land strata or freehold?** Not by looking — detached homes on a bare-land strata look like any detached homes. The words "bare land strata" appear on the first page of the strata plan at the Land Title Office; if the homes are drawn on the plan, it is a building strata instead. https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/
- **Q: Who insures the house on a bare-land strata lot?** You do. The strata corporation insures common property, common assets and buildings shown on the strata plan — a house entirely inside a bare-land strata lot is not shown on the plan, so it is the lot owner's policy, not the strata's. https://visoa.bc.ca/resources/about-bare-land-strata-corporations/
- **Q: How much of Chilliwack is agricultural land?** The City states agriculture accounts for 67% of the City's total land base and 29% of its economic activity, and its OCP carries an Agricultural Area Plan for that land. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- **Q: How do I check if a Chilliwack lot is in the ALR?** The City directs owners to the City Webmap to check ALR status; ALR properties are subject to the Agricultural Land Commission Act and regulations, and an ALC application may be required for a new house, fill or soil removal, a non-farm use, or a subdivision or boundary adjustment. An ALR residence cannot be stratified. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- **Q: Can a serviced Chilliwack lot carry more than one home?** The City's SSMUH amendment, Bylaw No. 5395 (adopted June 18, 2024), allows 3 to 4 dwelling units on parcels within the urban growth boundary zoned for single-detached and duplex housing, per the City's staff-report description — eligibility is parcel-specific and is confirmed with the City. Separately: one secondary suite (lesser of 90 m² and 40%), one ADU per lot, garden suites capped at 100 m². (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/rules-gaps-2026-11-01.md, items 6 and 7f)
- **Q: What permit areas should I check on a hillside lot?** The OCP's Schedule D lists D-2 Hillside Environment and D-5 Hillside Development, along with D-1 Drinking Water Supply and D-3 Riparian Area; which areas apply to a specific lot is a per-parcel check with the City. The current OCP list does not use the live page's "DPA2 (Hillside and Upland)" name. https://chilliwack.com/ocp
