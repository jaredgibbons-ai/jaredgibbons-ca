## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

The townhome-relevant frame only, as far as sources support: what selling a strata townhome actually runs into, behind the marketing.

- **The regulator's framework applies from the outset of dealings:** in BC, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings — whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA — and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. Practical consequence for this page: commission and fee information is part of what BCFSA requires a licensee to give a consumer, but the page itself states no fee or commission figure — so any fee discussion for a townhome seller reading this page happens off-page, in the listing conversation, and no fee is stated in this file. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://jaredgibbons.ca/marketing-plan.html
- **Form B — the document a townhome seller's marketing has to survive:** Strata Property Act s. 59 — on a resale strata townhome sale the strata must provide a Form B Information Certificate stating the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance, and a summary of the strata's insurance, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. Photography and promotion bring the buyers; this package is what their agents price against. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, stratas with five or more lots must obtain one every five years, no deferral by vote; stratas with four or fewer lots sit UNDER that threshold. **Minimum CRF contribution:** at least 10% of the operating fund since November 1, 2023. A current depreciation report is part of the document package a townhome listing presents — its findings shape the questions a marketed listing will face. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/ and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void** since Nov 24, 2022 — marketing copy for a townhome listing that repeats a directory or bylaw line stating "rentals not allowed" is repeating old bylaw text, not current law, and is flagged void wherever met in this program; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **55+ bylaws:** Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 — only a 55-or-older residency bylaw is lawful; exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children. Where a townhome being marketed is in a 55+ complex, the lawful buyer pool is defined by this bylaw — a fact the marketing has to state accurately, from the registered bylaws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple townhome contrast — stated plainly:** a fee-simple townhome carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees; municipal rules plus any registered covenants or homeowners' association govern instead. The marketing-plan page does not distinguish the two tenures at all — which documents a seller must produce is a tenure question the listing conversation, not the page, answers. (§2 above)
- **City rules behind a Surrey / Langley townhome listing (the page's own geography):** Surrey — OCP Surrey 2050 Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted June 15, 2026; the replaced Bylaw No. 18020 is never cited as current), Zoning Bylaw No. 12000, SSMUH in force July 8, 2024 (3 units on lots under 280 m², 4 units over 280 m², up to 6 on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop), one secondary suite per house capped at 40% of the principal unit's habitable floor area, and short-term rentals permitted owner-only with the City licence stated at $358/yr on the City's page (**disclosed conflict:** the City's 2024 corporate report proposed $350/yr plus fees — confirm on the City licence page). These rules govern the property being marketed, not the plan itself; they are stated here because pricing and presentation strategy — the plan's stated starting point — is set against them for a Surrey townhome. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/short-term-rentals
- **GST on new townhomes only:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — so a seller's GST position depends on whether the unit is a developer sale or a resale, and listing copy must not blur the two. First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122) — 100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031; GST New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out still exists. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property Transfer Tax (BC):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million — a page row reading "Over $3M — 5%," wherever met on the site, is a compression of the 3% tier plus that further 2%, flagged as a compression; it is not a rate stated on this page. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial $835,000–$860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000. These are buyer-side taxes, stated here because pricing strategy is set against the buyer's total cost, not the list price alone. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Presales (REDMA):** where the townhome being marketed is a developer-contract presale rather than a listed resale, REDMA s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust under s. 18 — a different sale from the MLS® listing route this page describes. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/
- **Federal savings vehicles and mortgage insurance:** Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer from RRSPs; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime, $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5%. No CMHC range defect is asserted for this page — this file's own research does not document one, so the known live-page 2.80%–4.00% pattern is NOT asserted here. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/second-home
- **Listing-data notice the site carries:** the standard site footer notice states the data relating to real estate on the website comes in part from the MLS® Reciprocity program of either the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® (GVR), the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board (FVREB) or the Chilliwack and District Real Estate Board (CADREB); listings held by participating firms are marked with the MLS® logo and include the listing agent's name; the representation is based on board-generated data the boards do not warrant for accuracy; and the materials may not be reproduced without the boards' express written consent. CREA owns the MLS® and REALTOR® trademarks, and agents must be members of a local board and CREA to access the MLS® system. https://jaredgibbons.ca/marketing-plan.html and https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
