## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **Form B diligence (the 1 strata complex above, and any strata unit in the valley):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. For a 1997 mountain strata this is the central diligence document — it is also where the Sakwi Creek Road strata's plan number, unit count and registered bylaws are confirmed, all UNVERIFIED from public sources in this pass. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote. The Sakwi Creek Road strata's lot count is UNCONFIRMED, so its 5+ status is confirmed on the Form B/strata plan; a sub-5-lot strata would sit UNDER that threshold. **CRF minimum:** since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. A 1990s strata at a ski hill carries exactly the roof, envelope and snow-load exposure these two rules price into fees — read the depreciation report before comparing Hemlock fees to a city building's. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since Nov 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. No directory "rentals not allowed" line is carried for the counted complex in §2, so there is no in-entry line to flag here; the Enzian Way apartment building's "rentals allowed with restrictions" line (§2) is flagged on exactly this rule — any long-term component is void. Hemlock's pattern is short-term/vacation use — the FVRD describes the valley's dwellings as mainly vacation homes and short-term rentals — but no specific property's rental permission, FVRD position or licensing is confirmed by that description. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/hemlock.md)

- **RURAL property tax regime — explicitly different from Mission's and Chilliwack's:** Hemlock Valley is unincorporated, so the owner receives the property tax notice from the Province's Surveyor of Taxes Office and pays property taxes to the Province, not to the FVRD or any city. Under the **Taxation (Rural Area) Act s. 11**, rural property taxes are due **July 2**, a **5% penalty is added to any portion unpaid on July 2, and a further 5% penalty is added to any portion unpaid on October 31**. Do NOT apply the City of Mission's flat 10% penalty (Community Charter scheme) or the City of Chilliwack's staged July/August dates here — this file's properties sit in the rural regime only. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/rural-area and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01
- **Taxes and rebates:** PTT is 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million; a page row reading "Over $3M — 5%" is a compression of the 3% tier plus that further 2% — the tier math governs. Exemptions: first-time buyers — exempt on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000; newly built home exemption — full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000. Worked example, inputs stated: a $500,000 resale townhouse with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $300,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**. FTHB GST rebate — Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122 (100% of GST to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031); New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350K–$450K; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5%. 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — the counted strata is 1997 resale stock. ## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The mailing address lies twice (2026-11-01).** Valley listings carry both "Mission, BC V0M 1A1" and "Agassiz, BC V0M 1A1" addresses for the same FVRD Electoral Area C ground — a buyer's tax notice comes from the Province's Surveyor of Taxes, permits come from the FVRD, and neither Mission nor Kent city hall has the file. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/hemlock.md)
- **The tax penalty is the rural one, and it is staged.** 5% after July 2 and a further 5% after October 31 under the Taxation (Rural Area) Act s. 11 — a buyer cross-shopping a Mission city townhouse (flat 10% on July 3) and a Hemlock unit is comparing two different penalty regimes, two different payees, and two different plans. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01
- **The whole village exists because of a Crown licence.** The Controlled Recreation Area was established by the Province in 2016 as part of approval of the Resort Master Plan, and it carries the licence of occupation that lets the resort developer use Crown land for the Master Plan's purposes — the resort's existence is a provincial tenure instrument, which is why the FVRD plan, not a municipal one, governs the village. https://pub-fvrd.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11691
- **The expansion numbers are a vision, not a build-out schedule.** The OCP records a phased vision toward 19,969 bed units and a Balanced Resort Capacity of 16,641 visitors per day, against a built base of 226 resort residential units (1,072 bed units) and a current Balanced Resort Capacity of 1,640 guests per day. The gap between those two pairs of numbers is the single most mis-sold fact in the valley. https://pub-fvrd.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11691
- **Three Edelweiss Drive addresses are three different tenure stories.** 20649 Edelweiss Drive reads as a townhouse in form with no tenure stated in its listing; 20668 Edelweiss Drive is expressly "NO STRATA" freehold fourplex product; and the directory's Rocky Bluff entries at 20619/20629 Edelweiss Drive are freehold strata with their built form UNCONFIRMED. A search that blurs the street blurs the diligence. (§2 above)
- **The homeowners have been organised since before the current resort name.** The Hemlock Valley Homeowners Association was established in 1980 — the same year the building directory gives for the Enzian Way building — and works on behalf of valley homeowners as a non-profit. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/hemlock.md)
- **There is an evacuation guide for this village.** The FVRD publishes a Hemlock Valley Evacuation Guide whose route runs past Sts'ailes First Nation and Harrison Mills — a single-road mountain community plans its exit in writing, and a full-time buyer should read that document before the strata documents. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/hemlock.md)
- **The ski hill runs on natural snow only.** The OCP records a 100% natural snowpack across 146.8 hectares on 35 named trails — no snowmaking figure appears in the plan, so season length is a weather fact, not an operator promise. https://pub-fvrd.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11691
