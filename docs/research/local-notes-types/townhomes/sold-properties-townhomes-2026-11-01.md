## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **Form B diligence (for any strata townhouse a buyer is pricing off this record):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. A sold card is not disclosure; the Form B package on the buyer's own purchase governs. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote; a strata with four or fewer lots sits UNDER that threshold. **CRF minimum:** since Nov 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since Nov 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. Any directory or listing line stating "rentals not allowed" for a strata townhouse is void for long-term rentals and is flagged as such wherever it appears in this program. **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2), with Regulation 7.01 exemptions expanded May 1, 2023. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple contrast:** a fee-simple townhouse carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — diligence is title, municipal rules and any registered covenants or HOA documents only, stated plainly. Tenure must be proved from title, not from a sold card. Basis: Section 2 above.
- **Taxes and rebates:** PTT general rates are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — a page row reading "Over $3M — 5%" is a compression of that 3% + 2% structure, not a single rate. First-time buyers' exemption: full on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000; newly built home exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000. FTHB GST rebate — Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 (100% to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, max $50,000, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031); New Housing Rebate 36% / max $6,300 / $350K–$450K; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5%. 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Presales (REDMA):** where a presale is in scope, the buyer must receive the developer's disclosure statement (REDMA s. 18, deposits in trust) and has a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing or acknowledging it (REDMA s. 21). https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Who owns and runs the MLS® system:** at the national level, the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA), a trade association, owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service® ("MLS®") and REALTOR®, and comprises over 100,000 real estate brokers and agents across 100 local real estate boards and associations across Canada. To use the MLS® system, agents must be a member of CREA and agree to comply with CREA's rules; agents and brokers must also become members of a local real estate board, and must adhere to both CREA's rules, such as the REALTOR® Code, and their local board's rules. For this site, the local boards named in the page's own Reciprocity notice are GVR, FVREB and CADREB — the sold feed spans all three board territories. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage and https://jaredgibbons.ca/sold-properties.html
- **The 2010 CREA consent agreement — a milestone, not a BC display rule:** in October 2010 a consent agreement with CREA was reached, registered with the Competition Tribunal to remain in force for a 10-year period; it required CREA not to adopt, maintain or enforce rules denying agents the ability to provide mere postings or discriminating against agents who offer them, and it also encompassed where a seller's information can be displayed. Its 10-year term as described has run; what replaced or followed it is UNVERIFIED. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **The BC regulator's consumer rules around any sale on this page:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure is required at the outset of dealings, and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
