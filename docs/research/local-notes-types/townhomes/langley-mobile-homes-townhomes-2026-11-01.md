## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**The core distinction for this file: there is no strata here. Park rules are NOT strata bylaws, there is no strata corporation, and there is no Form B for a pad-rental purchase.**

- **Form B — strata only, stated so the contrast is explicit.** The Form B Information Certificate (Strata Property Act s. 59, provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢ per page, with rules, budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached) is a strata document. A pad-rental park has no strata corporation to issue one. The park buyer's equivalent diligence is the written Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement, the park rules, and the Manufactured Home Registry record — a different paper trail under a different Act (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate; research file §4).
- **Depreciation reports and CRF (strata only).** Since July 1, 2024, stratas with 5+ lots must obtain a depreciation report every 5 years (a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold); since November 1, 2023, stratas must contribute at least 10% of the operating fund to the CRF. Neither duty applies to a manufactured-home park — there is no CRF behind a park home, which is precisely why the park's own capital and rules documents matter (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements).
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void — strata only.** Rental-restriction bylaws have been void since November 24, 2022 for strata property. A park's "rentals not allowed" rule is not a strata bylaw and is NOT voided by that change; it stands or falls under the MHPTA and the park's rules. Flagging a park's no-rentals line as "void" would be an error (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation).
- **55+ rules — two different regimes.** For strata, SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 allow only a 55-or-older residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and (from May 1, 2023) younger spouses/partners and defined children under SPA Regulation 7.01. A park's "55+ community" label is a park rule taken from listing copy only beyond the program-verified parks in §2.3 — and the SPA exemptions do not automatically apply inside a park (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws; research file §5).
- **Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhome (none on this page, §2.2) carries NO strata, NO Form B, NO strata fees — title, municipal rules and registered covenants/HOA only. A park home is a third structure again: the home is owned, the pad is rented, and neither strata law nor fee-simple title describes the purchase.

**The MHPTA frame — the rules that actually govern this page's stock.**

- **Pad rent increases (MHPTA).** A park landlord may increase rent only with 3 full months' written notice, only once every 12 months, and only up to the yearly limit — **2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027.** The proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park. The landlord may not round up and may not carry forward an unused portion of a prior year's limit (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes).
- **NO pad-rent dollar figure is published in this file.** No official source publishes a Langley pad-rent figure, average or range, and this page itself states no pad-rent range in the copy read. The site-wide defect stands as program context, flagged as UNVERIFIED site copy with amounts never restated as fact: the live Langley pad-fees page carries one unverified range and the cheap-mobile-homes page carries a different unverified range; neither is adopted here, and individual cards' pad-fee lines are individual listings' copy for individual parks, not Langley figures. Budget from the specific park's written agreement, never from a range (research file §5).
- **Assignment on a sale (MHPTA s. 28).** Assignment of the site tenancy permanently transfers the pad tenancy to the buyer; a tenant may assign only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it — s. 28(1)(b) wording is "an order of the director" (NOT arbitrator); the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent, and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf).
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s. 42).** Where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to another use, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date, with defined additional-compensation routes (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes).
- **Registration and certification.** In BC the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered with the Manufactured Home Registry, through a lawyer or notary filing the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership; a home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal (Z-240 for mobile homes, A-277 for modular), and a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry).
- **Taxes on a home in a park.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value will not include the value of the land if the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home).
- **Municipal zoning and plans.** The manufactured-home-park zone name and rules in Township of Langley Zoning Bylaw No. 2500 and City of Langley Zoning Bylaw 2025, No. 3300 are UNVERIFIED in this program — neither bylaw's park-zone section was opened; the City bylaw is cited by number only because its adoption date is conflicted in sources. Township SSMUH is governed by Bylaw 6020 (adopted November 18, 2024), which has NO 6-plex tier, and the Township secondary-suite cap is 120 m² — stated so a park parcel is never described with an entitlement it does not have (research file §4).

## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: Is a Langley mobile home a townhouse?**
A: No. The homes on this page sit in manufactured-home parks: you own the home and rent the pad under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement governed by the MHPTA. There is no strata corporation, no strata fees and no Form B — and pad-rental parks are never townhouse stock in this program (§§2, 3; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes).

**Q: How much is pad rent in Langley?**
A: UNCONFIRMED — no official source publishes a Langley pad-rent figure, average or range, so no figure is stated here. Sibling pages on this site carry two conflicting unverified ranges, neither adopted, and individual cards' pad-fee lines are one unit's copy for one park. The figure that matters is the specific park's current rent in its written site tenancy agreement (§3; research file §7).

**Q: How much can pad rent go up each year?**
A: Up to the Residential Tenancy Branch limit for manufactured home site tenancies: 2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027. The proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of sites in the park. Increases require 3 full months' notice and can occur only once every 12 months (§3; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes).

**Q: Does the park raise my pad rent when I buy the home?**
A: No. On assignment under s. 28 of the MHPTA, the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same and the scheduled date of a subsequent increase does not change; assignment requires the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director) and the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent (§3).

**Q: The cards say 55+ and no rentals — strata rental bans are void, doesn't that apply?**
A: No. The November 24, 2022 voiding covers strata rental-restriction bylaws only. Park rules are park rules under a tenancy regime — most first-page cards describe their parks as "55+ community," an age-restriction claim taken from listing copy only beyond the program-verified parks in §2.3; confirm the actual rule in the park's rules and the tenancy agreement (§3; research file §5).

**Q: One card says "co-op" and another says a lease to 2072 — what do I do with that?**
A: Treat both as listing copy needing verification, not as tenure facts. Pioneer Park's co-op position is program-verified (shares, not pad-rental); the "Lease runs to 2072" card's tenure is UNVERIFIED and needs title/tenancy verification for that unit before any claim is made about it (§2.5; research file §5).

**Q: What documents replace the Form B on a park purchase?**
A: The written Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement (landlords and tenants in a park must have one), the park rules, and the Manufactured Home Registry record — the sale is only effective if registered with the Registry through a lawyer or notary filing the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership (§3; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry).

**Q: What is the CSA label, and why does it matter?**
A: A home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal (Z-240 for mobile homes, A-277 for modular); a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification — label status is a price and timeline fact on every park purchase (§3).

**Q: What happens if the park is converted to another use?**
A: Under s. 42 of the MHPTA, the landlord must first have all necessary permits and approvals, then give 12 months' notice and pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the effective date, with defined additional-compensation routes where the home cannot be moved or no site is available within a reasonable distance (§3; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes).

**Q: Are there market statistics for Langley mobile homes?**
A: No — SnapStats has no manufactured-home category. The only SnapStats Langley figures are all-residential community context (September 2026: detached median $1,345,000 on 449 active / 64 sold; Condos & Townhomes median $611,000 on 699 active / 128 sold), and neither describes this page's manufactured-home shelf (§1; research file §7).
