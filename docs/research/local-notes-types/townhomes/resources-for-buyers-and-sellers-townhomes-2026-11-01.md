## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**Why a resources hub carries townhome rules at all:** the buyers and sellers using this page will meet the strata, tax and rebate frames below in their transactions — and the page's own categories (inspectors, lawyers, insurers, stagers) are the people who work inside those frames. Only what this file's own research documents is carried, plus the standard program blocks.

**The strata block (the frame behind the inspectors, lawyers and insurers a strata townhome buyer will use).**

- **The ownership frame, as the Province states it:** strata owners own their strata lot and together own the common property as a strata corporation, and strata disputes can be resolved using the Civil Resolution Tribunal — so work touching common property is the corporation's, not the individual owner's, to approve, which is where this page's painting, staging and repair categories meet strata bylaws (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing).
- **Form B diligence.** SPA s. 59: Form B within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢ per page — fees, amounts owing, special levies, CRF balance, insurance, rules, budget, Rental Disclosure Statement and depreciation report attached (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate).
- **Depreciation reports.** Stratas with 5+ lots must obtain a depreciation report every 5 years since July 1, 2024; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER the threshold. **CRF minimum:** 10% of the operating budget since November 1, 2023 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing).
- **Rentals.** Rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 — any directory "rentals not allowed" line for a strata townhome is void for long-term rentals and is flagged as void wherever it surfaces in this program; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation).
- **Age.** Only 55+ age bylaws are valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01; spouse/caregiver/legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion; a 45+ bylaw is void and does NOT convert to 55+) (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws).
- **Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhome carries NO strata, NO Form B, NO strata fees — municipal rules plus registered covenants/HOA only; the owner arranges their own trades and insurance, which is the freehold side of this hub's categories (research file §5).
- **City zoning/OCP and SSMUH/suite rules** are municipal and differ across the hub's four places (Surrey, White Rock, Langley City, Township of Langley); they are stated in those cities' area files, and no single city frame is stated for this multi-community hub (research file §§1, 2).

**The regulator and the referral question.**

- **BCFSA:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and BCFSA states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party; consumers can file a complaint with BCFSA (research file §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers).

**Purchase taxes, rebates and financing (the cost items every buyer using this hub is budgeting for).**

- **PTT (general rates, as the research states them):** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — an "Over $3M — 5%" row is flagged as a compression of the 3% + further 2% structure wherever met. The tax is based on fair market value on the day the transfer is registered at the Land Title Office unless an exemption applies, and in most cases transfers are completed by a legal professional — one of this page's own categories (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax). Exemptions: first-time buyers full on first $500,000 at ≤$835,000, phasing to $860,000; newly built full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/understand).
- **FTHB GST rebate (new housing only).** Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122 (March 13 governs; the condos sibling for this slug notes some secondary sources say March 12 — CRA governs, and no sibling here carries March 12 as its own date): 100% of GST to $1,000,000, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1,500,000, agreements March 20, 2025–2031 (CRA: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html). GST applies to new construction, not resales.
- **New Housing Rebate:** 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out (CRA: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html).
