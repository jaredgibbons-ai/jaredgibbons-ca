## 3. Rules that affect townhomes

**Why the strata block does not govern this page — and what governs instead.** The stock on this page is pad-rental, so the diligence is the MHPTA frame below, not a Form B. The strata block is still stated, because a buyer cross-shopping this band against a strata townhouse needs the contrast exact.

- **Form B diligence (strata purchases only).** SPA s. 59: Form B within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢ per page — fees, amounts owing, special levies, CRF balance, insurance, rules, budget, Rental Disclosure Statement and depreciation report attached. **No Form B exists for a pad-rental park purchase** — the equivalent diligence is the written Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement, the park rules, and the Manufactured Home Registry record (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate).
- **Depreciation reports / CRF (strata only).** Stratas with 5+ lots must obtain a depreciation report every 5 years since July 1, 2024 (a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER the threshold); CRF minimum 10% of the operating budget since November 1, 2023. Neither applies to a park — a park's capital planning is the landlord's, which is precisely the risk the pad rent buys (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing).
- **Rentals / age (strata law, for contrast).** Strata rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022, and only 55+ strata age bylaws are valid since November 24, 2022 (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01; spouse/caregiver/legacy exemptions including the May 1, 2023 expansion; a 45+ bylaw is void and does NOT convert to 55+). **A park's "55+" rule is a park rule under tenancy law, not an SPA bylaw** — the SPA voiding/validity frame does not decide it; verify a park's age rule with the park, in writing (§2.4) (BC Government: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation).
- **Fee-simple contrast.** A fee-simple townhome carries NO strata, NO Form B, NO strata fees — title, municipal rules and registered covenants/HOA only. A park home is a third thing: fee-simple ownership of the home itself, on rented land, with pad rent instead of strata fees and tenancy law instead of the SPA.

**The MHPTA frame — the rules that actually govern this page's stock.**

- **Pad-rent increase limits (Province / Residential Tenancy Branch):** a park landlord may increase rent only with 3 full months' written notice, only once every 12 months, and only up to the yearly limit — **2026: 2.3% plus a proportional amount; 2027: 2.2% plus a proportional amount.** The proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park. The landlord may not round up and may not carry forward an unused portion of a prior year's limit (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes).
- **NO pad-rent dollar figure is published in this file.** No official source publishes a Langley pad-rent figure, average or range. The live pages carry conflicting UNVERIFIED site-copy ranges — the Langley pad-fees page states $650–$950, this slug's page states $600–$900, the Abbotsford page states $650–$950 and the Chilliwack page states $550–$950 — **all four are flagged UNVERIFIED site copy and none is restated as fact**; one feed card's unit-specific pad figure is one listing's copy for one park home, not a Langley figure (research file §§1, 5).
- **Assignment on a sale (MHPTA s. 28):** assignment permanently transfers the pad tenancy to the buyer; a tenant may assign only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it — s. 28(1)(b) wording is "an order of the director" (NOT arbitrator). The landlord cannot charge a fee for consent, and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf).
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s. 42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to another use, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date, with defined additional-compensation routes (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes).
- **Registration and certification:** a manufactured home sale/transfer is only effective if registered with the Manufactured Home Registry (lawyer or notary filing the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership); a home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal (Z-240 for mobile homes, A-277 for modular), and a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification (research file §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry).
- **Taxes on a park home:** the owner is assessed and taxed like other residential owners, but the assessed value does not include the land's value where the home is in a park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner (research file §4; https://www2.gov.BC.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home).

## 4. What buyers ask

**Q: Is a mobile home under $500k in Langley a townhouse?**
A: No. The homes on this page sit on rented pads in manufactured-home parks — you own the home and rent the site under the MHPTA. There is no strata corporation, no Form B and no strata fees; the monthly land cost is pad rent. That tenure is why this townhomes file counts 0 complexes (§1, §2).

**Q: How much is pad rent on a Langley mobile home under $500k?**
A: No official source publishes a Langley pad-rent figure, average or range, so no dollar figure is stated in this file. The site itself carries conflicting UNVERIFIED ranges ($600–$900 on this page; $650–$950 on the pad-fees page; $650–$950 Abbotsford; $550–$950 Chilliwack) — all flagged, none adopted. The figure that matters is the specific park's current rent in its written site tenancy agreement (research file §7).

**Q: How much can pad rent go up each year?**
A: Up to the Residential Tenancy Branch limit for manufactured home site tenancies: 2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027. The proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of sites in the park. Increases require 3 full months' notice and can occur only once every 12 months (§3; research file §7).

**Q: Does the park raise my pad rent when I buy the home?**
A: No. On assignment under MHPTA s. 28 the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same and the scheduled date of a subsequent increase does not change; assignment requires the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director) and the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent (§3).

**Q: What happens if the park is converted to another use?**
A: Under MHPTA s. 42 the landlord must first have all necessary permits and approvals, then give 12 months' notice and pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the effective date, with defined additional-compensation routes where the home cannot be moved or no site is available within a reasonable distance (§3).

**Q: A card calls its park a "55+ community" — can I rely on that?**

**Q: What paperwork makes a park-home sale effective?**
A: Registration with the Manufactured Home Registry — a lawyer or notary files the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership — and the home needs its original legible CSA decal (Z-240 / A-277); a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification before the home can be sold (§3).
