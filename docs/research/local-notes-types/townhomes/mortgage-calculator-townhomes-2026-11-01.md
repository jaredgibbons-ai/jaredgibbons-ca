## 2. COMPLEXES

**This topic file carries NO complex roster of its own — verified townhouse complexes counted here: 0 (strata 0, fee-simple 0).** A province-wide calculator does not name complexes for one area; complex-by-complex notes belong in the area townhomes files in this series. What this section documents instead is the calculation itself — the numbers a townhome buyer is solving for, in the order the official rules require them, with the townhome-specific branches stated.

- **1. The branch points any correct townhome calculation has to encode.** At $500,000 the down-payment tier changes; at 20% down the insurance premium disappears and the amortization menu widens; at $1,500,000 insurance disappears entirely and 20% down becomes mandatory. Three hard branch points, all from the CMHC frame in Section 3. For a strata townhome there is a fourth branch a detached calculation does not have: 50% of the strata fee enters the GDS housing-cost stack (Section 3). For a fee-simple townhome there is NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees, so that line is $0 — the same price produces a different GDS position by tenure alone. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **2. Minimum down payment — CMHC's own worked example (inputs stated, as published).** Inputs: home price $750,000. Required minimum: $25,000 (5% of $500,000) plus $25,000 (10% of $250,000) = **$50,000** — not the $37,500 a flat 5% would suggest. A buyer with $60,000 saved (8% down) on that price still needs mortgage loan insurance because the down payment is under 20%, and CMHC's published example prices that file as a $690,000 mortgage ($750,000 − $60,000) at a 4% premium rate carrying a **$27,600** premium — the 4% rate is CMHC's example rate inside its published 0.6%–4.5% range, not a quote for any buyer. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **EXAMPLE 1 — qualifying rate (illustrative arithmetic only; inputs stated, not a market fact).** Inputs: contract rate 4.50% (an illustrative user input — no lender or Bank of Canada rate is researched or stated in this file); uninsured or insured purchase at a federally regulated lender. Qualifying rate = greater of (contract + 2% = 6.50%) or 5.25% = **6.50%**. The lender sizes the approval at the 6.50% payment; the buyer pays at 4.50%. If the input contract rate were 3.00%, the qualifying rate would be the 5.25% floor instead (3.00% + 2% = 5.00% < 5.25%). https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **EXAMPLE 2 — GDS with the townhome line (illustrative arithmetic only; inputs stated).** Inputs: gross household income $120,000/year ($10,000/month); monthly mortgage payment at the qualifying rate $2,400; property taxes $250/month; heating $100/month; strata fee $300/month (strata townhome). Housing costs for GDS = $2,400 + $250 + $100 + (50% × $300 = $150) = $2,900. GDS = $2,900 ÷ $10,000 = **29.0%** — inside the 39% guideline. The same unit bought fee-simple (strata fee $0, all other inputs unchanged) gives housing costs of $2,750 and GDS of **27.5%** — the tenure difference, priced. TDS then adds all other monthly debts against the 44% guideline. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **3. The scenarios this tool prices (not listings).** Insured purchases (under 20% down, price $1.5 million or less, minimum down payment on the 5%/10% tiers, premium of 0.6%–4.5% added to the mortgage or paid up front); uninsured purchases (20% or more down, including all homes over $1.5 million); first-time-buyer and new-build purchases (30-year insured amortization available since December 15, 2024); and renewal/refinance scenarios (stress test re-applied on refinance; not applied on a qualifying uninsured straight switch). A calculator computes payments and guideline ratios; it does not approve a mortgage — OSFI's framework leaves the underwriting decision with the federally regulated lender applying Guideline B-20, and the insurer sets its own eligibility on insured loans. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **4. Closing costs sit outside the mortgage math.** BC property transfer tax alone runs 1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000 and 3% above that on fair market value before any exemption — cash needed at completion on top of the down payment (full frame in Section 3). Legal/notary fees, title insurance, home inspection and property-tax adjustments are real closing-cost categories named on the live page, but the live page's dollar range for legal/notary fees ($1,500–$2,500) is not an official figure and was not verified on any official source: UNVERIFIED as a figure — a calculator should either take these as user inputs or omit amounts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/mortgage-calculator.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

### The verified frame the calculator must encode

- **CMHC flag (top-flag — documented defect on this page):** CMHC publishes premium rates ranging from **0.6% to 4.5%** of the total mortgage amount, based on the size of the down payment and the loan amount — the smaller the down payment, the higher the premium rate — payable in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. Mortgage loan insurance is required when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20%, it protects the lender rather than the buyer, and it allows qualification with as little as 5% down; insurance is needed where the down payment is under 20% AND the home price is $1.5 million or less, and it is not available at all on homes priced over $1.5 million. **The live page states the premium "ranges from 2.80% to 4.00% of the loan amount (plus 0.20% for 30-year insured amortizations)" — that range does not match CMHC's published range re-read for this research, and the 0.20% 30-year surcharge was not confirmed on a CMHC or Finance page in this pass: both are UNVERIFIED here and the page range is WRONG against CMHC (Jerry item, Section 5).** The other two approved insurers (Sagen and Canada Guaranty) are named on the live page; their premium schedules were not read in this pass and no figure from them is stated here. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **How interest is calculated — semi-annual compounding:** under the federal Interest Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. I-15, s. 6, where principal and interest secured by a mortgage on real property are payable on a blended-payment plan, no interest is chargeable unless the mortgage contains a statement showing the principal and the rate of interest chargeable on it, "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" — the statutory basis for the Canadian convention that fixed mortgage rates are stated as nominal rates compounded semi-annually, which is why a Canadian payment calculation converts the nominal rate using semi-annual compounding rather than dividing by 12. The live page states the same convention (fixed-rate compounded semi-annually by law; variable-rate typically compounded monthly) — the fixed-rate half is confirmed by the Interest Act wording; the variable-rate monthly-compounding practice is a lender-practice statement, not a statutory rule, and was not verified on a regulator page in this pass: UNVERIFIED as a rule. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **Minimum down payment:** for homes $500,000 or less, minimum 5%; for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require minimum 20% and mortgage loan insurance is not available. The Department of Finance Canada confirms the same structure as the insured-mortgage rule that took effect with the December 15, 2024 cap increase (5% on the first $500,000; 10% on the remaining amount up to the $1.5 million cap). https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **The stress test — OSFI Guideline B-20 / minimum qualifying rate:** OSFI's minimum qualifying rate (MQR) for uninsured mortgages is currently **the greater of the mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25%** (page date modified 2026-01-29); the buffer is currently 2% and the floor currently 5.25%, both reviewed at least annually. OSFI obliges federally regulated lenders to apply the test, and Guideline B-20 sets the expectation that lenders apply the MQR to most newly underwritten residential mortgagors. Insured mortgages are tested the same way: FCAC states federally regulated entities require borrowers to pass the test at the higher of 5.25% or the contract rate plus 2%, and "that's the case for insured and uninsured mortgages." **The renewal-switch exception:** OSFI does not expect lenders to apply the MQR to an uninsured straight switch at renewal — no increase to the amortization period and no increase to the loan amount — while a refinance or a new HELOC triggers the test again (FCAC). Provincially regulated lenders (e.g., credit unions) are not federally regulated; FCAC notes lenders that aren't federally regulated may also ask a borrower to pass a stress test — whether any given BC credit union applies it is lender-specific and UNVERIFIED here. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Amortization rules:** insured mortgages are those with less than 20% down, available only on homes priced at $1.5 million or less (the insured price cap, raised from $1 million effective December 15, 2024). **30-year insured amortization:** eligibility was extended to **all first-time homebuyers** and to **all buyers of new builds**, effective December 15, 2024, building on the August 1, 2024 measure for first-time buyers purchasing new builds. The exact general insured maximum (25 years) for insured borrowers outside those two groups was NOT stated on a regulator page re-read in this pass and is therefore not asserted as a verified figure in this file: UNVERIFIED. The Finance definition of "first-time homebuyer" for the 30-year measure was likewise not re-read: UNVERIFIED — do not encode a definition from secondary sources. On an uninsured mortgage, 30 years is a lender offering rather than an insured-program rule (the live page's statement to that effect is a lender-practice statement, not a rule re-read here). Effect of the choice (arithmetic, not a rule): a longer amortization lowers the payment at any given rate and raises total interest over the life of the loan. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Affordability ratios — GDS and TDS (FCAC):** Gross Debt Service — total monthly housing costs should not be more than **39%** of gross household income; monthly housing costs are the mortgage payments, property taxes, heating costs and **50% of condo fees (if applicable)** — for a strata townhome, read strata fees in that line (EXAMPLE 2, Section 2). Total Debt Service — total debt load should not be more than **44%** of gross income: total monthly housing costs plus all other debts (credit card balances, car loans, lines of credit, student loans, child or spousal support and any other debts). FCAC frames 39% and 44% as guidelines a borrower should stay within — slightly higher ratios may still qualify but carry more risk — so a calculator should present them as guideline thresholds, not hard approval cut-offs; FCAC's own Mortgage Qualifier Tool uses the same 39%/44%. The live page's GDS/TDS description matches the FCAC composition (principal and interest, property taxes, heating estimate, 50% of strata fees) — consistent with the official source. The ratios are calculated at the qualifying rate, not the contract rate. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html and https://itools-ioutils.fcac-acfc.gc.ca/mq-hq/MQCalc-EAPHCalc-eng.aspx
- **PTT flag (closing-cost cross-reference):** the general tiers are 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000, plus — if a property has residential property worth over $3,000,000 — a further 2% tax on the residential property value greater than $3,000,000 (only the residential portion on a mixed-class property). A single row reading "Over $3M — 5%" compresses the 3% tier and the further 2% into one rate and misstates how each portion is taxed — flag it wherever it appears (not documented on this page in this research). PTT is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office and is not annual property tax. GST on a newly built home: 5% GST applies to new housing in BC; the federal rebate programs that can offset it (FTHB GST rebate — Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122, 100% to $1M phasing to $1.5M, maximum $50,000, agreements March 20, 2025–2031; New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000) are researched in the batch-6 first-time-buyer-calculator file and are not restated in full here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html

### The strata frame behind a townhome purchase

- **Form B (Strata Property Act s. 59):** the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. The monthly fee on that Form B is the figure that feeds the GDS line above — a calculator that asks for "condo fees" is asking a strata townhome buyer for this number. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold. **CRF minimum:** since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. Neither figure changes the payment calculation — both change whether the fee in it is likely to hold. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since November 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1, 123.2), with Regulation 7.01 exemptions expanded May 1, 2023. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Presales (REDMA):** where the townhome priced into the calculator is a presale, the buyer must receive the developer's disclosure statement (REDMA s. 18, deposits in trust) and has a 7-day rescission period from the later of signing or acknowledging it (REDMA s. 21). https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/buying-presale-home-bc-legal-guide-redma/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What rate does the stress test use?** The greater of your mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25% — OSFI's minimum qualifying rate, applied by federally regulated lenders to insured and uninsured mortgages alike; OSFI reviews the 2% buffer and the 5.25% floor at least annually. EXAMPLE 1 in Section 2 works it at a 4.50% input contract rate: qualifying rate 6.50%. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html (Research file Q1)
- **Q: What is the minimum down payment in Canada?** 5% on homes of $500,000 or less; on homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000; at $1,500,000 or more, a minimum of 20% — and mortgage loan insurance is not available at that level. CMHC's own $750,000 example: $50,000 minimum (Section 2). https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained (Research file Q2)
- **Q: When do I need mortgage loan insurance, and what does it cost — is it 2.80%–4.00%?** Whenever your down payment is under 20% on a home priced at $1.5 million or less. And no — that range, stated on the live page, is WRONG against CMHC's published **0.6% to 4.5%** of the total mortgage amount, depending on down payment size and loan amount; the page's additional 0.20% for 30-year insured amortizations was not confirmed on any official page in this research: UNVERIFIED. The premium can be paid up front or added to the mortgage — CMHC's example prices a $690,000 mortgage at a 4% example rate as a $27,600 premium. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained (Research file Q3)
- **Q: Can I get a 30-year amortization on a townhome?** On an insured mortgage (under 20% down), 30-year amortizations have been available to all first-time homebuyers and to all buyers of new builds since December 15, 2024; on an uninsured mortgage (20% or more down) 30 years is a lender offering rather than an insured-program rule, so it is confirmed with the lender, not the calculator. The general insured maximum for borrowers outside those two groups is not asserted here: UNVERIFIED in this research. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html (Research file Q4)
- **Q: How much of my income can go to housing and debt — and where do strata fees land?** FCAC's guidelines: total monthly housing costs (mortgage payments, property taxes, heating, 50% of condo/strata fees) should not exceed 39% of gross household income (GDS), and total debt load including all other debts should not exceed 44% (TDS); slightly higher ratios may still qualify but carry more risk. EXAMPLE 2 in Section 2 works a strata townhome at 29.0% GDS against the same unit fee-simple at 27.5% — the strata fee enters at half its amount, not in full and not at zero. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html (Research file Q5)
- **Q: Why does a Canadian calculator not just divide my rate by 12?** Because fixed mortgage rates are nominal rates compounded semi-annually: the Interest Act (s. 6) requires a blended-payment mortgage to state its rate "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance," so the payment conversion uses semi-annual compounding. The live page's parallel statement that variable-rate mortgages are typically compounded monthly is lender practice, not a statutory rule: UNVERIFIED as a rule in this research. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html
- **Q: I'm renewing — do I face the stress test again?** Not on a qualifying straight switch: OSFI does not expect the MQR to be applied to an uninsured switch to another federally regulated lender with no increase to the loan amount or amortization. But a refinance or a new HELOC triggers the test again — model the two paths separately. Whether a BC credit union applies the test is lender-specific: UNVERIFIED here. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: What does the calculator NOT tell me about a townhome purchase?** The complex, and the closing statement. Strata fees beyond the GDS line, approved levies, CRF health and deductible exposure live in the Form B package, not in any payment calculator; PTT is deferred by the live page to the First-Time Buyer Calculator; legal/notary amounts on the live page ($1,500–$2,500) are UNVERIFIED figures; and the live tool's own fields and formulas are NOT CONFIRMED in this research, so treat its output as an estimate to take to a lender or broker — a calculator does not approve a mortgage. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/mortgage-calculator.md)
- **Q: Does a longer amortization actually help?** Arithmetically, yes on the payment and no on the total: a longer amortization lowers the payment at any given rate and raises total interest over the life of the loan. For a first-time buyer or a new-build buyer, the 30-year insured comparison is the single most useful output this calculator can show (Section 3). (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/mortgage-calculator.md, §6)
- **Q: At what price does the whole calculation change character?** $1,500,000: insurance disappears, 20% down becomes mandatory, and the premium line vanishes from the math — with $500,000 (down-payment tier) and 20% down (premium/amortization) as the two earlier branch points. A townhome priced either side of those lines is a different calculation, not just a bigger one. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **December 15, 2024 is the date inside this tool's two biggest branches.** The insured price cap rose from $1 million to $1.5 million that day, and 30-year insured amortizations were extended to all first-time homebuyers and all buyers of new builds the same day (building on the August 1, 2024 first-time-buyer/new-build measure) — a calculator, blog post or feature sheet using the old $1 million cap or a first-time-only 30-year rule is pre-reform material. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **The stress test has a renewal door most owners never hear about.** A straight uninsured switch at renewal — same amount, same amortization, federally regulated lender to federally regulated lender — is not expected to be stress-tested at all, while the refinance beside it is tested in full. That asymmetry is a concrete reason to model renewal and refinance as separate calculator runs. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **The insurance protects the lender, and the premium follows the loan, not the home.** CMHC is explicit that mortgage loan insurance protects the lender, not the buyer — and because the premium (0.6%–4.5%) is usually added to the mortgage, it raises both the balance and the payment the stress test is applied to. A buyer who budgets the premium as a closing cash item and a buyer who adds it to the loan get different qualifying payments on the same townhome. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
