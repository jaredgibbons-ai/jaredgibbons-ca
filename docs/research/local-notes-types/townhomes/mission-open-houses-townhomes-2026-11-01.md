## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), verbatim:** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, provides that a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. This file's own research **did** open that source (it was not left UNVERIFIED post-429), and the text above is carried in the form the sibling townhomes files that opened the same source record it. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-11/mission-open-houses.md)
- **The regulator:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); BCFSA states a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **How open-house dates and times are set and entered for publication** (the listing brokerage side, via board systems): UNVERIFIED — no BC Gov or BCFSA source states it. Identification or sign-in requirements for open-house visitors are likewise not stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found: UNVERIFIED. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-11/mission-open-houses.md)
- **Form B diligence (any strata townhouse visited at an open house):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states the lot's monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. An open-house visit is not diligence — the Form B package is. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years, not deferrable by vote; a sub-5-lot strata sits UNDER that threshold. **CRF minimum:** since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/strata-depreciation-report-mandates-a-guide-for-real-estate-professionals-599/
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID:** since Nov 24, 2022, no BC strata residential rental-restriction bylaw is valid; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw. Any directory "rentals not allowed" line for a complex visited on this feed (the assembly file flags Cherry Lane's and Luther Place's directory lines) is void for long-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Age bylaws:** only 55+ residency bylaws are lawful (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, exemptions expanded May 1, 2023). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple contrast — stated plainly.** A fee-simple townhouse carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees; municipal rules (City of Mission Zoning Bylaw 5949-2020) plus any registered covenants or homeowners' association govern instead. Most homes on this feed are detached fee-simple product — do not import strata expectations onto them, or detached expectations onto a strata townhouse card.
- **City plan:** Mission's current Official Community Plan is **Bylaw 6405-2026, adopted April 21, 2026**, replacing and repealing OCP Bylaw 6350-2025 with substantively the same plan plus a Regional Context Statement; zoning is Zoning Bylaw 5949-2020. **June 1, 2026 position for THIS page:** this slug's own research file does not document a "June 1, 2026" adoption date on this page, so no Jerry item is asserted from this file on that point. https://www.mission.ca/council-government/news/notice-public-hearing-april-7-2026 (Siblings: ~/workspace/local-notes/condos/mission-open-houses-condos-2026-11-01.md)
- **SSMUH and suites (Mission's own rules):** the City has adopted the provincial small-scale multi-unit housing legislation for zones previously restricted to single-family or duplex dwellings, subject to eligibility and servicing — per-parcel application UNVERIFIED. All secondary suites in Mission must be registered with the City; a suite is a self-contained living space in a single family home forming part of the same real estate entity; **suites are not permitted in townhouses or rowhomes**; a new suite goes through the building permit process, and an unregistered suite can be investigated and may require a decommission permit. One first-page card (33480 Blueberry Drive) describes a suite "registered w. City" in its listing text — listing text only, not independently verified. https://www.mission.ca/planning/small-scale-multi-unit-housing-ssmuh and https://www.mission.ca/business-building/building-development/land-development/secondary-suites-program
- **City property taxes — FLAT 10%:** City of Mission 2026 property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a **10% penalty added to any current balances outstanding on July 3, 2026** (Community Charter ss. 233–234 + Municipal Tax Regulation, B.C. Reg. 426/2003, s. 3; NO s. 235 alternative-scheme bylaw). The rural regime — 5% after July 2 + a further 5% after October 31 under the Taxation (Rural Area) Act — applies in FVRD electoral areas only and is never mixed with the City penalty here. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes
- **Taxes and rebates (worked example, inputs stated):** PTT is 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million; a page row reading "Over $3M — 5%" is a compression of the 3% tier plus that further 2% — the tier math governs. On a $700,000 resale townhouse with no exemption: 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. First-time buyers — exempt on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000; newly built home exemption — full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000. New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350K–$450K; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year; CMHC premiums 0.6%–4.5%. No CMHC page error is asserted for this slug — its own research does not document one. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

- **Q: The townhouse I visited has a suite — is that allowed?** On the City's own rules, no: secondary suites are not permitted in townhouses or rowhomes in Mission, and every suite in the city must be registered. If a townhouse card describes a suite, that is a claim to verify with the City, not a feature to price. https://www.mission.ca/business-building/building-development/land-development/secondary-suites-program
