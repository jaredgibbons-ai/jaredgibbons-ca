## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT TOWNHOMES

### The $1.5 million line is also the federal insured-mortgage line

- CMHC states mortgage loan insurance is required in Canada when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the purchase price; the insurance protects the lender, not the buyer, and lets a buyer qualify with a down payment as low as 5%. CMHC's down-payment rules as published: homes $500,000 or less require a minimum 5% down payment; homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999 require 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require a minimum down payment of 20% and mortgage loan insurance is not available. Every home on this page is, by the page's own price filter, on the insurable side of the line. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **CMHC premiums: 0.6% to 4.5%** of the total mortgage amount, payable in full at purchase or added to the mortgage (CMHC, as cited in the research file). A page stating premiums of 2.80%–4.00% would be WRONG — **no such statement is documented in this file's own research file**, and none is asserted here; the known 2.80%–4.00% error belongs to a different page's research and is not imported into this slug. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Worked example 1 — minimum down payment, inputs stated:** purchase price $1,400,000, insured purchase, no other adjustments. Minimum down payment = 5% of the first $500,000 ($25,000) + 10% of the remaining $900,000 ($90,000) = $115,000. The same home priced at $1,500,000 would require 20% ($300,000) and could not be insured at all. Arithmetic on CMHC's published rules only. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Worked example 2 — PTT, inputs stated:** purchase price $1,400,000, purely residential, no exemption. PTT = 1% of $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% of $1,200,000 ($24,000) = $26,000. Nothing in this band reaches the 3% tier (over $2,000,000) or the further 2% (residential value over $3,000,000); a page's "Over $3M — 5%" row is in any event a compression of 3% + 2% on the residential portion and is flagged wherever it appears — no such row is documented in this file's research. A specific purchase's tax is confirmed by the buyer's lawyer/notary. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

### Strata and municipal rules for a townhouse bought through this band

- **Form B diligence (strata townhouses):** Strata Property Act s. 59 — the Form B states monthly strata fees, amounts owing, approved special levies still payable, the CRF balance and an insurance summary, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; provided within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Depreciation reports / CRF:** since July 1, 2024, strata corporations with five or more lots must obtain a depreciation report every five years; a strata with fewer than five lots sits UNDER that threshold. Since November 1, 2023, at least 10% of the annual operating budget must go to the contingency reserve fund. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Rental and age bylaws:** rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022 — any directory "rentals not allowed" line for a complex found through this band is flagged void in that complex's home file. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid since November 24, 2022 (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, exemptions expanded May 1, 2023). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Fee-simple contrast (stated plainly):** a fee-simple townhouse or row home has NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees — diligence is title, municipal rules and registered covenants/HOA documents only. Basis: the tenure frame in Section 2 above.
- **Surrey frame:** Surrey's current Official Community Plan is the Surrey 2050 OCP, Bylaw No. 21900, adopted June 15, 2026 — never cite Bylaw No. 18020 as current. Surrey Zoning Bylaw, 1993, No. 12000 governs zoning; Surrey's SSMUH framework took effect July 8, 2024; the secondary-suite cap is 40% of the principal dwelling's habitable area — directly relevant to the suite-heavy detached stock on this page's first page. Surrey short-term rentals are permitted for owners only — licence figure CONFLICT: the program figure is $358/yr while the City's 2024 corporate report proposed $350/yr plus fees; carry $358 with the $350 figure disclosed alongside and confirm on the City's licence page before quoting either. Serpentine note: Serpentine IS a board/Paragon sub-area on Jared's ruling, with dual filing (live feed: South Surrey; Paragon: Cloverdale) — both stated, no market claim built on either. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/r133-plansurrey-2013-official-community-plan-surrey-2050-official-community-plan-alignment-related-amendments
- **Langley frame:** Township of Langley Zoning Bylaw No. 2500 governs in the Township; its SSMUH Bylaw 6020 (adopted November 18, 2024) allows 3 units on lots up to 280 m² and 4 units on lots under 4,050 m², lots over 4,050 m² are ineligible, and NO 6-plex tier exists in Bylaw 6020; secondary suite cap 120 m². Langley City is a separate municipality with its own bylaws — a Langley City card in the feed is not under Township rules. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/small-scale-multi-unit-housing.aspx
- **PTT exemptions inside this band:** the first-time home buyers' program exempts a qualifying purchaser from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price; a full exemption requires a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), a property of 0.5 hectares or smaller containing only residential improvements and used only as the buyer's principal residence, and a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000. The newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000, phasing out over the $50,000 above it with complete elimination at $1,150,000. The lower part of this page's range is exactly where both exemptions live; a buyer above $1,150,000 gets neither. The additional PTT rate for foreign nationals, foreign corporations and taxable trustees is published on a separate provincial page that was not re-read for the research file: UNVERIFIED here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **GST and rebates:** 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales. The FTHB GST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122 — 100% to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, maximum $50,000, agreements March 20, 2025–2031); the New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000); HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Presales (REDMA):** where a developer markets a new home in this band, REDMA s. 21 gives a 7-day rescission period and s. 18 requires deposits to be held in trust. https://northam-law.com/pre-sale-condo-purchases-in-british-columbia-key-legal-protections-for-buyers/

- **Q: Why does this page stop at $1.5 million — is that just an asking-price filter?** It is a price filter, but the line also matches a federal financing rule: CMHC states homes $1,500,000 or more require a minimum 20% down payment and mortgage loan insurance is not available, while homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999 need only 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the rest. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained

Whether the full feed reaches further is UNVERIFIED, because only the first page was read. https://jaredgibbons.ca/homes-under-1500000.html

- **Q: Are these Jared Gibbons' own listings?** No. The cards are MLS® Reciprocity listings from many brokerages; the page footer states the data comes in part from the Reciprocity program of GVR, FVREB or CADREB, and the boards assume no responsibility for its accuracy. https://jaredgibbons.ca/homes-under-1500000.html
- **Q: How much down payment do I need on a $1.4 million townhouse in this band?** On CMHC's published minimums, $115,000 — 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the remaining $900,000 (worked example, inputs stated in Section 3) — plus the CMHC premium of 0.6%–4.5% of the mortgage amount if you buy with less than 20% down. A lender confirms the specific file. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: How much property transfer tax would I pay on a $1.4 million home in this band?** On the general tiers alone, $26,000 — 1% on the first $200,000 plus 2% on the portion from $200,000 to $1,400,000 (worked example, inputs stated in Section 3). Nothing in this band reaches the 3% tier or the further 2%, and a BC exemption could reduce the bill further if you qualify. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Can a first-time buyer really pay no PTT on part of a purchase here?** Yes, at the band's lower end: a qualifying first-time buyer at $835,000 or less is exempt on the first $500,000, and a qualifying newly built purchase gets a full exemption to $1,100,000. Above $1,150,000 neither exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Are the suites in these listings legal rental suites?** A listing describing a basement suite or mortgage helper is listing text, not a legality finding. In Surrey the secondary-suite cap is 40% of the principal dwelling's habitable area; in the Township of Langley the suite cap is 120 m² — and suite legality, permits and (for a strata townhouse) the registered bylaws are confirmed per property, not from the card. Sources: Section 3 above.
- **Q: Can I rent out a townhouse I buy through this page?** Long-term, yes under provincial law for a strata unit — rental-restriction bylaws have been void since November 24, 2022. Short-term rentals are a separate question: a strata can restrict them by bylaw, and in Surrey short-term rentals are permitted for owners only (licence figure conflict $358/yr vs $350/yr plus fees — confirm on the City's licence page). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
