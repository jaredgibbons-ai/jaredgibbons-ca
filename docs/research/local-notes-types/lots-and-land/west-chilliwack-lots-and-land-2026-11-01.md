## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Plan and zoning:** Chilliwack 2050 OCP, Bylaw No. 5500, Agricultural Area Plan as Schedule B1; zoning under Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000 (AL zone minimums in §2; Zoning Bylaw 5000 §6 farm-zone framework as the research states it). Industrial zone standards (M2/M3 and related zones) were **not** confirmed from the bylaw text: UNVERIFIED — an industrial land buyer must pull the parcel's zone section from the bylaw itself before pricing any use. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- **SSMUH:** Chilliwack's small-scale multi-unit housing bylaw is **Bylaw 5395, adopted June 18, 2024**. On ALR farm parcels the practical added-unit rules are the ALR rules below. (Sibling files for this slug: ~/workspace/local-notes/condos/west-chilliwack-condos-2026-11-01.md)
- **ALR block — full form:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval, and ALR residences cannot be stratified — depositing a strata plan causes a subdivision (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s. 6; Strata Property Act, s. 239; Agricultural Land Commission Act, s. 21(1)). Residence caps: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m²; a secondary suite is permitted **only inside** the principal residence; one additional residence for farm use or another permitted use, capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form:** $2,500 of gross farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha — with applications by October 31 for the next taxation year. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR; this slug's research does not document such a flat line on this slug's live page, so no page defect is asserted on that point (see §5). https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf
- **Strata block — applies to corridor strata product (e.g., industrial strata bays, Creekside Estates), not to bare land here:** Form B (SPA s. 59) states the lot's fees, amounts owing, approved special levies, CRF balance and insurance summary; depreciation reports every five years for stratas with five or more lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01, May 1, 2023 exemptions); a "45+" listing line is NOT a valid age bylaw. Cottonwood Village's 55+ rule is NOT a strata bylaw — it is a manufactured-home park rule — one more reason the park is not strata or lot stock. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Property transfer tax on land:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked anchors: $700,000 → $12,000; $1,000,000 → $18,000; $1,500,000 → $28,000; $2,000,000 → $38,000; $3,500,000 → $93,000 including the further 2% tier. The tax's application to a purely commercial/industrial parcel is facts-specific (the further 2% tier applies to the residential portion) — confirm the parcel's classification before quoting a figure: UNCONFIRMED for corridor industrial land in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level only:** most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (land kept for personal use is the CRA's example), while a sale can be taxable where the land is used in a business, sold in the course of a business, or is a parcel created by subdividing another parcel into more than two parts; sales of newly subdivided lots by a developer/business are generally taxable. Industrial land sold in the course of a business sits on the taxable side of that line in the general case, but the position for any specific corridor parcel is UNCONFIRMED until vendor status and parcel history are established. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **First-home programs — the land boundary:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026) rebates 100% of the GST on new HOUSING up to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements March 20, 2025–2031 — new housing, NOT bare land. The GST New Housing Rebate is 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $8,000 per year to $40,000 lifetime. CMHC premiums run 0.6%–4.5%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can I buy a bare residential building lot in West Chilliwack?** There is no residential lot product to buy — no named bare-land subdivision, bare-land strata or presale lot offering exists here. The land that trades is whole fee-simple farm parcels on the west side and industrial/service-commercial land on the Lickman–Evans corridor. (§2 above)
- **Q: A listing calls a Cottonwood Village home a townhouse on a lot. Is it land I would own?** No. Cottonwood Village (7610 Evans Road) is a manufactured-home park: you own the home and rent the pad on leased land — no strata, no Form B, no land title. Its placement (West Chilliwack vs Sardis West Vedder) and its lease terms (2076 vs November 30, 2039 vs ~16 years remaining) are both UNRESOLVED conflicts in the sources; decide on the address, the head lease and the pad tenancy agreement, not the listing label. (§2 above; ~/workspace/local-notes/townhomes/west-chilliwack-townhomes-2026-11-01.md)
- **Q: Can I build a second house on a farm parcel here?** Only within the ALC limits: one principal residence per parcel (maximum 500 m²), a secondary suite only inside that residence, and an additional small residence for farm use or another permitted use — maximum 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less, 186 m² only on parcels over 40 ha. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Can I subdivide or stratify a west-side parcel later?** Subdivision in the ALR requires ALC approval — the AL zone's minimums (7.5 ha, or 0.2 ha for an approved homesite severance, 1 ha for a boundary adjustment, 25 m minimum width) decide what is even possible — and a strata plan cannot be deposited without the ALC's permission (ALCA s. 21(1); SPA s. 239; ALC Bulletin 09, s. 6). https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- **Q: What can I actually do with an industrial lot on Progress Way or Yale Road West?** That turns on the parcel's industrial zone section — the research did not confirm the M2/M3 standards from the bylaw text, so the zone section must be pulled from Zoning Bylaw 5000 for the specific parcel before any use is priced: UNCONFIRMED at the general level in this file. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- **Q: Is this parcel in the ALR?** Status is per address and no parcel was confirmed in this research — check the ALC map and the title before pricing subdivision, a second dwelling or farm value into the parcel. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Q: What does farm class require, exactly?** Three tiers, never a flat figure: $2,500 of gross farm income at 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha above that; $10,000 under 0.8 ha — application due by October 31 for the next taxation year. https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf
- **Q: Does GST apply when I buy land here?** Per CRA GI-003 it turns on the seller and the parcel's history: an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt, while a sale in the course of a business or of a parcel subdivided into more than two parts can be taxable — the corridor's business land sits closer to the taxable side of that line than a hobby-farm parcel does, but the position for a specific parcel is UNCONFIRMED until vendor status and history are established; have the contract state it. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: Which schools serve a home on Evans Road?** Only one address was run: for 7600 Evans Road the SD33 locator returns Evans Elementary → Vedder Middle → Sardis Secondary (single-address result, non-transferable). Every other address needs its own locator run; all other catchments here are UNVERIFIED. https://sd33.bc.ca/index.php/find-your-school
- **Q: Is the corridor transit-served for workers?** Route 52 Evans runs Cottonwood Mall–Downtown via Evans Road, but route 59 Industrial is peak-only (a handful of weekday trips via the Lickman Park & Ride) — do not price corridor land as transit-served for shift workers without checking the actual timetable. https://www.bctransit.com/chilliwack/schedules-and-maps/route-overview/?route=59
