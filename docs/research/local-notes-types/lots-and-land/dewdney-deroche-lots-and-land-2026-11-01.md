- **PTT on land:** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. First-time buyers: exempt on the first $500,000 at $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 — a new HOME, not bare land. **Worked example, inputs + tier math stated:** a $500,000 fully residential acreage parcel, no exemption = $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

- **Rural property taxes — the jurisdiction rule in full:** a property not located in a city, town, district or village is in a rural area; its owner receives the property tax notice from the Province's Surveyor of Taxes Office and pays property taxes to the Province, not to the FVRD or the City of Mission. - **Q: Is a manufactured home in Oasis Country Estates or Nicomen Village a land purchase?** No. On the sources checked, those parks appear in listing text only, with pad rents stated at $440–$497/month — you would own the home and rent the pad, under park rules and park approval on resale/assignment. - **Q: Who do I pay property tax to, and when is it due?** The Province: rural-area properties receive their tax notice from the Surveyor of Taxes Office. A specific property's flood status, dyke protection and flood-construction requirements are UNVERIFIED at parcel level — run the check by PID before valuing building potential. (§§1, 3 above)

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The mailing address is the trap.** Every listing here carries a Mission address, the page title says "Mission BC," and the editorial heading calls this "Mission's Rural Frontier" — and none of it governs anything. The FVRD is the local government, the Province collects the tax, and Mission's bylaws, OCP and flat 10% tax penalty stop at the city boundary. The governance call in §1 is the first diligence item on every parcel. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Document~Library/EA%20Housing%20Needs%20Report.pdf
- **The tax penalty calendar is split, not flat.** A buyer who imports Mission's "10% after July 2" rule into an Area G closing misprices the risk in both directions: the rural regime is 5% on amounts unpaid on July 2 and a further 5% on amounts unpaid on October 31 — the second penalty date is three months later than any municipal date in the neighbouring files. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01
- **The Deroche Bench crosses an electoral-area line.** The eastern portions of the Deroche Bench sit in Electoral Area C, not Area G — two otherwise similar bench parcels can answer to different FVRD plan areas, and which side of the G/C line a specific parcel sits on is UNVERIFIED at parcel level. Check the electoral area for the PID, not the community name. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/areacocp?sort_by=recent&tool=forum_topic
- **"Area G" is not a unique bylaw address.** At least two other BC regional districts (Nanaimo; Okanagan-Similkameen) also have an Electoral Area G with its own OCP bylaws — a web search for "Area G OCP bylaw" returns those districts' numbers, which have nothing to do with Dewdney or Deroche.
