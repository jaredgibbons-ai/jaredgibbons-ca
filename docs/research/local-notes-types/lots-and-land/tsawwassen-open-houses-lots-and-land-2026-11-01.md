## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), carried verbatim as the sibling open-houses files sourced it:** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, states that a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Representation at an open house:** a licensed real estate professional in BC must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party to the transaction. The regulator is the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and the Province's Buying and selling a home page directs prospective purchasers to BCFSA for information on purchasing property in BC. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Do I need my own agent to attend a Tsawwassen open house?** No official source requires it. BCFSA's Real Estate Rules excuse disclosure of representation where a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or answering general factual questions — but the moment you share your motivation, financial qualifications or needs, that changes, and BCFSA requires unrepresented consumers to be told the risks of dealing with a licensee who represents the other party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Q: How do I know the open house time is current?** Carefully, on this feed: each listing carries an OPEN HOUSE label with a date and start and end times, but on the cards read for this slug's research the card time block (e.g. 12:00–2:00 PM) and the time inside the listing's own text (e.g. 11:00 AM–1:00 PM) differ by one hour, the page states no feed update interval, and which time is authoritative is UNVERIFIED — confirm the actual time with the listing brokerage before you go. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-8/tsawwassen-open-houses.md)
- **Q: Is the host at an open house the seller's agent?** The host is a licensee connected to the listing side of the transaction; BCFSA requires a licensee to work for either the buyer or the seller and to inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients — assume anything non-factual you tell the host can reach the seller. Use the open house for facts; save your story for your own agent. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Do I have to sign in or show ID at an open house?** No BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source states an identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors — UNVERIFIED as a rule; an individual host may still ask, on the seller's instructions.
