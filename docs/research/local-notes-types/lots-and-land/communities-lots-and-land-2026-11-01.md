- **No single jurisdiction governs this hub — stated plainly:** the seven blocks span the Township of Langley and Langley City, Surrey, White Rock, Abbotsford, Chilliwack (CADREB ground), Maple Ridge, Pitt Meadows and Delta, each with its own OCP, zoning bylaw, SSMUH amendment and subdivision/servicing bylaw. **No bylaw number for any one city is asserted in this hub file** — importing one city's bylaw into a multi-city topic file would misstate the others. Each community's own lots-and-land files carry its verified ledger position (for example: Township of Langley Subdivision and Development Servicing Bylaw 2019 No. 5382 CONFIRMED; Surrey servicing Bylaw 8830 (1986) verified on City pages; Delta's DSSS 8288, 2024 adoption HELD UNCONFIRMED; Abbotsford's development bylaw number HELD UNCONFIRMED; Chilliwack's servicing number HELD UNCONFIRMED) — read the specific community's file for the specific ground. (Research file above, §§1–2.)
- **SSMUH / subdivision potential, general frame only:** every hub municipality adopted provincially required small-scale multi-unit housing changes, and added SSMUH units are not subdivision and do not create new lots in any of them — the unit counts, lot-size thresholds and servicing conditions differ by city and are stated only in each community's own files: UNCONFIRMED at hub level.
- **ALR block (ALR ground exists in several hub communities; parcel status UNCONFIRMED per parcel):** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)). Residence caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR wherever met; no such line is documented for this slug. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Strata block (bare-land strata parcels, wherever a community's own files document them):** Form B within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + May 1, 2023 exemptions). A "45+" listing line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw. A fee-simple lot carries none of this. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **PTT — worked example (inputs + tier math; provincial, applies in every hub community):** on a $500,000 lot purchase — 1% on the first $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% on $200,000–$500,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**. Tiers: 1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above, plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000. At $3,500,000 the full tier math including the further 2% is $93,000; a townhomes sibling in this program carries $78,000 for $3,500,000 — that figure is an OPEN reconciliation item, flagged here and never silently carried. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land generally exempt. Finer points UNCONFIRMED. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST rebate:** new HOUSING, not bare land (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026, CRA governs; 100% to $1M phasing to $1.5M, max $50,000, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031). New Housing Rebate 36% / max $6,300 / $350K–$450K; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Purchase prohibition:** stated ONLY as this file's own sources support it — the hub research cites the BCFSA consumer-protection frame (a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and the risks for unrepresented consumers, and may work for only the buyer or the seller in a transaction) and encodes no purchase-prohibition position, so none is asserted: UNCONFIRMED here. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
