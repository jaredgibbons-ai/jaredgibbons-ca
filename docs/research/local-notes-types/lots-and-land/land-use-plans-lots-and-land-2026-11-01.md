- **What the plans govern, in the one fully verified case (Delta):** housing location and density are set through the OCP's land use designations (s. 1.5.1, applied on Map 2, including Agriculture (A) for farm land) and then parcel by parcel through the zoning bylaw — zoning is assigned parcel by parcel on the zoning maps. A land buyer reads a parcel in that order: designation, then zone, then any development permit area. https://hdp-ca-prod-app-delta-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/8917/7677/0068/OCP_2024_web.pdf and https://maps.delta.ca/Utilities/ZoningPDF/RS1.pdf
- **What the plans mean for land, in Delta:** the 2024 OCP simplified land use descriptions, targeted strategic growth to urban centres and corridors, and enabled SSMUH (up to four units on single-detached and duplex lots) — added-unit permission under a plan is not subdivision permission and creates no new lots. https://www.delta.ca/building-development/housing-our-future

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Delta — the verified model:** Delta Council adopted a new Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 8400 (2024), on July 8, 2024; the City describes an OCP as the document that describes where and what type of housing can be added across the city and contains long-term objectives and policies guiding land use, development, services, amenities and infrastructure decisions. The current OCP is a single consolidated plan — Land Use Plan at s. 1.5, designations at s. 1.5.1 on Map 2 — with **NO lettered schedules: there is no Schedule D.1 in Bylaw 8400**; the Tsawwassen Area Plan was Schedule D.1 under the superseded OCP, Bylaw No. 3950, and farm land under the current OCP is designated "Agriculture (A)". Any source attributing "Schedule D.1 of Bylaw 8400" is in error. https://www.delta.ca/building-development/housing-our-future and https://hdp-ca-prod-app-delta-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/8917/7677/0068/OCP_2024_web.pdf
- **Delta zoning number: CONTESTED — flagged, never asserted.** City pages cite Zoning Bylaw No. 7600, 2017, with zoning assigned parcel by parcel on the zoning maps that form part of that bylaw, while an internal consolidated title in this program reads "8400, 2025." Neither is asserted as settled here. https://maps.delta.ca/Utilities/ZoningPDF/RS1.pdf
- **Delta SSMUH: Bylaws 8394 and 8395, adopted June 24, 2024** — Delta's provincially required zoning updates allow up to four units on all single-detached and duplex lots, under the framework the 2024 OCP provides for Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing. https://www.delta.ca/building-development/housing-our-future

- **ALR block — full form (applies in all four municipalities where ALR ground exists):** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval, and ALR residences cannot be stratified — depositing a strata plan causes a subdivision (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s. 6; Strata Property Act, s. 239; Agricultural Land Commission Act, s. 21(1)). Residence caps: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m²; a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence; one additional residence for farm use or another permitted use, capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, wherever farm land is priced in any of the four municipalities:** $2,500 of gross farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha — applications by October 31 for the next taxation year. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR. https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf
- **Strata block — stated for completeness, governing no stock in this topic file:** if a parcel in any of the four municipalities is a bare-land strata lot, the BC block applies to the LOT — Form B (Strata Property Act s. 59) within 7 days, maximum $35 plus up to 25¢/page; depreciation reports every five years for stratas with five or more lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2, May 1, 2023 exemptions); a "45+" listing line is NOT a valid age bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Property transfer tax on land:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked anchors: $700,000 → $12,000; $1,000,000 → $18,000; $1,500,000 → $28,000; $2,000,000 → $38,000; $3,500,000 → $93,000 including the further 2% tier. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level only:** most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (land kept for personal use is the CRA's example), while a sale can be taxable where the land is used in a business, sold in the course of a business, or is a parcel created by subdividing another parcel into more than two parts; anything finer for a specific parcel in any of the four municipalities: UNCONFIRMED. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **First-home programs — the land boundary:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026) rebates 100% of the GST on new HOUSING up to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements March 20, 2025–2031 — new housing, NOT bare land. The GST New Housing Rebate is 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $8,000 per year to $40,000 lifetime. CMHC premiums run 0.6%–4.5%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Which municipalities does this topic cover?** Four, per the live page's title: Surrey, Langley, Abbotsford and Delta — each planning through its own OCP and zoning bylaw; there is no single shared land use plan document for the four. https://jaredgibbons.ca/land-use-plans.html
- **Q: What is an Official Community Plan?** In Delta's own description — the verified example here — an OCP describes where and what type of housing can be added across the city and contains long-term objectives and policies that guide decisions about land use, development, services, amenities and infrastructure. https://www.delta.ca/building-development/housing-our-future
- **Q: What is Delta's current land use plan?** The Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 8400 (2024), adopted July 8, 2024: a single consolidated plan with the Land Use Plan at s. 1.5, designations at s. 1.5.1 on Map 2, and no Schedule D.1 or community area-plan schedules (those belonged to the superseded Bylaw No. 3950). https://hdp-ca-prod-app-delta-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/8917/7677/0068/OCP_2024_web.pdf
- **Q: What is Delta's zoning bylaw number?** CONTESTED — City pages cite No. 7600, 2017; an internal title in this program says "8400, 2025." Never assert one as settled; what governs is the parcel's own zone on the City zoning maps. https://maps.delta.ca/Utilities/ZoningPDF/RS1.pdf
- **Q: How do I check zoning and future land use for an Abbotsford property?** The City of Abbotsford's Development Planning pages direct property checks to the City's property information search or a property search in WebMap for current zoning, future land uses and development permit areas. The Abbotsford OCP bylaw number itself: UNVERIFIED in this pass. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/development-planning
- **Q: What are Surrey's and Langley's current plan bylaws?** For Surrey, the standing correction governs: the current OCP is Bylaw 21900, and Bylaw 18020 is never current. Beyond that, and for both Langley municipalities, the bylaw numbers and plan structures are UNVERIFIED in this pass — no source from those municipalities was opened for the research file, and this file states nothing about them from general knowledge. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-8/land-use-plans.md)
- **Q: Does an OCP designation settle what I can build on a lot?** No. In the verified Delta model the designation is the first of three layers — designation (s. 1.5.1 / Map 2), then the parcel's zone, then any Development Permit Area — and on ALR land the Agricultural Land Commission's rules apply on top of all three. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Can farmland in any of these cities be turned into strata lots by stratifying the houses?** No — ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6 treats a strata plan as a subdivision in the ALR, ALR residences cannot be stratified, and ALR subdivision needs ALC approval; the ALC layer sits on top of the municipal designation in all four municipalities. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Q: A listing cites an area plan — how do I know it is current?** Date it. Delta's governing OCP is Bylaw No. 8400 (2024); any Tsawwassen Area Plan / Schedule D.1 reference is the superseded Bylaw No. 3950, and a "Schedule D.1 of Bylaw 8400" citation is a conflation error wherever it is met. Run the same dating check in the other three municipalities before a plan claim goes into an offer. https://www.delta.ca/building-development/housing-our-future
- **Q: Does a favourable plan designation change the tax on buying the land?** No — the plan governs use, not transaction tax: PTT runs on the standard tiers in §3, GST on vacant land turns on the seller and the parcel's history at the CRA GI-003 level, and the first-home GST rebate is for new housing, not bare land. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
