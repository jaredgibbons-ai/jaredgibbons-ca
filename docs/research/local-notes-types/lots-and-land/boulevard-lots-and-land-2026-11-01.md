## 2. THE LAND ITSELF

**There is no meaningful bare-land market on the Boulevard corridor — stated plainly at the top. No named fee-simple bare-land subdivision, no bare-land strata community, no assembly or development site, and no presale lot offering was verified on any City, District or developer source. Land trades as built fee-simple detached houses facing Grand Boulevard and on the side streets. The corridor's real land facts are three: (1) which municipality the lot sits in decides its minimums, density and taxes entirely; (2) the City-side heritage character / Green Necklace frontage shapes what can be built facing the boulevard; and (3) one single-lot strata record (below) is documented as its own form — it is not a subdivision and not inventory beyond that one house lot.**

### Fee-simple lots — the default and only verified form

- **Established boulevard and side-street lots:** detached houses facing Grand Boulevard and on the side streets — the corridor's identity is its wide, park-like boulevard frontage — predominantly freehold detached; strata product on the corridor is UNVERIFIED (none confirmed on an official source) in the research, and both siblings record zero verified condo and townhouse complexes. - **Lot character — City side, as the City states it:** Ground Oriented (GO) Zone subdivision minimum lot width is 7.5 m in the Low Rise Neighbourhood 1 designation (10.0 m corner) and 15.0 m in other designations. https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/Housing/Zoning-Bylaw-1995-No-6700-Amendment-Bylaw-2025-No-9137-City-Initiated-Amendment-Related-to-Provincia.pdf

Which side of Highway 1 a lot sits on changes its minimums entirely. Zone minimums only — not a claim about any existing lot's size. https://docs.dnv.org/documents/zoning-guide-section-525.pdf
- **Heritage character — a land constraint, not just a label:** Grand Boulevard is a City heritage character area and the park median is part of the Green Necklace — frontage and character rules, not just zoning, shape what can be built facing the boulevard on the City side. http://www.cnv.org/~/media/7BBC365465654E02B81F82788C2E01DC.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Two rulebooks, one corridor — read the side that matches the title:** City side — City of North Vancouver Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 8400 (2014, amended December 8, 2025) and Zoning Bylaw No. 6700. District side — District of North Vancouver Official Community Plan, Bylaw 7900, and Zoning Bylaw 3210. Neither municipality's bylaws apply on the other side of Highway 1. https://docs.dnv.org/documents/official-community-plan.pdf
- **City side — GO Zone density (adopted December 8, 2025):** a lot of 280 m² or less allows up to 3 units; a lot over 280 m² allows up to 4 units in the Low Rise Neighbourhood 1 designation and up to 6 units in the Low Rise Neighbourhood 2 or Residential Level 4 designations; the GO Zone adoption raised permitted units from 3 to 4 on nearly 4,300 properties. Subdivision minimum widths are in Section 2. https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/Housing/Zoning-Bylaw-1995-No-6700-Amendment-Bylaw-2025-No-9137-City-Initiated-Amendment-Related-to-Provincia.pdf
- **District side — added units:** an R2 lot may hold a principal dwelling plus a secondary suite and a coach house, and since June 2024 three units total are permitted on single-family lots within the Urban Containment Boundary; the District GeoWeb SSMUH classification (potentially up to 6 units above 280 m² within 400 m of a frequent-transit stop) is parcel-specific. Never quote one side's density numbers for the other. https://docs.dnv.org/documents/zoning-guide-section-525.pdf
- **Subdivision + servicing — servicing-bylaw numbers held UNCONFIRMED on both sides:** no City or District servicing-bylaw number for this corridor is confirmed on a primary source in this file's research, so none is quoted for either side. (The City's Subdivision Bylaw 8014 is a City instrument — it is not asserted for any District-side lot, and its parcel-level application even on the City side is UNCONFIRMED in this file's research.) The process is documented instead: subdivision runs off the side-specific minimums in Section 2, and hazard permit areas (below) and, on the City side, heritage character expectations gate boulevard-facing work. Confirm requirements with the lot's own municipality.
- **Hazards — City side:** the City's Streamside Protection Development Permit Area covers land within 15 metres of the top of bank of a watercourse or within 10 metres of the top of a ravine bank, and a development permit is required before new development there. https://www.cnv.org/Business-Development/Building/Land-Use-Approvals/Development-Permits/Streamside-Development-Permit-Areas
- **Hazards — District side:** District OCP Bylaw 7900, Schedule B sets Development Permit Areas for Streamside Protection, Creek Hazard, Slope Hazard and Wildfire Hazard, any of which can apply on the District portion toward Lynn Valley. https://dnv-docs.simplicitycms.ca/documents/ocp-schedule-b-dpa.pdf
- **Heritage / covenant diligence (City side):** Grand Boulevard is a City heritage character area in the Green Necklace system — a boulevard-facing rebuild carries character expectations a side-street lot does not; check the title and the City's heritage record for the specific lot before pricing a teardown. http://www.cnv.org/~/media/7BBC365465654E02B81F82788C2E01DC.pdf
- **Bare-land strata rules (the 1555 Grand Boulevard form — the BC strata block applies in full IF title confirms strata tenure):** Form B diligence (fees, amounts owed, levies, CRF balance, insurance summary; provided within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 (a single-lot form sits outside that 5+ threshold on its face — confirm the plan's lot count on title); minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 (short-term restrictions may still apply by bylaw); only 55+ age bylaws valid under Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2, with May 1, 2023 exemptions (younger spouse/partner, caregivers, legacy residents, defined children); a listing's "45+" line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **ALR rules (for completeness — no corridor ALR parcel is confirmed here):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6; Strata Property Act s. 239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s. 21(1)); ALC residence caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within the principal residence only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of the land's value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by October 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Property transfer tax:** general 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million; First-Time Home Buyers' Program (first $500,000 exempt at values of $835,000 or less, maximum saving $8,000, partial $835,000–$860,000); Newly Built Home Exemption (full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000 — a new-HOME exemption; bare-land eligibility UNCONFIRMED). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **GST on vacant land — CRA level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt (CRA GI-003). Finer points UNCONFIRMED. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST rebate / programs — land angle:** the FTHB rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122; 100% to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M; agreements March 20, 2025–2031) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself — a corridor lot purchase alone earns no rebate. New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Speculation/vacancy + purchase prohibition:** no corridor-specific position is documented in the research — none imported for either municipality. Vancouver Empty Homes Tax (3%) is Vancouver ONLY, not applicable here. The federal non-Canadian purchase prohibition position for this corridor's land is UNCONFIRMED in this file — the research cites no prohibition source for Boulevard, and the position is not conformed from other files.
- **Property taxes — stated per side:** City side — 2026 taxes due July 2, 2026; the 2026 residential municipal-general rate is 1.89202 and the total residential rate is 3.34028 per $1,000 of assessed value, plus a separate residential storm drainage rate of 0.10697 per $1,000. District side — 2026 taxes due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty after July 2, 2026 and a further 5% after September 1, 2026; the 2026 residential municipal rate is 1.83419 and the total residential rate is 3.28195 per $1,000, and single-family utility fees total $2,571.20 in 2026. https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/City-Finances/2026/2026_TAX_RATE_SUMMARY_CITY_OF_NORTH_VANCOUVER-2.PDF and https://docs.dnv.org/documents/tax-newsletter-2026.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is there a vacant lot or new subdivision I can buy into on Grand Boulevard?** No verified one — the corridor is established detached housing on both sides of Highway 1; land trades as built fee-simple house lots, and no bare-land subdivision or presale lot offering was verified. (Section 2.)
- **Q: Which municipality is this lot in — and why does it matter?** Confirm on title: City south of Highway 1, District north of it, and the split point itself is UNVERIFIED. It decides the zoning minimums, the density (City GO Zone up to 4–6 units by designation versus District principal + suite + coach house), the tax rates (City total 3.34028 versus District total 3.28195 per $1,000) and which hazard rules apply. https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/City-Finances/2026/2026_TAX_RATE_SUMMARY_CITY_OF_NORTH_VANCOUVER-2.PDF
- **Q: Can I subdivide a corridor lot?** On the City side the GO Zone subdivision minimum width is 7.5 m in Low Rise Neighbourhood 1 (10.0 m corner) and 15.0 m in other designations; on the District side minimums run from R2(S1) at 335 m² to R1/R2(S3) at 4,000 m². There is no corridor-wide answer — the municipality and designation decide. https://docs.dnv.org/documents/zoning-guide-section-525.pdf
- **Q: What does the strata plan number on the house at 1555 Grand Boulevard mean?** A directory record carries strata plan VAP1904 for that single detached house — a bare-land / single-family strata form, not a townhouse complex. If title confirms strata tenure, diligence runs through the Form B and the filed bylaws exactly as with any strata lot; the plan detail is single-source and held as documented-but-single-source here. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/listing/r2931743-1555-grand-boulevard-north-vancouver-bc-v7l-3x9
- **Q: Does the heritage character area stop a rebuild facing the boulevard?** It shapes it: Grand Boulevard is a City heritage character area in the Green Necklace system, so a boulevard-facing rebuild on the City side carries character expectations a side-street lot does not — check the City heritage record and title before pricing a teardown. http://www.cnv.org/~/media/7BBC365465654E02B81F82788C2E01DC.pdf
- **Q: What creek or slope permits apply?** On the City side, the Streamside Protection Development Permit Area (15 m watercourse / 10 m ravine); on the District side, Schedule B creek, slope and wildfire permit areas — run the check that matches the lot's municipality. https://www.cnv.org/Business-Development/Building/Land-Use-Approvals/Development-Permits/Streamside-Development-Permit-Areas
- **Q: How many units can a City-side lot hold now?** Under the GO Zone adopted December 8, 2025: up to 3 units on a lot of 280 m² or less, up to 4 units over 280 m² in Low Rise Neighbourhood 1, and up to 6 units over 280 m² in Low Rise Neighbourhood 2 or Residential Level 4 — designation-specific. https://www.cnv.org/-/media/City-of-North-Vancouver/Documents/Housing/Zoning-Bylaw-1995-No-6700-Amendment-Bylaw-2025-No-9137-City-Initiated-Amendment-Related-to-Provincia.pdf
- **Q: Will GST apply if I buy a newly subdivided lot from a developer?** Generally yes — developer sales and newly subdivided lots are generally taxable under CRA's vacant-land rules, unlike an individual's personal-use resale (CRA GI-003). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: Which schools serve a Boulevard lot?** By exact address only — no corridor catchment is settled in this file, and the 2026/27 Cloverley catchment review may move Queensbury and Ridgeway addresses beside this corridor. Do not carry the Lynnmour/Lynn Valley Windsor chain onto a Boulevard address. Check the address with SD44. https://www.sd44.ca/media/nvsd/nvsd/content-assets/documents/schools/Catchment-Review-Presentation-Boards-May-20-Revised-Graphics.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The boulevard was a priced-to-exclude garden suburb (1906, City heritage record):** the historic-place record states the 1906 development required residences to cost more than $4,000 to construct so the area would maintain a high status, with owners signing a covenant binding heirs and successors — the heritage character area on today's City books descends from that scheme. https://www.historicplaces.ca/fr/rep-reg/place-lieu.aspx?id=2425
- **The park median is the scale of a garden, not a traffic island:** 105 metres wide and 1.6 kilometres long, part of the Green Necklace with Victoria Park, Ottawa Gardens and Mahon Park — boulevard-facing lots front a park, which is the frontage premium and the character constraint in one fact. https://www.historicplaces.ca/fr/rep-reg/place-lieu.aspx?id=2425
- **The City rewrote its side's density on December 8, 2025:** the Ground Oriented Zone adopted that day raised permitted units from 3 to 4 on nearly 4,300 City properties and allows up to 6 units in the higher designations — a City-side side-street lot's value now turns on its OCP designation, while the District side still runs on principal + suite + coach house.
