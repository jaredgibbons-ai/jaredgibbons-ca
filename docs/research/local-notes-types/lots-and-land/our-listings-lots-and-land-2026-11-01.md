## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **The Reciprocity route, in the page's own words:** the page's footer states the data relating to real estate on the website comes in part from the MLS® Reciprocity program of either the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® (GVR), the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board (FVREB) or the Chilliwack and District Real Estate Board (CADREB); listings held by participating firms are marked with the MLS® logo and detailed information includes the name of the listing agent; the representation is based in whole or part on data generated by the boards, which assume no responsibility for its accuracy; and the materials may not be reproduced without the express written consent of either board. The Penticton card shows the feed is not limited to those three boards' data in practice — the aggregation mechanics themselves are UNVERIFIED. https://jaredgibbons.ca/our-listings.html
- **Who owns the system and who may use it:** the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA), a trade association, owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service® ("MLS®") and REALTOR®, and comprises over 100,000 real estate brokers and agents across 100 local real estate boards and associations across Canada. Agents and brokers must become members of a local real estate board and CREA in order to access the MLS® system — described by the Competition Bureau as a comprehensive database of current and historical listing information crucial to residential real estate brokerage services — and must adhere to both CREA's rules, such as the REALTOR® Code, and the rules of their respective local real estate boards. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Display governance — documented milestone only:** the October 2010 consent agreement between the Commissioner of Competition and CREA — registered with the Competition Tribunal to remain in force for a 10-year period — required CREA not to adopt, maintain or enforce rules denying agents the ability to provide mere postings or discriminating against agents who offer them, and it also encompassed negotiations regarding the cooperating seller's commission and where a seller's information can be displayed. That agreement is cited as the documented federal milestone on MLS® display governance, not as a current BC display rule; its 10-year term as described has run, and what followed it is UNVERIFIED in this file. The operative display rules for this page today are the boards' own MLS®/Reciprocity rules, which are not public documents — UNVERIFIED. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **The BC regulator's rules that apply to every listing on this page:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed real estate professional in BC must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings, and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. Practical consequence: a buyer who clicks "Schedule viewing / Email" on one of Jared's listings and is unrepresented is a non-client dealing with the seller's professional until a client relationship is formed. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Property transfer tax — the base calculation for any purchase off this page:** PTT is based on fair market value on registration day and is not annual property tax. General rates: 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion over $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion over $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000 where that applies. (The "Over $3M — 5%" shorthand sometimes shown for that top band is a compression of the 3% tier plus the further 2% — flagged here as a compression, not a separate tier.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **BC first-time home buyers' program (PTT exemption):** where all conditions are met, the purchaser is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price (maximum saving $8,000); full exemption requires fair market value $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), 0.5 hectares or smaller, only residential improvements, principal-residence use only, with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy to the first anniversary. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **BC newly built home exemption (PTT):** full-exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), phasing out over the $50,000 above it and completely eliminated at $1,150,000; a person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may be eligible for a refund of the tax paid — the documented vacant-land refund route, directly relevant to the development-land listings this page carries. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **GST on vacant land — CRA level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt (CRA GI-003). Anything finer than GI-003 level is UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST/HST rebate — new HOUSING, not bare land:** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent March 13, 2026 (CRA News No. 122). The rebate is 100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, maximum $50,000, for agreements entered March 20, 2025–2031; buyers apply via Form GST190 (builder purchases) or GST191 (owner-built). It applies to newly constructed or substantially renovated HOUSING — a bare-land purchase itself attracts no FTHB rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST/HST new housing rebate:** 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, on homes $350,000–$450,000 (phase-out range), for a buyer (or related person) who uses the housing as a primary place of residence and is the first to live in it, where all requirements are met. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **FHSA:** $40,000 lifetime limit, $8,000 annual participation room. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html **Home Buyers' Plan:** withdraw up to $60,000 from an RRSP to buy or build a qualifying home, repaid within the published repayment period — with the 2026–2028 repayment-relief deferral as the program states it for the relief window. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Down payment and mortgage loan insurance (CMHC):** insurance is required under 20% down; minimum down payment 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 up to $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more, where insurance is unavailable; CMHC's published premium range is 0.6%–4.5% of the total mortgage amount. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Home owner grant (annual, not closing money):** it reduces annual property tax on a principal residence; the 2026 grant threshold is $2,075,000 for a full grant, and $0 over $2,189,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **Strata block (for a listing on this page that is a strata lot, including a bare-land strata lot or strata industrial unit):** Form B diligence (provided within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid under SPA ss.123.1/123.2, with May 1, 2023 exemptions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **ALR rules (for an acreage or farm listing on this page):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)); ALC residence caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Purchase prohibition:** the federal non-Canadian purchase prohibition is stated in this file ONLY as its own research supports it: the research encodes no prohibition position for this page, so none is asserted.
