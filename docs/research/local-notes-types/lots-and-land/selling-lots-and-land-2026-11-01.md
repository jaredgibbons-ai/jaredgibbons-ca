## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **The regulator's framework (BCFSA, opened in the research pass):** in BC a licensed real estate professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; disclosure of whether the professional can represent the consumer, the duties owed, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA must happen at the outset of dealings, and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Commission — this slug's defect (b), documented precisely:** the page states no commission rate, amount or structure anywhere, and its BCFSA-attributed costs list omits commission entirely, even though commission is the cost BCFSA's consumer framework leads with. What a seller pays Jared's brokerage is therefore UNVERIFIED from this page — under the BCFSA framework above, commission and fee information is given to the consumer in the listing conversation, off-page. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html
- **Cooling-off / rescission, verified at the regulator:** if a buyer rescinds an accepted offer under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining balance of the deposit to the buyer, with no separate release form signed by either party. The page's own statement matches: a cancelling buyer must tell the seller in writing and pay 0.25% of the offer price. Deposits generally: a buyer's deposit is usually held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder; where subject clauses are not removed, the deposit is not automatically returned and both parties must sign a release, except where the buyer exercises the right of rescission. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits and https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html
- **BC home flipping tax, verified on the Province page the selling page links to:** the tax applies to profit from selling a BC property (including presale contracts) owned for less than 730 days, under the Residential Property (Short-Term Holding) Profit Tax Act effective January 1, 2025; the rate is 20% of net taxable income for a property disposed of within 365 days and gradually decreases over the next 365 days, with no tax at 730 days; a return must be filed within 90 days of the sale where the seller is subject to the tax or an exemption applies only after filing. The page's shorter wording — "20% of the profit," "drops to zero by day 730," "a return may be due within 90 days" — is a simplification of "net taxable income" and the filing conditions as the Province states them. The tax applies to a bare lot sold inside 730 days on the same terms as to a home, on the Province's wording ("a BC property"). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/bc-home-flipping-tax
- **Municipal frames for a land seller (kept separate — the page spans both cities):** Surrey ground — OCP Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted June 15, 2026; Bylaw 18020 is REPLACED, never cited as current), Zoning Bylaw No. 12000, suite cap 40% of principal habitable area, subdivision/servicing under Bylaw 8830 (1986), verified on City pages. Township of Langley ground — Zoning Bylaw 2500, SSMUH Bylaw 6020 (NO 6-plex tier), suite cap 120 m², coach house fee-simple under s.102 (never strata), Subdivision and Development Servicing Bylaw 2019 No. 5382 CONFIRMED. Langley City ground — Zoning 2025 No. 3300 by number (adoption date UNVERIFIED), SSMUH Bylaw 3284, suite = lesser of 100 m² and 40%. Which frame governs a lot sale depends on the parcel's own municipality; the selling page states none of this and no frame is asserted for the page itself. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/12000 and https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/subdivision.aspx
- **ALR block (where ALR ground exists — parcel status UNCONFIRMED per parcel):** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)). ALC residence caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR (that error is documented on the horse-properties page, which owns it — NOT in this file's research and not asserted for this slug). https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Strata block (a bare-land strata lot sale, where one exists):** Form B within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + May 1, 2023 exemptions). A "45+" listing line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw. A fee-simple lot carries none of this. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **PTT — worked example (inputs + tier math; a buyer's tax, stated here because sellers price against it):** on a $900,000 lot purchase — 1% on the first $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% on $200,000–$900,000 ($14,000) = **$16,000**. Tiers: 1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above, plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **FTHB PTT exemption / Newly Built Home Exemption:** first $500,000 exempt, full at value ≤$835,000 (max saving $8,000), partial to $860,000; Newly Built Home Exemption full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 — a new-HOME exemption; a person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may be eligible for a refund of the tax paid. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land generally exempt. Finer points UNCONFIRMED — a land seller's own GST position is an accountant question, as the page itself directs for tax questions. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST rebate:** new HOUSING, not bare land (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026, CRA governs; 100% to $1M phasing to $1.5M, max $50,000, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031). New Housing Rebate 36% / max $6,300 / $350K–$450K; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
