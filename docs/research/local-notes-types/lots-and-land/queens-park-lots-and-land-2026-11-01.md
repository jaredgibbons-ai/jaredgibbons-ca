## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Heritage Conservation Area — the defining rule for lots here:** the Queen's Park HCA (Schedule B of the OCP, adopted by OCP Amendment Bylaw No. 7926, 2017) classes every property Protected or Non-protected regardless of age. Building Permit applications for work such as new buildings, demolition, or changes affecting the front, sides or visible roofline of Protected properties, **and subdivision applications**, require a Heritage Alteration Permit (HAP) reviewed for design-guideline compliance; Protected properties also need an HAP for exterior changes that do not require a Building Permit. Protected properties are also under the City's Heritage Property Maintenance Standards Bylaw; standard in-kind maintenance does not require an HAP. Protected-property incentives include additional density, special BC Building Code provisions, density transfer, relaxed laneway/carriage house guidelines and no heritage permit fees. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Zoning — Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001:** houses sit under the RS series (s.310) — RS-1 minimum site 557.40 sq. m. (6,000 sq. ft.), FSR 0.5 — overlaid by the HCA. Standing citywide position: New Westminster's townhouse districts are RT-2 through RT-2C plus RT-3/3A/3B only; RT-A/RT-B do not exist, and 836.10 m² is an RM apartment-district density threshold, not an RT figure. This slug's own research carries no RT-A/RT-B wording, so no in-file correction of its own text is needed. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Zoning_Bylaw_No_6680_2001_as_of_June_30_2022_(Complete).pdf
- **OCP — Our City 2041, Bylaw No. 7925, 2017:** the Official Community Plan governing the area, including the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area (Schedule B). https://www.newwestcity.ca/ocp/past-event-materials-and-council-reports
- **SSMUH / added units as the City states it:** the RS framework above, as overlaid by the HCA — Protected-property incentives include additional density and relaxed laneway/carriage house guidelines, but any added-unit or subdivision path runs through the HAP process; parcel-specific, confirm with the City. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Subdivision + servicing — Bylaw No. 7142, 2007 (VERIFIED), plus the HAP layer:** the Subdivision and Development Control Bylaw No. 7142, 2007 requires Approving Officer approval and ensures new lots are adequately serviced and meet the Zoning Bylaw's minimum lot size and frontage requirements, through the City's pre-application / application / Works & Services Agreement process — and in Queens Park a subdivision application additionally requires a Heritage Alteration Permit. Neither process alone settles a Queens Park split. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/7142_Subdivision.pdf
- **Flood position — this file's own research only:** the governing overlay here is heritage, not floodplain; a floodplain zone for this area is UNVERIFIED in the sources read. This file imports no zone — not Downtown/Quay's Zone 3, not Queensborough's Zone 1. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What does the Heritage Conservation Area mean for a lot owner?** Every property is classed Protected or Non-protected regardless of age; new buildings, demolition, subdivision and specified exterior changes need a Heritage Alteration Permit reviewed against the design guidelines, and Protected properties are also under the Heritage Property Maintenance Standards Bylaw. Confirm the parcel's classification with the City before pricing any work. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Q: Can I subdivide a Queens Park lot that meets RS-1's minimum?** Not on the zone alone — a subdivision application inside the HCA also requires a Heritage Alteration Permit, on top of Approving Officer approval and servicing under Bylaw No. 7142, 2007. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Every lot is classified, old or new:** the HCA classes every property Protected or Non-protected regardless of construction age — a brand-new Queens Park house carries the HCA layer exactly as an 1890s house does, and Protected homes need an HAP even for some exterior changes that need no Building Permit. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Deferred maintenance is a bylaw issue here:** the Heritage Property Maintenance Standards Bylaw sets minimum standards for Protected properties so heritage properties are not lost through neglect — deferred exterior maintenance on a Protected home is both a compliance issue and a negotiation point. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **The city's only HCA:** the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area is currently the only HCA in New Westminster — no other New Westminster neighbourhood carries this permit layer, which is why Glenbrooke North next door is documented as adjoining-but-not-covered in its own file. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
