## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- "Central Abbotsford" is the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board sub-area name used in the SnapStats FVR September 2026 report; it is not confirmed as an official City of Abbotsford neighbourhood name — the City plans the core through the City Centre Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2913-2019) and the Historic Downtown Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2920-2019). https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/community-planning/neighbourhood-plans
- The City's own commercial study describes the City Centre as a roughly rectangular area bordered approximately by Peardonville Road and Mill Lake to the south; Ware Street, Mill Lake Road and Horn Street to the east; Parkview Street and Tims Street to the west; and to the north by a line roughly parallel to George Ferguson Way and Maclure Road, centred on South Fraser Way. The Historic Downtown plan's focused redevelopment area is bounded in plan material by Pine Street, Cyril Street, McDougall Avenue and McCallum Road. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/docs/business-development/HDNP%2BCommercial%2BMarket%2BStudy.pdf
- Where land actually trades: there is no greenfield ground in the core. Land changes hands as (1) individual infill / teardown parcels on the older detached streets, (2) small subdivisions carved out of those streets through rezoning, and (3) development sites assembled for the plan-designated multi-family forms — the Rail District being the named example (Section 2). Sources: the research file's housing profile (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-1/central-abbotsford-guide.md, §5).
- This is Abbotsford's attached-housing centre of gravity: 256 attached listings and 35 attached sales in September 2026 — the most of any Abbotsford sub-area — against 70 detached listings. SnapStats publishes no land category (Detached vs "Condos & Townhomes" combined only), so no figure in this file is presented as a land figure. Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report (research file above).
- Bourquin Exchange, the central transit exchange, is in the area; BC Transit routes include 1 (Highstreet/UFV), 2 (Highstreet/McMillan), 4 (Saddle/Montrose), 5 (Clearbrook/Bourquin via Hospital), 12 (UFV/Bourquin), 17 (Industrial/Bourquin), 21 (Aldergrove/Abbotsford) and 31 (Abbotsford/Mission). Highway 1 interchanges at McCallum Road and Clearbrook Road serve the central area. There is no SkyTrain or West Coast Express station in Abbotsford. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/cfv_map_21.pdf
- Schools physically in the central area (SD34 sites): Godson Elementary (33130 Bevan Ave), Dormick Park Elementary (32161 Dormick Avenue), Abbotsford Middle and Abbotsford Senior Secondary. Two settled locator chains are carried in Section 4. https://godson.abbyschools.ca/

## 2. THE LAND ITSELF

**The honest position, stated plainly at the top: Central Abbotsford has little to no meaningful bare-land market. There is no bare-land subdivision, no bare-land strata and no lot-release product in the urban core — the sub-area is fully built. What trades as "land" here is an infill parcel, a teardown lot, or a development site held for a plan-designated building form. Verified named land developments counted here: 0.**

- **Fee-simple infill parcels are the default land form.** The central area mixes older single detached streets (RS3, RS5 and infill RS3-i / RS7 zones) with the city's deepest strata stock. A vacant or teardown parcel on those streets is a fee-simple lot: the buyer's own title, no strata corporation, no Form B, no strata fee — zoning, the two neighbourhood plans and any registered covenants govern what can replace the existing house. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58178/
- **Lot character, as the research states it:** RS3 minimum interior lot 540 m²; RS5 minimum interior lot 420 m²; and RS7 (Infill Residential Zone) minimum lot size 300 m² for new interior lots, with minimum lot width 10.0 m and depth 22.5 m — among the smallest detached lot minimums in the city. RS3-i (Urban Residential Zone, Infill) is named in City council material for Maple Street in the Historic Downtown plan area. These minimums are why the core's land market is an infill market: the plans contemplate existing streets being re-cut into small lots, not new subdivisions being opened. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/52433/ and https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58178/
- **The infill process, documented on a real central file:** a September 16, 2025 City council report (PDS 172-2025) processes a rezoning of 2330 Fir Street (Lots 30 and 31) from RS3-i to RS7 to facilitate a three-lot subdivision, consistent with the Urban 3 – Infill designation — with the standard conditions in that report being a development agreement / cash-in-lieu for road and utility upgrades under the Development Bylaw, a Community Amenity Contribution, a s.219 covenant limiting density on the new lots, and resolution of unbudgeted funding items. That report is documented here as the area's subdivision PROCESS in action — a rezoning-led, condition-heavy route to three lots — not as for-sale inventory: the Fir Street lots are not a named development and no lot is offered here. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
- **The Rail District is a development site / land assembly, documented as such — never as lot inventory.** The sibling townhomes file documents Infinity Properties' 19-acre Rail District master plan on Montrose Avenue in Historic Downtown, delivering built strata product (The Abby Townhome Series; The Mason apartment building at 2771 Montrose Avenue). Nineteen acres of assembled downtown land is the core's real "land" story — but it is a developer's master-plan site selling homes, and no source checked offers individual lots in it. Do not present Rail District ground as buyable lots. https://www.mikestewart.ca/presale/the-abby-townhome-series-at-the-rail-district-by-infinity-properties-presale-abbotsford-townhomes/ (~/workspace/local-notes/townhomes/central-abbotsford-guide-townhomes-2026-11-01.md)
- **Bare-land strata: none verified in this sub-area.** No bare-land strata plan with a Central Abbotsford address was verified in the research file or any sibling (the sub-area's one verified bare-land strata, Downes Road Estates LMS2380, is filed under Abbotsford West — see its home file). A bare-land strata lot, wherever it exists, is strata tenure: Form B, strata fees and bylaws apply to the lot. Any "bare-land strata" claim for a central address is UNCONFIRMED until a strata plan is produced. (~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/abbotsford-west-guide-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Zoning: Abbotsford Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014 governs.** The land-relevant central zones as the research states them: RS3 (minimum interior lot 540 m²), RS5 (420 m²), RS7 Infill (300 m² minimum, 10.0 m width, 22.5 m depth) and RS3-i infill. In the core, the zone plus the neighbourhood-plan designation — not the street's current houses — decides a parcel's real value. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/52433/ and https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58178/
- **Plans: two neighbourhood plans inside Abbotsforward 2050.** The City Centre Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2913-2019) and the Historic Downtown Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2920-2019) are the adopted plans for the core, under Abbotsforward 2050 — Abbotsford's CURRENT Official Community Plan, adopted December 2025. The research file's framing of the plans under the old OCP and any live-page "2500-2016" / research "2600-2016" citation are STALE / conflicted: apply 2050 and flag both numbers as a Jerry sweep item. Historic Downtown designations that price land: Urban 1 – Midrise (up to 6 storeys), Urban Centre – Mixed Use, Urban 2 – Ground Oriented (rowhouses, townhouse, multiplex or duplex housing) and Urban 3 – Infill (single detached with some ground-oriented duplexes). Abbotsforward 2050 also states a new mixed-use centre between the hospital and Mill Lake Park and a Primary Transit Corridor along South Fraser Way between Clearbrook and McCallum Road. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/community-planning/neighbourhood-plans and https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/Historic%20Downtown%20Neighbourhood%20Plan%20Final%20Event%20Boards.pdf and https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/making-progress-housing-abbotsford
- **SSMUH / added units on a lot:** the bylaw's s.140 small-scale multi-unit provision allows 3 units on lots 280 m² or less and 4 units on lots greater than 280 m² in the stated conditions, and s.140.3.8 permits one secondary suite in all single detached dwellings. June 2024 Council adoption of the SSMUH amendments is City-confirmed (a City council report describes the amendments as permitting suites more broadly and up to four units on lots within the Urban Development Boundary zoned for single detached dwellings and/or duplexes); the SSMUH bylaw NUMBER is UNCONFIRMED ("3560-2024" is asserted in research files and must not be asserted). Abbotsford's suite cap is under 50% of the principal dwelling, and a coach/garden suite is capped at 15% of lot area to a maximum of 100 m² — Abbotsford's rules ONLY (standing corrections, commissioning brief, Nov 1, 2026). This 3–4-unit provision is the core's main lot-value lever: a teardown parcel's price is routinely its unit count, so the s.140 conditions must be read for the specific lot before paying for potential. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400140 and https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
- **Subdivision and servicing requirements:** as the Fir Street file shows, a central subdivision typically needs rezoning to an infill zone first, then Approving Officer approval, with Development Bylaw works and services secured by agreement or cash-in-lieu, amenity contributions and s.219 covenants as conditions. The Development Bylaw's current number/year and full servicing schedule were NOT confirmed on a City page read for this file (a 2021 draft and 2022 references both circulate): detailed servicing requirements for a specific parcel are UNCONFIRMED until the current bylaw text and the file's Works and Services Report are read. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
- **Flood / watercourse diligence:** Willband Creek runs through the Historic Downtown / central area and is named in City Historic Downtown plan material. The central upland is not Sumas Prairie — but parcel-level floodplain status still requires a City floodplain map check for any lot near the creek, and no lot should be priced as flood-free on the neighbourhood name alone. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- **Heritage diligence:** the Gur Sikh Temple (Abbotsford Sikh Temple) at 33089 South Fraser Way is a designated National Historic Site in the core — a reminder that Historic Downtown parcels can carry heritage and design expectations under the neighbourhood plan even where no single-lot heritage designation is documented. Parcel-level heritage status: UNCONFIRMED until the plan schedules and title are read for the lot. https://www.pc.gc.ca/culture/designation/lieu-site/temple-sikh-abbotsford-sikh-temple
- **ALR rules — stated for completeness, with their local weight:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval, and ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)); ALC residence caps are a principal residence of 500 m² or less, a suite only within the principal residence, and an additional residence of 90 m² (40 ha or less) / 186 m² (over 40 ha). No ALR parcel is documented in this sub-area (Section 2), so these rules are carried here only so a mislabelled listing can be tested against them — they are not the core's governing frame. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, for any farm-claimed parcel:** $2,500 of farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value in excess of 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha — B.C. Reg. 411/95, s.5, applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR and is not asserted here. https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf
- **If a lot were bare-land strata (no verified case in the core):** the BC strata block would apply to the LOT — Form B diligence (fee, approved levies, CRF balance, insurance summary; provided within 7 days, max $35 plus up to 25¢/page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2, May 1, 2023 exemptions). The core's verified strata stock is built condos/townhomes under the same block — see the sibling files. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) on land:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Land routes into the home-based exemptions: the First-Time Home Buyers' Program can be kept by a buyer who registers a vacant lot and builds, if lot value at registration plus construction cost is $835,000 or less (partial under $860,000), building and moving in by the first anniversary of registration; the Newly Built Home Exemption can refund PTT paid on a vacant lot once a qualifying newly built home is constructed on it ($1,100,000 full / $1,150,000 partial). An infill parcel bought to hold or to resell as land gets neither. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/bc-property-transfer-tax-exemptions-guide/ and https://wowa.ca/calculators/bc-property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — stated only at the level the CRA source supports:** CRA Info Sheet GI-003 states most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (land kept for personal use is the example), while an individual's sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a parcel created by subdividing another parcel into more than two parts. That last branch is the central-area trap: the Fir Street pattern — subdivide, then sell the lots — sits on the taxable side of the CRA's line when carried on as a business, while a homeowner's one-time sale of a personal-use lot generally does not. Treatment of any specific parcel is UNCONFIRMED until vendor status and parcel history are established; state the GST position in the contract. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Speculation and vacancy tax:** the Province's taxable-areas list names the City of Abbotsford — owners of residential-class property must complete the annual declaration. A vacant infill parcel's class and any under-construction exemption are parcel-specific: UNCONFIRMED for a single lot until its assessment class and the current exemption list are read. Vancouver's Empty Homes Tax (3%) is Vancouver ONLY and does not apply here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- **Federal prohibition on purchase by non-Canadians:** the March 27, 2023 regulatory amendments repealed the provision that had made vacant land zoned residential or mixed-use inside a census metropolitan area or census agglomeration "residential property" — as amended, vacant land itself can be purchased by a non-Canadian, including for residential development, per the law-firm summaries; dwellings remain governed by the prohibition and specific application is UNCONFIRMED at the official-text level in this file. https://www.mross.com/what-we-think/article/update-canadian-government-prohibits-non-canadians-from-purchasing-residential-real-estate
- **First-home programs — the land boundary, stated precisely:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent MARCH 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself — 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. It becomes relevant on a central parcel only when a qualifying new home is built and bought on it. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out, likewise housing-only. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property taxes / holding cost:** due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty September 16, 2026 — the holding-cost clock on any assembled or infill parcel starts at closing, and mid-year closings inherit whichever penalty step has landed. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice
- **Market context, labelled as community-level only:** Abbotsford IS a SnapStats-covered community (September 2026, Central Abbotsford: detached inventory 70 / solds 12 at a 17% sales ratio — joint-fastest detached in the city — and attached inventory 256 / solds 35 at 14%, the fastest attached ratio; Abbotsford-wide medians detached $949,000, attached $497,500). SnapStats has NO land category: these figures describe the housing demand a central parcel builds into, never a land value. Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report (research file above).

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can I buy a bare lot in Central Abbotsford?** Not from a subdivision or a lot release — none exists in the core. What you can buy is an individual infill or teardown parcel on the older detached streets (fee-simple), priced on its RS3/RS5/RS7 zoning and its plan designation, or — as a developer — assembled ground like the Rail District site, which is a master-plan land play, not lot inventory. (Section 2 above.)
- **Q: How small can a new central lot be?** RS7 (Infill Residential Zone) allows new interior lots down to 300 m², with minimum width 10.0 m and depth 22.5 m; RS3 minimum interior lot is 540 m² and RS5 is 420 m². But reaching RS7 usually takes a rezoning — the documented Fir Street route rezoned RS3-i to RS7 for a three-lot subdivision, with servicing, amenity and covenant conditions attached. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58178/ and https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
- **Q: What is an older central house actually worth as land?** Usually its unit potential: s.140 allows 3 units on lots of 280 m² or less and 4 units on lots over 280 m² in the stated conditions, one secondary suite is permitted in all single detached dwellings (s.140.3.8), and the plan designations (Urban 2 – Ground Oriented; Urban 1 – Midrise to 6 storeys) set the larger forms. Confirm the zone section and designation for the parcel before paying for any of it — the SSMUH bylaw number in some material ("3560-2024") is UNCONFIRMED. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400140
- **Q: Is the parcel serviced, and who pays for upgrades?** The core is built urban ground, but subdivision and redevelopment files carry Development Bylaw works-and-services conditions — the Fir Street report secures road and utility upgrades and extensions by development agreement or cash-in-lieu, plus a $5,000 Community Amenity Contribution on that file. The servicing bill for a specific parcel is in its Works and Services Report: UNCONFIRMED until that report is read. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
- **Q: Do I pay GST on a teardown or a subdivided lot?** It turns on the seller and the parcel's history, per CRA GI-003: an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt, but a sale in the course of a business or of a parcel created by subdividing into more than two parts can be taxable. A developer-sold newly created lot is the taxable pattern; a long-held family lot usually is not. Have the contract allocate the GST expressly. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: Can PTT exemptions help on a lot purchase?** Only through building: a first-time buyer who registers a vacant lot and builds can keep the FTHB exemption if lot value plus build cost totals $835,000 or less (partial under $860,000) and they build and move in by the first anniversary; the Newly Built Home Exemption can refund lot PTT once a qualifying new home stands on it ($1.1M / $1.15M thresholds). Holding land bare earns neither. https://wowa.ca/calculators/bc-property-transfer-tax
- **Q: What are the holding costs while I assemble or permit?** Property taxes on the due-date cycle (July 2, 2026; 5% July 3; second 5% September 16), the annual speculation and vacancy tax declaration for residential-class property in Abbotsford, insurance, and any tenant/tenancy obligations on a house bought as a teardown. There is no strata fee on a fee-simple parcel. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- **Q: Is flood risk a central-area issue?** The core is upland, not Sumas Prairie — but Willband Creek runs through the Historic Downtown / central area, so parcels near the creek still need the City floodplain map check. Neighbourhood-level reassurance is not a parcel-level answer. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- **Q: Which schools serve a central lot?** Two locator chains are settled: 33130 Bevan Ave returns Godson Elementary → Abbotsford Middle → Abbotsford Senior Secondary, and 32161 Dormick Avenue returns Dormick Park Elementary → Abbotsford Middle → Abbotsford Senior Secondary (SD34 School Locator, mybaragar.com, run 2026-11-01). Any other address needs its own run. https://swift.abbyschools.ca/ourschool/kindergartenregistration/swift
- **Q: How long does a small subdivision take here?** No official timeline is published in any source checked: UNCONFIRMED. The documented sequence is rezoning (where the infill zone is not already in place), Approving Officer review, works-and-services conditions, amenity contribution and s.219 covenants before final approval — price the carrying time on that sequence, not on a promised date. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
