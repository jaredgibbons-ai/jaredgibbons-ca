## 2. THE LAND ITSELF

**This topic file documents the land FORM, not a subdivision roster: fee-simple farm parcels, almost always in the ALR in the Fraser Valley belt, carrying barns, a farmhouse residence and quota where included. No named bare-land subdivision, no bare-land strata, and no presale lot offering belongs to this topic — none is documented, and none is invented here. A poultry operation is bought as a working farm parcel, not as lots.**

### Fee-simple farm parcels — the land form, as the research states it

- **Tenure:** operations sit on freehold farm land, almost always in the ALR in the Fraser Valley belt; the licence side is annual — hatcheries, growers, transporters and processors are licensed by the BC Chicken Marketing Board on an annual basis. https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf
- **What is actually being bought:** land (usually ALR), the barn package (barns are "necessary farm use structures" for an intensive livestock operation under the ALR Use Regulation), the residence, and quota where the seller includes it — note the Abbotsford West listing behind the current pages states quota is not included, which is a common structure. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html
- **Operation types on this land:** broiler grower operations (the BCCMB sector), broiler hatching egg (breeder) operations, turkey operations, and layer (egg) operations — each under its own board and quota system. Lot character (parcel sizes, zones) is parcel- and municipality-specific: UNCONFIRMED at the topic level — no lot size, zone or servicing figure is stated for "poultry land" in general, because the topic has no single municipality. https://bcchicken.ca
- **Barns as land improvements:** under the Agricultural Land Reserve Use Regulation, B.C. Reg. 30/2019, s.5, the use of agricultural land to construct, maintain or operate a structure, other than a residential structure, that is necessary for a farm use is designated as a farm use and, under s.4, may not be prohibited by a local government enactment; s.5 states for greater certainty that this includes a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation — the wording under which a poultry barn is treated as a farm building in the ALR. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019
- **Servicing:** servicing (municipal water/sewer vs well/septic) for poultry parcels: UNCONFIRMED at the topic level in the research — it follows the municipality and parcel, and no general servicing claim is made here.
- **ALR vs non-ALR split:** no province-wide ALR percentage for poultry ground is stated in the research; the research position is qualitative — Fraser Valley belt operations sit "almost always in the ALR." Check ALR status parcel by parcel; no split figure is invented. https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf

### Bare-land strata — structurally barred on ALR ground

- **No bare-land strata exists as a poultry-farm form, and on ALR ground it cannot be created out of a residence:** ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)) — a strata plan causes a subdivision, and subdivision of ALR land requires Commission permission. Stated plainly for the general form: a bare-land strata lot IS strata tenure — Form B / strata fees / bylaws apply to the lot — but that form belongs to non-ALR subdivision ground in the area files, not to a poultry operation. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **No single zoning/OCP — by design:** this topic covers BC as a whole and has no single municipality, so there is no one municipal zoning bylaw or OCP for it; municipal zoning for a poultry operation is set by the municipality where the land sits (for example, Chilliwack's Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000 defines POULTRY as any domesticated fowl including chickens, turkeys, ducks, geese and swans, and lists General Agriculture as a permitted principal use in its AL, AU and AS zones). Confirm the host municipality's bylaw for the parcel. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- **Subdivision + servicing requirements:** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval (below); off-ALR subdivision follows the host municipality's subdivision/servicing rules — a servicing-bylaw number for the topic as a whole cannot exist and none is asserted; held UNCONFIRMED at the topic level, documented parcel-by-parcel in the area files instead.
- **SSMUH/subdivision potential:** SSMUH is a municipal residential-lot framework and does not create subdivision or strata potential on ALR farm parcels — the ALC bar governs. Where Langley poultry ground is concerned, the Township frame carried in the sibling file is: SSMUH Bylaw 6020 (NO 6-plex tier); suite cap 120 m²; coach house = fee-simple s.102 unit under Zoning Bylaw 2500, never strata; R-CL s.403 = 220 m² min / 464.5 m² max where applicable — none of which overrides the ALR rules on a farm parcel. Basis: ~/workspace/local-notes/townhomes/poultry-farms-townhomes-2026-11-01.md, §3.
- **ALR rules — the full frame:** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)); ALC residence caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha); BC Assessment farm-class thresholds in full three-tier form ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Barn rule:** a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation is treated as a necessary farm use structure under ALR Use Regulation B.C. Reg. 30/2019, ss.4–5; it may not be prohibited by a local government bylaw, but it may be regulated, and it must be shown to be required for the farm use on the property and in line with the level of that farm use. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/763/2025/10/Necessary-Farm-Use-Structures-in-the-ALR-Guidelines.pdf
- **Quota — stated only as far as the research file's board sources go:** BC Chicken Marketing Board quota is the amount of chicken, expressed in kilograms of live weight, that the board allots to a grower for production and marketing; the General Orders name mainstream quota and specialty quota. Quota transfers require board approval (the board checks good standing and cycle allocation), transfers are facilitated privately rather than through a board-run central platform, and listings may state quota is not included. Whether the BC Chicken Quota Exchange is operating is NOT CONFIRMED (board material states it is in development). Egg (layer) sector quota transfer rules from the BC Egg Marketing Board's own material: UNVERIFIED in the research file — quota/marketing-board items beyond the cited board sources stay UNVERIFIED. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Policy-Directions-Update-Sep-2026.pdf
- **Sector boards and appeals:** the BC Chicken Marketing Board (chicken), the BC Broiler Hatching Egg Commission (hatching eggs — mainstream one quota unit = one placeable broiler breeder hen; specialty one unit = one hatched chick), and the BC Turkey Marketing Board (turkey quota in pounds of turkey meat per specified period), supervised by the BC Farm Industry Review Board, which reviews a board decision on appeal, with appeals filed within 30 calendar days of the board's final decision. https://bcbhec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2022-Annual-Report.pdf https://bcturkey.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/174_66.pdf https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/organizational-structure/ministries-organizations/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/npma-appeals
- **Biosecurity / avian influenza:** highly pathogenic avian influenza is a reportable disease in Canada and cases must be reported to the CFIA; the CFIA establishes primary control zones where avian influenza has been detected and permits are required to move birds, their products and by-products through a control zone; on a farm under quarantine, no birds, bird products such as eggs, or bird by-products such as manure may enter or leave the property without CFIA permission — which can affect bird placement, showings and sale timing on a poultry farm. https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected
- **Property transfer tax:** general 1%/$200K, 2% to $2M, 3% above, +2% on residential portion above $3M — confirm the farm/ALR PTT position for the specific parcel rather than assuming a residential exemption applies to a farm purchase; FTHB Program and Newly Built Home Exemption are home-buyer exemptions (bare-land/farm eligibility UNCONFIRMED). (Program standard frame; research file cites no parcel-level PTT position.)
- **GST on vacant/farm land — CRA level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land generally exempt (CRA GI-003). Farm-land GST treatment finer than GI-003 (e.g. farming-business sales, zero-rating questions): UNCONFIRMED — do not price a poultry parcel's GST from this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST rebate / programs — land angle:** the FTHB rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122; 100% to $1M, max $50,000, phasing to $1.5M; agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031) applies to new HOUSING, not bare/farm land itself. New Housing Rebate 36%/$6,300/$350K–$450K. HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000/$8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

The BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies only in designated taxable regions as the Province states them — confirm for the parcel's municipality on the provincial source.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What am I actually buying when I buy a "poultry farm"?** A package: fee-simple land (usually ALR), the barn package, the residence, and quota where the seller includes it — plus a step into an annually licensed grower role. Any one element can be excluded (the Abbotsford West feed listing states quota is not included). https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html
- **Q: The feed card says "poultry farm" — is it a working operation?** Not on the feed's word alone. The research records the live pages' cards as residential/land listings whose text mentions a possible poultry use, a barn, or an operation with quota not included — a card is never a confirmed operation in this file. (Research file §1.)
- **Q: Does quota come with the land?** No, not automatically. Quota transfers need BC Chicken Marketing Board approval, the General Orders treat transfer with registered premises separately from acquisition/disposition without premises, and a listing may state quota is not included. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Q: Can I subdivide the farm or stratify the farmhouse to sell lots?** Subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval, and an ALR residence cannot be stratified at all (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)). There is no lot-creation route priced into a poultry parcel from this file. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Q: How big can the farmhouse and a second home be?** Principal residence at most 500 m² (suite within the principal residence only); one additional residence at most 90 m² at 40 ha or less, or 186 m² over 40 ha, subject to local bylaws. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: What does farm class actually require?** The three tiers in full: $2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of land value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha — applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" claim is an error. (Section 3.)
- **Q: Can a municipality stop me building a poultry barn in the ALR?** Under ALR Use Regulation B.C. Reg. 30/2019, an intensive-livestock structure is a designated farm use a local enactment may not prohibit — though it may be regulated, and the structure must be required for the farm use and in line with its level. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019
- **Q: Can I get a quota price from a listing or a board page?** No official source publishes quota transaction prices, and trades are facilitated privately — quota prices are UNVERIFIED in this program. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf
- **Q: What happens to a sale during an avian influenza outbreak?** HPAI is reportable; CFIA control-zone permits govern movement of birds, eggs and by-products, and a quarantined farm cannot move anything bird-related without CFIA permission — placements, showings and completion dates all move with that status. https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected
- **Q: Is there a SnapStats land or farm median I can price against?** No — SnapStats publishes no farm or quota category. The Abbotsford/Langley residential medians in the research are residential context only, never land figures. (Research file §2.)

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The licence is annual, the quota is the asset:** hatcheries, growers, transporters and processors are licensed by the BC Chicken Marketing Board on an annual basis, and a "grower" in the General Orders is a person the board has allotted quota or issued a permit — a buyer steps into a licensed, quota-bound role, not just a land purchase. https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf
- **Quota is measured differently in every sector:** broiler quota in kilograms of live weight per cycle; turkey quota in pounds of turkey meat per specified period; hatching egg quota in hen units (mainstream) or hatched chicks (specialty). Comparing two "quota included" operations without the unit basis compares nothing. https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf
- **A quota exchange was in development, not confirmed operating:** the board's Policy Directions Update states staff were directed to develop a BC Chicken Quota Exchange platform — whether it is operating is NOT CONFIRMED, so do not tell a buyer they can simply buy quota on an exchange. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Policy-Directions-Update-Sep-2026.pdf
- **Biosecurity has zones with practical weight:** provincial poultry guidance works with a Controlled Access Zone for the production area and a more restricted Restricted Access Zone where birds are housed, with only essential visitors and no contact between poultry and wild birds — showings on a working operation follow the farm's biosecurity protocol, not a normal open-house pattern. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/ai_enhanced_biosecurity_for_small_scale_poultry_producers.pdf
- **The ALC's barn guidance cuts both ways:** a necessary farm use structure may not be prohibited by a local bylaw, but the ALC guidance requires it be shown to be required for the farm use on the property and in line with the level of that farm use — an oversized barn plan for a small quota package fails that test on the ALC's own wording. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/763/2025/10/Necessary-Farm-Use-Structures-in-the-ALR-Guidelines.pdf
