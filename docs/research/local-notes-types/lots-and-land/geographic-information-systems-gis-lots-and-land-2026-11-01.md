## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What is GIS, in plain language?** As this site's own page puts it, Geographic Information Systems are "digital maps that carry data behind them" — click a property and, on the page's description, you can pull up zoning, lot dimensions, flood plain status, ALR boundaries and easements. The one tool reference verified in the research is the Province's: iMapBC lets users view and analyze thousands of geographic datasets and upload, print or email their work. Which specific layers any one tool carries is UNVERIFIED in this file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/data/geographic-data-services/web-based-mapping
- **Q: Do I need GIS skills to buy a house?** On the page's own answer: no — for a standard townhome or detached home in an established neighbourhood GIS is rarely critical, and it matters most for land, acreage, property near water or in a flood zone, and anything you plan to build on, subdivide or significantly modify. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html
- **Q: What land questions can a GIS check actually answer?** On the page's own list: whether a lot is actually buildable, whether a property is in a flood zone, whether part of the backyard is technically a creek setback, and whether a "big lot" is actually mostly unbuildable. (§2.)
- **Q: What is the difference between zoning and an OCP?** In the page's plain-language framing: zoning is the current rule for what may be built today, while the Official Community Plan is the municipality's long-term direction over the next 10–30 years. Those are the page's simplified definitions, recorded as site copy, not as bylaw-verified definitions. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html
- **Q: How do I find out if a property is in a flood plain?** The page's route: toggle the flood plain layer on a municipal GIS map — it names Chilliwack MapOnline, Abbotsford CityMap and Delta's map — with BC iMap holding provincial flood data; and it warns that flood plain status affects insurance, lending and rebuilding rules. The current names, layers and mechanics of those municipal tools are UNVERIFIED in this file — none was opened in the research pass. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html
- **Q: Can a GIS map tell me about easements and rights-of-way?** Sometimes, on the page's own answer: GIS layers often show registered easements and obvious utility rights-of-way, but not every encumbrance appears on a map — use GIS as a first look and confirm with the title search, which the page calls the source of truth. https://jaredgibbons.ca/geographic-information-systems-gis.html
- **Q: Can I rely on a clean GIS screen when I buy land?** No — on this page's own framing, a clean screen is not a clean title. Anything that affects value gets confirmed with title, the municipality and the relevant provincial layer before writing. (§3.)
- **Q: Does this page have land listings or market figures?** No — it is an explainer with 0 listings and no geography of its own, so no market figure describes it and none is stated: UNVERIFIED / not applicable. (§1.)
- **Q: What tax does a bare-land purchase carry?** PTT on the general tiers — for example $800,000 of fully residential land → $14,000, or $6,000 for a fully-qualifying first-time buyer — and GST depends on the seller and the land's use at the CRA GI-003 level: developer/business and newly subdivided sales are generally taxable, an individual's personal-use sale is generally exempt. (§3.)
