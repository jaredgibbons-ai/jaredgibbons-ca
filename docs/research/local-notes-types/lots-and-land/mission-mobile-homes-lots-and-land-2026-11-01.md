## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **The governing tenancy law (the rule that replaces land law on a pad):** manufactured home park tenancies in BC are governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and its Regulation, administered through the Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB). In a park, the tenant typically owns the home and rents the site it sits on — the structure behind every pad-rent card on this feed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Pad-rent increases — Act percentages ONLY:** landlords can increase rent only with 3 full months' notice, only once every 12 months, and by no more than the yearly limit for manufactured home site tenancies — **2.3% for 2026 and 2.2% for 2027, plus a proportional amount** for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees (the change in levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). A landlord may apply to the RTB for an additional increase above the limit. **No pad-rent dollar figure is published as fact in this file** (see §5). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Selling a home that stays in the park — assignment (MHPTA s.28):** a tenant may assign the site tenancy agreement to the buyer only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed obtained under the Regulation), an order of the director, or authorization in the tenancy agreement itself; the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting (s.28(3)); before requesting consent the seller must give the proposed buyer written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; on assignment the pad rent and the scheduled date for a subsequent increase stay the same; an unreasonable refusal can be taken to RTB dispute resolution. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s.42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute within 15 days, may in defined circumstances apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Local frame split:** inside the City of Mission, manufactured-home parks sit under City planning — OCP Bylaw 6405-2026, adopted April 21, 2026 (standing correction governing; the research records the April 7, 2026 public hearing to replace and repeal OCP Bylaw 6350-2025), zoning under Zoning Bylaw 5949-2020 — while the Dewdney Deroche and Hatzic Island parks on this feed sit under FVRD electoral-area zoning instead. City SSMUH incorporation (Bylaw 6299-2024, July 17, 2024) does not create lots in a rental park, and Mission unit counts are held UNCONFIRMED in this program in any event. Park-specific zoning, redevelopment or closure-notice rules for any named park in either system: UNVERIFIED — no park bylaw document was opened. https://www.mission.ca/council-government/news/notice-public-hearing-april-7-2026 and https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Document~Library/EA%20Housing%20Needs%20Report.pdf
- **ALR block (applies parcel by parcel, only where the underlying parcel is in the reserve):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)); caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tier — $2,500 on 0.8–4 ha / $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha / $10,000 under 0.8 ha, applications by Oct 31 — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR. Whether any park parcel on this feed is in the ALR: UNVERIFIED at parcel level. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Bare-land strata block (applies only if a community IS bare-land strata — none is verified in this topic, §2):** Form B (SPA s.59) within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page, with rules, budget and most recent depreciation report attached; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since Jul 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since Nov 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since Nov 24, 2022 (short-term may still be restricted); only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01 + May 1, 2023 exemptions) — a "45+" listing line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw, and a park's listing-stated age claim is not a strata bylaw in any event. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **PTT — stated for land transfers, with the pad position stated honestly:** the general tiers are 1% up to and including $200,000, 2% over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. First-time buyers: exempt on the first $500,000 at $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 — a new HOME, not bare land. How PTT applies to a specific manufactured-home purchase depends on whether land transfers with the home (a park unit transfers no land) — no source in the research settles the treatment for a specific unit: UNCONFIRMED per unit; take conveyancing advice before writing. **Worked example for an own-lot purchase, inputs + tier math stated:** a $500,000 parcel, fully residential, no exemption = $2,000 (1% × $200,000) + 2% × $300,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and (~/workspace/local-notes/condos/mission-mobile-homes-condos-2026-11-01.md)
- **GST — CRA GI-003 level only for land; park-home position UNCONFIRMED per unit:** for vacant land generally, most sales by individuals are exempt (e.g. personal-use land), while developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally taxable. For a manufactured home, how GST applies depends on the unit and whether land transfers with it: UNCONFIRMED per unit — CPA/conveyancing advice per transaction. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html

HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What am I actually buying with a Mission manufactured home in a park?** The manufactured home itself, plus an assignment of the site tenancy for the pad it sits on — you rent the land under the MHPTA. That is why the pad rent, the park rules and the assignment process matter as much as the home's price, and why a park home is not lot stock. (§§2–3 above)
- **Q: How much can my pad rent go up?** Once every 12 months at most, on 3 full months' notice, and by no more than the yearly limit — 2.3% for 2026 and 2.2% for 2027 — plus the proportional levy/utility amount divided across the park's sites; anything above that requires an RTB order. No dollar figure for any park's current pad rent is stated as fact in this file. (§3 above)
- **Q: Can the park refuse my buyer when I sell?** The sale works by assigning your site tenancy, which under MHPTA s.28 requires the landlord's prior written consent (or deemed consent, or an order of the director, or authorization in the agreement itself); the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent; you must give the proposed buyer the park rules and written tenancy terms before requesting consent; the pad rent carries over unchanged on assignment; and an unreasonable refusal can be taken to RTB dispute resolution. (§3 above)
- **Q: What happens if the park closes or converts?** Under MHPTA s.42 the landlord must have all permits in place first, then give 12 months' notice and pay $20,000 compensation, with defined additional-compensation routes and a further payment (greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent) if the conversion does not proceed within a reasonable period. Confirm the park's ownership and any redevelopment signals as due diligence. (§3 above)
- **Q: Is any home on this feed on its own land?** One card claims "MOBILE ON ITS OWN LOT" (Triple Creek Estates) — a listing claim, not a tenure finding. A pad home, an owned-lot home and a strata lot finance, insure and resell differently, so verify on title which one is being sold before comparing prices. (§2 above)
- **Q: Is there a 55+ park in this market, and is it land I own?** Mission MHP is the one verified 55+ park in the siblings' tenure records — and it is pad tenure: you own the home and rent the pad; you do not own the land. Its own City/FVRD side is UNVERIFIED at parcel level in this file. Mission's other verified 55+ forms (Cherry Lane and Luther Place strata, Cedar Valley Manor life lease, St. Andrew's Place co-op) are tenure records in the 55-plus siblings, cross-referenced only. (§2 above)
- **Q: What is the median price of a manufactured home in Mission?** None is published — SnapStats has no manufactured-home split; its Mission medians ($814,000 detached; $430,000 attached, September 2026) describe the whole dwelling market, not park homes. The feed's first-page prices are a listing snapshot, not a median. (§1 above)
- **Q: Are all the parks on this page in the City of Mission?** No — the first page includes parks in Dewdney Deroche and on Hatzic Island, which are FVRD Electoral Area G ground with rural taxes paid to the Province, alongside City of Mission parks; the MHPTA applies in both, but zoning and property-tax systems differ. (§1 above)
- **Q: Do I pay PTT and GST on a park home?** UNCONFIRMED per unit in the research — the treatment depends on whether land transfers with the home, and a park unit transfers no land. The §3 worked example ($500,000 → $8,000) is for an own-lot land purchase only. Take conveyancing advice before writing. (§3 above)
- **Q: Can I rely on a park's stated age rule, pet rule or pad rent from a listing?** No — feed text variously claims "no age restrictions," "Park Approval Is Needed" and pet limits, all listing text only: UNVERIFIED.
