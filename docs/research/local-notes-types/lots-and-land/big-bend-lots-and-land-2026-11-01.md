## 2. THE LAND ITSELF

**Big Bend is the one file in this batch where the land itself is genuinely the market — but it is farm and industrial land, not residential lots. The honest summary: Big Bend's land trades as (1) ALR farm parcels in the A1 agricultural district — the core of the district, governed by the Agricultural Land Commission framework before any city rule; (2) industrial parcels and buildings on the Fraser River employment lands; and (3) a small rural-residential pocket (Riverside) of freehold homes on large flat lots. There is NO residential subdivision market here: no named fee-simple bare-land subdivision, no bare-land strata community and no presale LOT offering was verified, and no individual farm, building or complex name was confirmed on an official or developer source. Every parcel in the lowland also sits in a Fraser River floodplain behind dykes — flood diligence leads every file.**

### ALR farm land — the core form

- **The defining fact:** land in Big Bend is in the provincial Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), administered by the Agricultural Land Commission, and the Burnaby 2050 Official Community Plan supports protecting and expanding agriculture in Big Bend. The City runs a Burnaby Farm Tour Map for the Big Bend farming district, promoting farm-gate and nursery sales, and states Big Bend has a culturally diverse farming heritage, with historic Chinese Canadian small-scale farms that still exist today. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-12-11/city-burnaby-adopts-burnaby-2050-official-community-plan and https://yourvoice.burnaby.ca/agriculture-big-bend
- **Farm stock character:** working farms and nurseries in the A1 agricultural district, including the historic Chinese Canadian small-scale farms the City documents. Farm parcel sizes, soil class and farm-status details are per-property checks: UNVERIFIED at district level — this file states no typical farm lot size, because no source states one. (Research file above, §5)
- **ALR vs non-ALR split:** the research documents Big Bend's farmland as ALR land and the district as the City's main agricultural area; it does not publish a parcel-count or hectare split between ALR farm ground, industrial ground and the Riverside residential pocket, so **no ALR percentage split is stated**. ALR status and permitted farm / non-farm uses for a specific property are checked in the Agricultural Land Commission's ALR Property and Map Finder before any value is priced in. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Servicing position for farm ground:** the district is dyke-protected lowland with pumped drainage as part of the City's flood-protection system (see §3); parcel-level water, sewer and utility servicing for a specific Big Bend farm or industrial property: UNVERIFIED — farm and industrial utility charges for a specific property are likewise UNVERIFIED. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **OCP — Burnaby 2050 Official Community Plan (adopted December 9, 2025):** the City adopted the Burnaby 2050 OCP on December 9, 2025, and the plan supports protecting and expanding agriculture in Big Bend. The OCP is the policy frame; for ALR parcels the provincial ALC framework governs what the land can actually do, and the two are read together on every farm file. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-12-11/city-burnaby-adopts-burnaby-2050-official-community-plan
- **Zoning — Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026, No. 14815 (in effect July 1, 2026):** the new Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026 was adopted June 9, 2026 and took effect July 1, 2026, and the City is updating zoning regulations for agricultural districts through that rewrite. A1 is the City's agricultural zoning district in Big Bend (chickens may be kept in the A1 district under Zoning Bylaw rules). Parcel-level minimum lot sizes and the current district for a specific Big Bend farm parcel: UNVERIFIED — an A1 assumption from the old bylaw is not enough for a farm purchase; verify the current zoning district in writing with the City and the ALC. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **Subdivision + servicing — process documented, base number HELD UNCONFIRMED:** subdivision of a Big Bend parcel runs through the City's subdivision process under its subdivision servicing bylaw framework, with servicing standards confirmed with the City for the specific parcel and, for ALR parcels, ALC approval engaged first (below). **The servicing bylaw's BASE number is held UNCONFIRMED in this file** — the bylaw was renamed by Amendment Bylaw No. 14667, 2024, and no base number is asserted here. No servicing standard is quoted from a bylaw whose citation this file does not hold as verified; confirm the current instrument and its standards with the City in writing for the parcel. (Position per this batch's brief; research file above.)
- **ALR block — the governing rules for Big Bend farm ground:** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6; SPA s. 239; ALCA s. 21(1)); ALC residence caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR. ALR status and permitted farm / non-farm uses for a specific property are checked in the ALC's ALR Property and Map Finder, and ALR land is governed by the provincial Agricultural Land Commission Act framework — run the ALR check first on any farm parcel, before pricing development or non-farm value into it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Floodplain diligence — the second defining fact:** the City states Burnaby's southern boundary borders nearly 7 km of the North Arm of the Fraser River and the nearly 800 hectares of low-lying area between the steep south slope and the Fraser River is a floodplain; the Fraser River poses a substantial flood hazard from snowmelt in late spring and early summer and from combined heavy rain and winter storm surge events. The City protects the lowland areas with river and shoreline dykes, pump stations and flood boxes, maintains a Flood Response Plan, and receives regular updates from the BC River Forecast Centre during flooding events and freshet. Confirm the parcel's flood construction requirements, dyke position and insurance before subjects come off, not after. http://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/water-and-sewers/flood-prevention
- **Property transfer tax:** general 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million; First-Time Home Buyers' Program (first $500,000 exempt at values of $835,000 or less, maximum saving $8,000, partial $835,000–$860,000); Newly Built Home Exemption (full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000 — a new-HOME exemption; bare-land eligibility UNCONFIRMED). Farm and industrial parcels take PTT advice on their own facts; no Big Bend-specific PTT position is documented in the research. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **GST on vacant land — CRA level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt (CRA GI-003). Finer points UNCONFIRMED — and for working farm and industrial parcels, GST treatment turns on the buyer's and seller's business facts well beyond the vacant-land summary, so nothing finer is stated here. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST rebate / programs — land angle:** the FTHB rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122; 100% to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M; agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself — a Big Bend farm, industrial or rural-residential lot purchase alone earns no rebate. New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Speculation/vacancy + purchase prohibition:** Vancouver Empty Homes Tax (3%) is Vancouver ONLY — it does NOT apply to this Burnaby file. No Big Bend-specific speculation/vacancy position is documented in the research — none imported. The federal non-Canadian purchase prohibition position for this land is UNCONFIRMED in this file — the research cites no prohibition source for Big Bend, and the position is not conformed from other files (a live question on farm and industrial parcels in particular; take it as a per-file legal check, not a program answer).

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is Big Bend a residential neighbourhood where I can buy a building lot?** No — on the City's own sources it is primarily an industrial and agricultural district: ALR farmland, river industrial lands, a foreshore park and a small rural-residential pocket (Riverside). There is no subdivision housing market and no verified lot offering; residential buying here means an existing Riverside home on its lot. (Section 1; Section 2.)
- **Q: What actually trades here as land?** Farm parcels on ALR land (the core of the district), industrial parcels and buildings on the river employment lands, and rural-residential homes in the Riverside pocket. The listed footprint is correspondingly tiny: 2 detached and 3 condos & townhomes listings in September 2026, with no sales. (Section 2.)
- **Q: Can I subdivide or build a second home on a Big Bend farm?** Start with the ALR, not the city: subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval, ALR residences cannot be stratified, and residence caps apply (principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² on parcels ≤40 ha / 186 m² over 40 ha). Then verify the parcel's current district under Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026 (No. 14815, in effect July 1, 2026) in writing — agricultural-district rules were being updated through the rewrite. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **Q: How do I check what a farm parcel is allowed to do?** In the ALC's ALR Property and Map Finder — confirm ALR status, permitted farm uses and any non-farm-use restrictions before pricing development or non-farm value into the property. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Q: Is the floodplain a real cost, or a formality?** Real. The district is a roughly 800-hectare Fraser River floodplain protected by dykes, pump stations and flood boxes, with flood hazard from late-spring/early-summer snowmelt and from combined heavy rain and winter storm surge. Confirm flood construction requirements, dyke position and insurance before subjects come off. http://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/water-and-sewers/flood-prevention
- **Q: How should a Big Bend parcel be priced when nothing sold last month?** Not off its own month: September 2026 recorded zero sales on five total listings, so a price opinion needs a long window, farm/industrial comparables from the wider Fraser Valley ALR market, and the property's own assessment file. Burnaby-wide medians (detached $1,825,000; condos & townhomes $686,000) describe other sub-areas' housing stock, not Big Bend land. (Section 1.)
- **Q: Does farm class change what I pay to carry the land?** It can drive the carrying cost — and it is a per-property BC Assessment / tax-notice check, not a sub-area statistic. Confirm farm-class assessment status and what transfers with the sale before writing. The ALC farm-class thresholds themselves are three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31). https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Which Burnaby servicing bylaw governs a subdivision here — what is its number?** UNCONFIRMED in this file, deliberately: the servicing bylaw was renamed by Amendment Bylaw No. 14667, 2024, and its BASE number is held UNCONFIRMED rather than asserted from a stale citation. The process is documented in §3; confirm the current instrument and its standards with the City in writing for the parcel. (Section 3.)
- **Q: Will GST apply to a farm or industrial parcel purchase?** At CRA GI-003 level, developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally taxable and an individual's personal-use land sale is generally exempt — but working farm and industrial purchases turn on business facts beyond that summary, so the finer position is UNCONFIRMED here and belongs in the buyer's tax advice. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
