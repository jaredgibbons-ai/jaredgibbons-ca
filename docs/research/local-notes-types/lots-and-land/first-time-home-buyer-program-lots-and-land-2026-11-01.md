## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **PTT general rates — Province:** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion over $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000; based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office. The live page's rate table compresses the over-$3M structure into one "Over $3,000,000 — 5%" line (site copy, flagged in §5). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **PTT — worked examples (inputs + tier math):** $500K → $8,000; $700K → $12,000; $800K → $14,000 ($6,000 with the FTHB exemption); $900K → $16,000; $1.0M → $18,000; $1.2M → $22,000; $1.5M → $28,000; $1.8M → $34,000; $2.0M → $38,000; $3.5M → $93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M (a townhomes sibling carries $78,000 for $3.5M; OPEN reconciliation item — follow $93,000, flag the conflict wherever met). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-time buyers' PTT program — Province:** a qualifying buyer (Canadian citizen or permanent resident; lived in BC at least a year immediately before registration or filed at least 2 BC income tax returns in the last 6 taxation years; never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere; never received the exemption before) is exempt on the first $500,000 where the property is used only as a principal residence, has a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective Apr 1, 2024), is 0.5 hectares or smaller and contains only residential improvements; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and occupy to the first anniversary to keep the full exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Newly built home exemption — Province:** full exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective Apr 1, 2024), phasing out over the $50,000 range above it and eliminated at $1,150,000 — a new-HOME exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **FTHB GST rebate — CRA (new HOUSING, not bare land):** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent March 13, 2026 (CRA governs; where a source states Mar 12, that is noted in-line and never silently overwritten — none is met in this slug's research); together with the new housing rebate it provides 100% of the GST on a newly constructed or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million, gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, none at $1.5 million or more, maximum $50,000; eligibility turns on a builder agreement entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036. The phase-out mechanism between $1M and $1.5M is stated by CRA only as "gradually reduced" — the $1.25M straight-line phase-out row is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **New Housing Rebate — CRA:** generally 36% of the GST paid, maximum $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, not available at $450,000 or more. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **FHSA and HBP — ledger figures, with the research disclosure in the status block:** FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 participation room in the first year the account is opened, contributions generally deductible; the CRA confirms a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and an HBP withdrawal can be used for the same qualifying home when each program's conditions are met. HBP $60,000 (ledger figure; this slug's research held it UNVERIFIED — status block). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Down payment and mortgage insurance — CMHC:** mortgage loan insurance is required when a down payment is less than 20%; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more (where insurance is not available); **CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount**, and the premium can be paid in full or added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land generally exempt. Finer points UNCONFIRMED. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Strata block (a bare-land strata lot purchase):** Form B within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for 5+ lots since Jul 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF since Nov 1, 2023; rental bylaws VOID since Nov 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + May 1, 2023 exemptions). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Does the first-time buyer GST rebate apply if I buy a vacant lot?** No — the rebate is for new HOUSING: 100% of the GST on a newly constructed or substantially renovated home up to $1 million (max $50,000), not on bare land itself. A lot purchase is priced under the GI-003 vacant-land frame instead (§3). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: How much PTT does a first-time buyer actually save?** A qualifying buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 where the fair market value is $835,000 or less, the property is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as a principal residence; between $835,000 and $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: How much PTT on an $800,000 first home?** $14,000 before the exemption — 1% on the first $200,000 ($2,000) plus 2% on the next $600,000 ($12,000) — and $6,000 with the first-$500,000 exemption applied. (§3.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: What happened to the old GST new housing rebate?** It still exists on its own terms: generally 36% of the GST paid, maximum $6,300, gradually reduced between $350,000 and $450,000, unavailable at $450,000 or more. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?** Yes — the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the HBP for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal; FHSA room starts at $8,000 in the first year the account is opened, $40,000 lifetime, and the HBP ledger figure is $60,000 (research disclosure in the status block). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Q: Is a newly built home treated differently from a resale?** Yes: the newly built home exemption's full PTT threshold is $1,100,000 with a phase-out to $1,150,000, and a new home may also attract GST, which the FTHB rebate can offset by up to $50,000 for eligible buyers whose builder agreement dates from on or after March 20, 2025. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: What down payment do I need, and what does the insurance cost?** Under 20% down requires mortgage loan insurance; the minimum is 5% to $500,000, then 5% plus 10% on the portion over $500,000, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more. CMHC's published premium range is 0.6%–4.5% of the total mortgage amount — not the range the live page states (§5). https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: I paid PTT on land and then built — is there relief?** The newly built home frame is the one to check: a person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may be eligible under the newly built home exemption frame — confirm eligibility on the Province page before relying on it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: What must I do after closing to keep the PTT exemption?** Move in within 92 days of registration and occupy the home as your principal residence to the first anniversary of registration, or part of the exemption is repayable. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Does GST apply when I buy a vacant lot as a first-time buyer?** Generally yes if the seller is a developer/business or the lot is newly subdivided; generally no for an individual's personal-use resale, per CRA GI-003 — first-time-buyer status does not change that frame, and no FTHB rebate attaches to the lot. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
