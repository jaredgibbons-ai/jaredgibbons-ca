## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Settled court frame — and the narrower-stance flag:** the settled program treatment (carried in this slug's condos and townhomes siblings from BC Laws sources) is: Supreme Court Civil Rules **Rule 21-7** (foreclosure proceedings) and **Rule 13-5** (court-ordered sales), Practice Direction **PD-66** ("Foreclosure Proceedings"), and **Form 60** certification of a sale's result. **This slug's research file carries a narrower stance** — it confirms only Rule 21-7 (1)–(5)(a)–(c) verbatim and marks Rule 13-5, PD-66 and the later Rule 21-7 subrules UNVERIFIED program-wide (the 55-plus sibling follows the research file's narrower stance). The divergence is noted here rather than followed silently; the rule-text points below state which layer each rests on. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Step 1 — the petition (confirmed verbatim in this slug's research):** "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7 (1). All persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished and all persons against whom any relief is sought must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner may also join a claim arising out of the mortgage or collateral security and a person liable to pay the mortgage debt — Rule 21-7 (3). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Later interests + information notice (confirmed verbatim):** if the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation against the mortgaged property, a person who subsequently registers or files an interest in a land title office need not be served with the petition, is bound by an order made in the proceeding, and may file a response to petition — Rule 21-7 (4). A petitioner who serves the petition must include with it an informational notice for foreclosure proceedings in Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Powers of the court (confirmed verbatim):** in a Rule 21-7 proceeding the court may make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7 (5)(a); order that a respondent must, within a redemption period that the court may fix, pay to the petitioner what is due under the mortgage and for costs, and that in default of payment the respondent is to be foreclosed of the respondent's equity of redemption — Rule 21-7 (5)(b); and determine summarily, or order that an account be taken of and that a registrar certify, the amount due — Rule 21-7 (5)(c). The redemption period has no fixed length in the Rule text read: it is the period the court fixes in its order. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Beyond (5)(c) — settled sibling treatment (the research file marks this zone UNVERIFIED; see the flag above):** in default, a final order may be granted — Rule 21-7 (6); a party may apply at any time for an order to sell — Rule 21-7 (7); the person with conduct may apply, even before the redemption period expires, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7 (9). The vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. (Townhomes sibling §3; condos sibling §3.) https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale — settled sibling treatment:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5 (3). The court's directions may appoint who has conduct, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of value — Rule 13-5 (4), including sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5 (4)(b). The result of a sale by order of the court is certified in **Form 60** — Rule 13-5 (6). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Competing bids + PD-66:** the Supreme Court's practice direction PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings," governs practice in these proceedings and replaced the earlier sealed-bid practice direction PD-62 (per the Court of Appeal). The timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66: **UNVERIFIED beyond the rule text** — the live page's statement that competing offers can come in at the courthouse on the day an offer is heard is consistent with the Rule 13-5 (4)(d) bid-rights frame but is recorded as the page's own account of the day's mechanics, not adopted as verified mechanics. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm and https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rule text read in this program — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5)(b). Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections: the court may also make a final order of foreclosure under Rule 21-7 (5)(a). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can land — a vacant lot or acreage — be foreclosed on at all?** In process terms, yes: Rule 21-7 covers "mortgaged property" without a property-type limit, so a mortgaged fee-simple lot or a bare-land strata lot can be the subject of a proceeding. No specific parcel is named or claimed to be in foreclosure in this file — a specific parcel's status lives only in its court file. (§2.)
- **Q: Can my accepted offer still lose?** Yes, two ways the rules themselves create: until the confirming order is made the sale is not final (the conduct holder may apply to confirm even before the redemption period expires — Rule 21-7 (9)); and the court's directions may define rights to bid, make offers or meet bids (Rule 13-5 (4)(d)), so a competing bid at the approval stage is a real possibility. The timing/manner of competing bids under PD-66 is UNVERIFIED beyond the rule text — get the specific file's bid instructions from the seller's counsel, confirmed by your own lawyer. (§3.)
- **Q: Will I get a Form B and strata documents for a bare-land strata lot?** UNCONFIRMED as a general promise. In an ordinary resale the strata must provide a Form B within 7 days of a proper request (Strata Property Act s. 59, maximum $35 plus up to 25 cents per page, depreciation report attached); no official source read for this program guarantees a court sale will produce a full package, and the conditions of sale are court-settled particulars. Price the information risk, and read the conditions of sale line by line with a lawyer. (§3.)
- **Q: Is the title clean if I win?** That is what the confirming order does: on the Rule 21-7 (9) application the court may order the sale confirmed, direct the disposition of the proceeds and vest title in the purchaser; the result of the sale is certified in Form 60 (Rule 13-5 (6)). The vesting order, not the listing, is the title-cleaning step. (§3.)
- **Q: Do I pay PTT or GST differently because it's a court sale?** UNCONFIRMED — no official source read for this program states a court-sale-specific PTT or GST rule, and the GI-003 vacant-land frame is not extended to court sales here. Budget on the general PTT tiers in §3 (e.g. $800,000 → $14,000; $3,500,000 → $93,000 including the further 2% tier) and have a BC real-estate lawyer confirm the treatment on the specific file before the approval hearing, not after. (§3.)
- **Q: Where is the court file for a property in Surrey, Delta or Mission?** Not in those cities — Surrey, Delta and Mission have **NO** Supreme Court registry (mapping UNVERIFIED; New Westminster is a working assumption only for Surrey, per the townhomes sibling). Abbotsford HAS a registry — the exception. Chilliwack and Maple Ridge positions are UNCONFIRMED. Check the specific file on Court Services Online (class "H - Foreclosure"), which shows the file number, registry, party names and filed documents. (§3.)
- **Q: Are foreclosures cheaper than regular listings?** No published source in this program supports a standard foreclosure discount, and SnapStats publishes no foreclosure category, so no foreclosure price or discount figure is stated in this file. The live page's own caution stands as editorial: "foreclosures aren't always cheap, and they're rarely as dramatic as people expect." https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html
- **Q: Is the lot serviced, vacant and buildable?** Occupancy, servicing and condition are matters of the individual court file, not published statistics — UNCONFIRMED as any general statement. Do the diligence the conditions allow before the final offer, as the live page itself advises — financing approval, inspection and title review up front. (§2, §3.)
