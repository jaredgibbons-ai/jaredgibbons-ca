## 2. THE LAND ITSELF

**This topic file carries NO land roster of its own — stated plainly at the top. There are no named subdivisions, bare-land developments, bare-land strata plans, assemblies, development sites or presale lot offerings to document for a province-wide mortgage-calculator slug, and none is invented. What §2 documents instead is how land itself prices through the tool the page offers: the branch points in the mortgage rules that turn on price and down payment, and what they mean for a buyer pricing a lot or a lot-plus-build purchase.**

- **The three branch points any correct calculator has to encode:** at $500,000 the down-payment tier changes; at $1,500,000 insurance disappears and 20% down becomes mandatory; at 20% down the insurance premium disappears and the amortization menu widens. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Insured purchases (the lot-buyer-with-less-than-20%-down case):** under 20% down, price $1.5 million or less, minimum down payment on the 5%/10% tiers, premium of 0.6%–4.5% added to the mortgage or paid up front. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Uninsured purchases:** 20% or more down, including all homes over $1.5 million, where insurance is not available at all. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **First-time-buyer and new-build purchases:** 30-year insured amortization available to both groups since December 15, 2024 — directly relevant to a buyer financing vacant land plus a newly built home, where the home (not the bare land alone) is what the insured-mortgage frame prices. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Renewal/refinance scenarios the tool also prices:** the stress test is re-applied on a refinance, and is not applied on a qualifying uninsured straight switch at renewal. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Bare-land strata / fee-simple distinction, for land run through the tool:** a bare-land strata lot IS strata tenure (Form B / strata fees / bylaws apply — §3 strata block), while a fee-simple lot carries no strata layer; the mortgage math does not change with that distinction, but the closing-cost and GDS picture does (50% of condo/strata fees feed GDS), and no specific lot of either form is documented in this topic file. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Presale LOT offerings, counted separately: none documented** — a topic file documents no offerings, and presales are never counted as resale stock; presale lot offerings, where they exist, are documented in their home area files only.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **PAGE ERROR — CMHC premium range (Jerry fix item, this slug only):** the live page states the premium "ranges from 2.80% to 4.00% of the loan amount (plus 0.20% for 30-year insured amortizations)." https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html That range does NOT match CMHC's own published range of 0.6% to 4.5%, re-read for the research file, and the 0.20% 30-year surcharge was not confirmed on a CMHC or Finance page in that pass. This file carries CMHC's published figures as the correct position; the live page's range and the surcharge are UNVERIFIED here. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Down payment and mortgage loan insurance (CMHC, correct position):** mortgage loan insurance is required when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the purchase price; it protects the lender, not the buyer, and allows qualification with as little as 5% down. Minimum down payment: homes $500,000 or less — 5%; homes $500,000–$1,499,999 — 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more — 20%, and insurance is not available. CMHC's published premium range is 0.6%–4.5% of the total mortgage amount, based on down payment and loan amount, payable in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. CMHC's worked example: on a $750,000 home the required minimum is $25,000 (5% of $500,000) plus $25,000 (10% of $250,000) = $50,000. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **How interest is calculated — semi-annual compounding:** under the federal Interest Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. I-15, s.6, where principal and interest secured by a mortgage on real property are payable on a blended-payment plan, no interest is chargeable unless the mortgage contains a statement showing the principal and the rate of interest chargeable on it, "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" — the statutory basis for the Canadian convention that fixed mortgage rates are stated as nominal rates compounded semi-annually. The live page states the same convention for fixed rates; its variable-rate monthly-compounding statement is a lender-practice statement, not a statutory rule, and is UNVERIFIED as a rule. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **The mortgage stress test — OSFI minimum qualifying rate:** the greater of the mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25%; the buffer is currently 2% and the floor is currently 5.25%, and OSFI reviews both at least annually. Federally regulated lenders must apply it to most newly underwritten residential mortgagors under Guideline B-20, to insured and uninsured mortgages alike; OSFI does not expect lenders to apply it to an uninsured straight switch at renewal with no increase to the amortization period and no increase to the loan amount. Whether any given BC credit union (provincially regulated) applies it is lender-specific and UNVERIFIED here. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Amortization rules:** insured mortgages are those with less than 20% down, available only on homes priced at $1.5 million or less (cap raised from $1 million effective December 15, 2024). Eligibility for 30-year insured amortizations was extended to all first-time homebuyers and to all buyers of new builds, effective December 15, 2024. The exact general insured maximum (25 years) for borrowers outside those two groups was NOT stated on a regulator page re-read in the research pass and is UNVERIFIED here; the Department of Finance's detailed first-time-homebuyer criteria for the 30-year measure are likewise UNVERIFIED here. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Affordability ratios — GDS and TDS (FCAC):** total monthly housing costs (mortgage payments, property taxes, heating costs and 50% of condo fees, if applicable) should not be more than 39% of gross household income (GDS); total debt load (housing costs plus all other debts) should not be more than 44% of gross income (TDS). FCAC frames both as guidelines, not hard approval cut-offs. The live page's GDS/TDS description matches the FCAC composition. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **Property transfer tax — closing-cost cross-reference:** PTT is based on fair market value on registration day and is not annual property tax. General rates: 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion over $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion over $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000 where that applies. (The "Over $3M — 5%" shorthand sometimes shown for that top band is a compression of the 3% tier plus the further 2% — flagged here as a compression, not a separate tier.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **BC first-time home buyers' program (PTT exemption):** where all conditions are met, the purchaser is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price (maximum saving $8,000); full exemption requires fair market value $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), 0.5 hectares or smaller, only residential improvements, principal-residence use only, with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy to the first anniversary. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **BC newly built home exemption (PTT):** full-exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), phasing out over the $50,000 above it and completely eliminated at $1,150,000; a person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may be eligible for a refund of the tax paid — the documented vacant-land refund route. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **GST on vacant land — CRA level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt (CRA GI-003). Anything finer than GI-003 level is UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST/HST rebate — new HOUSING, not bare land:** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent March 13, 2026 (CRA News No. 122). The rebate is 100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, maximum $50,000, for agreements entered March 20, 2025–2031; buyers apply via Form GST190 (builder purchases) or GST191 (owner-built), and a builder can credit the rebate at transfer. It applies to newly constructed or substantially renovated HOUSING — a bare-land purchase itself attracts no FTHB rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST/HST new housing rebate:** 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, on homes $350,000–$450,000 (phase-out range), for a buyer (or related person) who uses the housing as a primary place of residence and is the first to live in it, where all requirements are met. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **FHSA:** $40,000 lifetime limit, $8,000 annual participation room; a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and a Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal can both be made for the same qualifying home if all conditions are met at each withdrawal. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Home Buyers' Plan:** withdraw up to $60,000 from an RRSP to buy or build a qualifying home, repaid within the published repayment period — with the 2026–2028 repayment-relief deferral as the program states it for the relief window. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Home owner grant (annual, not closing money):** it reduces annual property tax on a principal residence and must not be netted against purchase taxes or mortgage math in a calculator; the 2026 grant threshold is $2,075,000 for a full grant, and $0 over $2,189,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **Strata block (for a purchase that is a strata lot, including a bare-land strata lot):** Form B diligence (provided within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid under SPA ss.123.1/123.2, with May 1, 2023 exemptions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **ALR rules (for a purchase on ALR land):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)); ALC residence caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Closing-cost figures that are NOT official:** legal/notary fees, title insurance, home inspection and property-tax adjustments are real closing-cost categories named on the live page, but the live page's dollar range for legal/notary fees ($1,500–$2,500) is not an official figure and was not verified on any official source: UNVERIFIED as a figure. GST on a newly built home: 5% GST applies to new housing in BC. https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **What this tool does NOT decide:** a calculator computes payments and guideline ratios; it does not approve a mortgage — the underwriting decision sits with the federally regulated lender applying Guideline B-20, and the insurer sets its own eligibility on insured loans. The federal non-Canadian purchase prohibition is stated in this file ONLY as its own research supports it: the research encodes no prohibition position for the tool, so none is asserted. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What rate does the stress test use?** The greater of your mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25% — OSFI's minimum qualifying rate, applied by federally regulated lenders to insured and uninsured mortgages alike; OSFI reviews the 2% buffer and the 5.25% floor at least annually. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: What is the minimum down payment in Canada?** 5% on homes of $500,000 or less; on homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000; at $1,500,000 or more, a minimum of 20% — and mortgage loan insurance is not available at that level. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: When do I need mortgage loan insurance, and what does it cost?** Whenever your down payment is under 20% on a home priced at $1.5 million or less. CMHC publishes premium rates ranging from 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount, depending on down payment size and loan amount; the premium can be paid up front or added to the mortgage. (The live page's stated 2.80%–4.00% range does not match CMHC's published range — see §3.) https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: Can I get a 30-year amortization?** On an insured mortgage (under 20% down), 30-year amortizations have been available to all first-time homebuyers and to all buyers of new builds since December 15, 2024; on an uninsured mortgage (20% or more down), 30 years is a lender offering rather than an insured-program rule, so it is confirmed with the lender, not the calculator. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Q: How much of my income can go to housing and debt?** FCAC's guidelines: total monthly housing costs (mortgage payments, property taxes, heating, 50% of condo fees) should not exceed 39% of gross household income (GDS), and total debt load including all other debts should not exceed 44% (TDS); slightly higher ratios may still qualify but carry more risk. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: Why does a Canadian calculator not just divide my rate by 12?** Because fixed mortgage rates are stated as nominal rates compounded semi-annually — the Interest Act requires the mortgage to state the rate "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" for blended-payment mortgages, so the payment calculation converts the nominal rate using semi-annual compounding. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html
- **Q: Do I face the stress test again at renewal?** On a straight switch of an uninsured mortgage to another federally regulated lender, with no increase to the loan amount or amortization, OSFI does not expect the test to be applied; a refinance or a new home equity line of credit triggers the test again. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: I am buying vacant land to build on — what does the calculator need to handle?** PTT is payable on the land's fair market value at registration (general tiers in §3, with the newly built home exemption's documented refund route once a newly built home is on the land); GST on the land itself follows CRA GI-003 (developer/subdivided lots generally taxable; an individual's personal-use sale generally exempt); and the FTHB GST rebate reaches the new HOUSING, not the bare land. Whether the land-plus-build financing itself is an insured mortgage depends on the price and down-payment branch points in §2. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: Does the calculator approve my mortgage?** No — it computes payments and guideline ratios only; the lender applying OSFI Guideline B-20 (and the insurer, on an insured loan) makes the underwriting decision, so the output is an estimate to take to a lender or broker. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **Q: What closing costs sit outside the mortgage math?** BC property transfer tax alone runs 1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000 and 3% above that on fair market value before any exemption — cash needed at completion on top of the down payment; the live page's legal/notary fee range is UNVERIFIED as a figure (no official source publishes it). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
