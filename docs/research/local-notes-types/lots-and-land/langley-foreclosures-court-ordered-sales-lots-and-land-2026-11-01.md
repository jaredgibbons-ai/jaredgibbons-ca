## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

### The settled court-ordered-sale treatment (BC Supreme Court)

- **Step 1 — the petition:** a foreclosure proceeding must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7(1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). Rule 21-7 sets out no demand step before the petition; in the standard mortgage terms prescribed under the Land Title Act, on default the lender may demand payment of all the mortgage money, apply for an order that the land be sold on terms approved by the court, or apply to foreclose the borrower's interest. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo61/loo61/53_90_03
- **Step 2 — the order and the redemption period:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption — Rule 21-7(5)(b); the court may determine or certify the amount due and pronounce judgment for it — Rule 21-7(5)(c) and (e). The Land Title Act refers to this order as an "order nisi," with a later final order of foreclosure — Land Title Act s. 271(1). The redemption period has NO fixed length in the Rules: it is the period the court fixes, and interest is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem — Law and Equity Act s. 19. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol33/consol33/96250_18 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Final order / applying for a sale:** in default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7(6); a party may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7(7). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5(3); the court's directions may appoint who has conduct, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of value — Rule 13-5(4). For land, those directions are where reserve price, servicing disclosure and access terms live — read them line by line. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Court approval of the sale:** the manner of sale may be fixed as sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5(4)(b); the person having conduct may apply to the court, even though the time for redemption has not expired, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7(9); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in **Form 60** by the person having conduct of the sale — Rule 13-5(6). That vesting order is what cleans the title for the land buyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Bids and practice direction PD-66:** the court's directions may define "the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids" — Rule 13-5(4)(d); the Supreme Court's civil practice direction **PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings,"** governs practice in these proceedings and replaced the earlier sealed-bid practice direction PD-62, per the Court of Appeal. The text of PD-66 was not retrieved from the court's website in the research pass, so the timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66 are UNVERIFIED in detail. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm
- **Where the proceeding is filed — registry position, stated exactly:** unless the court otherwise orders, a foreclosure proceeding is commenced at the Supreme Court registry in the municipality where the land is located, if there is one there, and otherwise at a registry in the judicial district in which the land is located, and applications are heard at that registry — Law and Equity Act s. 21(2). SURREY has NO Supreme Court registry, so the foreclosure mapping for Langley-area files is UNVERIFIED — New Westminster is stated as a working assumption only in the sibling programs, not as a verified mapping, and this file does not upgrade it. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Court records:** Court Services Online is BC's electronic court registry; eSearch is available to anyone with Internet access, Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry location, party names and filed documents, and its file classes include "H - Foreclosure." It publishes no area statistics. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure over land start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period on a court-ordered lot?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — it is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b), and interest runs to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act s. 19). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Q: What does "subject to court approval" mean for my land offer?** Where the court has directed sale by contract conditional on its approval (Rule 13-5(4)(b)), the person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7(9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title in you — until that order, the sale is not final. Do not book servicing contractors on acceptance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer on a lot?** Yes, it is a real possibility in this process: the court's sale directions may define the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids (Rule 13-5(4)(d)), and practice direction PD-66 governs foreclosure proceedings. Price with room, or know your ceiling in advance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: Will I get a Form B if the lot is a bare-land strata lot?** UNCONFIRMED — that is the honest answer at topic level. In an ordinary resale you would; in a court sale, what documents are produced is a per-file fact stated in the file/listing documents, and no source in this research states a general rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Q: Do I pay PTT and GST the normal way on court-ordered land?** UNCONFIRMED at topic level — no source in the research file states the PTT/GST treatment of court sales. The general vacant-land GST frame (CRA GI-003: developer/business and newly subdivided lots generally taxable; individual personal-use sales generally exempt) is the starting frame only — confirm both taxes for your specific court transaction before removing subjects. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: Where can I check the court file myself?** Court Services Online eSearch — Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." The petition, the amount certified due and the orders made are the facts of the deal. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: Can the owner still save the land after I offer?** The owner may redeem by paying what is due plus interest to the date of payment, and the person with conduct of sale may apply to confirm a sale even before the redemption period expires — ask which stage the file is at before spending on surveys, perc tests or geotechnical work. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Q: Is court-ordered land cheaper than market?** No official source publishes Langley foreclosure counts, discounts or pricing statistics, and SnapStats has no foreclosure or land category — any "typical discount" figure is UNVERIFIED. The process trades certainty (as-is conditions, court timelines, possibly thin documents) for whatever price the approval hearing produces. Source: the research file, read 2026-11-01.
- **Q: I'm the owner in difficulty — what is my window?** The redemption period the court fixes is the window to pay what is due or sell before a final order of foreclosure can be granted under Rule 21-7(6). Earlier advice is worth more than late advice. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Form 60 is the paper that closes the loop:** the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in Form 60 by the person having conduct of the sale (Rule 13-5(6)) — the document a land buyer's lawyer looks for to confirm the sale the court approved is the sale that completed. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **PD-66 replaced PD-62:** the current foreclosure practice direction is PD-66; material citing the old sealed-bid direction PD-62 is citing a replaced practice. The Court of Appeal's note is the source for the replacement in this pass, because PD-66's own text was not retrieved — its bid-timing detail stays UNVERIFIED here. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm
- **The Law and Equity Act's 6-month redemption direction does NOT govern these sales:** s. 16 covers a vendor's proceeding under an agreement for sale, not the Rule 21-7 mortgage process — a page or advisor quoting "six months by law" for a mortgage foreclosure over land is mixing two regimes. Source: the research file's UNVERIFIED list, read 2026-11-01.
- **A demand letter is not a required first step in the Rules:** the only demand wording found on an official source is the lender's option, in the prescribed standard mortgage terms, to demand payment after a default. Treat any "the bank must demand first" sequence as UNVERIFIED. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo61/loo61/53_90_03
- **There is no court-published foreclosure guide for buyers:** the court material found on official sources is the Rules, the practice directions and Form 125 — the process literacy in Section 3 is assembled from those primary texts, not from a guide. Source: the research file, read 2026-11-01.
- **The vesting order, not the contract, is what cleans the title:** on confirmation the court directs the disposition of proceeds and vests title in the purchaser (Rule 21-7(9)) — which is why a court-ordered lot can be buyable even when the owner's own title picture is a mess of mortgage debt. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
