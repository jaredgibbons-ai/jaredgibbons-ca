## 2. THE LAND ITSELF

**Steveston Village has no meaningful bare-land market — stated plainly at the top. This is a built-out historic village core inside a heritage conservation area, on the Fraser River floodplain behind the City dike system. No named bare-land subdivision, no verified bare-land strata plan, and no presale lot offering was verified on an official or developer source. Land here trades as a house on its lot on the village side streets, or as a strata unit in the low-rise mixed-use core — never as a vacant-lot program on this evidence.**

### Fee-simple lots — the only residential land form, built out

- **Village side streets — built house lots, not a lot program:** detached houses on the village side streets sit in Richmond Zoning Bylaw 8500 Single Detached zones RS1 and RS2, with secondary suite rules in Section 5.4 (a suite must be at least 33.0 m² and no more than the lesser of 40% of the dwelling's floor area or 110 m²). The documented form is fee-simple house lots; no lot size, dimension or minimum is stated in the research beyond the zone names, and none is invented here — confirm the parcel's zone and dimensions with the City. https://Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/RS1-RS2-RS324187.pdf
- **Village core — mixed-use parcels, not lots:** the core itself is mixed use — City reports name Commercial Mixed Use (ZMU32) – Steveston Village on Chatham Street, Steveston Commercial (CS3) and Steveston Office Commercial (ZC49) – Chatham Street (Steveston) in the core, so the strata housing in the core is in low-rise mixed-use buildings over Moncton/Chatham Street retail. These are development parcels and strata buildings, not bare-land inventory. https://citycouncil.richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Heritage_Alteration_Permit_for_3711_and_3731_Chatham_Street__Steveston78783.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Plan — Steveston Area Plan within OCP Bylaw 9000:** the current plan is the Steveston Area Plan (Schedule 2.4 of the Official Community Plan), within the City of Richmond 2041 Official Community Plan framework, Bylaw 9000. Note the research also cites an older council document referencing Bylaw 7100 for the Steveston schedule — the 2041 OCP framework number carried here is 9000, as the condos sibling states it. https://richmond.ca/__shared/assets/steveston566.pdf and https://www.richmond.ca/city-hall/strategies/ocp/about.htm
- **Zoning — Richmond Zoning Bylaw 8500:** RS1/RS2 Single Detached on the side streets (suite rules in Section 5.4, §2 above); CS3, ZMU32 and ZC49 named in the core. Confirm the parcel's zone with the City before pricing any rebuild, addition or split. https://Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/RS1-RS2-RS324187.pdf
- **Heritage Alteration Permits:** properties inside the Steveston Village Heritage Conservation Area are subject to Heritage Alteration Permit requirements under the Steveston Area Plan — check heritage status before anything else, because it affects renovation plans, timelines and resale positioning. https://www.richmond.ca/business-development/planning/heritage/village-conservation.htm?PageMode=HTML
- **SSMUH / subdivision potential:** no village-specific SSMUH or subdivision position is stated in the research — UNCONFIRMED for this ground, and no citywide Richmond figure is imported as a village finding. Heritage controls and flood rules (§ below) will govern any split or rebuild in practice; confirm with the City for the parcel. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/steveston-village.md)
- **Subdivision + servicing requirements:** subdivision in Richmond follows the City process under "Subdivision and Development — Bylaw 8751 (2011)", with cost recovery under "Works and Services Cost Recovery Bylaw 8752 (2011)" — Richmond's verified ledger entry is 8751/8752. Parcel-level standards are confirmed with the City. https://RICHMOND.CA/city-hall/bylaws/alpha.htm
- **Flood / dike diligence — Bylaw 8204:** the village sits on the South Arm of the Fraser River behind the City dike system — Richmond is a low-lying city in the Fraser River floodplain protected by a perimeter dike system (49 km of dikes, 39 pump stations, designed for a 1:500 flood event, dikes being raised from 3.5 metres to 4.7 metres) with drainage pump stations; the Flood Plain Designation and Protection Bylaw 8204 sets dike setbacks and flood construction level requirements. The City's Steveston Island Dike project — a proposed dike around Steveston Island from London Farm to Garry Point Park with a navigation gate for Steveston Harbour — addresses flood protection for the Steveston waterfront and harbour. Verify the lot's flood construction level and dike setback before planning a rebuild. https://www.letstalkrichmond.ca/StevestonIslandDike and https://www.Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Bylaw_8204_0410201225280.pdf and https://richmond.ca/services/water-sewer-flood/dikes.htm
- **ALR rules (for completeness — no village ALR parcel is confirmed here):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6; SPA s. 239; ALCA s. 21(1)); ALC residence caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Property transfer tax:** general rates 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked examples (inputs + tier math): $500,000 → $2,000 + $6,000 = $8,000; $800,000 → $2,000 + $12,000 = $14,000 (a fully qualifying first-time buyer at this value claims the full first-$500,000 exemption — an $8,000 saving, the program maximum — and pays $6,000; see the exemption line below); $1,200,000 → $2,000 + $20,000 = $22,000; $2,000,000 → $2,000 + $36,000 = $38,000; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + $45,000 (3% on $1.5M) + $10,000 (further 2% on $500,000) = $93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: first $500,000 exempt at values of $835,000 or less, maximum saving $8,000, partial $835,000–$860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000 — a new-HOME exemption; bare-land eligibility UNCONFIRMED. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **GST on vacant land — CRA level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt (CRA GI-003). Finer points UNCONFIRMED. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST rebate / programs — land angle:** the FTHB rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA; 100% to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M; agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself. New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Foreclosure / court-ordered sales:** the settled court process applies (BC Supreme Court Civil Rules 21-7/13-5, Practice Direction PD-66, Form 60). Form B and PTT/GST treatment on court sales: UNCONFIRMED, kept distinct from the GI-003 position above. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can I buy a vacant lot in Steveston Village?** No verified offering exists — the core is a built-out heritage conservation area and the side streets are built house lots. Land here trades as a house on its lot, not as bare land. (§2.)
- **Q: What does the heritage conservation area actually restrict?** Properties inside the Steveston Village Heritage Conservation Area need a Heritage Alteration Permit for alterations under the Steveston Area Plan, and protected heritage buildings, docks and landscape features are identified in the OCP — check heritage status before pricing any renovation or rebuild. https://www.richmond.ca/business-development/planning/heritage/village-conservation.htm?PageMode=HTML
- **Q: What zones apply — side street vs core?** Side streets: Bylaw 8500 RS1/RS2 Single Detached, with suites under Section 5.4 (minimum 33.0 m²; maximum the lesser of 40% of the dwelling floor area or 110 m²). Core: CS3, ZMU32 and ZC49 mixed-use/commercial zones. https://Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/RS1-RS2-RS324187.pdf
- **Q: Can I subdivide or add units on a village lot?** UNCONFIRMED as a village-specific position in the research — no SSMUH or subdivision figure is imported. Subdivision follows the City process under Bylaws 8751/8752, and heritage and flood rules apply on top; confirm for the parcel with the City. https://RICHMOND.CA/city-hall/bylaws/alpha.htm
- **Q: What is the flood position?** The village is behind the City dike system on the Fraser River floodplain; Bylaw 8204 sets flood construction levels and dike setbacks, and the proposed Steveston Island Dike is the City's long-term waterfront project. Verify the lot's level and setback before planning a rebuild. https://www.letstalkrichmond.ca/StevestonIslandDike
- **Q: Is any village land in the ALR?** None is documented in the research — ALR status is a per-parcel check on ALC mapping, and the ALR rules in §3 apply only if a parcel is in the reserve. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/

No such lot offering is verified in the village today. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
