- **Strata diligence (the BC strata block — applies in full to any bare-land strata lot):** Form B diligence (fees, amounts owed, levies, CRF balance, insurance summary; provided within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page) — read it with the depreciation report, contingency reserve and any special levies before pricing an offer; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 (short-term restrictions may still apply by bylaw); only 55+ age bylaws valid under Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2, with May 1, 2023 exemptions (younger spouse/partner, caregivers, legacy residents, defined children); a listing's "45+" line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **ALR rules (carried for any parcel where reserve status is confirmed — the SCPA's south edge is ALR land per §1):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s. 6; Strata Property Act s. 239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s. 21(1)); ALC residence caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within the principal residence only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm-class figures are **not asserted in this file** — no farm-class tier is documented in this slug's own research; confirm farm status and thresholds with BC Assessment for a specific parcel. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Property transfer tax:** general 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million; First-Time Home Buyers' Program (first $500,000 exempt at values of $835,000 or less, maximum saving $8,000, partial $835,000–$860,000); Newly Built Home Exemption (full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000 — a new-HOME exemption, not bare land). **Worked examples, inputs + tier math stated (fully residential, no exemption unless stated):** $500,000 → $2,000 + $6,000 = **$8,000**; $700,000 → $2,000 + $10,000 = **$12,000**; $800,000 → $2,000 + $12,000 = **$14,000** (fully-qualifying first-time buyer: **$6,000**); $900,000 → $2,000 + $14,000 = **$16,000**; $1,000,000 → $2,000 + $16,000 = **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → $2,000 + $20,000 = **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → $2,000 + $26,000 = **$28,000**; $1,800,000 → $2,000 + $32,000 = **$34,000**; $2,000,000 → $2,000 + $36,000 = **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 (3% tier) + $10,000 (further 2% on the $500,000 over $3M) = **$93,000**. Where a $78,000 figure for $3.5M is met in program siblings, it omits the further 2% tier and is an OPEN reconciliation item — this file follows **$93,000**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level ONLY:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt. Finer points UNCONFIRMED. (A condos sibling's finer GST framing is NOT carried into this land file.) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST rebate / programs — land angle:** the FTHB rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026 per CRA) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself (100% to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M; agreements March 20, 2025–2031). New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **City of Mission property taxes — CITY regime only:** 2026 property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, and a **flat 10% penalty** is added to any current balances outstanding (including unclaimed home owner grants) on July 3, 2026; the City states it has no authority to waive or modify the penalty amounts (Community Charter ss. 233–234 frame). The City does not accept credit card payments. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes and https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/payment-methods

That regime does **not** apply inside the City of Mission — do not blend the two. (Program standing correction.)
