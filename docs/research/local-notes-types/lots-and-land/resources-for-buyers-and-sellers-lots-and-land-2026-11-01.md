## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

Only the rules relevant to a land buyer or seller using this hub, per the research, plus the standing frames. Where the research opened no source, the rule is UNCONFIRMED rather than filled in.

- **The regulator behind the professionals:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and BCFSA states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers

- **Zoning / OCP — municipal, across the hub's four places, kept separate:** Surrey-side properties sit under the Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted June 15, 2026) and Surrey Zoning By-law No. 12000, with subdivision / servicing under Surrey Subdivision and Development Bylaw, 1986, No. 8830; White Rock properties sit under White Rock's own plans and Zoning Bylaw No. 2506; Langley City and Township of Langley properties sit under those municipalities' own plans and bylaws (Township frame, where it applies: Zoning Bylaw No. 2500; SSMUH Bylaw No. 6020, with no 6-plex tier). Confirm the parcel's municipality first — no single frame governs the hub. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/official-community-plan
- **SSMUH / suites, Surrey frame only where a Surrey parcel is in scope:** added-unit permission is the municipality's, as that city states it; in Surrey, one secondary suite per single-family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit, at most 40% of the principal unit's habitable floor area, never a separate strata lot. No hub-wide SSMUH or suite frame is stated. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- **ALR block (where ALR ground is in scope for a hub-area parcel — no ALR parcel is confirmed in this slug's research):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)); principal residence ≤500 m², suite within the principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31). A parcel's ALR status is checked on the ALC maps by PID or civic address. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Bare-land strata block (for any bare-land strata lot in the hub's ground):** Form B (SPA s.59) within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page, with rules, budget and most recent depreciation report attached; depreciation reports every 5 years for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; CRF minimum 10% since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 (short-term restrictions may still apply by bylaw); only 55+ age bylaws valid under SPA ss.123.1/123.2, with May 1, 2023 exemptions; a "45+" listing line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw. Strata disputes can be resolved using the Civil Resolution Tribunal. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Property transfer tax — the cost item every buyer using this hub is budgeting for:** general 1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000; in most cases property transfers are completed by a legal professional — one of this page's own categories — and the tax is based on fair market value on the day the transfer is registered at the Land Title Office unless an exemption applies. **Worked example, inputs + tier math stated:** $700,000 fair market value, fully residential, no exemption = $2,000 (1% × $200,000) + $10,000 (2% × $500,000) = **$12,000**. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: first $500,000 exempt at $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000, maximum saving $8,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000 — a new HOME, not bare land. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt (CRA GI-003). Anything finer: UNCONFIRMED. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Rebates attach to housing, not bare land:** FTHB GST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026; 100% to $1M, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1.5M; agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself. New Housing Rebate 36% / maximum $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; FHSA $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

- **Q: Will GST apply to the lot I buy?** At the CRA GI-003 level only: a developer/business sale or a newly subdivided lot is generally taxable; an individual's personal-use land sale is generally exempt. - **Q: Is a bare-land strata lot bought through the same diligence as a house?** No — it is strata diligence: the Form B package (within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page) with fees, levies, CRF balance, insurance, rules, budget and depreciation report, because the lot itself is a strata lot. A fee-simple lot has none of that (§3). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing
- **Q: What protections do I have when dealing with a real estate professional from a referral?** BCFSA states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and work for only the buyer or the seller in a transaction — and consumers can file a complaint with BCFSA. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
