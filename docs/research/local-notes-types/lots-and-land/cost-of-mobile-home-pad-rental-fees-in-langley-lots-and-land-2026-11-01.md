## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **MHPTA pad-rent increases — the complete regulated mechanism, Act percentages ONLY:** a park landlord may increase rent only with 3 full months' written notice, only once every 12 months, and only up to the yearly rent increase limit; the 2026 limit is 2.3% plus the proportional amount and the 2027 limit is 2.2% plus the proportional amount; the landlord may not round up, may not carry forward an unused portion of a prior year's limit, and if proper notice was not given the increase takes effect on the earliest date that would comply. The proportional amount is exactly: the change in local government levies, plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park — only levies and regulated utility fees for the park's common property qualify. A landlord may apply to the RTB for an additional increase above the limit (Form 52) for financial loss from increased operating or financing costs, or for substantial and essential repairs/renovations; tenants do not have to pay an increase above the legal amount and may deduct an overpayment from future rent or apply for dispute resolution. Landlords and tenants in a manufactured home park must have a written tenancy agreement for the site. **No pad-rent dollar figure is stated in this file.** https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes
- **Assignment on a sale (MHPTA s.28):** assignment permanently transfers the pad tenancy to the buyer; a tenant may assign only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it; before asking, the seller must give the proposed buyer written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent; and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s.42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute the notice within 15 days, may in defined circumstances apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Manufactured Home Registry / CSA labels / moving:** manufactured homes include modular and mobile homes, typically built in a factory off-site; the CSA label states the standard — modular under A-277, mobile under Z-240; in BC the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered with the Manufactured Home Registry, through a lawyer or notary filing the original signed and witnessed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership; a home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal, and a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification. A permit to move a home is issued by the Registry, a tax certificate for transport purposes is required (with exceptions including a move to a different pad within the same park), and the transport permit expires 30 days after issue. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Tax on a home in a park:** a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value will not include the value of the land if the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner — the accurate version of the live page's "you pay taxes on the structure only" line. https://www2.gov.BC.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home

Township rules are never applied to a City park, or the reverse. (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/rules-gaps-2026-11-01.md, via the research file)

- **PTT on land (own-land purchases only):** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. First-time buyers: exempt on the first $500,000 at $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 — a new HOME, not bare land. **Worked example, inputs + tier math stated:** a $700,000 own-land parcel, fully residential, no exemption = $2,000 (1% × $200,000) + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. - **The proportional amount makes two Langley parks' increases differ in the same year.** The headline limit (2.3% for 2026; 2.2% for 2027) is only half the formula — the proportional amount is the park's own levy + regulated-utility change divided by its own site count, so two parks across the Township/City line (or even on the same street) can lawfully post different total increases in the same year. Ask the park for its last proportional-amount calculation. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
