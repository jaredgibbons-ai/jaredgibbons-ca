## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Zoning (City side):** City of Mission Zoning Bylaw 5949-2020, as amended — verify the rural zone in the current bylaw, not an old consolidation: older Bylaw 5050-2009 texts still circulate in mirrors, and the current rural-zone minimums are UNVERIFIED on a City-hosted text (§2). Get the zone and its minimum parcel size from the City before pricing subdivision or a second dwelling. FVRD-side parcels are zoned under FVRD bylaws, not the City bylaw — which bylaw governs is a jurisdiction check (§1). https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-zoning-bylaw-housekeeping-amendments
- **OCP: Bylaw 6405-2026, adopted April 21, 2026** — replaces and repeals OCP Bylaw 6350-2025, keeping the same vision, land use framework, mapping and policy direction with a Regional Context Statement added. Any source stating a "June 1, 2026" adoption date is WRONG; this topic's research does not document that wrong date on the mission-farms page itself, so it is carried as the program-wide standing correction, **not asserted as a defect of this page** (see §5). https://www.mission.ca/media/file/public-hearing-notice-april-7-2026 and https://www.mission.ca/council-government/plans-strategies/official-community-plan
- **ALR block — full form:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval, and ALR residences cannot be stratified — depositing a strata plan causes a subdivision (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s. 6; Strata Property Act, s. 239; Agricultural Land Commission Act, s. 21(1)). Residence caps: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m² (with no more than 1,000 m² of fill placed for its construction, per the ALC housing page as the research states it); a secondary suite is permitted **only inside** the principal residence; one additional residence without an ALC application where its conditions are met — detached, capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha — **and City of Mission bylaws (or, on FVRD ground, the applicable FVRD bylaws) must also permit it**. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form:** $2,500 of gross farm income (at farm gate prices) on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha — with applications by October 31 for the next taxation year. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR; this topic's research states the tiers in full and documents no flat line on this page, so no page defect is asserted on that point (see §5). A parcel that stops meeting its tier loses the classification and its tax treatment — farm classification matters to value. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Tax regimes — kept strictly separate:** **City of Mission ground** (including Stave Falls, Steelhead and Hatzic Bench): property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a **FLAT 10% penalty** on unpaid taxes after July 2 (Community Charter ss. 233–234; the City's tax page states a 10% penalty added to current balances outstanding on July 3, 2026) — there is no second City step. **FVRD electoral-area ground** (including Hatzic Prairie, Area F, and Hatzic Island, Area G): the **separate rural regime** under the Taxation (Rural Area) Act s. 11 — **5% after July 2 + 5% after October 31**. The two regimes are never blended, and which one applies follows the parcel's jurisdiction, not its mailing address (§1). City taxes also carry additional diking levies on top of the municipal, school, regional district and BC Assessment levies, per the City's tax rates page. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes and https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-rates
- **Strata block — stated for completeness, governing no farm-topic stock:** if a strata lot were ever lawfully produced on non-ALR ground in this topic, the BC block would apply to the LOT — Form B (SPA s. 59); depreciation reports every five years for stratas with five or more lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + Reg 7.01, May 1, 2023 exemptions); a "45+" listing line is NOT a valid age bylaw. On ALR parcels the strata form is barred outright (§2). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Property transfer tax on land:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked anchors: $1,500,000 → $28,000; $1,800,000 → $34,000; $2,000,000 → $38,000; $3,500,000 → $93,000 including the further 2% tier. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level only:** most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (land kept for personal use is the CRA's example), while a sale can be taxable where the land is used in a business, sold in the course of a business, or is a parcel created by subdividing another parcel into more than two parts; sales of newly subdivided lots by a developer/business are generally taxable. A working-farm sale sits close to the business side of that line — the GST treatment of any specific Mission farm parcel is UNCONFIRMED until the vendor's status, the parcel's history and the operation's structure are established; have the contract state it. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **First-home programs — the land boundary:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026) rebates 100% of the GST on new HOUSING up to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements March 20, 2025–2031 — new housing, NOT bare land. The GST New Housing Rebate is 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $8,000 per year to $40,000 lifetime. CMHC premiums run 0.6%–4.5%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What kind of farm operation is this parcel?** From the feed alone, nobody can say — the mission-farms feed is generic, and this program presents **no card as a confirmed operation in any named sector**. The operation type (and any quota position) is established from the parcel's own records — farm income history, licences, ALC approvals, board records — not from the listing card: UNVERIFIED at the feed level for every parcel. (§2 above)
- **Q: Is this parcel in the City of Mission or the FVRD — and why does it matter?** It decides the tax penalty regime, the zoning bylaw and the planning authority. Stave Falls, Steelhead and Hatzic Bench are City ground (flat 10% penalty after July 2); Hatzic Prairie (Area F) and Hatzic Island (Area G) are FVRD rural ground (5% after July 2 + 5% after October 31, Taxation (Rural Area) Act s. 11). A "Mission" mailing address settles nothing — check the parcel's jurisdiction on its tax folio. (§1/§3 above)
- **Q: Can I build a second house on a Mission farm?** On ALR land, one additional residence is permitted without an ALC application where the conditions are met — detached, 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller, 186 m² or less on larger parcels — and the applicable local bylaws must also permit it. The principal residence cap is 500 m², and a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Can I subdivide or stratify a farm parcel later?** Subdivision in the ALR requires ALC approval, and a strata plan cannot be deposited without the ALC's permission (ALCA s. 21(1); SPA s. 239; ALC Bulletin 09, s. 6) — a Mission farm home cannot later be converted into strata lots and sold separately as of right. The current rural-zone minimum parcel sizes are UNVERIFIED on a City-hosted bylaw text, so no subdivision math is priced from the predecessor bylaw's mirror figures. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Q: Is this parcel in the ALR?** Reserve status controls residences (the 500 m² principal cap), the additional-residence size limits (90 m² / 186 m²) and most use permissions — check the parcel on the ALC maps by PID or civic address before pricing anything, and note the ALC's own caution that its online tools are for general reference, with official confirmation from its Mapping and GIS team. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Q: What income does the farm need to keep farm classification?** Three tiers, never a flat figure: $2,500 of gross farm income (farm gate) at 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha above that; $10,000 under 0.8 ha — and a continuing farmer must apply by October 31 for the next tax year. If the buyer's plan can't meet the tier for the operation's size, the classification — and its tax treatment — is at risk. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Q: Can I irrigate from the well?** Only with the licence position confirmed: non-domestic groundwater use (irrigation) requires a water licence under the Water Sustainability Act, with water fees and rentals payable, before the water can lawfully be used; domestic use is exempt. Ask for the licence in the diligence package. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-rights
- **Q: Does GST apply when I buy a farm?** Per CRA GI-003 it turns on the seller and the parcel's history: an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt, while a sale in the course of a business or of a parcel subdivided into more than two parts can be taxable. A working-farm purchase needs the vendor's status and the operation's structure established first — UNCONFIRMED for any specific parcel until then; have the contract state it. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: What will it cost to hold the farm?** Property taxes under the regime for the parcel's jurisdiction (City flat 10% penalty after July 2, 2026, plus diking levies on the City side; rural 5% + 5% on the FVRD side), water licence rentals where a licence is held, insurance, and septic/well maintenance under the Authorized Person regime. There is no strata fee: the parcel is fee-simple. Farm classification, where it is kept, is the main tax-treatment reducer — and it depends on the income actually being produced and reported. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/health-topics-a-to-z/onsite-sewerage-systems/regulatory-requirements-for-onsite-sewerage-systems
- **Q: Are the Mission market numbers in this file farm prices?** No. SnapStats publishes no farm category; the §3 figures are Mission residential context only (detached median $814,000 and condos & townhomes median $430,000 in September 2026), and the rural districts traded 0–3 detached sales each that month — price a farm from the parcel's own comparables, income history and ALR position. (as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report)
