## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) — the CRTC's framework:** CASL and its regulations target spam, computer programs installed without consent, and unsolicited redirections; the CRTC is the regulator that publishes the compliance framework. The core rule for a message like this newsletter: when sending a commercial electronic message (CEM), you must (1) obtain prior consent from the recipient (either express or implied); (2) provide identification and contact information; and (3) include a working unsubscribe mechanism. Violations may result in monetary penalties. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti/reg.htm
- **Why a listings newsletter is squarely a CEM:** the CRTC FAQ defines the trigger as a CEM sent to an electronic address, and a CEM includes offers to purchase, sell, barter or lease land or an interest in land, and messages promoting a person as someone who does those things. A REALTOR®'s newsletter carrying latest listings and market updates, sent to subscribers' email addresses, sits squarely in that definition on the CRTC's own examples. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Express versus implied consent:** there are two types of consent under CASL — express and implied; express consent does not expire, however the recipient has the right to withdraw their consent at any time; express consent can be obtained in writing or orally, and in either case the onus is on the person sending the message to prove they obtained consent. A voluntary signup through this page is the classic express-consent route — and the sender, not the subscriber, carries the burden of proving it. Whether the current signup flow creates and keeps a consent record (who, when, how) is UNVERIFIED, because neither the form nor the provider could be seen. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Identification:** the second requirement is identification information in the message itself. The signup page carries Jared's name, brokerage, street address, phone and email on its face (§1) — whether each newsletter issue carries the required identification is UNVERIFIED, because no issue was available to the research. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Unsubscribe mechanics, in the CRTC's detail:** an unsubscribe mechanism must be included in CEMs; a clear and prominent hyperlink allowing the recipient to unsubscribe with a simple click is the CRTC's example; the mechanism must be "readily performed" — simple, quick and easy; the unsubscribe link must remain valid for at least 60 days after the CEM is sent; and an unsubscribe request must be processed without delay, and no later than 10 business days after receiving it. The page's own promise — "You can unsubscribe any time" — matches the direction of the law, but CASL compliance is about the mechanism in every issue, not the promise on the signup page. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Privacy — PIPEDA federally, and BC's own law provincially:** the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada states PIPEDA sets the ground rules for how private-sector organizations collect, use and disclose personal information in the course of for-profit, commercial activities across Canada; personal information includes any factual or subjective information, recorded or not, about an identifiable individual. Businesses subject to PIPEDA must follow its 10 fair information principles: 1. Accountability; 2. Identifying purposes; 3. Consent; 4. Limiting collection; 5. Limiting use, disclosure, and retention; 6. Accuracy; 7. Safeguards; 8. Openness; 9. Individual access; 10. Challenging compliance. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/?wbdisable=false
- **The BC wrinkle, from the OPC itself:** British Columbia (with Alberta and Quebec) has its own private-sector privacy law deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA, and organizations subject to a substantially similar provincial law are generally exempt from PIPEDA for collection, use or disclosure within that province — while businesses handling personal information that crosses provincial or national borders remain subject to PIPEDA regardless. A BC REALTOR®'s subscriber list therefore sits primarily under BC's provincial regime for in-province handling, with PIPEDA behind it for cross-border handling; this file states the framework only and does not give legal advice on which statute governs a specific list. Mapping the page's promises onto the principles: "we will not share your email address with others" speaks to Limiting use, disclosure, and retention (principle 5) and Consent (principle 3); the page's statement of what the newsletter contains speaks to Identifying purposes (principle 2). Whether actual list handling follows those principles is UNVERIFIED. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/?wbdisable=false
- **Property transfer tax — the base calculation for a purchase a newsletter item may prompt:** PTT is based on fair market value on registration day and is not annual property tax. General rates: 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion over $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion over $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000 where that applies. (The "Over $3M — 5%" shorthand sometimes shown for that top band is a compression of the 3% tier plus the further 2% — flagged here as a compression, not a separate tier.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **BC first-time home buyers' program (PTT exemption):** where all conditions are met, the purchaser is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price (maximum saving $8,000); full exemption requires fair market value $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), 0.5 hectares or smaller, only residential improvements, principal-residence use only, with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy to the first anniversary. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **BC newly built home exemption (PTT):** full-exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), phasing out over the $50,000 above it and completely eliminated at $1,150,000; a person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may be eligible for a refund of the tax paid — the documented vacant-land refund route. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **GST on vacant land — CRA level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt (CRA GI-003). Anything finer than GI-003 level is UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST/HST rebate — new HOUSING, not bare land:** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent March 13, 2026 (CRA News No. 122). The rebate is 100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, maximum $50,000, for agreements entered March 20, 2025–2031; buyers apply via Form GST190 (builder purchases) or GST191 (owner-built). It applies to newly constructed or substantially renovated HOUSING — a bare-land purchase itself attracts no FTHB rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST/HST new housing rebate:** 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, on homes $350,000–$450,000 (phase-out range), for a buyer (or related person) who uses the housing as a primary place of residence and is the first to live in it, where all requirements are met. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **FHSA:** $40,000 lifetime limit, $8,000 annual participation room. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html **Home Buyers' Plan:** withdraw up to $60,000 from an RRSP to buy or build a qualifying home, repaid within the published repayment period — with the 2026–2028 repayment-relief deferral as the program states it for the relief window. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Down payment and mortgage loan insurance (CMHC):** insurance is required under 20% down; minimum down payment 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 up to $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more, where insurance is unavailable; CMHC's published premium range is 0.6%–4.5% of the total mortgage amount. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Home owner grant (annual, not closing money):** it reduces annual property tax on a principal residence; the 2026 grant threshold is $2,075,000 for a full grant, and $0 over $2,189,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant

Farm class is three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
