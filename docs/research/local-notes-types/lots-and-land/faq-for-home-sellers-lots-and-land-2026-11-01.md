## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Selling without a licence — BCFSA:** a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves — for example, by selling or renting out their own property — while a person must not provide real estate services to or on behalf of another, for or in expectation of remuneration, unless licensed or exempt. That supports the page's "not legally required to use a REALTOR®" answer. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information
- **The regulator's consumer framework — BCFSA:** a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; the representation, duties and complaint-route disclosures must happen at the outset of dealings. The page states no commission rate or amount. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **PTT is the buyer's tax — Province:** PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office; the page's repeated statement that "property transfer tax is paid by the buyer, not the seller" matches that frame. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **PTT — worked example (inputs + tier math):** on a $700,000 purchase — 1% on the first $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% on $200,000–$700,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000** (a qualifying first-time buyer pays $4,000 after the first-$500,000 exemption — see the first-time-buyer topic files). Tiers: 1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above, plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000. (At $3.5M the total is $93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier — a townhomes sibling carries $78,000 for $3.5M; OPEN reconciliation item, flagged wherever met.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Cooling-off / deposits — BCFSA (carried from the sibling selling research, as this slug's research records it):** where a buyer rescinds under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining deposit balance to the buyer, with no separate release form; otherwise a deposit is held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder, and is not automatically returned if subjects are not removed. The live FAQ page does not itself raise the rescission period. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **Strata block (a seller of a bare-land strata lot):** Form B within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for 5+ lots since Jul 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF since Nov 1, 2023; rental bylaws VOID since Nov 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + May 1, 2023 exemptions). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level only (for a seller of land):** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land generally exempt. Finer points UNCONFIRMED. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST rebate:** new HOUSING, not bare land (Bill C-4, Royal Assent March 13, 2026, CRA governs; 100% to $1M phasing to $1.5M, max $50,000, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031) — relevant to a seller only as the buyer's frame on a new home, never as a land rebate. New Housing Rebate 36% / max $6,300 / $350K–$450K; HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I am selling — do I need a REALTOR® at all?** No — BCFSA's licensing guidance confirms a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves, such as selling their own property; a licence (or an exemption) is required to provide real estate services to or on behalf of another person for remuneration. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information
- **Q: Why isn't my home selling?** The page's answer: generally one or more of three reasons — it is overpriced relative to comparable recent sales and current competition, it is not marketed well enough, or it does not show well; the fix starts with a home evaluation against recent sales, active listings and expired or terminated listings. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: What are the costs of selling?** The page lists commission (split between the listing and buyer's agents), cleaning, staging and preparation, legal fees and disbursements, and a possible mortgage penalty — and states no commission figure, which BCFSA's framework places in the listing conversation. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Do I have to accept an offer?** The page's answer: no — you can accept it as-is, counter, or reject it; a counter voids the previous offer. Read your own listing contract's stated terms before relying on any general statement about when an offer must be accepted. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: Who pays the property transfer tax when I sell?** The buyer: PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office — when you buy your next home, you pay it as the buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: How much PTT would a buyer pay on a $700,000 purchase?** $12,000 before any exemption — 1% on the first $200,000 ($2,000) plus 2% on the next $500,000 ($10,000). (§3.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Should I price high and lower later?** The page's answer: no — the first two weeks on market are critical and a reduced listing reads as stale; price from sold evidence at launch. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: Will renovations increase my sale price dollar-for-dollar?** The page's position: renovations rarely return 100% of cost, so get a walkthrough opinion before spending. Its return ranges, attributed on the page to experience and the Appraisal Institute of Canada, are UNVERIFIED — no Institute source was opened in this pass. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: If I sell a vacant lot, does GST apply?** Generally yes if you are a developer/business or the lot is newly subdivided; generally no for an individual's personal-use sale, per CRA GI-003 at the general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: How long is a listing contract?** UNVERIFIED as the page states it: the page says Fraser Valley and Greater Vancouver listing contracts are typically 4–6 months with a claimed 60-day minimum under the named board's rules — no board rule page was opened in this pass, and the board name on the page is dated wording (§5). https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
