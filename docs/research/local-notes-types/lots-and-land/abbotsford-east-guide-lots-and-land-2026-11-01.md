A bare lot sold in one of those subdivisions is a fee-simple parcel: the buyer's own title, no strata corporation, no strata fee and no Form B — municipal zoning, the OCP and any registered covenants or building scheme on title govern instead. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/52433/ and (research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-1/abbotsford-east-guide.md, §5)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Zoning: Abbotsford Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014 governs.** The lot-relevant urban zones as the research states them: RS3 (minimum interior lot 540 m²), RS5 (420 m²) and RS6 compact-lot. The zone section for the specific lot — not the neighbourhood name — decides whether a subdivision, a suite or added units are possible at all. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/52433/
- **OCP: Abbotsforward 2050 is CURRENT.** Abbotsford's current Official Community Plan is Abbotsforward 2050, adopted December 2025. The research file's "Bylaw No. 2600-2016, as updated" citation and any live-page "2500-2016" citation are STALE / conflicted — apply 2050 and flag both numbers as a Jerry sweep item wherever a page carries them. The McKee Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 3400-2023) sits under it as Schedule E for the mountainside. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/making-progress-housing-abbotsford and https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2025bschedulea
- **SSMUH / added units on a lot:** in June 2024 Council adopted Zoning Bylaw amendments implementing provincial small-scale multi-unit housing — June 2024 adoption is City-confirmed; the SSMUH bylaw NUMBER is UNCONFIRMED ("3560-2024" is asserted in research files and must not be asserted — mark it UNCONFIRMED wherever met). Zoning Bylaw s.140 carries the small-scale rules and s.140.3.8 permits one secondary suite in all single detached dwellings, with City-report criteria including a lot of at least 400 m², at least 12.0 m frontage, a Collector or Local road and not on a cul-de-sac bulb. Abbotsford's suite cap is under 50% of the principal dwelling, and a coach/garden suite is capped at 15% of lot area to a maximum of 100 m² — Abbotsford's rules ONLY (standing corrections, commissioning brief, Nov 1, 2026). https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/making-progress-housing-abbotsford and https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400140
- **Subdivision and servicing requirements:** subdivision runs through the City's Approving Officer against the zone's minimum lot size/width/depth and the City's works-and-services (Development Bylaw) requirements, as City council reports apply them file by file — a servicing agreement and Works and Services Report are the standard instruments in those reports. The Development Bylaw's current number/year and its full servicing schedule were NOT confirmed on a City page read for this file (a 2021 draft and 2022 references both circulate): the detailed servicing requirements for a specific subdivision are UNCONFIRMED until the City's current Development Bylaw text and the file's Works and Services Report are read. Never quote a servicing standard for an east-side lot from a listing. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
- **ALR rules, where ALR ground exists:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval, stated as the ALC states it, and ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). ALC residence caps: one principal residence of 500 m² or less per parcel; a secondary suite only within the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. East-side application: parcel-level ALR status is UNCONFIRMED in the built neighbourhoods (Section 2) — confirm on the ALC map before pricing any parcel as subdividable. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form:** $2,500 of farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the farm operation for farm purposes in excess of 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha — Classification of Land as a Farm Regulation, B.C. Reg. 411/95, s.5, with applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR; no such flat line is asserted in this file, and none is documented in this area's research file. https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf
- **Floodplain and slope — the east side's two land-specific diligence items.** (1) The low land at the south and southeast edge toward Sumas Prairie is floodplain land by the Sumas River; City flood notices name properties south of the Sumas River and along Whatcom Road in evacuation order and alert areas, and the OCP includes a Drainage and Flood Control map (Map 12) — check the parcel in the City's WebMap before offering. (2) The OCP applies a Steep Slope Development Permit Area to slope land, and the McKee plan's open-space network includes geotechnical areas and slopes over 40% — budget for a geotechnical report on hillside lots. Clayburn Village is additionally protected through a Heritage Conservation Area in the OCP, which reaches exterior work on village lots. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/evacuation-orders-modified-additional-properties-sumas-prairie and https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2025bschedulea
- **Building scheme / covenants:** newer hillside subdivisions commonly carry registered building schemes or s.219 covenants (design, tree and geotechnical covenants appear in east-side City files); whether a specific lot carries one is a title question — UNCONFIRMED for any lot until its title and the scheme document are read. (City-report pattern: https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/52433/)
- **If the lot is bare-land strata (redirect case):** the BC strata block applies to the LOT — Form B diligence (strata fee on the lot, approved levies, CRF balance, parking/storage, insurance summary; the strata must provide it within 7 days, max $35 plus up to 25¢/page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions). No east-side bare-land strata is verified (Section 2) — this block governs the Downes Road Estates redirect and any strata-plan lot produced later. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) on land:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. The exemptions are home-based, and both have a land route: under the First-Time Home Buyers' Program a buyer who registers a vacant lot and builds can keep the exemption if the lot's fair market value at registration plus the construction cost is $835,000 or less (partial under $860,000), building and moving in by the first anniversary of registration; under the Newly Built Home Exemption, PTT paid on a vacant lot can be refunded once a qualifying newly built home is constructed on it, at the $1,100,000 full / $1,150,000 partial thresholds. Bare land bought to hold, with no qualifying home built, gets neither exemption. https://www.paxlaw.ca/blog/real-estate-law/bc-property-transfer-tax-exemptions-guide/ and https://wowa.ca/calculators/bc-property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — stated only at the level the CRA source supports:** CRA Info Sheet GI-003 (Sales of Vacant Land by Individuals) states that most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt — the sale of land kept for personal use is the CRA's example — while sales by an individual can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a parcel created by subdividing another parcel into more than two parts. Sales of newly subdivided lots by a developer or a business are the taxable side of that line as a general matter; the treatment of any specific Abbotsford East parcel — farmland sales in particular, which the CRA treats in a separate info sheet — is UNCONFIRMED until the vendor's status and the parcel's history are established. Get the GST position in the contract, not from the listing. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Speculation and vacancy tax:** the Province's taxable-areas list names the City of Abbotsford — owners of property classed as residential in Abbotsford must complete the annual speculation and vacancy tax declaration. Whether a specific vacant parcel is classed residential, and which exemption (if any) covers land under construction or held for development, is parcel-specific: UNCONFIRMED for any single lot until its assessment class and the current exemption list are read. Vancouver's Empty Homes Tax (3%) is a City of Vancouver tax ONLY and does not apply in Abbotsford. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- **Federal prohibition on purchase by non-Canadians — the land point most summaries miss:** the March 27, 2023 amendments to the Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act regulations repealed the provision that had made vacant land zoned residential or mixed-use inside a census metropolitan area or census agglomeration "residential property" — as amended, vacant land itself can be purchased by a non-Canadian, including for residential development, per the law-firm summaries of the amendments. The prohibition still governs dwellings; its application to any specific purchase is UNCONFIRMED at the official-text level in this file — a non-Canadian buyer needs advice on the current Act and regulations before offering. https://www.mross.com/what-we-think/article/update-canadian-government-prohibits-non-canadians-from-purchasing-residential-real-estate
- **First-home programs — the land boundary, stated precisely:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent MARCH 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) rebates GST on new HOUSING — 100% to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. It does NOT apply to bare land itself: a lot purchase attracts no FTHB GST rebate; the rebate question arises on the new home built on it, if the buyer and the home qualify. The older GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) is likewise a housing rebate. Home Buyers' Plan: up to $60,000 per buyer from an RRSP; First Home Savings Account: $8,000 per year, $40,000 lifetime — both are home-purchase programs, applied at the home purchase, not a land-holding subsidy. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property taxes / holding cost:** City of Abbotsford taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty September 16, 2026 — a land holder closing mid-year inherits whichever step has landed on an unpaid balance. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice
- **Market context, labelled as community-level only:** Abbotsford IS a SnapStats-covered community. September 2026 sub-area figures for Abbotsford East are detached inventory 165 / solds 15 (9% sales ratio) and attached inventory 69 / solds 4 (6%) — housing figures, published beside Abbotsford-wide medians (detached $949,000; attached $497,500). SnapStats has NO land category: these numbers describe the housing market a lot buyer is building into, never a land price. Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report (research file above).

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can I actually buy a bare building lot in Abbotsford East?** Yes — but as an individual fee-simple lot inside an existing subdivision or as an acreage parcel, not from a named bare-land subdivision: no named bare-land subdivision, bare-land strata or presale lot offering was verified for this sub-area on an official or developer source. The lot's zone (RS3/RS5/RS6) and its registered covenants decide what can be built. (Section 2 above.)
- **Q: Is a lot here serviced, or will I need a well and septic?** Inside the built subdivisions, lots are urban parcels — but the research file confirms no servicing statement for any individual lot, and it lists septic/well use in the urban east side as unverified. For an acreage parcel toward Whatcom Road or the Sumas Mountain foot, confirm water and sewer connection status with the City parcel by parcel before offering: UNCONFIRMED for any single lot until the City record is read. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-1/abbotsford-east-guide.md, UNVERIFIED item 7)
- **Q: How big are the lots, and can I split one?** Zoning minimums are RS3 540 m² and RS5 420 m² for interior lots, with smaller compact lots in RS6 subdivisions — those are the floors a NEW lot must meet, so a purchase only has subdivision room if the parcel is comfortably larger than twice its zone minimum and meets frontage, road and servicing requirements through the Approving Officer. Most built east-side lots sit at or near their minimum and cannot be split. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/52433/
- **Q: What extra units can go on a detached lot?** One secondary suite is permitted in all single detached dwellings (s.140.3.8), subject to criteria including a 400 m² minimum lot, 12.0 m frontage, a Collector or Local road and no cul-de-sac bulb; Abbotsford caps a suite at under 50% of the principal dwelling and a coach/garden suite at 15% of lot area to a maximum of 100 m². Small-scale multi-unit rules sit in Zoning Bylaw s.140 — the lot's zone section governs, and the SSMUH bylaw number cited in some material ("3560-2024") is UNCONFIRMED. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400140
- **Q: Is this lot in the ALR — and why does it matter?** Check the parcel on the ALC map: the research file does not confirm ALR status parcel by parcel inside the built neighbourhoods. If the parcel is in the ALR, subdivision needs ALC approval, residences cannot be stratified, and the residence caps (principal 500 m²; suite inside the principal only; additional residence 90 m² / 186 m² by the 40 ha line) replace the urban rules. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Q: What taxes do I pay on the lot itself?** PTT at the general rates on registration, with the first-time and newly-built exemptions reachable only through the build-and-occupy routes in Section 3. GST turns on the seller, not the lot: an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt per CRA GI-003, while a sale in the course of a business or of a parcel subdivided into more than two parts can be taxable — have the contract state the GST position. Abbotsford is a speculation and vacancy tax area for residential-class property (annual declaration). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- **Q: What will it cost to hold the land while I plan a build?** Property taxes (due July 2; 5% penalty July 3 and a second 5% September 16, 2026), plus insurance and any servicing or covenant obligations on title. There is no strata fee on a fee-simple lot; if the lot were bare-land strata there would be — which is one way to test a "bare-land strata" claim: no Form B and no strata fee means it is not strata. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice
- **Q: What are the hillside risks on a McKee or Eagle Mountain lot?** Slope and geotechnical conditions: the OCP's Steep Slope Development Permit Area applies to slope land, the McKee plan's open-space network includes geotechnical areas and slopes over 40%, and low lots toward Sumas Prairie carry flood exposure (City notices name properties south of the Sumas River and along Whatcom Road). Price the geotechnical report and the flood check into the offer timeline. https://letstalkabbotsford.ca/mckee-trail-strategy and https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/evacuation-orders-modified-additional-properties-sumas-prairie
- **Q: Which schools would a home on this lot feed into?** Catchments are address-based in SD34. Two locator runs are settled for the east side: 36367 Stephen Leacock Drive returns Auguston Traditional Elementary → Clayburn Middle → Robert Bateman Secondary, and 3836 Old Clayburn Road returns Sandy Hill Elementary → Clayburn Middle → Robert Bateman Secondary (SD34 School Locator, mybaragar.com, run 2026-11-01). Any other lot needs its own locator run — do not carry these two chains across the sub-area. https://www.abbyschools.ca/schools/catchments
- **Q: How long does a subdivision take if I buy a splittable parcel?** No official timeline is published in any source checked: UNCONFIRMED. What the City files show is the sequence — rezoning if the zone must change, Approving Officer review of the lot layout, a Works and Services Report and servicing agreement, and any s.219 covenants registered before final approval — so a buyer should price time and carrying cost on the sequence, not on a promised date. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
