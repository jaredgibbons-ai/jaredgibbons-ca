## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

### The settled court-ordered-sale treatment (BC Supreme Court)

- **Step 1 — the petition:** a proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property, or for redemption, must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7(1). All persons whose interest in or claim to the property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner may also join a claim arising out of the mortgage and a person liable to pay the mortgage debt — Rule 21-7(3). If the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation, a person who subsequently registers an interest need not be served, is bound by an order made in the proceeding, and may file a response — Rule 21-7(4). A petitioner who serves the petition must include the informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Step 2 — the court's powers and the redemption period:** the court may make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7(5)(a); order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period the court fixes, with foreclosure of the equity of redemption in default — Rule 21-7(5)(b); and determine summarily, or order an account be taken and certified, the amount due — Rule 21-7(5)(c). The Rule text states no fixed redemption-period length. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Step 3 — conduct of sale, approval and vesting (Rule 13-5 / PD-66 / Form 60):** under the settled treatment, a sale inside the proceeding is conducted under the court's directions (Rule 13-5): the court may give conduct of sale to a party, direct the particulars and conditions of sale and pricing directions, and a sale by contract is conditional on court approval; on approval the court confirms the sale, directs the proceeds and vests title in the purchaser, with the registrar's certificate in Form 60 evidencing the vesting. Competing offers are dealt with at the approval hearing under the court's directions and Practice Direction PD-66. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02

- **Surrey frame for the underlying parcel:** Surrey 2050 OCP Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted Jun 15, 2026 — never Bylaw 18020 as current); Zoning By-law No. 12000; Subdivision and Development Bylaw, 1986, No. 8830; SSMUH in force Jul 8, 2024 (3 units at 280 m² or less, 4 units larger, up to 6 near frequent transit, on eligible lots under 4,050 m²); suite cap 40% of the principal dwelling's habitable area, never a separate strata lot; streamside setbacks in Part 7A of Bylaw No. 12000, varying by stream class — no single riparian figure stated; STRs permitted owner-only, licence $358/yr with the City's 2024 $350/yr proposal disclosed as a conflict; Surrey property taxes due July 2, 5% penalty after July 2, further 5% after September 2. - **ALR block, in full (where ALR ground is in a file):** subdivision of ALR land requires ALC permission, apart from limited approving-officer cases. ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)). Principal residence 500 m² or less (fill ≤1,000 m²); suite only inside the principal residence; one additional residence at 90 m² or less (≤40 ha) or 186 m² or less (>40 ha), where local bylaws also permit it. **Farm class — three-tier, in full:** $2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. (§3 above)
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rule text — the court fixes the period in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b). Until that period expires (or the court orders otherwise), the respondent can pay what is due and end the proceeding. (§3 above)
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer at the approval hearing?** Plan for the possibility: under the settled treatment, a sale by contract is conditional on court approval and competing offers are dealt with at the approval hearing under the court's directions and PD-66. This slug's research file marks those mechanics UNVERIFIED program-wide — either way, get the specific file's bid instructions in writing from the seller's counsel before spending money. (§3 above)
- **Q: Which court registry handles a South Surrey foreclosure?** UNVERIFIED. Surrey has no Supreme Court registry; New Westminster is carried as a working assumption only in the townhomes sibling, never as a verified mapping. The court file itself states its registry — check the specific file on Court Services Online. (§3 above)
- **Q: Can I buy vacant land through this process?** In principle yes — any mortgaged property can be the subject of a Rule 21-7 proceeding, with no property-type limit — but this file counts no land inventory; whether a specific lot is in a proceeding is a per-file Court Services Online check. (§2 above)
- **Q: Do I get a Form B if the lot is a bare-land strata lot?** UNCONFIRMED — Form B availability in a court-ordered sale is not established by any source read for this program. Ordinary-resale Form B practice (SPA s.59) cannot be assumed; confirm per file with the strata and your lawyer before bidding. (§3 above)
- **Q: Is the property sold "as is"?** UNVERIFIED as a standard rule — one feed card states "SOLD AS IS, WHERE IS" in its own listing text, but no official source read states a standard conditions form. Expect the conditions in the court-approved form for that specific file, confirmed with a lawyer. (§3 above)
- **Q: Do I pay PTT and GST differently because it is a court sale?** UNCONFIRMED — the tax treatment of a court-ordered purchase is left open in this file by design, and it is not answered by importing the general CRA GI-003 vacant-land frame. The general PTT tiers are stated in §3 for scale; cost the specific purchase with a lawyer before setting your ceiling. (§3 above)
- **Q: Are court-ordered properties cheaper than market?** No official source publishes South Surrey foreclosure counts, discounts or pricing statistics, and SnapStats has no foreclosure category — any "typical discount" figure is UNVERIFIED. The general market sold at 102% (detached) and 99% ("Condos & Townhomes") sale-to-list in September 2026 — all-property figures, not foreclosure figures. (§1 above)
- **Q: Can the owner still save the land after I offer?** The redemption structure is the risk: the court may order payment of what is due within the court-fixed redemption period, with foreclosure in default — Rule 21-7(5)(b) — and may also make a final order of foreclosure under Rule 21-7(5)(a). Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections. (§3 above)
