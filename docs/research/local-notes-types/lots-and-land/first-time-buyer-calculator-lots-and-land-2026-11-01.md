## 2. THE LAND ITSELF

**This topic file carries no land roster of its own — stated plainly at the top. There are no named subdivisions, bare-land developments, bare-land strata plans, assemblies, development sites or presale lot offerings to document for a province-wide calculator slug, and none is invented. What §2 documents instead is how land itself prices through the tool the page offers: the three purchase shapes a first-time buyer can run, and which of them the exemptions and rebates actually reach.**

### The three purchase shapes the tool must price (from the research)

- **Resale homes:** PTT with the first-time home buyers' program exemption where it applies — the general tiers run first, then the exemption reduces the bill (figures in §3). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Newly built homes:** PTT with the newly built home exemption, and GST with the federal rebates where they apply. The thresholds shape the stock: the first-time program's full exemption runs to a fair market value of $835,000, while the newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 — a first-time buyer of a newly built home can be inside one program's range and outside the other's, so the tool must test both. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Vacant land bought to build on:** both BC exemption programs carry a refund route — a person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may be eligible for a refund of the tax paid under the newly built home exemption, and the first-time program is tested on the same principal-residence conditions. The land purchase itself is the taxable event the tool must not skip: PTT is based on the fair market value of the property (land and improvements) on registration day. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Property size is part of the profile:** both BC exemptions set 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) as the size line, with a partial exemption possible above it — acreage and large-lot first purchases price differently in the tool than strata or small-lot purchases. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Bare-land strata / fee-simple distinction, for land run through the tool:** a bare-land strata lot IS strata tenure (Form B / strata fees / bylaws apply — §3 strata block), while a fee-simple lot carries no strata layer; the calculator's tax figures do not change with that distinction, but its closing-cost picture does, and no specific lot of either form is documented in this topic file.
- **Presale LOT offerings, counted separately: none documented** — a topic file documents no offerings; presale lot offerings, where they exist, are documented in their home area files only (document-once).

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Property transfer tax — the base calculation:** when a person purchases or gains an interest in property registered at the Land Title Office, the person or their legal professional must file a PTT return and pay PTT unless an exemption applies; PTT is based on fair market value on registration day and is not annual property tax. General rates: 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion over $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion over $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000 where that applies (on a mixed-class property, only on the residential portion). Additional PTT for foreign nationals, foreign corporations and taxable trustees applies in specified areas; its rate was not re-read for the research file and is UNVERIFIED here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **BC first-time home buyers' program (PTT exemption):** the buyer must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident; must have lived in BC for at least a year immediately before registration, or filed at least 2 income tax returns as a BC resident in the last 6 taxation years; must never have owned a registered interest in a property that was the buyer's principal residence anywhere in the world; and must never have received the exemption or refund before. Foreign entities and taxable trustees are not eligible, and where only some purchasers qualify, only their percentage of interest is eligible. Full exemption conditions: principal-residence use only, fair market value $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), 0.5 hectares or smaller, only residential improvements — the purchaser is then exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price (maximum saving $8,000). Partial exemption: fair market value over $835,000 and under $860,000, and/or larger than 0.5 hectares, and/or another building besides the principal residence. Keeping it: move in within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy to the first anniversary (part may be kept if the buyer moves out early); the legal professional claims it in the return, or a refund may be applied for from the first anniversary to 18 months after registration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **BC newly built home exemption (PTT):** full-exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), phasing out over the $50,000 above it and completely eliminated at $1,150,000. A newly built home includes a house constructed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since construction, a unit in a newly built condo building not occupied since construction, a manufactured home placed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since placement, and a non-residential-to-residential conversion not occupied as a residence since the conversion began. The buyer must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, the property in BC, principal-residence use only, 0.5 hectares or smaller, with the same 92-day / first-anniversary occupancy rules; a person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may be eligible for a refund of the tax paid. This is a new-HOME exemption — bare-land eligibility beyond that documented refund route is UNCONFIRMED. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **GST on vacant land — CRA level only:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt (CRA GI-003). Anything finer than GI-003 level is UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **FTHB GST/HST rebate — new HOUSING, not bare land (governed figures in full):** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** (CRA News No. 122). The rebate is **100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, maximum $50,000**, for agreements entered **March 20, 2025–2031**; the application window is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction, a builder can credit the rebate at transfer, and buyers otherwise apply via Form GST190 (builder purchases) or GST191 (owner-built). It applies to newly constructed or substantially renovated HOUSING — a bare-land purchase itself attracts no FTHB rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST/HST new housing rebate (governed figures):** **36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, on homes $350,000–$450,000** (phase-out range), for a buyer (or related person) who uses the housing as a primary place of residence and is the first to live in it, where all requirements are met. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **The $1.25M phase-out row — UNVERIFIED as a mechanism:** where this file's research touches the rebates' phase-outs, it records the thresholds as not re-read on the detailed CRA pages (research UNVERIFIED item 7). Stated for the live gst-rebate page as the program governs it: a **$1.25M straight-line phase-out row is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism** — CRA's published wording for the FTHB rebate is only that the rebate is "gradually reduced" across its phase range; no straight-line $1.25M mechanism is asserted in this file.
- **FHSA (governed figures in full):** a registered plan to save to buy or build a qualifying first home tax-free — **$40,000 lifetime limit, $8,000 annual participation room** (first-year room $8,000 in the year the account is opened); contributions are generally deductible, transfers from RRSPs to FHSAs are not deductible, and a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and a Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal can both be made for the same qualifying home if all conditions are met at each withdrawal. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Home Buyers' Plan (governed figures in full):** withdraw **up to $60,000** from an RRSP to buy or build a qualifying home, repaid to the plan within the published repayment period — with the **2026–2028 repayment-relief deferral** as the program states it for the relief window. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Down payment and mortgage loan insurance (CMHC):** mortgage loan insurance is required when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the purchase price; it protects the lender, not the buyer, and allows qualification with as little as 5% down. Minimum down payment: homes $500,000 or less — 5%; homes $500,000–$1,499,999 — 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more — 20%, and insurance is not available. **CMHC's published premium range is 0.6%–4.5% of the total mortgage amount**, based on down payment and loan amount, payable in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. (The mortgage-calculator page's premium-range error is a different slug's defect and is NOT asserted here — see FILE STATUS.) https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Home owner grant (annual, not a purchase tax):** it reduces annual property tax on a principal residence and must not be netted against purchase taxes in a calculator. As published (page last updated September 22, 2026): effective January 1, 2027 the regular grant amount is $570; the 2026 grant threshold is $2,075,000 for a full grant, reduced by $5 per $1,000 over that, and $0 over $2,189,000; the claimant must be the registered owner, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, living in BC and occupying the residence as a principal residence, must pay at least $350 in property taxes before the regular grant, and must apply each year (one grant per property per year). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **Strata block (for a first purchase that is a strata lot, including a bare-land strata lot):** Form B diligence (provided within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022 (short-term restrictions may still apply by bylaw); only 55+ age bylaws valid under SPA ss.123.1/123.2, with May 1, 2023 exemptions; a listing's "45+" line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **ALR rules (for a first purchase on ALR land):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)); ALC residence caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tiered ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha; applications by Oct 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Taxes deliberately out of this tool's scope:** BC's speculation and vacancy tax (annual, on use in designated areas) and the BC home flipping tax (on income from a short-held residential sale) are not purchase-time costs a first-time-buyer calculator computes, and the page title names only PTT, a GST rebate and the FHSA — neither tax is encoded in the research for this tool, and whether the live tool mentions them is UNVERIFIED. The first-home buyer's tax credit is described in words only in the research; its amount was not re-read and is UNVERIFIED as a figure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home
- **Purchase prohibition / zoning / subdivision:** no single municipality's zoning, OCP or subdivision bylaw governs a province-wide tool — those rules live in the buyer's area file (for example New Westminster Subdivision and Development Control Bylaw No. 7142, 2007; Vancouver Subdivision By-law No. 5208 with fees under No. 14538; Coquitlam No. 3558, 2003; Township of Langley No. 5382 (2019); Richmond Nos. 8751/8752 — each cited in its home area files, not conformed here). The federal non-Canadian purchase prohibition is stated in this file ONLY as its own research supports it: the research encodes no prohibition position for the tool, so none is asserted. Vancouver's Empty Homes Tax (3%) is Vancouver only and is not a calculator figure for a BC-wide tool.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much PTT does a first-time buyer in BC actually save?** A qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price, provided fair market value is $835,000 or less, the property is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the buyer's principal residence; over $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: What are the general PTT rates the calculator starts from?** 1% to $200,000, 2% on the portion over $200,000 to $2,000,000, and 3% over $2,000,000 — plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000 where that applies. Run the tiers on the full price first, then subtract the exemption actually qualified for. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: I am buying vacant land to build on — do the exemptions help?** At the land purchase, PTT is payable on the land's fair market value; the newly built home exemption then carries a refund route for a person who paid PTT on vacant land and has a newly built home on it, on the documented conditions. GST on the land itself follows CRA GI-003 (developer/subdivided lots generally taxable; an individual's personal-use sale generally exempt), and the FTHB GST rebate reaches the new HOUSING, not the bare land. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: Is a newly built home treated differently from a resale home?** Yes — the newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 with phase-out to $1,150,000, and a new home may also attract GST, which the FTHB GST/HST rebate (100% to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, maximum $50,000; Bill C-4 assent March 13, 2026) can offset for eligible buyers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: How much can I put in an FHSA?** $8,000 of participation room in the first year the account is opened, to a $40,000 lifetime limit; contributions are generally tax deductible. Open it early — room starts when the account is opened, not at offer time. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?** Yes — CRA states both can be used for the same qualifying home if all conditions are met at each withdrawal; the HBP limit is $60,000. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Q: How much down payment do I actually need?** The floor is 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any portion above $500,000, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more, where insurance is unavailable. Under 20% down, CMHC insurance is required and its premium (published range 0.6%–4.5% of the mortgage amount) is usually added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: Does the calculator's GST rebate phase out in a straight line from $1.25M?** That mechanism is UNVERIFIED — CRA's published wording is only that the FTHB rebate is "gradually reduced" across its phase range (100% to $1M, phasing to $1.5M); no $1.25M straight-line row is asserted in this file. (Section 3.)
- **Q: Do I keep the first-time exemption if I move out early?** Protect it: move in within 92 days of registration and stay as your principal residence to the first anniversary, or part of the exemption is repayable (part may be kept on an early move-out). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Is the home owner grant closing money?** No — it is an annual reduction of property tax on a principal residence ($570 regular grant as published, effective January 1, 2027), claimed each year after you own and occupy; a calculator should not net it against purchase taxes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The two BC exemptions peak at different prices:** the first-time program's full exemption runs to $835,000 while the newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 — a first-time buyer of a new build can be inside one program and outside the other, which is the single most valuable test the tool runs. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **The half-hectare line is a tax line:** both BC exemptions set 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) as the size line, with a partial exemption possible above it — a large-lot first purchase is not priced like a strata purchase even at the same price. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **The builder can credit the GST rebate at closing:** the FTHB GST/HST rebate can be credited by the builder when ownership transfers; where the builder does not credit it, the buyer applies directly (Form GST190 / GST191), usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction — ask before signing, not after. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
