## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **OCP: Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 21900 — adopted June 15, 2026 — current.** Bylaw 18020 is the replaced predecessor only and is NEVER cited as current. Zoning is under Surrey Zoning Bylaw No. 12000. The live page's guide claims an RM-M (Manufactured Home Residential) zone under Bylaw 12000 and the absence of a manufactured-home-park zone in White Rock's Zoning Bylaw 2506 — neither bylaw text was read for these points in the research pass, so both claims are UNVERIFIED as stated. https://www.surrey.ca/planning-development/plans-strategies/surrey-official-community-plan and (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-12/south-surrey-mobile-homes.md)
- **Subdivision / servicing (Surrey):** the Surrey Subdivision and Development Bylaw, 1986, No. 8830 is the City's subdivision/servicing bylaw (verified on the City's own pages in an earlier batch of this program), and the City's process runs through its Engineering Land Development / Servicing Agreement process — read a parcel's servicing position off the City's process, never off another city's bylaw. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-development-application-process/servicing-agreement
- **SSMUH / suites / short-term rentals (Surrey lots):** Surrey's SSMUH bands are 3 units on lots under 280 m², 4 units on larger lots, and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop. A secondary suite is capped at 40% of the principal dwelling's habitable floor area. Short-term rentals are permitted only in the operator's own principal residence, owner-only, with a City business licence at $358/year as stated on the City's licence page — DISCLOSED CONFLICT: a City of Surrey 2024 corporate report proposed $350/year; the licence page's $358 figure is carried with the conflict disclosed. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing and https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/permits-licences/secondary-suites and https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/permits-licences/short-term-rentals
- **MHPTA frame (pad-rental product — the frame that replaces land law for a park home):** manufactured home site tenancies are covered by the MHPTA and landlords and tenants must have a written tenancy agreement for the site. On a sale where the home stays in the park, assignment under MHPTA s.28 requires the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or authorization in the tenancy agreement; before asking, the seller must give the proposed buyer written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent; and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Pad-rent increases — Act percentages ONLY; NO pad-rent dollar figure is published as fact in this file:** a park landlord may increase rent only with 3 full months' notice, only once every 12 months, and only up to the yearly limit — 2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027. The proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park; further increases require a Residential Tenancy Branch application. Any pad-rent range or fee figure on the live page (including a feed card's monthly-fee line) is UNVERIFIED site/listing copy, flagged as such and never republished as a figure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s.42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute within 15 days, may in defined circumstances apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Registration and certification (every manufactured-home sale, every tenure in this topic):** the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered with the Manufactured Home Registry, through a lawyer or notary filing the original signed and witnessed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership forms. Manufactured homes include modular and mobile homes; the CSA label states the standard — modular homes under A-277, mobile homes under Z-240. A home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal; a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification. A permit to move a home is issued by the Registry, a tax certificate for transport purposes is required (with exceptions including a move to a different pad within the same park), and the transport permit expires 30 days after issue. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Bare-land strata block (for a Sea Ridge Bays-type lot, wherever one trades):** Form B (SPA s.59) within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page, with rules, budget and most recent depreciation report attached; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since Jul 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since Nov 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since Nov 24, 2022 (short-term may still be restricted); only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + May 1, 2023 exemptions) — a "45+" listing line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw. A pad-rental park home has NO Form B, no CRF and no depreciation report — the tenure decides which document set exists. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **ALR block (applies parcel by parcel, only where the parcel is in the reserve):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)); caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha). Farm class is three-tier — $2,500 on 0.8–4 ha / $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha / $10,000 under 0.8 ha, applications by Oct 31 — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR, and no farm-class claim is asserted for any parcel in this topic. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **PTT on land:** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. First-time buyers: exempt on the first $500,000 at $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 — a new HOME, not bare land. **Worked example, inputs + tier math stated:** a $500,000 parcel, fully residential, no exemption = $2,000 (1% × $200,000) + 2% × $300,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level only:** most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (e.g. land kept for personal use); taxable cases include land used primarily in a business, land sold in the course of a business, and a parcel created by subdividing another parcel into more than two parts — developer/newly subdivided lots generally TAXABLE. Anything finer for a specific parcel: UNCONFIRMED — CPA per transaction. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Rebates attach to housing, not bare land:** FTHB GST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA; 100% of GST to $1M, max $50,000, phasing to $1.5M; agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031) — a qualifying new HOME, not raw land; New Housing Rebate 36% / max $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000 underneath. HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Surrey property taxes:** due July 2, with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2. A manufactured home owner in a park is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value does not include the value of the land where the home is in a park not on reserve land or on land owned by someone else. **Foreclosure note:** Surrey has NO Supreme Court registry — a Surrey foreclosure runs through a registry outside the city. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and (~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/king-george-corridor-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Do I own the land when I buy a manufactured home in South Surrey?** It depends on the community, and this page's feed holds both forms: in a manufactured home park you generally own the home and rent the site under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement governed by the MHPTA, while in own-land communities you buy the land with the home — the Cranley Drive and 20 Avenue cards in this feed state the buyer owns the land, and the 160 Street cards are park-pad product. https://jaredgibbons.ca/south-surrey-mobile-homes.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Q: Is a pad-rental home lot stock I can treat like a vacant lot?** No — a rented pad is a tenancy, not a lot purchase: no title to land transfers, there is no Form B, no CRF and no depreciation report, and the home's assessed value excludes the land. The land questions for a park home are the park rules, the site tenancy agreement and the MHPTA assignment in §3. (§2, §3 above)
- **Q: What is Sea Ridge Bays, in land terms?** A bare-land strata: you own the home AND the strata lot at 15875 20 Avenue, pay a strata fee instead of pad rent (listing-stated at $210/month including water), and take a Form B — strata tenure on the lot itself, not fee-simple and not pad-rental. Its full record is in the 55-plus sibling; it is cross-referenced here, never re-counted. (§2 above)
- **Q: Is Breakaway Bays a 55+ park?** NOT verified 55+ — the directory says age-restricted (55+), the MLS listings say "NO AGE RESTRICTION" / "Family Park," and the siblings follow the MLS basis with the conflict flagged. Read the written park rules for the specific home before relying on any age claim. (§2 above)
- **Q: Does the park have to approve me, and can it charge for that?** Yes to approval, no to a fee: assignment of the site tenancy requires the landlord's prior written consent under MHPTA s.28 (or consent deemed given under the regulations), the seller must first give you the written park rules and the relevant tenancy agreement terms, and the landlord cannot charge a fee for giving consent. (§3 above)
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up?** Up to the yearly limit — 2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027 — once every 12 months, on 3 full months' notice. On assignment to a new owner the rent and the next increase date carry over unchanged. No pad-rent dollar figure is published as fact in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: What happens if a South Surrey park closes or is converted?** Under MHPTA s.42, as the Province states it: the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, then give 12 months' notice and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the effective date; tenants may leave early on 10 days' written notice, may dispute within 15 days, and may in defined circumstances claim additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000; if the landlord does not proceed within a reasonable period, a further payment of the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent applies. (§3 above)
- **Q: What tax do I pay on a land purchase here?** PTT on the general tiers — on the §3 worked example ($500,000, illustration only) the bill is $8,000 with no exemption. GST depends on the seller and the lot's history at the CRA GI-003 level — confirm with a CPA per transaction. A manufactured-home purchase itself closes through the Manufactured Home Registry, not a land title transfer, where no land is bought. (§3 above)
- **Q: Are the market numbers in this file manufactured-home prices?** No. SnapStats publishes no manufactured-home category; the §1 figures are S Surrey White Rock residential context only (detached median $1,628,000 and Condos & Townhomes median $700,000 in September 2026), and manufactured-home-specific statistics, including any South Surrey pad-rent figure, are UNVERIFIED. (SnapStats FVR September 2026 report)
- **Q: Can I rely on the live page's park claims — the operator, the 50+ covenant, the RM-M zone?** Not yet — the research could not confirm any of them on a City, developer, strata or park-operator source, so each is UNVERIFIED in this file. Pull the park rules, the tenancy agreement and the title documents for the specific home before writing. (§1, §2 above)
