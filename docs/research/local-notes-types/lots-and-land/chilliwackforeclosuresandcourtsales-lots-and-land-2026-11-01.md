In BC a "court-ordered sale" is a sale inside a foreclosure proceeding, not a separate process; "bank foreclosure" is a misnomer — sales run through the court, not the bank. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Step 1 — petition (verified in this slug's own research):** "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7(1). All persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished and all persons against whom any relief is sought must be made respondents — Rule 21-7(2); a petitioner may also join a claim arising out of the mortgage or collateral security and a person liable to pay the mortgage debt — Rule 21-7(3). If the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation, a person who subsequently registers or files an interest need not be served, is bound by an order made in the proceeding, and may file a response to petition — Rule 21-7(4). A petitioner who serves the petition must include with it an informational notice for foreclosure proceedings in Form 125 — Rule 21-7(4.1). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Order / redemption (verified in this slug's own research):** the court may make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7(5)(a); order that a respondent must, within a redemption period that the court may fix, pay to the petitioner what is due under the mortgage and for costs, and that in default of payment the respondent is to be foreclosed of the respondent's equity of redemption — Rule 21-7(5)(b); and determine summarily, or order that an account be taken of and that a registrar certify, the amount due — Rule 21-7(5)(c). The redemption period has no fixed length in the Rule text read: it is the period the court fixes in its order. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Later subrules / confirming a sale (settled treatment; this slug's research held these UNVERIFIED in its own pass — see FILE STATUS):** everything in Rule 21-7 after subrule (5)(c) — including the final-order application, an application for an order that the property be sold, and an application to confirm a sale, direct the proceeds and vest title in the purchaser — is carried here on the program's settled treatment only: even before redemption expires, the person with conduct of sale may apply for an order confirming the sale, directing proceeds and vesting title. This slug's research does not adopt those subrules as its own verification. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7(1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rule text read — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7(5)(b). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What can the court order at the first stage of a Chilliwack foreclosure?** Under Rule 21-7(5) the court may make a final order of foreclosure, order payment of what is due within a court-fixed redemption period with foreclosure in default, or determine or have certified the amount due. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Is my accepted offer final?** On the settled treatment, no — conduct of sale applies for confirmation, proceeds direction and vesting, and until that order the sale is not final. Note the basis: this slug's research held the confirming subrules UNVERIFIED in its own pass (FILE STATUS), so confirm the stage and the approval step for the specific file with your lawyer. (§3.)
- **Q: Can someone outbid me at the approval hearing?** UNVERIFIED as a stated process rule in this slug's research — the bid-rights wording in Rule 13-5 and the PD-66 practice direction could not be opened in its pass. Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for, and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing before spending on inspections. (§3.)
- **Q: Where is a Chilliwack foreclosure file heard?** UNCONFIRMED — no BC Courts page was opened in this slug's research, and the live page's Chilliwack Law Courts (46085 Yale Road) / New Westminster / Vancouver claim is unverified site copy. Check the registry shown on the individual court file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwackforeclosuresandcourtsales.html
- **Q: Will I get a Form B on a bare-land strata lot in foreclosure?** UNCONFIRMED — no source in the research states a general rule; what is produced is a per-file fact. (§3.)
- **Q: Do I pay PTT/GST the normal way on court-ordered land?** UNCONFIRMED — court-sale tax treatment is not stated in any source in the research, which states no PTT/GST position at all; the general PTT and GI-003 frames in §3 are starting frames only, confirmed by your lawyer per transaction.
- **Q: Are foreclosures discounted in Chilliwack?** No foreclosure discount statistic exists in any official source — UNVERIFIED. Chilliwack is a CADREB market that SnapStats does not cover and CADREB publishes board-wide only, so there is no published "foreclosure discount" for Chilliwack in this file. https://www.cadreb.com/market/
- **Q: As a seller in difficulty, what is my window?** The court-fixed redemption period under Rule 21-7(5)(b) is the window to pay what is due before the equity of redemption is foreclosed — earlier advice is worth more than late advice. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
