- **Fee-simple lots — the default form:** land parcels are overwhelmingly freehold in the feed's framing. - **Bare-land strata — its own form, stated plainly:** bare-land strata lots appear in newer subdivisions — the research records that a card shown in the feed is a unit-numbered land listing on Chilliwack Mountain — but no Chilliwack-wide freehold/strata split is published on a source opened in the pass: UNVERIFIED, and no bare-land strata plan is named. Where a lot IS a bare-land strata lot, it is strata tenure on the lot: the buyer owns the lot as a strata lot, pays strata fees on the lot for the common property, and is bound by the strata's bylaws on the lot (what may be built, when, and to what standard is set by the registered bylaws and the Form B package, SPA s. 59). The Strata Property Act defines a "bare land strata plan" as a strata plan on which the boundaries of the strata lots are defined on a horizontal plane by reference to survey markers, not by reference to the floors, walls or ceilings of a building (condos sibling, citing the Act). ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **Zoning and OCP:** Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000 applies city-wide (s. 6 carries the farm zones), under the 2050 Official Community Plan, Official Community Plan Bylaw 2025, No. 5500 — including Schedule B1 Agricultural Area Plan, Schedule B4 Eastern Hillsides and Schedule B5 Sardis. Zone minimum lot areas and widths are UNVERIFIED in this slug's research (see §2) — confirm the zone figures for a parcel from the consolidated bylaw or the City directly. https://chilliwack.com/ocp
- **SSMUH / subdivision potential as the City states it:** Chilliwack's small-scale multi-unit housing amendment is Zoning Bylaw Amendment Bylaw 2024, No. 5395, which amended Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000 and was adopted June 18, 2024 — the date is recorded in s. 14's amendment record in the consolidated Zoning Bylaw 5000, whose entry for Bylaw 5395 reads "Text Amendments – Small-scale, multi-unit housing and maximum building height." The City's signed staff report for Bylaw 5395 describes it as allowing 3 to 4 dwelling units on parcels within the urban growth boundary zoned for single-detached and duplex housing. Eligibility is parcel-specific (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/rules-gaps-2026-11-01.md, item 6). Suite/ADU figures confirmed in the same verification (item 7f, citing Zoning Bylaw 5000, ss. 2, 4.06, 5.04): a secondary suite is capped at the lesser of 90 m² and 40% of the principal dwelling, one accessory dwelling unit is permitted per lot, garden suites are capped at 100 m², and parking rules sit at s. 5.04.
- **Subdivision and servicing:** the subdivision process and the Approving Officer position are UNVERIFIED in this slug's research — no City subdivision page was opened (429) — so the process is documented here only as a per-parcel confirmation with the City, with conditions and timeline confirmed for the specific parcel. **The Chilliwack servicing-bylaw number is UNCONFIRMED** — no number is asserted, and no other city's number is borrowed. The one subdivision fact confirmed on a source opened in the pass is the ALR one below: an ALC application may be required for a subdivision or boundary adjustment of ALR land. Development cost charges, frontage/off-site works and servicing requirements: UNVERIFIED in the pass. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- **ALR block — full form:** properties in the Agricultural Land Reserve are subject to the Agricultural Land Commission Act and ALR regulations; ALC approval may be required before the City can approve related development applications, and the City directs owners to check ALR status on the City Webmap; an ALC application may be required for a new house, coach house or suite, for placing fill or removing soil, for non-farm uses, and for subdivision or a boundary adjustment, with ALC applications referred to the City for review. Subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval, and ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s. 6; Strata Property Act, s. 239; Agricultural Land Commission Act, s. 21(1)). Residence caps: principal residence ≤500 m² total floor area; a secondary suite only inside the principal residence; one additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. Farm Home Plate: OCP Development Permit Area 4 exists to reduce the long-term impact of residential uses on agricultural land, and a Development Permit is required when the Zoning Bylaw's Farm Home Plate regulations are not met. The Agricultural Area Plan was created as part of the OCP in 2012 in partnership with the Chilliwack Agricultural Commission, and the Agricultural and Rural Advisory Committee (ARAC) monitors its implementation. Farm class (BC Assessment) in full three-tier form: $2,500 of gross farm income on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha above that; $10,000 under 0.8 ha — applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR and is not used here. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009 and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **BC strata block — applies in full to any bare-land strata lot:** Form B (SPA s. 59), provided within 7 days, max $35 plus up to 25¢/page, stating the lot's monthly strata fee, amounts owed, approved special levies, the CRF balance and a summary of the strata's insurance, with the rules, current budget, any Rental Disclosure Statement and the most recent depreciation report attached; depreciation reports every five years for stratas with five or more lots since July 1, 2024 (for a bare-land strata the report covers the common property — roads, systems, amenities — not the houses on the lots); minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss. 123.1/123.2 + May 1, 2023 exemptions); a "45+" listing line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw. Insurance difference: in a bare-land strata the house sits entirely inside the strata lot and is not shown as a building on the plan, so the lot owner insures the house; the strata insures common property only (condos sibling). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing
- **Property transfer tax:** the Province's general rates are 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion above $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% on the portion above $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion of value above $3,000,000. First-time-buyer and newly-built exemptions are HOME exemptions — a bare lot's eligibility is UNCONFIRMED here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much of Chilliwack is agricultural land?** The City states agriculture accounts for 67% of the City's total land base and 29% of its economic activity, and its Official Community Plan carries an Agricultural Area Plan for that land. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- **Q: How do I check if a Chilliwack lot is in the ALR?** The City directs owners to the City Webmap to check ALR status; ALR properties are subject to the Agricultural Land Commission Act and regulations, and an ALC application may be required for a new house, fill or soil removal, a non-farm use, or a subdivision or boundary adjustment. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- **Q: Can a serviced Chilliwack lot carry more than one home?** The City's SSMUH amendment, Zoning Bylaw Amendment Bylaw 2024, No. 5395 (adopted June 18, 2024), allows 3 to 4 dwelling units on parcels within the urban growth boundary zoned for single-detached and duplex housing, per the City's staff-report description — eligibility is parcel-specific and is confirmed with the City. (~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/rules-gaps-2026-11-01.md, item 6)
- **Q: What is a bare-land strata lot — and is it still "land"?** Yes, and it is strata tenure: the lot is a strata lot defined by survey markers, not by a building; you pay strata fees on the lot for the common property and the registered bylaws govern what may be built on it, and a resale purchase runs on a Form B. A fee-simple lot has none of that. You cannot tell by looking — the words "bare land strata" appear on the first page of the strata plan. (§2/§3 above)
- **Q: What permit areas should I check on a hillside lot?** The OCP's Schedule D lists D-2 Hillside Environment and D-5 Hillside Development among its development permit areas, along with D-1 Drinking Water Supply and D-3 Riparian Area; which areas apply to a specific lot is a per-parcel check with the City.
