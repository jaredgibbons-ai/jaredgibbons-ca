## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT LOTS AND LAND

- **The regulator frame that governs any listing taken under this plan (BCFSA):** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority; a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings; dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. The page itself states no fee or commission figure — so any fee discussion for a seller, landowner included, happens off-page. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://jaredgibbons.ca/marketing-plan.html
- **Listing-data notice the site carries:** the standard footer states the data relating to real estate on the website comes in part from the MLS® Reciprocity program of either the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® (GVR), the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board (FVREB) or the Chilliwack and District Real Estate Board (CADREB); listings held by participating firms are marked with the MLS® logo and include the listing agent's name; the representation is based on board-generated data the boards do not warrant for accuracy; and the materials may not be reproduced without the boards' express written consent. CREA owns the MLS® and REALTOR® trademarks, and agents must be members of a local board and CREA to access the MLS® system. https://jaredgibbons.ca/marketing-plan.html and https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Surrey frame, where the page's own geography is Surrey (South Surrey sellers):** the governing plan is the Surrey 2050 OCP, Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted Jun 15, 2026) — Bylaw 18020 is the replaced predecessor, never cited as current — and zoning is Surrey Zoning By-law, 1993, No. 12000, parcel by parcel in COSMOS. For a landowner-seller, the marketing conversation starts from the same documents a land buyer checks: the parcel's designation, zone and servicing position. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/r133-plansurrey-2013-official-community-plan-surrey-2050-official-community-plan-alignment-related-amendments and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_Zoning_12000.pdf
- **SSMUH / subdivision potential, as the City states it (Surrey parcels):** a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop, on eligible lots under 4,050 m² within the Urban Containment Boundary (framework adopted Jul 8, 2024). Added units are not subdivision: subdivision runs against the zone minimums in Bylaw No. 12000 and the Surrey Subdivision and Development Bylaw, 1986, No. 8830, through the City's Servicing Agreement process. A marketing claim about a lot's potential is only as good as that parcel check. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-development-application-process/servicing-agreement
- **Secondary suites (Surrey):** one suite per single-family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit, at most 40% of the principal unit's habitable floor area, never inside a coach house or garden suite, and not a separate strata lot. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- **Short-term rentals (Surrey):** permitted, owner-only, with a business licence at **$358 per year** on the City's program page; **conflict disclosed:** the City's 2024 corporate report on the STR amendments proposed $350/yr plus fees — $358 is carried with $350 disclosed. Relevant to a seller marketing a property's STR potential; it is not a land rule and creates no lot. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/short-term-rentals and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2024-R094.pdf
- **ALR block (for a landowner-seller whose parcel is in the reserve — the page itself states no ALR position):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)); caps: principal ≤500 m², suite within principal only, additional residence 90 m² (≤40 ha) / 186 m² (>40 ha); farm class is three-tier — $2,500 on 0.8–4 ha / $2,500 + 5% of value over 4 ha / $10,000 under 0.8 ha, applications by Oct 31 (a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR). Reserve status is checked per parcel on the ALC maps. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **PTT on land (the tax a land buyer pays, which shapes what a land listing nets):** 1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. First-time buyers: exempt on the first $500,000 at $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000 — a new HOME, not bare land. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — CRA GI-003 level only:** most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (e.g. land kept for personal use); taxable cases include land used primarily in a business, land sold in the course of a business, and a parcel created by subdividing another parcel into more than two parts — developer/newly subdivided lots generally TAXABLE. For a landowner-seller this is the threshold question of whether GST is charged on top of the price — anything finer: UNCONFIRMED, CPA per transaction. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Rebates attach to housing, not bare land:** FTHB GST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122; 100% of GST to $1M, max $50,000, phasing to $1.5M; agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031) — new HOUSING, not raw land; New Housing Rebate 36% / max $6,300 / $350,000–$450,000. HBP $60,000; FHSA $40,000 / $8,000 per year. A seller cannot market a bare lot as carrying a housing rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Bare-land strata block (the strata form that applies to land, for a seller of a bare-land strata lot):** Form B (SPA s.59) within 7 days, max $35 + 25¢/page; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for 5+ lots since Jul 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF since Nov 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since Nov 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + May 1, 2023 exemptions) — a "45+" listing line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw. A fee-simple lot carries none of this. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
