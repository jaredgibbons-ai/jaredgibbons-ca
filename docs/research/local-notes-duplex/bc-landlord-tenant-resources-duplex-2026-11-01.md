## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The tenancy framework (province topic — the rules this slug's research documents, each as the live page states it with its cited RTB source):** (i) **Rent increases:** the 2026 limit for residential tenancies is 2.3% and the published 2027 limit is 2.2%; rent can be increased only once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' written notice on form RTB-7 showing the exact dollar amount and effective date; only the current year's limit can be used (no carry-forward, no rounding up); a larger increase requires an arbitrator application on form RTB-52 or the tenant's voluntary written agreement. (ii) **Agreements:** a landlord must prepare a written agreement for every tenancy on the RTB-1 template or an equivalent containing all standard terms required by the Residential Tenancy Act; both parties sign and date it; the landlord must give the tenant a copy within 21 days of signing; a tenancy can be month-to-month or fixed term. (iii) **Inspections:** landlord and tenant inspect together at move-in and move-out and complete the Condition Inspection Report (RTB-27); the landlord books the move-in inspection and gives the tenant a copy of the move-in report within 7 days; to keep the right to claim against a deposit a landlord must offer at least 2 opportunities to inspect, using form RTB-22 when the tenant was unavailable at the times first offered. (iv) **Deposits:** a security deposit can be no more than half of the first month's rent, and a pet damage deposit the same cap no matter how many pets, with no pet damage deposit for certified guide or service dogs and no other pet fees; a landlord cannot keep a deposit without the tenant's written permission or an RTB order; once the landlord has the tenant's forwarding address in writing (form RTB-47), the deposits must be returned with any interest within 15 days if there are no issues, failing which the tenant can apply through the direct request process and the landlord may be ordered to pay double; deposit interest for 2026 is 0% per the page — interest owed for years before 2026 is UNVERIFIED at year-by-year level in this file. (Research file, §4; https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html)
- **Ending a tenancy (as the page documents it):** a landlord can end a tenancy only for reasons allowed under the Residential Tenancy Act, on the correct form, in writing, signed and dated, with the unit address, effective date and reason; eviction notices cannot be served by text message. RTB-30 (10 Day Notice for Unpaid Rent or Utilities — tenant has 5 days to pay or dispute); RTB-33 (One Month Notice for Cause — 10 days to dispute); RTB-32L / RTB-32P (Three Month Notice for landlord's use / purchaser's use — these must be generated through the RTB web portal and a non-generated notice is legally unenforceable; 3 months' notice, 21 days to dispute, one month's rent compensation, the person moving in must live in the unit at least 12 months, and personal-occupancy notices are not allowed in some buildings with 5 or more rental units); RTB-29 (Four Month Notice for Demolition or Conversion — permits/approvals required, 30 days to dispute, one month's rent compensation; major renovations are a separate process requiring an RTB order of possession). A tenant's own notice needs no RTB form, must be in writing, and for a month-to-month tenancy must be received at least one full rental month before the effective date and before the day rent is due; on form RTB-8 (Mutual Agreement) neither side can be required to sign and a text message is not a legal way to end a tenancy by mutual agreement. (Research file, §4.)
- **Repairs, disputes and sales of tenanted property (as the page documents it):** landlords are responsible for most repairs including heating, plumbing, common areas and normal wear and tear; tenants are responsible for minor upkeep and damage caused by them, their pets or their guests; emergency repairs can be arranged by the tenant at reasonable cost after 2 phone attempts and a reasonable wait to reach the landlord's emergency contact. Dispute resolution runs four processes — direct request, facilitation, participatory hearing, expedited hearing (RTB tries to schedule within 12 days); monetary claims must generally be $35,000 or less; tenants must keep paying rent during the process; orders are enforced through the courts. On a sale, a landlord cannot end a tenancy just because they want to sell; the buyer becomes the new landlord and the tenancy continues on the same terms; showings and open houses need written notice to the tenant or the tenant's permission; the owner at the time the tenant moves out is responsible for returning the deposits. RTB contact as stated on the page: Lower Mainland 604-660-1020, Victoria 250-387-1602, elsewhere in BC 1-800-665-8779; email HSRTO@gov.bc.ca (the Branch's own address, exactly as the page gives it); hours Monday to Friday 9 am to 4 pm except statutory holidays (11 am to 4 pm on the last Wednesday of the month); RTB information officers cannot give legal advice. Whether each form number and version described on the page is still the current published version at rebuild time is UNVERIFIED — a per-form check against the RTB forms page. (Research file, §4.)
- **MHPTA boundary (settled program wording, for the regime boundary only):** pad rent for a manufactured home site tenancy is governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, not the rates above; the 2026 limit is 2.3% plus a proportional amount for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees, and the 2027 limit is 2.2% plus the proportional amount, on 3 full months' notice, once every 12 months; where the MHPTA speaks of the decision-maker, the settled wording in this program is "an order of the director." MHPTA rules beyond the pad-rent limits and that wording are UNVERIFIED in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **STRATA block — applies where a duplex / half duplex a buyer is looking at is a strata duplex (2-lot strata plan), in any municipality.** Form B diligence applies in full. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022 — stated here as the strata position; the tenancy framework above is the separate Residential Tenancy Act position, and the two are never blended. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the applicable municipality's Approving Officer process — no single municipal process is stated for this province topic, and none is invented. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math.** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. Have a lawyer or notary model the exact bill from the current Province pages before offering. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. GST applies to new construction, not resales. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Does buying a tenanted duplex end the tenancy?** No. A landlord cannot end a tenancy just because they want to sell; on a sale the buyer becomes the new landlord and the tenancy continues on the same terms. Showings and open houses need written notice to the tenant or the tenant's permission. (§3.)
- **Q: Can a purchaser move into one half of a duplex they just bought?** Only through the purchaser's-use route as the page documents it: once all conditions of sale are met, a purchaser who intends to live in the unit (or have a close family member live there) for at least 12 months can ask the seller in writing to give a Three Month Notice (RTB-32P) — which must be generated through the RTB web portal, carries 3 months' notice, 21 days to dispute and one month's rent compensation, and is not allowed in some buildings with 5 or more rental units. A non-generated notice is legally unenforceable. (§3.)
- **Q: Who returns the deposits when a tenanted half duplex sells?** The owner at the time the tenant moves out is responsible for returning the deposits — so buyer and seller should deal with the deposits in their contract or at closing. Each deposit is capped at half the first month's rent, and once the landlord has the tenant's forwarding address in writing (RTB-47) the deposits, with any interest, must be returned within 15 days if there are no issues. (§3.)
- **Q: How much can the rent on a duplex unit go up in 2026?** Up to 2.3% for a residential tenancy (2.2% published for 2027), only once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' written notice on RTB-7, with no carry-forward of unused room. Anything above the limit needs an RTB-52 arbitrator application or the tenant's voluntary written agreement. (§3.)
- **Q: Does the 2.3% limit also cap manufactured home pad rent?** Not on its own: a manufactured home site tenancy sits under the MHPTA, whose limit is 2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026 and 2.2% plus the proportional amount for 2027, on 3 full months' notice once every 12 months. (§3.)
- **Q: Is a strata half duplex's tenancy handled differently from a fee-simple half's?** The tenancy framework is the same — the page's rules are unit rules. What differs is ownership diligence: a strata half carries Form B diligence and the strata block in §3; a fee-simple half is title-and-covenant diligence. (§§2–3.)
- **Q: What inspections protect the deposit positions on a tenanted purchase?** Move-in and move-out RTB-27 reports, with at least 2 inspection opportunities offered and RTB-22 used if the tenant is unavailable — that is what protects the deposit positions on both sides of the deal. (§3.)
- **Q: What PTT do I pay at $1,000,000? At $3,500,000?** On the worked tiers in §3 — fully residential, no exemption — $18,000 at $1,000,000 and $93,000 at $3,500,000 including the further 2% tier. Have a lawyer or notary model the exact bill before offering. (§3.)
