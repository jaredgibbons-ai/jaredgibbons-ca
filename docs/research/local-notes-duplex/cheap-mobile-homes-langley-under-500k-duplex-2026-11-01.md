## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **Where duplexes are permitted — no single frame asserted for this band.** The band spans two municipalities; a duplex takes the zoning of the municipality and parcel where it sits. - **MHPTA frame — the rules that actually govern this band's stock.** (i) **Pad rent increases (Province / Residential Tenancy Branch, verified at source in batch 12 and re-read for this slug's research):** a park landlord may increase rent only with 3 full months' written notice, only once every 12 months, and only up to the yearly limit; the 2026 limit for manufactured home site tenancies is **2.3% plus a proportional amount**, and the 2027 limit is **2.2% plus a proportional amount**. The proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park. The landlord may not round up and may not carry forward an unused portion of a prior year's limit. No pad-rent DOLLAR FIGURE is published as fact in this file — no official source publishes a Langley pad-rent figure, average or range. (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes, read 2026-11-01.) (ii) **Assignment on a sale (MHPTA s.28):** assignment permanently transfers the pad tenancy to the buyer; a tenant may assign only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it — s.28(1)(b) wording is "an order of the director" (NOT arbitrator); the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent, and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. (Research file above, §4.) (iii) **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s.42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to another use, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date, with defined additional-compensation routes. (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes, read 2026-11-01.) (iv) **Registration and certification:** in BC the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered with the Manufactured Home Registry, through a lawyer or notary filing the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership; a home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal (Z-240 for mobile homes, A-277 for modular), and a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification. (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry, read 2026-11-01.) (v) **Taxes on a home in a park:** a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value will not include the value of the land if the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. (Research file above, §4.) (vi) **Written agreement:** landlords and tenants in a manufactured home park must have a written tenancy agreement for the site, and the MHPTA covers most manufactured home tenancies. (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes, read 2026-11-01.)
- **The monthly-cost stack a Langley park buyer actually carries (structure, not figures):** pad rent under the regulated formula above, plus home insurance, plus property tax on the home's assessed value excluding the land in a park, plus utilities as the specific park's agreement allocates them — no dollar figure for any element is stated here because no official source publishes a Langley figure for any of them. - **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (for transfers where PTT applies — own-land / strata-lot transfers; a chattel home sale on a rented pad is a different transfer, modelled with advice on the specific deal).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000, based on fair market value at registration unless an exemption applies. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan) wherever it sits; it does NOT describe a pad-rental park home, which is not strata.** Form B diligence applies in full to a strata half: depreciation report, financial statements, CRF balance, approved special levies still payable and the insurance summary. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much is pad rent on a Langley mobile home under $500k?** No official source publishes a Langley pad-rent figure, average or range, so no dollar figure is stated in this file. The figure that matters is the specific park's current rent in its written Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement. (§3; research file §7.)
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up each year?** Up to the Residential Tenancy Branch limit for manufactured home site tenancies: 2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027. The proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of sites in the park. Increases require 3 full months' notice and can occur only once every 12 months. (§3; research file §7.)
- **Q: Does the park raise my pad rent when I buy the home?** No. On assignment under s.28 of the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same and the scheduled date of a subsequent increase does not change; assignment requires the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director) and the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent. (§3; research file §7.)
- **Q: If I buy a home in this band, do I own land?** No — THE TENURE LINE: a home on a rented pad is not land ownership. You own the home as a chattel and rent the pad under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement; the home's assessed value for tax excludes the land in a park. That is the opposite tenure from a half duplex, where the buyer takes title to a strata lot or a fee-simple lot. (§2, §3.)
- **Q: Is a double-wide in a park a duplex?** No. "Double-wide" describes how the manufactured home was built and shipped, not two dwelling units on one title. It is one chattel home on one rented pad — not a duplex, not a half duplex, and not strata. (§2.)
- **Q: What happens if the park is converted to another use?** Under s.42 of the Act, as the Province states it: the landlord must first have all necessary permits and approvals, then give 12 months' notice and pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the effective date, with defined additional-compensation routes where the home cannot be moved or no site is available within a reasonable distance. (§3; research file §7.)
- **Q: What should I check before offering on a park home?** The CSA label first — without the original legible CSA decal the home cannot be sold until Technical Safety BC re-certification — then the written site tenancy agreement (current rent, last increase date, what the rent includes), and any unusual card claim (a lease running to 2072, a "55+" restriction, pet rules) against the tenancy agreement, park rules and title. Registration of the sale with the Manufactured Home Registry, through a lawyer or notary, is what makes the transfer effective. (§3; research file §6.)
- **Q: Are there market statistics for Langley mobile homes under $500k?** No manufactured-home statistics are published in the SnapStats report — it has no manufactured-home category. The only SnapStats figures for Langley are all-residential community context, as of September 2026: Detached median $1,345,000 (449 active, 64 sold) and Condos & Townhomes median $611,000 (699 active, 128 sold) — neither describes this page's manufactured-home shelf.
