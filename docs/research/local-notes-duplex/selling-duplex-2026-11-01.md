## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing.)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The eight steps and the regulator's framework (as this slug's research documents them):** the page's eight steps are (1) First chat — goals, timeline, how Jared works and the BC disclosure forms, free; (2) Pricing — comparison with recent sales and current listings nearby, a price range with reasons, with a link to a free home evaluation; (3) Getting ready — cleaning, small repairs and clearing clutter, no renovation needed, staging help with every listing; (4) Photos and listing — professional photos, video, floor plans and drone photos, then the home goes on the MLS® and Jared's website, with a link to the marketing plan; (5) Showings — buyers book through their agents, feedback shared after each showing; (6) Offers — price, deposit, conditions and dates walked through, decide together to accept, counter or wait; (7) Conditions and cooling-off — most buyers have conditions such as financing or inspection, and for most homes a three business day cooling-off period after acceptance; (8) Closing — lawyer or notary handles paperwork and pays out the mortgage, keys handed over on possession day. The regulator's framework (BCFSA, opened in the research pass): a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; disclosure of representation, duties owed, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA must happen at the outset of dealings, and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. (Research file, §4; https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Cooling-off / rescission and deposits (verified at the regulator):** if a buyer rescinds an accepted offer under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining balance of the deposit to the buyer, with no separate release form signed by either party — the page's own statement matches (a cancelling buyer must tell the seller in writing and pay 0.25% of the offer price). Generally, a buyer's deposit is usually held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder, a neutral party; where subject clauses are not removed, the deposit is not automatically returned and both parties must sign a release, except where the buyer exercises the right of rescission. (Research file, §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits, read 2026-11-01.)
- **BC home flipping tax (verified on the Province page the selling page links to):** the tax applies to profit from selling a BC property (including presale contracts) owned for less than 730 days, under the Residential Property (Short-Term Holding) Profit Tax Act effective January 1, 2025; the rate is 20% of net taxable income for a property disposed of within 365 days and gradually decreases over the next 365 days, with no tax at 730 days; a return must be filed within 90 days of the sale where the seller is subject to the tax or an exemption applies only after filing. The page's shorter wording — "20% of the profit", "drops to zero by day 730", "a return may be due within 90 days" — is a simplification of "net taxable income" and the filing conditions as the Province states them. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/bc-home-flipping-tax, read 2026-11-01; https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html.)
- **STRATA block — for a seller whose duplex / half duplex is a strata lot (2-lot strata plan).** Form B diligence runs the other way for a seller: the buyer will read the Form B, minutes and bylaws the seller's strata produces, so a duplex seller orders them early. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple half duplex sells on its own subdivided lot; a duplex that was never subdivided or strata-divided sells whole. No finer process detail is stated because no official source opened for this slug states it. Party-wall obligations rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on title — a seller discloses it, a buyer reads it. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — the buyer's tax, stated here because sellers price against it (worked examples with inputs + tier math).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. GST applies to new construction, not resales — a resale duplex sale is not new housing for this rebate. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What are the steps to sell a home with Jared, according to the page?** Eight steps: a free first chat; pricing from recent nearby sales and current listings; getting ready with cleaning, small repairs and decluttering; professional photos, video, floor plans and drone photos, then MLS® listing; showings with feedback after each one; offers walked through on price, deposit, conditions and dates; conditions and the cooling-off period; and closing through a lawyer or notary, with keys on possession day. (§3.)
- **Q: What does it cost to sell a home in BC?** The page lists, attributing the list to BCFSA: legal or notary fees for the title transfer, legal fees to discharge the mortgage, GST on legal fees, any lender prepayment penalty, and the seller's share of the current year's property taxes if unpaid — **and states no commission figure at all** (this slug's owned defect (ii), §5). Commission is UNVERIFIED from the page because BCFSA requires commission and fee information to be given to the consumer in the listing conversation — ask the commission question at the first chat and budget past the page's list with your lawyer or notary before listing. (§§3, 5.)
- **Q: Can a buyer back out after I accept their offer?** For most homes, yes, inside the Home Buyer Rescission Period: the buyer may rescind within three business days after acceptance, in writing, and the brokerage then pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the balance of the deposit to the buyer, with no release form required. (§3.)
- **Q: Do I pay tax if I sell soon after buying?** Possibly the BC home flipping tax: it applies to profit on a BC property owned less than 730 days, at 20% of net taxable income within 365 days, gradually decreasing to no tax at 730 days, with a return due within 90 days of the sale where the tax or a filing-based exemption applies. (§3.)
- **Q: What disclosures and protections apply when I hire a BC realtor?** BCFSA states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, explain the duties owed to clients and non-clients, warn unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, work for only the buyer or the seller in a transaction, and make the representation, duties and BCFSA complaint-route disclosures at the outset of dealings. (§3.)
- **Q: How is my list price decided?** From sold evidence, as the page frames it: what similar homes nearby recently sold for and what is listed now, delivered as a price range with reasons — ask for that reasoning in writing at the pricing step. For a duplex, the comparable set is duplex / half-duplex sales in the same legal form, not detached houses generally. (§3.)
- **Q: I am selling a half duplex — does anything change?** The legal form drives the paperwork: a strata half sells with Form B diligence and the strata block in §3; a fee-simple half sells on its own title with its party-wall agreement; a never-divided duplex sells whole, both units together. A suite inside the duplex must be disclosed on its permit/registration status, not on listing language. (§§2–3.)
- **Q: Should I renovate before selling?** Per the page's own guidance: do the small jobs — cleaning, small repairs and clearing clutter, with staging help included — and skip the renovation. (§3.)
- **Q: How long will selling take?** UNVERIFIED as a figure — the page states none, and its own answer is that timing depends on your home, price and the market at the time. (§2.)
