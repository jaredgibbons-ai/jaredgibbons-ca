## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. In the Township, a coach house is a fee-simple second unit under Zoning Bylaw 2500 s.102 — never strata, and never a half duplex. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The regulator.** In BC, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and the Province's Buying and selling a home page directs prospective purchasers to BCFSA for information on purchasing property in BC. A licensed professional must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party, and must disclose at the outset whether they will be able to represent the consumer as a client. Dual agency is prohibited in BC (since 2018) except in the rarest of circumstances — the host at an open house cannot simply "also represent" a buyer who walks in. (Research file above, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home, read 2026-11-01; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Open-house conduct rule — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), verbatim.** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, provides that **a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate**. In practice: chatting factually with the host does not by itself make you their client — but once you share your motivation, budget or needs with a licensee who represents the seller, the disclosure rules engage. An identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors is NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED (a host may still ask, on the seller's instructions). (Research file above, §4; http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Where duplexes are permitted (both Langley frames, as the topic spans both).** Township of Langley: Zoning Bylaw 2500 governs; the Township's SSMUH bylaw is Bylaw 6020, adopted November 18, 2024, with NO 6-plex tier — a duplex is the baseline two-unit form, and SSMUH does not by itself split a duplex into separately titled halves. Township servicing is Bylaw 2019 No. 5382, CONFIRMED in the verified ledger. City of Langley: Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300 (cite the number only; its adoption date is conflicted in sources and is UNVERIFIED where a date is needed); the City's SSMUH bylaw is 3284 (City-only). A Langley City open house is not under Township rules, and the reverse. The zone for a specific property seen at an open house is read off the governing municipality's current zoning for that lot. (Townhomes sibling, §3; standing Langley frames, D9 brief.)
- **Secondary suites inside a duplex — each municipality's own rule, never borrowed.** Township: secondary suite cap 120 m². Langley City: suite = lesser of 100 m² and 40%. A coach house in the Township is a fee-simple second unit under Zoning Bylaw 2500 s.102 — never strata. The Township's R-CL zone (s.403) is 220 m² minimum lot / 464.5 m² maximum lot — a "355 m²" figure is NOWHERE in the bylaw and is never carried. (Townhomes sibling, §3; standing Langley frames, D9 brief.)
- **Fee-simple contrast, stated plainly (carried from the siblings):** a fee-simple townhome — and, by the same title logic, a fee-simple half duplex — carries NO strata, NO Form B and NO strata fees; municipal rules plus any registered covenants or HOA documents govern instead. If the property on the feed is fee-simple, diligence is title, zoning and covenants. (Townhomes sibling, §3.)
- **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan) seen through any feed.** Form B diligence applies in full. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum contingency reserve fund (CRF) contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the governing municipality's Approving Officer process, against the current zoning minimums; no finer process detail is stated. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math.** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier** — $93,000 governs at $3.5M. First-time buyers' exemption: full on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial to $860,000. Newly built home exemption: full to $1,100,000, phasing out by $1,150,000. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. 5% GST applies to new construction only, not resales — the frame if an event on this feed is a developer / presentation-centre event rather than a resale open (REDMA s.18 disclosure statement, deposits in trust; s.21 7-day rescission from the later of signing or acknowledging it). A resale duplex or half duplex is not new housing for this rebate. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

- **Q: Do I have to sign in or show ID?** No BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source states an identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors — UNVERIFIED as a rule; an individual host may still ask, on the seller's instructions. (§3.)
- **Q: Is the host at an open house the seller's agent?** The host is a licensee connected to the listing side; BCFSA requires a licensee to work for either the buyer or the seller, to inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and to tell unrepresented consumers the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party — and dual agency is prohibited except in the rarest circumstances.
