## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

**TOPIC — NO ROSTER at the top: this file names no duplex stock as its own and takes no count.** The "stock" this page actually surfaces is not a set of listings — it is the full range of BC homes a first-time buyer might price into the tool: resale homes (PTT with the first-time exemption where it applies), newly built homes (PTT with the newly built home exemption and GST with the federal rebates where they apply), and homes bought on vacant land the buyer then builds on. A first-time buyer's duplex purchase prices through exactly those same routes — the form of the home changes the diligence (§3 strata block), not the tax programs. Named duplex-form stock lives in the home-area duplex files. (Research file above; sibling set per the FILE STATUS block.)

- **The form class with its basis (calculator view):** the exemption thresholds shape which stock the tool's savings are largest on — the first-time program's full exemption runs to a fair market value of $835,000, while the newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 — so a first-time buyer of a newly built duplex can be inside one program's range and outside the other's, and the tool must test both. Property size is part of the profile: both BC exemptions set 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) as the size line, with a partial exemption possible above it. (Research file above; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers, read 2026-11-01.)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The rules the tool must encode (the topic's "city rules" are province-wide).** There is no municipal frame for this slug: the governing rules are the BC property transfer tax and its exemptions, the federal FHSA / Home Buyers' Plan / GST rebates, and CMHC's down-payment and mortgage-insurance rules — each carried below at the verification status its research gives it. A duplex or half duplex in any municipality prices through these same programs; the municipality's own zoning/suite rules live in the home-area files. (Research file above.)
- **Secondary suites inside a duplex — no figure borrowed.** Suite caps are each city's own rule (for reference only where a home-area file states them: Surrey 40% of principal habitable area; Township 120 m²; Langley City lesser of 100 m² / 40%; Abbotsford under 50%) — no suite figure is asserted for this BC-wide topic, and none is needed by the calculator. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan) a first-time buyer prices into the tool.** Form B diligence applies in full. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum contingency reserve fund (CRF) contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the municipality's Approving Officer process; no finer process detail is stated. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math.** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. Run the general tiers on the full price first, then subtract whatever exemption the buyer actually qualifies for — the exemption reduces the bill, it does not change the tiers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **BC first time home buyers' program (PTT exemption) — verified in the research.** Full-exemption property conditions: fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), 0.5 hectares or smaller, only residential improvements, used only as the buyer's principal residence — where all conditions are met, the purchaser is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price. A partial exemption may apply at a fair market value over $835,000 and under $860,000, or where the property is larger than 0.5 hectares and/or has another building. Buyer conditions include Canadian citizenship or permanent-resident status, BC residence history, and never having owned a principal-residence interest anywhere in the world. Keeping it: move in within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy to the first anniversary of registration. (Research file above; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers, read 2026-11-01.)
- **BC newly built home exemption (PTT) — verified in the research.** Full-exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), phasing out over the $50,000 range above it, with complete elimination at $1,150,000; the property must be in BC, used only as the principal residence, and 0.5 hectares or smaller, with the same occupancy pattern (move in within 92 days, occupy to the first anniversary). (Research file above; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption, read 2026-11-01.)
- **FHSA — verified figures only.** The CRA describes the FHSA as a registered plan letting a first-time buyer save to buy or build a qualifying first home tax-free, up to certain limits; **FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000** (verified from the CRA page in the research), and contributions are generally deductible. The CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at each withdrawal. **The FHSA lifetime contribution limit ($40,000 in program shorthand) and maximum participation period are flagged UNVERIFIED in this slug's research** — the CRA's detailed pages could not be retrieved — so the lifetime figure is NOT stated as verified anywhere in this file. (Research file above; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Home Buyers' Plan — limit UNVERIFIED here.** The CRA describes the HBP as allowing an RRSP withdrawal to buy or build a qualifying home, repayable within the published repayment period. **The current HBP withdrawal limit ($60,000 in program shorthand) and the repayment-period length are flagged UNVERIFIED in this slug's research** (the CRA's HBP page could not be retrieved) and are deliberately NOT stated as figures in this file. (Research file above; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **GST rebates = new HOUSING only — verified figure + UNVERIFIED thresholds.** The first-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) provides eligible first-time buyers a full or partial rebate of GST on newly constructed or substantially renovated homes, **up to $50,000** (verified from the CRA tax-tips page in the research); a builder can credit it at closing, and the application window is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction. **The FTHB rebate's phase shape / value thresholds and the GST New Housing Rebate's figures (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 in program shorthand) are flagged UNVERIFIED in this slug's research** — the detailed CRA rebate pages could not be retrieved — so no threshold or phase figure for either rebate is stated as verified in this file. A resale duplex or half duplex is not new housing for these rebates. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. (Research file above; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Down payment and mortgage loan insurance (CMHC) — as the research verifies it.** CMHC states mortgage loan insurance is required when a buyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the purchase price, that it protects the lender rather than the buyer, and that it allows qualification with as little as 5% down. Minimum down payment as published by CMHC: 5% for homes $500,000 or less; for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require 20% down and insurance is not available. CMHC's published premium range, as read in the research, is **0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount**, payable in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. (Research file above; https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained, read 2026-11-01.) **No CMHC premium-range error is asserted against this slug's page** (see FILE STATUS) — whether the live calculator's own figures match CMHC's published range is NOT CONFIRMED, because the calculator body is not present in the page text.
- **Home owner grant — annual, not closing money.** The grant reduces annual property tax on a principal residence; as published (page last updated September 22, 2026), the regular grant amount effective January 1, 2027 is $570, the 2026 threshold is $2,075,000, reduced by $5 per $1,000 over it, and $0 over $2,189,000. A calculator should not net it against purchase taxes. The additional-PTT rate and the first-home buyer's tax credit amount are UNVERIFIED in this slug's research and are not stated. (Research file above; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant, read 2026-11-01.)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I'm buying half a duplex as my first home — does the calculator treat it differently?** The tax programs are the same: PTT is computed on the half's own purchase price as its own transfer, then the first-time exemption is tested against that price. The difference is diligence, not tax — a strata half carries the §3 strata block (Form B on the 2-lot strata). (§2; §3.)
- **Q: How much PTT does a first-time buyer actually save?** A qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price, provided the fair market value is $835,000 or less, the property is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the principal residence; over $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. (§3.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: What are the general PTT rates the calculator starts from?** 1% to $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000 — plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 where that applies. It is not a single "5% over $3M" rate. (§3.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Is a newly built duplex treated differently from a resale duplex?** Yes. The newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 with elimination at $1,150,000, and a new home may also attract GST, which the first-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate can offset by up to $50,000 for eligible buyers. A resale duplex attracts neither GST rebate. (§3.)
- **Q: How much FHSA room do I get in the first year?** $8,000 of participation room in the first year the account is opened (verified from the CRA page in the research); contributions are generally deductible. The lifetime limit is UNVERIFIED in this slug's research and is not stated as a figure here. (§3.) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?** Yes — the CRA states both can be used for the same qualifying home as long as all conditions are met at each withdrawal. The current HBP limit itself is UNVERIFIED in this file. (§3.)
- **Q: Does a 2-lot strata need a depreciation report?** The 5-year cycle applies to stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; a 2-lot strata duplex is below that threshold on lot count. (§3.)
- **Q: Can a strata restrict my rentals?** A strata rental-restriction bylaw is VOID since November 24, 2022 — no. (§3.)
- **Q: How much down payment do I need on a duplex under $500,000?** CMHC's published minimum is 5% at $500,000 or less; between $500,000 and $1,499,999 it is 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000; under 20% down, mortgage loan insurance is required. (§3.) https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: Can I trust the live calculator's own numbers?** Its inputs, outputs and formulas are NOT CONFIRMED in the research (the calculator body is not present in the page text and no calculation was submitted) — test any result against the verified rules in §3 and confirm with a mortgage professional, lawyer or notary.
