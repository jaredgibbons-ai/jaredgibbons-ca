## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

**TOPIC — NO ROSTER at the top: this file names no duplex stock as its own and takes no count.** An open house is an event on a listing, not a land form and not a housing stock: open houses attach to individual listings and change weekly, across every form. This file therefore verifies no named strata duplex development and no named fee-simple half-duplex subdivision for this topic — the research names none, only page 1 (12 cards of 1,500 displayed) was recorded, and the one card recorded verbatim (115 6336 197 Street, Langley, §1) carries no duplex description and no complex name. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **Where duplexes are permitted — NO frame asserted for this topic.** A duplex takes the zoning of the municipality and parcel where it sits; this file asserts no city's zoning, SSMUH, suite, servicing or short-term-rental rule for the topic, because the topic has no ground. Read the home-area file for the property in question. (Brief chain discipline; research file above.)
- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), carried where the settled treatment applies.** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report, provides that a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about their motivation, financial qualifications or needs. Use the open house for facts; save your story for your own agent — once you discuss your motivation, finances or needs with the seller's licensee, you are sharing with the other side's representative. (Research file above, §§4, 6; http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download)
- **Representation at an open house (from the research).** In BC, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party — an unrepresented buyer at an open house is exactly that consumer. (Research file above, §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers)
- **How open-house times are set — UNVERIFIED, and the authoritative-time position.** How open house dates and times are set and entered for publication (the listing brokerage side, via board systems) is UNVERIFIED — no BC Gov or BCFSA source states it; identification or sign-in requirements for open-house visitors are likewise not stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found: UNVERIFIED. **Authoritative open-house times are therefore UNVERIFIED everywhere in this program**: on this page the only on-card time is the one inside the listing's truncated description text (there is no card time block for it to conflict with — §1), and on the settled offset feeds (§5) neither stated figure can be treated as authoritative from a page alone. Confirm the specific listing's time with the listing brokerage before setting out. (Research file above, §§4, 6.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (the cost a buyer touring an open house is budgeting around).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000, based on fair market value at registration unless an exemption applies. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan) wherever it sits.** Form B diligence applies in full — after an open house on a strata unit, ask for the strata package behind the Form B: depreciation report, financial statements, CRF balance, approved special levies still payable and the insurance summary. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through that municipality's Approving Officer process, against its current zoning minimums; no finer process detail is stated because no official source opened for this slug states it. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. A resale duplex or half duplex is not new housing for this rebate — a developer unit at an open house can carry GST where the resale next door does not. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
