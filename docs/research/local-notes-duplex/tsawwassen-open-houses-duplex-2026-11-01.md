## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The regulator.** In BC, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and the Province's Buying and selling a home page directs prospective purchasers to BCFSA for information on purchasing property in BC. A licensed professional must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party to the transaction. (Research file above; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home, read 2026-11-01; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Open-house conduct rule — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), verbatim.** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, provides that **a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate**. In practice: chatting factually with the host does not by itself make you their client — but once you share your motivation, budget or needs with a licensee who represents the seller, the disclosure rules engage. An identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors is NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED (a host may still ask, on the seller's instructions). (Research file above; http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Where duplexes are permitted (Delta frame for the topic).** Delta's current OCP is Bylaw No. 8400 (2024), a single consolidated plan with NO lettered schedules (farm land designated "Agriculture (A)"; the Tsawwassen Area Plan was Schedule D.1 under the PREVIOUS Bylaw 3950 — a "Schedule D.1 of Bylaw No. 8400" citation is an error wherever met). - **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan) seen through any feed.** Form B diligence applies in full. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)

- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math.** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. A resale duplex or half duplex is not new housing for this rebate. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: A Tsawwassen open-house card shows a duplex — can I buy just the side I walked through?** Only if that side is legally separate — a strata lot in a 2-lot strata plan, or its own fee-simple lot. The card settles nothing; check the title. (§2; §3.)
- **Q: How do I know the open house time is current?** Carefully, on this feed: each listing carries an OPEN HOUSE label with a date and start and end times, but on the cards read for the research the card time block (for example 12:00 PM–02:00 PM) and the time inside the same listing's own text (11:00 AM–1:00 PM) differ by one hour, the authoritative time is UNVERIFIED, and the page states no feed update interval — confirm the actual time with the listing brokerage before you go. (§2.) https://jaredgibbons.ca/tsawwassen-open-houses.html
- **Q: Do I need my own agent to attend, and what can I safely tell the host?** No official source requires your own agent. Rule 5-10(3) excuses disclosure of representation only while the host is hosting an advertised open house or answering general factual questions — once you discuss your motivation, finances or needs with the seller's licensee, you are sharing with the other side's representative. (§3.) http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Q: Do I have to sign in or show ID?** No BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source states an identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors — UNVERIFIED as a rule; an individual host may still ask, on the seller's instructions. (§3.)
- **Q: Is the host at an open house the seller's agent?** The host is a licensee connected to the listing side of the transaction; BCFSA requires a licensee to work for either the buyer or the seller and to inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients — assume anything non-factual you tell the host can reach the seller. (§3.) https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Which Tsawwassen areas appear on the open-house feed?** On the cards read for the research: Tsawwassen Central, Tsawwassen North, English Bluff and Cliff Drive, across condos, townhomes and detached houses — the feed is Tsawwassen-wide and changes as listings come and go. (§1.)
- **Q: Does this topic file list Tsawwassen's duplexes?** No. It is a topic file with no roster; duplex stock lives in the home-area files. (§2.)
- **Q: What diligence is different on a strata half duplex?** Form B diligence on the 2-lot strata: bylaws, rules, financials, insurance, and any age bylaw read against the settled SPA position (only 55+ valid). (§3 strata block.)
- **Q: Does a 2-lot strata need a depreciation report?** The 5-year cycle applies to stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; a 2-lot strata is below that threshold on lot count. (§3.)
- **Q: How is PTT calculated on a half duplex?** On the half's own price, on the standard tiers — $800,000 fully residential with no exemption = $14,000 (worked in §3). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
