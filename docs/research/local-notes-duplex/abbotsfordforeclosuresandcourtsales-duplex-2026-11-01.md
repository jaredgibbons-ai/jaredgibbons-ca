## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

**TOPIC — NO ROSTER at the top: this file names no duplex stock as its own and takes no count.** In process terms, any mortgaged property type can be the subject of a Rule 21-7 proceeding, which by its terms covers "mortgaged property" without a property-type limit — so a duplex (whole building, one title) or a strata half duplex (one strata lot) can each come to market this way, and each is a listing event for a property whose home is its own area file. The live page's exact feed composition is UNVERIFIED in the research (not re-checked on 2026-11-01). The market the feed draws from: 440 detached and 572 attached listings in Abbotsford in September 2026 (general market, not foreclosures). (Research file above; https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03, read 2026-11-01; SnapStats FVR September 2026 report.)

- **What the court process changes for a duplex buyer:** the seller in a foreclosure sale is not an owner preparing the home for market in the ordinary way — the sale runs inside a court proceeding, and buyers should expect the conditions in the court-approved form for that file, not a standard residential contract. On confirmation, the court's order directs the disposition of the proceeds and vests title in the purchaser — the vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer. Whether the property is vacant or occupied is a matter of the individual court file, not a published statistic. (Research file above.)
- **Strata half duplex in a court sale — the open diligence point:** whether Form B diligence runs in the ordinary way on a court-ordered sale of a strata lot is UNCONFIRMED in this program's settled treatment (see §3) — a buyer of a strata half duplex through this process confirms with their lawyer what strata documents, if any, the file provides before spending on inspections. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The court process (settled treatment, carried at the settled level).** A foreclosure proceeding is started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7 (1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). The court may order payment of what is due within a redemption period the court fixes (there is NO fixed length in the Rules), and in default a final order of foreclosure may be granted — Rule 21-7 (5)(b), (6). A party of record may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7 (7). Even before the redemption period expires, the person having conduct of a sale may apply for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7 (9). Conduct-of-sale mechanics (court directions, reserve/upset price, bid and offer rights) sit in Rule 13-5 and the Supreme Court's foreclosure practice direction PD-66, with Form 60 certification — carried here as the settled treatment; **in this slug's own research those texts were NOT retrieved (browser rate limit), so no Rule 13-5 / PD-66 mechanic beyond the settled level is asserted from this file** — get the seller's counsel's bid instructions for the specific file in writing before spending. Court records: Court Services Online eSearch is available to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry location, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure". (Research file above; https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03, read 2026-11-01; https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do, read 2026-11-01.)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can a duplex be sold through a court-ordered sale?** Yes — Rule 21-7 covers "mortgaged property" without a property-type limit, so a whole duplex on one title, or one strata half duplex as its own strata lot, can each be the subject property. Which title is being sold is read off the court file. (§2; §3.)
- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. (§3.) https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — it is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5)(b). (§3.)
- **Q: Is my accepted offer final?** No — the person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7 (9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title, and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires. Until that order, the sale is not final — do not book movers on acceptance. (§3.)
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** The bid mechanics sit in the court's sale directions (Rule 13-5) and PD-66, which this slug's research did not retrieve — treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for, and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing before spending on inspections. (§3.)
- **Q: Where do I check the court file?** Court Services Online eSearch (class "H - Foreclosure") shows the file number, registry, parties and filed documents; Abbotsford also has its own Supreme Court registry at 32375 Veterans Way for in-person inquiries — confirm the file's registry location on CSO per file. (§1; §3.) https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do
- **Q: Do I get a Form B if the court sale is a strata half duplex?** UNCONFIRMED — Form B treatment on court-ordered sales is an open item in the settled treatment. Confirm with your lawyer what strata documents the specific file provides before you spend. (§2; §3.)
- **Q: What PTT and GST do I pay on a court-ordered purchase?** UNCONFIRMED as a court-sale-specific treatment — have your lawyer cost both on the specific property before you set your ceiling. The ordinary tiers are worked in §3 ($497,500-scale attached purchases sit where first-time-buyer relief is a live question). (§3.)
- **Q: Does a 2-lot strata need a depreciation report?** The 5-year cycle applies to stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; a 2-lot strata is below that threshold on lot count. (§3.)
- **Q: Are foreclosure duplexes cheaper in Abbotsford?** No figure is stated — SnapStats publishes no foreclosure category and no official source publishes foreclosure counts or discounts; Abbotsford's general-market context (detached median $949,000, 39 days on market, September 2026) says nothing about how any individual court-ordered sale will price, because the court confirms the sale.
