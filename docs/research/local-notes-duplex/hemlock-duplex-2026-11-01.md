## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

- **Property taxes — RURAL regime only, never blended with a city regime.** A property not located in a city, town, district or village is in a rural area: its owner receives the property tax notice from the Province's Surveyor of Taxes Office and pays property taxes to the Province, not to the FVRD or any city. Under the Taxation (Rural Area) Act, s.11, rural property taxes are due and payable on **July 2** of the year they are first levied, a **5% penalty** is added to any portion that remains unpaid on July 2, and a **further 5% penalty** is added to any portion that remains unpaid on **October 31**. No city penalty scheme (Mission's or any other) applies here. (Research file above; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/rural-area; https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math.** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. A resale duplex or half duplex is not new housing for this rebate — the Snowflake Crescent strata is 1981 resale stock. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is Hemlock Valley part of Mission?** No. The board groups the "Hemlock" sub-area under the community Mission and many listings carry a Mission mailing address, but Hemlock Valley is unincorporated FVRD Electoral Area C land governed by the FVRD's Hemlock Valley Official Community Plan; Agassiz mailing addresses do not make it District of Kent land either. Title, permits and taxes run through the FVRD and the Province. (§1.)
- **Q: Can I buy just one side of a duplex in Hemlock Valley?** Only if that side is legally separate — a strata lot in a strata plan, or its own fee-simple lot from a subdivision. The one named strata half duplex documented here is the Snowflake Crescent strata (Plan NWS2041, §2); no fee-simple half duplex is verified in the valley. A single-lot duplex sells whole. Check the title first. (§2; §3.)
- **Q: Is the Snowflake Crescent half duplex a townhouse?** No — its listing records dwelling type Half Duplex, Freehold Strata, Plan NWS2041, built 1981. It is strata and attached, but built form governs: it is documented as its own form in this file (its form home) and counted nowhere in the townhomes total. (§2.)
- **Q: What diligence is different on a strata half duplex?** Form B diligence on the strata: bylaws, rules, financials, insurance, and any age bylaw read against the settled SPA position (only 55+ valid). For Snowflake Crescent, the Form B / strata plan is also where the plan's unit count is confirmed — UNCONFIRMED from public sources. (§3 strata block.)
- **Q: Does a 2-lot strata need a depreciation report?** The 5-year cycle applies to stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; a 2-lot strata is below that threshold on lot count. The Snowflake Crescent plan's lot count is UNCONFIRMED — confirm on the Form B. (§3.)
- **Q: Who do I pay property tax to, and what is the penalty?** The Province — rural-area properties receive their tax notice from the Surveyor of Taxes Office. Rural taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty on amounts unpaid on July 2 and a further 5% on amounts unpaid on October 31 (Taxation (Rural Area) Act s. 11). No city regime applies here. (§3.)
- **Q: Can I rent my Hemlock half duplex out?** The valley's pattern is vacation and short-term use (the FVRD describes the dwellings as mainly vacation homes and short-term rentals), and long-term rental-restriction bylaws have been void province-wide since November 24, 2022. But a specific unit's permission stack — strata bylaws on short-term rental, FVRD rules, and any licensing — is parcel-level and UNCONFIRMED until the Form B and the FVRD position are read; one listing's "no short-term rental ban" line is listing copy, not a bylaw record. (§2; §3.)
- **Q: How is PTT calculated on a half duplex?** On the half's own price, on the standard tiers — $1,200,000 fully residential with no exemption = $22,000 (worked in §3). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Where do Hemlock Valley children go to school?** There is no school in the valley — the FVRD plan states the permanent population is too small to make a school inside the Controlled Recreation Area viable, so students travel to outside communities (§5). (§5.)
- **Q: Who maintains the shared wall on a fee-simple half?** Whatever the registered party-wall agreement / easement on the two titles says. No agreement on title = UNCONFIRMED obligations — get legal advice before removing subjects. (§3.)
