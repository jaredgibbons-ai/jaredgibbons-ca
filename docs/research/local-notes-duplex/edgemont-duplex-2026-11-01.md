## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. A "duplex-style" listing description is marketing wording for a built form, not a tenure — it verifies neither a strata plan nor a subdivision. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **District plans and zoning — District of North Vancouver ONLY.** The Official Community Plan is Bylaw 7900, under which District growth is directed to its town and village centres, with Edgemont designated as one of the six village centres while surrounding established streets remain low-density. The zoning bylaw is Zoning Bylaw 3210 (cited in District material as amended by Bylaw 8698, June 2024, for the suite and coach-house rules); the District's zoning rewrite was in progress in 2026 per the research, so verify the current consolidated text and zone for a specific lot with the District. **City of North Vancouver bylaws (OCP 8400 / Zoning 6700) do not apply anywhere in this file's ground.** Hazard frame: Schedule B of the OCP establishes Development Permit Areas for Protection of the Natural Environment, Streamside Protection, Wildfire Hazard, Creek Hazard and Slope Hazard, and the District designates Natural Hazard DPAs for slope and creek hazards with property-level reports through GeoWeb — Edgemont's creek ravines and sloped lots warrant a check before building or buying. (Research file above; townhomes sibling above; https://docs.dnv.org/documents/official-community-plan.pdf; https://docs.dnv.org/documents/zoning-guide-single-family-residential-1-5.pdf)
- **Suites and coach houses — the District's own rule, and this ground's documented second-unit form.** Since June 2024, District zoning permits a principal dwelling, a secondary suite and a coach house — **three units total** — on all single-family zoned properties within the Urban Containment Boundary. December 2023 changes let a secondary suite be **up to 49% of the house** with no minimum size and raised the maximum coach house height to two storeys. That 49% figure is the District of North Vancouver's own suite rule, stated for this District only — no other municipality's suite cap is borrowed or exported. (Research file above, §4; https://docs.dnv.org/documents/rtc-bylaws-8787-8799.pdf)
- **SSMUH — the District's classification, with its compliance position stated.** The District's GEOweb SSMUH classification shows non-exempt lots potentially allowing up to 3 units at 280 m² or less, up to 4 units above 280 m², and up to 6 units above 280 m² within 400 m of a frequent-transit bus stop. The Province's Bill 25 (November 2025) requires full SSMUH density by June 30, 2026; the District's compliance amendments were still in preparation in spring 2026, tied to the Zoning Bylaw Rewrite project, whose timeline extends into Q3 2027. A duplex (two units) sits inside even the lowest of those tiers where the lot is non-exempt — but the specific lot's classification is read off GEOweb, not assumed. (Research file above, §4; https://geoweb.dnv.org/Products/Reports/PHIPO/SSMUH_Classification.pdf)

- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math.** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. - **Q: Does a 2-lot strata need a depreciation report?** The 5-year cycle applies to stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; a 2-lot strata is below that threshold on lot count. (§3.)
- **Q: What hazard checks are specific to Edgemont?** Slope and creek hazards — the District maps Natural Hazard Development Permit Areas (including Creek Hazard and Slope Hazard) and offers property-level GeoWeb reports; ravine and steep lots should be checked before purchase or design work. (§3.)
