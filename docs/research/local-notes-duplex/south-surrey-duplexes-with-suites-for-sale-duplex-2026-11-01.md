## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. A secondary suite inside a Surrey duplex is an accessory dwelling unit within the duplex building — it is **not** a separate strata lot and cannot be strata-titled or otherwise legally separated from the rest of the home, per the City's own definition in §3. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing; suite form: https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite, read 2026-11-01.)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **What Surrey counts as a secondary suite — directly on point for this page (City page, opened directly):** a dwelling unit that is an accessory use, with habitable floor area **not exceeding 40% of the principal dwelling unit**, within a single family, semi-detached or duplex residential building, and not a separate strata lot. The current cap is the 40% rule in Surrey Zoning Bylaw 12000; a "90 m²" cap is NOT current and is not stated as a rule anywhere in this file. Conditions: only one secondary suite is permitted per single family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit; a secondary suite is not permitted within a coach house or garden suite; one suite may be permitted in dwellings within small-scale, multi-unit housing zones, including Comprehensive Development zones; and each application is reviewed for legal restrictions such as Land Use Contracts and Building Schemes. How a suite becomes legal: the owner must meet the conditions of zoning, hold the necessary building permits, meet the BC Building Code, and pay the secondary suite utility and service fees. Site copy carried as site copy only: the live page's editorial states all suites in Surrey must be registered with a Secondary Suite Declaration Form, a Secondary Suite Service Fee of $893 per suite, and a $1,000 fee for each unregistered suite the City becomes aware of, with a Secondary Suite Fee Web Inquiry tool to check registration — carried as the page's statements, not adopted beyond the City-page rules above. (Research file, §§4, 5; https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite, read 2026-11-01; https://jaredgibbons.ca/south-surrey-duplexes-with-suites-for-sale.html.)
- **Plan, zoning and SSMUH (Surrey frame):** the current Surrey plan is the Surrey 2050 OCP, Bylaw 21900 (adopted Jun 15, 2026) — Bylaw 18020 is replaced and is NEVER cited as current. Zoning Bylaw 12000 governs. SSMUH adopted Jul 8, 2024: a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop (a stop with 15-minute service, identified with TransLink) — duplexes are the baseline SSMUH form — and the new zoning does not retroactively legalize suites built without permits. Streamside setbacks sit in Part 7A of Zoning 12000 and vary by stream class — no single riparian figure is ever stated. (Research file, §4; https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing, read 2026-11-01; https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/12000.)
- **STR and servicing (Surrey frame).** Short-term rental licence $358/yr carried, with the City's 2024 corporate-report proposal of $350/yr disclosed alongside it — the conflict is stated, neither figure is silently adopted. Subdivision and Development (Servicing) Bylaw 8830 (1986) is VERIFIED from the City's own pages. Surrey has NO Supreme Court registry — foreclosure registry mapping is UNVERIFIED and is never filled in. (Settled Surrey frame, D15 brief.)
- **STRATA block — applies where a half duplex IS a strata lot (2-lot strata plan).** Form B diligence applies in full. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. Note the interaction this page turns on: a suite inside a strata half duplex needs both the City's suite rules above AND the strata's bylaws — Surrey's suite pages do not address strata permission. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the City's Approving Officer process; no finer process detail is stated because no official source opened for this slug states it. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. For an undivided-interest half, read the registered co-ownership / party-wall documents on title instead — the card's "NO STRATA FEES" line is not a tenure document. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How big can a secondary suite be in a Surrey duplex?** Its habitable floor area cannot exceed 40% of the principal dwelling unit, it must sit within the duplex building, and it cannot be a separate strata lot. Measure the suite against the 40% cap — a suite over the cap falls outside Surrey's definition, whatever the listing text says. (§3.)
- **Q: Can a Surrey half duplex have two secondary suites?** No — only one secondary suite is permitted per single family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit, and a suite is not permitted inside a coach house or garden suite. A card advertising both a legal suite and a second guest suite needs the second unit's status explained in writing before subjects come off. (§3.)
- **Q: The listing says the suite is "legal" — can I rely on that?** No. The page itself warns that a listing note is not proof of permits, no permit or registration record for any card property was opened in this program, and Surrey's new zoning does not legalize an unpermitted suite retroactively. Pull the City registration status and the permit history. (§§2–3.)

Do not price from a "duplex median"; price the property from its own half-duplex comparables. (§1.)
- **Q: Does Surrey's SSMUH zoning legalize an existing unpermitted suite?** No. The City states the legislation does not provide retroactive legalization for suites built without the necessary building permits; owners must still go through the permit process. (§3.)

Establish the exact tenure from title (subdivided lot, undivided interest, or strata lot) before comparing prices; if it IS a strata lot, the strata block in §3 and the strata's bylaws apply on top of the City's suite rules.
