## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together, and a seller of such a building is selling both units and the land in one transfer. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

**TOPIC — NO ROSTER, stated at the top of this section.** This slug has no ground and verifies no duplex stock of any form — no named duplex, no strata half duplex, no fee-simple half duplex, and no form-class claim for any place. None is invented and none is imported from any area file. What this file carries instead is the seller-side position the definitions create, because it is the part a duplex seller most often gets wrong:

- **Selling a whole duplex (never divided):** one listing, one transfer, both units and the lot — the buyer pays PTT on the whole fair market value (§3), and any suite or second-unit legality is a per-parcel question for the buyer's diligence, not a seller representation to improvise. (Program definition above; research file, §4.)
- **Selling one half of a STRATA duplex:** the seller is selling a strata lot in a 2-lot strata plan — the buyer's diligence runs through the Form B, the strata's bylaws and records, and the §3 strata block; the other half is a separate strata lot owned by someone else and is not part of the sale. (Program definition above; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)
- **Selling one half of a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex:** the seller is selling a fee-simple parcel — no Form B, no strata fees — with the party-wall / easement documents on title as the buyer's diligence route (§3). (Program definition above.)
- **What this page itself says to sellers (page content, carried as such):** a home doesn't sell because it is overpriced, not marketed well enough, or doesn't show well; pricing high and reducing later fails because the first two weeks on market are critical; selling costs are commission, cleaning/staging/prep, legal fees and disbursements, and a possible mortgage penalty, with property transfer tax paid by the buyer; a seller is not legally required to use a REALTOR®; and listing contracts carry an agreed expiry date. None of that is duplex-specific on the page, and none is made duplex-specific here. (Research file, §4; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The regulator's consumer framework (BCFSA, verified in the sibling selling pass and reused in this slug's research):** a licensed real estate professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; the representation, duties and BCFSA complaint-route disclosures must happen at the outset of dealings. This page — like the sibling selling page — states no commission rate or amount, which BCFSA's framework places in the listing conversation. (Research file, §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers)
- **Selling without a licence (BCFSA):** a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves — for example, by selling or renting out their own property — while a person must not provide real estate services to or on behalf of another, for or in expectation of remuneration, unless licensed or exempt. That supports this page's "not legally required" answer. (Research file, §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information, read via search result 2026-11-01)
- **Deposits and the rescission period (BCFSA, verified in the sibling selling pass and reused here):** where a buyer rescinds an accepted offer under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining deposit balance to the buyer, with no separate release form; otherwise a deposit is held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder, and is not automatically returned if subjects are not removed. The live FAQ page does not itself raise the rescission period; the sibling selling page does. (Research file, §4; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits)
- **STRATA block — applies where the thing being sold is a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan).** The buyer's Form B diligence applies in full, and a seller should expect it. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; what reports the strata holds is still a buyer question. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022 — an old "no rentals" bylaw line is not a selling feature a seller can promise. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. This block does NOT apply to a fee-simple half duplex or an undivided duplex — those have no strata, no Form B and no strata bylaws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A seller cannot create a sellable "half" by wording: a fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the applicable Approving Officer process under the municipality's servicing bylaw, and party-wall obligations between two existing fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. No municipal process detail is asserted in this topic file beyond that. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — the buyer's tax, worked examples with inputs + tier math.** BC property transfer tax is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office — the page's repeated statement that "property transfer tax is paid by the buyer, not the seller" matches that frame; it becomes the seller's own tax when they buy their next home. Rates: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax)
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. GST applies to new construction, not resales — so a resale duplex sale does not attract GST on this frame, and a seller of a newly built duplex is in new-housing territory where the buyer's rebate position is part of the price conversation. GST is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

These are the seller-FAQ questions this page itself answers, carried with the duplex seller's position where the form changes the answer. Page statements are labelled as such.

- **Q: Why isn't my home selling?** The page's answer: generally one or more of three reasons — it is overpriced relative to comparable recent sales and current competition, it is not marketed well enough, or it does not show well; the fix starts with a home evaluation and an honest comparison against recent sales, active listings and expired or terminated listings. For a duplex, the comparable set is other duplexes and half duplexes in the same legal form — a whole-duplex price cannot be built from half-duplex sales without adjusting for the land and the second unit. (Research file, §7; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html)
- **Q: Should I price high and lower later?** The page's answer: no — the first two weeks on market are when buyer and agent attention peaks, and a reduced listing reads as stale. (§§1–2 of the research; page content.)
- **Q: Do I need a REALTOR® to sell my home in BC?** No — BCFSA's licensing guidance confirms a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves, such as selling their own property; a licence (or an exemption) is required to provide real estate services to or on behalf of another person for remuneration. (§3.)
- **Q: What are the costs of selling a home in BC?** The page lists real estate commission (split between the listing agent and the buyer's agent), cleaning, staging and preparation, legal fees and disbursements, and a possible mortgage penalty if the mortgage is broken early — and states no commission figure, which BCFSA's framework places in the listing conversation, where a licensee must give consumers information about commissions and fees. (§3.)
- **Q: Do I have to accept an offer on my home?** The page's answer: no — on receiving an offer you can accept it as-is, counter, or reject it altogether; a counter voids the previous offer. Read your own listing contract's stated terms before relying on any general statement about when an offer must be accepted, and take that question to your agent or lawyer. The page's further "may be required to accept" claim is UNVERIFIED as stated — see §5. (§5.)
- **Q: Who pays the property transfer tax when I sell?** The buyer: PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office — when you buy your next home, you pay it as the buyer. (§3.)
- **Q: Can I sell just half of my duplex?** Only if the half already exists as its own legal interest — a strata lot in a 2-lot strata plan, or a fee-simple parcel created by subdivision. An undivided duplex sells whole; creating a sellable half is a strata-conversion or subdivision project with its own approvals, not listing wording. (§§2–3.)
- **Q: Will renovations increase my duplex's value?** The page's position: renovations rarely return 100% of cost, so get a walkthrough opinion on what will actually move buyers before spending. The page's specific return ranges are UNVERIFIED as stated — see §5. (Research file, §§4–5; page content.)
