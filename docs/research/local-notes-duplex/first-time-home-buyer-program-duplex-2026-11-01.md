## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together at the whole price, which is the price the program thresholds in §3 are tested against. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

**TOPIC — NO ROSTER, stated at the top of this section.** This slug has no ground and verifies no duplex stock of any form — no named duplex, no strata half duplex, no fee-simple half duplex, and no form-class claim for any place. None is invented and none is imported from any area file. What this file carries instead is the program treatment of a duplex purchase, stated as program rules only:

- **A resale duplex or half duplex as a first home:** the purchase is tested against the BC first-time buyers' PTT program like any other principal-residence purchase — exempt on the first $500,000 at a fair market value of $835,000 or less, partial between $835,000 and $860,000, on a property 0.5 hectares or smaller containing only residential improvements and used only as the buyer's principal residence (§3). Whether a duplex containing a rental unit or a suite meets the "used only as a principal residence" and "only residential improvements" conditions is a per-purchase question for the buyer's lawyer or notary on the Province's stated conditions — this file asserts no answer for any specific property. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers)
- **A newly built duplex or half duplex as a first home:** two further programs come into play — the provincial newly built home exemption (full threshold $1,100,000, phase-out to $1,150,000) and the federal FTHB GST/HST rebate on new housing (100% of GST to $1,000,000, maximum $50,000, phasing to $1,500,000) — each on its own conditions in §3. A resale attracts no GST; a new build does, which is why the two forms price differently to a first-time buyer. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html)
- **What the page itself presents (content inventory, not stock):** a cash-to-close explainer whose "stock" is any BC first home — resale homes (PTT with the first-time exemption), new construction (GST with the federal rebates, plus the newly built home exemption) and partner-split purchases — worked through a $700,000 resale townhouse example, a nine-program table, and PTT price-band examples ($500,000 / $510,000 Surrey / $500,000 Langley with a non-qualifying partner). ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **BC PTT — general rates and the first-time buyers' program (Province, verified via the sibling passes and reused in this slug's research):** general rates are 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000; the tax is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office. A qualifying first-time buyer (Canadian citizen or permanent resident; lived in BC at least a year immediately before registration or filed at least 2 income tax returns as a BC resident in the last 6 taxation years; never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world; never received the exemption or refund before) is exempt on the first $500,000 where the property is used only as a principal residence, has a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), is 0.5 hectares or smaller and contains only residential improvements; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000; where only some purchasers qualify, only the qualifying purchasers' percentage of interest is eligible; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and occupy the home as a principal residence to the first anniversary of registration to keep the full exemption. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers)
- **Newly built home exemption (Province, verified in the sibling passes):** effective April 1, 2024, the fair market value threshold for a full exemption is $1,100,000, with a partial exemption phasing out over the $50,000 range above it and complete elimination at $1,150,000 for qualifying purchasers. The page's FAQ figure of "up to $20,000" at the $1,100,000 threshold was checked by arithmetic in the research and is consistent with the verified rates ($2,000 at 1% on the first $200,000 plus $18,000 at 2% on the remaining $900,000). (Research file, §§4–5; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (the anchors this file follows; the page's own compressed row is dealt with in §5, never here):** fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. Have a lawyer or notary model the exact bill, exemption included, from the current Province pages before offering. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Federal GST rebates = new HOUSING only (CRA, verified via the sibling passes):** the FTHB GST/HST rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) together with the GST/HST new housing rebate provides 100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million — a maximum of up to $50,000 — is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more; eligibility turns on a builder agreement entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (owner-built construction beginning on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031), construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036, and the buyer being at least 18, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and a first-time buyer under the four-calendar-year look-back; claiming is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction, by builder credit at closing or directly to the CRA (Form GST190; Form GST191 for owner-built homes). The pre-existing New Housing Rebate stands on its own terms: generally 36% of the GST paid, maximum $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and not available at $450,000 or more. Any straight-line formula for the FTHB reduction between $1 million and $1.5 million is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism — the CRA publication says only "gradually reduced." GST is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. (Research file, §4; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html)
- **Savings accounts and down payment (CRA / CMHC frame, verified in the sibling passes):** FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000, and contributions are generally deductible; the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. The settled ledger figures carried in this program are HBP $60,000 and FHSA lifetime $40,000 ($8,000/yr) — in THIS slug's own research the page's HBP figures and the FHSA lifetime figure are carried as UNVERIFIED (the CRA HBP page returned 403 in the sibling passes, and the lifetime limit was not stated on the CRA main page read), so they are stated here as the ledger's carried figures with that research position disclosed, never as this file's verification. Mortgage loan insurance is required when a down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more (where insurance is not available); **CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount**, and the premium can be paid in full or added to the mortgage. (Research file, §4; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html; https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained)
- **STRATA block — applies where the first home is a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan).** The buyer's Form B diligence applies in full. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. This block does NOT apply to a fee-simple half duplex or an undivided duplex purchase. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the applicable Approving Officer process under the municipality's servicing bylaw; party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. No municipal process detail is asserted in this topic file beyond that. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **Home owner grant context (verified in the sibling passes):** the grant reduces annual property tax on a principal residence and must be applied for each year; as published, the regular grant amount is $570 effective January 1, 2027 and the 2026 full-grant threshold is $2,075,000 assessed value — the live page's wording on those two points matches that frame. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much property transfer tax does a first-time buyer in BC actually save on a duplex?** The same program, the same test: a qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price where the property's fair market value is $835,000 or less, it is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the buyer's principal residence; above $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. Test a specific duplex against each stated condition with a lawyer or notary — this file asserts no answer for a property with a rental unit or suite. (§3.)
- **Q: How much GST can a first-time buyer get back on a newly built duplex?** Up to $50,000: together with the new housing rebate, the FTHB GST/HST rebate returns 100% of the GST on a new or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more, for builder agreements entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. A resale duplex attracts no GST and uses none of this. (§3.)
- **Q: What happened to the old GST new housing rebate?** It still exists on its own terms: generally 36% of the GST paid, to a maximum of $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and unavailable at $450,000 or more. (§3.)
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?** Yes — the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal; FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000. (§3.)
- **Q: Is a newly built home treated differently from a resale home?** Yes: the newly built home exemption has a full-exemption PTT threshold of $1,100,000 with a phase-out to $1,150,000, and a new home may also attract GST, which the federal FTHB rebate can offset by up to $50,000 for eligible buyers whose builder agreement dates from on or after March 20, 2025. (§3.)
- **Q: What PTT do I pay at $1,500,000 without any exemption? At $3,500,000?** On the worked tiers in §3 — fully residential, no exemption — $28,000 at $1,500,000 and $93,000 at $3,500,000 including the further 2% tier. Do not price from the live page's compressed "Over $3,000,000 — 5%" table row — see §5. (§§3, 5.)
- **Q: The page's worked example shows a $26,200 premium on a $700,000 townhouse — can I use its premium range for my duplex budget?** No — the example's premium leg uses the page's own 2.80%–4.00% premium range, which is UNVERIFIED and conflicts with CMHC's published 0.6%–4.5% range; its PTT legs ($4,000 with the exemption versus $12,000 without) do follow the verified rates and exemption. Price insurance from the CMHC range and a lender quote. (§5.)
- **Q: Do the two "first-time buyer" tests match?** No — BC's PTT program is a never-owned test (never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world, never received the exemption before), while the federal GST rebate uses a four-calendar-year look-back. A buyer can pass one and fail the other; test the specific purchase against each program's own conditions in §3. (Research file, §4.)
