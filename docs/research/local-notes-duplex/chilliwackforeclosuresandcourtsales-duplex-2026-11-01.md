## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

**TOPIC — NO ROSTER at the top: this file carries no duplex roster, no count and no named duplex stock. In process terms, any mortgaged property can be the subject of a Rule 21-7 proceeding — the Rule covers "mortgaged property" without a property-type limit — so a whole duplex, a strata half duplex or a fee-simple half duplex can each appear inside a Chilliwack foreclosure file, but no specific duplex property, complex or proceeding is verified or listed here.** https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

- **Whole duplex vs half duplex inside a proceeding — the distinction that matters:** a non-strata duplex is one mortgaged property on one title — the proceeding, the redemption amount and any court-ordered sale all attach to the whole building and lot. A strata half duplex is its own strata lot and its own title — a proceeding against that lot does not, by itself, sell the other half, and the strata layer (§3) applies on top of the court layer. A fee-simple half duplex is likewise its own parcel and title. Which form a given listing is must be read off title, never assumed from the word "duplex" in listing copy.
- **Directory / listing mentions = RECORDS ONLY.** A feed card or listing description calling a Chilliwack property a court-ordered duplex is a record of marketing wording, never verification of title form, proceeding stage or sale conditions. Occupancy (vacant or occupied), condition, and whether a property is in fact under an order are matters of the individual court file — UNVERIFIED for any general statement. (Research file above, §5.)
- **Age restrictions inside a court sale:** the settled SPA position still governs the strata lot being sold — only a 55+ age bylaw is valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts — but whether a specific court sale's conditions surface the bylaws at all sits inside the UNCONFIRMED document position in §3. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The settled court-process treatment (carried program-wide).** A foreclosure proceeding is started **by petition** in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7 (1), read directly in this slug's research. All persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished and all persons against whom relief is sought must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner may also join a claim arising out of the mortgage and a person liable to pay the mortgage debt — Rule 21-7 (3). If the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation, a person who subsequently registers an interest need not be served, is bound by an order made, and may file a response to petition — Rule 21-7 (4). A petitioner serving the petition must include the informational notice for foreclosure proceedings in **Form 125** — Rule 21-7 (4.1). The court may make a **final order of foreclosure** — Rule 21-7 (5)(a); order payment of what is due within a **redemption period the court fixes**, with foreclosure of the equity of redemption in default — Rule 21-7 (5)(b) (the Rule text read states **no fixed length** — the period is the one the court fixes); and determine summarily, or order an account taken and certified, the amount due — Rule 21-7 (5)(c). Conduct of sale, court directions and sale-approval mechanics sit in **Rule 13-5**; the foreclosure practice direction is **PD-66**; sale certification is by **Form 60** — each carried here as the program's settled treatment (see the FILE STATUS research-stance note). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. The court file, not the listing copy, is the deal's foundation. (§3.)
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rule text — it is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5)(b). Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections; redemption can end the deal. (§3.)
- **Q: Can the first order already be final?** Yes — the court's powers under Rule 21-7 (5) include making a final order of foreclosure. Do not assume every file is at an early, reversible stage. (§3.)
- **Q: If the property is a duplex, am I buying the whole building or half of it?** Read it off title: a non-strata duplex sells whole on one title; a strata or fee-simple half sells on its own title, and a proceeding against one half does not by itself sell the other. Listing wording never settles this. (§2.)
- **Q: Will I get a Form B and strata documents on a court-ordered strata half duplex?** UNCONFIRMED as a general rule — no source states a court sale will or will not produce the full package. Get the specific sale's conditions and document list in writing from the seller's counsel, confirmed by your own lawyer. (§3.)
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer at the approval hearing?** Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for: conduct-of-sale and competing-bid mechanics sit in Rule 13-5 and PD-66 (settled treatment, §3), but this slug's research could not open those texts (429), so rely on the bid instructions for the specific file — not on the live page's sealed-bid account, which is not adopted here. (§3; research file §4.)
- **Q: How are PTT and GST handled on a court-ordered purchase?** The general PTT tiers are worked in §3, but any court-sale-specific PTT/GST treatment is UNCONFIRMED — have your lawyer cost the exact position for the specific property before setting a ceiling. (§3.)
- **Q: Why are there no foreclosure counts or discount figures in this file?** Chilliwack is a CADREB market SnapStats does not cover, CADREB publishes board-wide statistics only with no foreclosure category, and no official source publishes Chilliwack foreclosure counts — so any count or "foreclosure discount" is UNVERIFIED here; a figure would require a Paragon pull. (§1.)
- **Q: I'm the owner in difficulty — what matters most?** The redemption period the court fixes under Rule 21-7 (5)(b) is the window to pay what is due before the equity of redemption is foreclosed. Earlier advice is worth more than late advice. (§3.)
