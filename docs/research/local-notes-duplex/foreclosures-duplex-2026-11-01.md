## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The proceeding — CONFIRMED text (Rule 21-7 (1)–(5)(c), BC Laws, batch-10 verification; declared reuse in this slug's research).** Step 1 — the petition: "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7 (1). Parties: all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished and all persons against whom any relief is sought must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner may also join a claim arising out of the mortgage or collateral security and a person liable to pay the mortgage debt — Rule 21-7 (3). Later interests: if the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation, a person who subsequently registers or files an interest need not be served, is bound by an order made in the proceeding, and may file a response to petition — Rule 21-7 (4). Information notice: a petitioner who serves the petition must include the informational notice for foreclosure proceedings in Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). Powers of the court: the court may make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7 (5)(a); order payment of what is due within a redemption period the court may fix, with foreclosure of the equity of redemption in default — Rule 21-7 (5)(b); and determine summarily, or order an account and registrar certification of, the amount due — Rule 21-7 (5)(c). The redemption period has no fixed length in the Rule text read: it is the period the court fixes in its order. A duplex or half-duplex subject property is in the same proceeding, on the same rules — the Rule covers "mortgaged property" without a property-type limit. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md, §4; https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03, read 2026-11-01; batch-10 verification: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-10/chilliwackforeclosuresandcourtsales.md, read 2026-11-01.)
