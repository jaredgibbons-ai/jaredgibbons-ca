## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The settled court process (carried over this slug's narrower research stance — see FILE STATUS).** A foreclosure proceeding for mortgaged property must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7 (1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). The court may order payment of what is due within a redemption period the court fixes (there is no fixed length in the Rules) — Rule 21-7 (5) (b); in default, a final order of foreclosure may be granted — Rule 21-7 (6); a party of record may apply at any time for an order that the property be sold — Rule 21-7 (7); and the person having conduct of a sale may apply, even before the redemption period expires, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7 (9). (Research file above; https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. (§3.)
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5) (b). Ask which stage the file is at before spending money. (§3.)
- **Q: Is my accepted offer final?** No. The person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7 (9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title — and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires. Until that order, the sale is not final; do not book movers on acceptance. (§3.)
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for: bid and offer rights sit in Rule 13-5 and the court's sale directions / PD-66 for the specific file — get the seller's counsel's bid instructions in writing before spending on inspections. (§3.)
- **Q: Where do I check the court file for a Ladner foreclosure?** Court Services Online eSearch — file number, registry, party names and filed documents; foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure". Delta has no registry of its own, and which registry serves Ladner/Delta is UNVERIFIED — the file itself states its registry. (§3; https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do)
- **Q: What PTT and GST will I pay on a court-ordered duplex purchase?** UNCONFIRMED for court-ordered sales specifically — have your lawyer cost both on the specific property before you set your ceiling. The general PTT tiers are worked in §3; do not use any shorthand rate. (§3.)
- **Q: Will I get a Form B on a court-ordered strata half duplex?** UNCONFIRMED whether a Form B package is even available on a court-ordered strata sale — ask the seller's counsel what strata documents exist for the file, and price the information gap in. (§2/§3.)
- **Q: What cleans the title on a court-ordered purchase?** The court's vesting order on confirmation under Rule 21-7 (9), which directs the disposition of the proceeds and vests title in the purchaser. (§2/§3.)
