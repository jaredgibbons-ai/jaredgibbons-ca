## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. A farm residence on ALR land is neither form by itself: it is a residence within the ALC housing frame in §3, and ALR residences cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately on the sibling position carried in §3. A hobby property's chicken coop or shed is not a dwelling unit in either form and carries no quota position as stated here. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing; ALR frame per the research file below.)

- **ALR housing frame (province-wide, ALC) — the rule that shapes the farm residence.** On ALR land, one principal residence is permitted without an ALC application at a maximum total floor area of 500 m² (no more than 1,000 m² of fill), a secondary suite is permitted only when located within the principal residence, and at most one additional residence is permitted of up to 90 m² on properties 40 ha or less or up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha; local government or First Nation government bylaws may further regulate or restrict residences, and may not be more permissive than the ALC's requirements. (Research file above, §4; https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Additional manufactured-home residence on farm ground (as this slug's siblings carry it).** A manufactured home encountered on farm ground is a farm residence under the ALR frame above — and, for an additional manufactured-home residence that is not a pre-existing structure, the siblings' position is: ONLY via ALR Use Regulation s.34(3) / ALC Policy L-25 (9 m or less in width; occupied only by the owner or specified relatives) or via a Non-Adhering Residential Use (NARU) approval. **A general farm-worker manufactured-housing entitlement is UNCONFIRMED.** No pad tenancy exists on a farm parcel unless a park tenancy is separately documented. The siblings also carry: no stratification of ALR residences — ALR residences cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.23, as cited in the sibling). (Mobile-homes sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/mobile-homes/chicken-farms-mobile-homes-2026-11-01.md; acreages sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/acreages/chicken-farms-acreages-2026-11-01.md.)
- **BC Assessment farm classification (province-wide).** Farm classification is a voluntary, property-by-property program; the Classification of Land as a Farm Regulation income thresholds are $2,500 if the total area of the farm operation is between 0.8 ha (1.98 acres) and 4 ha (10 acres), $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if the operation is greater than 4 ha, and $10,000 if the operation is less than 0.8 ha; applications must be submitted to BC Assessment by the legislated deadline of October 31 for consideration for the next assessment and tax year, late applications are not accepted, and a buyer who plans to continue farming a farm-classified property must submit a General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase.
