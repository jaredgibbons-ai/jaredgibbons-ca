## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **Plan (Surrey frame):** the corridor's designations now come from the Surrey 2050 OCP (Bylaw 21900, adopted Jun 15, 2026), following Council's December 2, 2024 endorsement of Corporate Report R246 — work from the current plan, not the 1995 concept plan, and check the parcel's current designation and zone in the City's COSMOS map before pricing development potential. Every Surrey property carries a zone under Surrey Zoning By-law No. 12000. (Research file, §§1, 4, 6; https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/king-george-corridor-south)
- **Zoning, SSMUH and suites (Surrey frame — SETTLED in the research):** Surrey's SSMUH framework allows a maximum of **3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop**, applying to eligible residential lots under 4,050 m² within the Urban Containment Boundary, with exemptions including certain hazard lands and properties not connected to community water and sewer — the City adopted the SSMUH zoning changes on July 8, 2024. Homes fronting the boulevard's frequent bus service may fall inside a Frequent Bus Stop Area — check the parcel in COSMOS. Duplexes are the baseline SSMUH form. Surrey permits only **one secondary suite per single-family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit, at most 40% of the principal unit's habitable floor area, and never inside a coach house or garden suite** — Surrey's own cap, never borrowed; the old 90 m² Surrey suite cap is NOT a current rule. Servicing Bylaw 8830 (1986) is VERIFIED. Streamside setbacks sit in Part 7A of Zoning 12000 and vary by stream class — no single riparian figure is ever stated. (Research file, §4; https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing; https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite)
- **Floodplain at the north end:** the corridor's north edge is the Nicomekl River, and Surrey's coastal floodplain runs along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers and the Serpentine–Nicomekl lowlands. Parcel-level flood levels: UNVERIFIED — confirmed with the City per parcel. (Research file, §4; https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands)
- **Heritage frontage:** the boulevard follows part of the Semiahmoo Trail, a route in use by 1858, widened for wagons in 1872 and protected by Surrey Heritage Designation Bylaw, 2004, No. 15280; royal oaks grown from seedlings sent from England in 1937 for the coronation of King George VI still line the road, most survivors between the Nicomekl River and the Highway 99 junction at 8 Avenue — heritage trees and the trail can affect frontage work near them (guide page, site copy attributed to City of Surrey heritage pages). (Research file, §4; https://jaredgibbons.ca/king-george-corridor-guide.html)
- **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan) on Surrey ground.** Form B diligence applies in full. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the City's Approving Officer process under Servicing Bylaw 8830 (1986), and the parcel's zone decides whether a corridor lot can split at all. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief; research file, §4.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math.** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. Have a lawyer or notary model the exact bill from the current Province pages before offering. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. GST applies to new construction, not resales. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Surrey taxes:** City of Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2. (Research file, §4; https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes)
