## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **No city frame is carried for this topic slug.** No single city zoning bylaw, OCP, suite cap, SSMUH position or servicing bylaw applies to a province-wide FAQ page, and none is stated — the zoning, suite rule (e.g. Surrey 40% of principal habitable area; Township 120 m²; Langley City lesser of 100 m² / 40%; Abbotsford under 50% — each city's own rule, never borrowed) and SSMUH position for a specific duplex are the ones named on that property's own area file. (Condos sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/condos/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc-condos-2026-11-01.md; research file, §4.)
- **Secondary suites inside a duplex:** governed by the municipality the duplex sits in, under that city's own suite rule as above — no province-wide suite cap is stated in this slug's research, so none is asserted here. (Research file, §4.)
- **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan) wherever in BC it sits.** Form B diligence applies in full. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds (the FAQ page's own subject list names strata documents including the depreciation report). Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the Approving Officer process of the municipality the parcel sits in; party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. No municipality-specific process detail is asserted for this province-wide topic. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (verified — Province).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000; the tax is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office, and PTT cannot be blended into the mortgage. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. First-time buyers' program: a qualifying buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 of the price where the property is a principal residence with fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), 0.5 hectares or smaller, residential improvements only; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000. Have a lawyer or notary model the exact bill before offering. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers)
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** GST of 5% applies to newly built and presale homes and not to resale homes. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) provides 100% of the GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), gradually reduced between $1,000,000 and $1,500,000, none at $1,500,000 or more, for agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, phasing out $350,000–$450,000. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. A resale duplex or resale half duplex carries no GST on this frame; a newly built one does. (Research file, §4; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html)
- **Down payment and mortgage insurance (verified — CMHC, via the research):** insurance is required under 20% down; minimum down payment is 5% to $500,000, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 to $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more; CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount. (Research file, §4; https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Does this FAQ page cover buying half a duplex?** Only in process terms. The page is a general BC buyer FAQ with no roster and no ground (§2); a half duplex is bought either as a strata lot (2-lot strata plan — strata-document subject, Form B diligence, §3 strata block) or as a fee-simple lot after subdivision (title search subject carries the party-wall position). Whether a specific listing is either form is UNCONFIRMED from this file — it is settled in that property's own area file. (§§2–3.)
- **Q: What is a subject, and why does it matter more on a strata half duplex?** A subject is a condition that must be met before the deal becomes firm — the page lists financing, inspection, property disclosure statement, title search and strata document review as the common subjects. On a strata purchase the strata-document subject is the window to read the Form B material and whatever depreciation reporting the strata holds before removal; after removal the deal is firm and the deposit is at risk. (Research file, §§6–7; https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html)
- **Q: How much PTT on a $1,500,000 duplex? On a $2,000,000 one?** On the worked tiers in §3 — fully residential, no exemption — $28,000 at $1,500,000 and $38,000 at $2,000,000. A half duplex is taxed on its own price as its own transfer. Have a lawyer or notary model the exact bill before offering. (§3.)
- **Q: Do I pay GST on a duplex?** Only if it is new housing: 5% GST applies to newly built and presale homes, not to resales. A qualifying first-time buyer can recover up to $50,000 through the FTHB GST/HST rebate (100% to $1M, gradually reduced to $1.5M, none at $1.5M+), and the pre-existing new housing rebate alone returns 36% of the GST to a maximum of $6,300, phasing out $350,000–$450,000. (§3.)
- **Q: What does a first-time buyer save on PTT?** A qualifying buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 of the price at a fair market value of $835,000 or less, with only a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000 — test the target price against $835,000 first. (§3.)
- **Q: Can I add a suite inside a duplex I buy?** That is the host municipality's rule, not this page's: each city sets its own suite cap and conditions (Surrey 40% of principal habitable area is one city's rule, never a provincial one). Check the property's own area file and the municipality before pricing rental potential. (§3.)
- **Q: The page states a 3-business-day rescission period with a 0.25% fee — can I rely on it for a subject-free offer?** Not on this file: that statement is UNVERIFIED here — no BCFSA rescission-period page was opened in this slug's research pass, and it is recorded as the page's statement only. Confirm the current rescission rules with your REALTOR® or lawyer before writing without subjects. (Research file, §§4, 6 and UNVERIFIED list.)
- **Q: Can I combine the Home Buyers' Plan and the FHSA, and how much can I take out?** The CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the HBP for the same qualifying home if all conditions are met at each withdrawal (verified in the sibling passes). The amounts are a different matter in THIS slug's research: the page's HBP figure ($60,000) and FHSA figure ($40,000) are UNVERIFIED there — the CRA HBP page returned 403 in this program and the CRA FHSA main page verifies only the $8,000 first-year room. (The 55-plus sibling of this slug verifies both figures from live CRA pages in its own pass — a sibling position, stated there, not imported as this research file's verification.) Confirm both limits with the CRA or your lender before planning around them. (Research file, §7 and UNVERIFIED list; 55-plus sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md)
- **Q: What deposit and timing norms does the page state?** As page statements only, each UNVERIFIED as a stated norm in this file: a deposit typically around 5% due on or within 24 hours of subject removal; a typical subject period of 7 days; an inspection fee of $400–$700; closing costs of 1.5%–4% of price; pre-approval holds of 90–120 days; completion commonly 2–3 months after acceptance.
