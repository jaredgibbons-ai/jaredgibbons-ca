## 1. THE TOPIC FRAME — NO STREETS, NO BOUNDARIES

- Scope: a buyer's guide to purchasing a manufactured home (the term BC legislation uses; "mobile home" is the everyday term) anywhere in British Columbia. There is no MLS sub-area for this guide and no single municipality — the rules below are the provincial framework, with municipal zoning varying by city. Search names: "mobile home," "manufactured home" (the legal term), and "mobile home park" / "manufactured home park" / "manufactured home community"; buyers also search by city — those are separate listing pages, and this guide is the how-it-works page. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-1/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-guide.md, §1; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes; https://jaredgibbons.ca/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-guide.html)
- The two ownership forms the guide is built around: (1) you own the home and rent the pad (site) it sits on in a manufactured home park — the land is the park owner's; (2) you own both the home and the land it sits on. Which law applies follows the form: the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA) applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land you own is dealt with through the land title system instead. (Research file, §1; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes)
- Terminology caution: a manufactured home is factory-built to the CSA standard and registered in the Manufactured Home Registry; it is not the same product class as a site-built home, and the registry / CSA-label rules in §3 are what separate a registrable manufactured home from an unregistered unit. The product is factory-built single-section ("single-wide") and multi-section ("double-wide") homes carrying a CSA certification label — the label is a legal requirement at the point of sale. (Research file, §§1, 5; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry)

## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK — TOPIC POSITION: NO ROSTER

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing.)

**TOPIC — NO ROSTER, stated at the top of this section.** This topic file has no ground, so it verifies no duplex, no half duplex and no park, and it invents none. The duplex-program point of this topic is a classification point, stated plainly:

- **A manufactured home is never duplex stock in either legal form.** A manufactured home is a factory-built, CSA-labelled chattel registered in the Manufactured Home Registry — it is not one half of a two-unit building on a single title, not a 2-lot strata plan, and not a subdivided fee-simple pair. A listing or directory line that calls a park home a "duplex," "half duplex" or "townhouse" is a RECORD ONLY of marketing wording, never verification of a separable title — the guide mobile-homes sibling carries the standing program example (a park home described in townhouse terms is still a manufactured home under the MHPTA if you own the home and rent the pad). (Research file, §§1, 5; guide mobile-homes sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/mobile-homes/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-guide-mobile-homes-2026-11-01.md.)
- **THE TENURE LINE, in full: a home on a RENTED PAD is not land ownership — the buyer owns the home as a chattel and rents the pad.** In the pad form there is no land title, no strata lot and no Form B; the ongoing cost is pad rent under a tenancy agreement, and park rules govern daily life. The same physical home on land you own is a different purchase entirely: it is taxed as an improvement and dealt with as real property through the land title system, and where the ground is held as a bare-land strata the buyer owns the lot as a strata lot (Form B, strata fees and registered bylaws — no pad rent), and in a co-operative the land is held through the co-op. Tenure, not the home, decides which file a purchase belongs in. (Research file, §§1, 5; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home)
- **Park setting:** parks are purpose-zoned land — there is no province-wide park zoning; each municipality zones manufactured home parks in its own bylaw (for example, Surrey's Zoning Bylaw No. 12000 has a Manufactured Home Residential Zone (RM-M) permitting manufactured home parks, and Maple Ridge's Zoning Bylaw 7600-2019 has an RMH Manufactured Home Park Residential zone). Park names and addresses from an official municipal or provincial list: UNVERIFIED — no such list was found in the research. No specific park's age restriction is verified at topic level; the Province's general rule for a designated 55-plus park is that at least one tenant on the site must be 55 years of age or older — a park rule under tenancy law, not a Strata Property Act age bylaw. The age/style profile of BC's manufactured home stock and buyer-type data are UNVERIFIED. **SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies.** (Research file, §§4–5; https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7915-0417-00.pdf; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes)
- **Age statements (strata side, for contrast only):** where a manufactured home sits in a bare-land strata, the settled strata position governs that strata: only a 55+ age bylaw is valid (Strata Property Act ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. That position does not convert a pad-rental park's 55-plus rule into a strata bylaw — the two age rules live in different statutes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT A MANUFACTURED-HOME PURCHASE (THE MHPTA BLOCK)

- **Assignment — MHPTA s.28 (the core of a park purchase).** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply to the tenancy. On assignment, the purchaser becomes the tenant of the park owner, assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the tenancy agreement from the assignment date, and the agreement continues on the same terms as before. (Research file, §4; https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003; https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000)
- **Consent — RTB Form 10, the 10-day deemed-consent rule, and no consent fee.** Assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the **director** — never "arbitrator" in this program's wording). The request is made in writing on the approved form (RTB Form 10); the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, and if there is no response by the end of the 10th day, consent is deemed to have been given. **The park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent.** If consent is unreasonably withheld, the home owner can apply for dispute resolution. Build the 10-day window into subject dates. (Research file, §§4, 6–7; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf)
- **Park rules — once a year, 3 months' notice.** Park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a landlord may change park rules only once a year and only with 3 full months' notice to tenants (the settled program position, carried with the research's notice rule). A rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. Park rules — set by the park committee or landlord within the Act — govern matters like parking, pets and exterior upkeep. (Research file, §§4–5; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy)
- **Pad rent — caps as RTB percentages ONLY.** A landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, capped by formula: the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) **plus a proportional amount** (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). **The verified RTB percentages are: 2026 = 2.3% + proportional; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional (verified live from RTB Nov 1, 2026, per the settled program ledger). NO pad-rent DOLLAR FIGURE is published as fact anywhere in this file** — a specific park's pad rent, and its increase history, are per-park facts to be read off the tenancy agreement and the park's own notices, never stated at topic level. Ask for the park's increase history to see its pattern. (Research file, §§4, 6; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes)
- **Park closure / redevelopment — MHPTA s.42 terms.** A landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have **all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice**, must give **12 months' notice** to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant **$20,000 compensation** on or before the notice's effective date; the settled program position also carries a **15-day dispute window** and **additional amounts tied to the home's assessed value and to $5,000 / 12 months' pad rent** — carried at that settled level here, with the transaction-specific figures confirmed against the Act and the RTB before reliance. A tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. A park with an active rezoning application is a different risk than a stable one — search the municipality's development applications for the park's address. (Research file, §§4, 6; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes)
- **Ownership registry + CSA label.** A sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership; on a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. A missing label is a red flag for financing and resale. (Research file, §§4, 6; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry)
- **Property tax and Home Owner Grant.** A manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home; https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant)
- **PTT — pad-resale position UNCONFIRMED; owned-land tiers worked in full.** **For a RESALE home on a rented pad, PTT treatment is UNCONFIRMED in this program** — the purchase is of the home and an assigned tenancy, not a registered transfer of land, and the stronger pad claims met elsewhere in the program (a flat "PTT does not apply on a pad" claim and the opposite PTT-on-a-pad claim) are both flagged UNVERIFIED wherever met and neither is adopted here, exactly as the guide acreages sibling carries them. Confirm a specific transaction against BC PTT guidance / a lawyer before writing. Separately, on the owned-land side, the Province's newly built home exemption from PTT expressly includes "a manufactured home that is placed and affixed on a parcel of vacant land" (not previously occupied) as a newly built home — relevant to buyers placing a NEW manufactured home on their own land, not to a resale home on a rented pad. For a registered land transfer where PTT does apply, the tiers are: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. (Research file, §4; guide acreages sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/acreages/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-guide-acreages-2026-11-01.md; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax)
- **STRATA contrast block — applies only where the ground is a strata (never to a pad tenancy).** A bare-land strata purchase runs on Form B diligence in full; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 (a 2-lot strata duplex, on the duplex side of this program, is BELOW that threshold on lot count alone); minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, May 1, 2023 exemptions). None of that machinery exists in a pad assignment — which is the point of the contrast the guide townhomes sibling draws. (Guide townhomes sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/townhomes/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-guide-townhomes-2026-11-01.md; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only, and GST at GI-003 level only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; whether a new manufactured home placed on owned land qualifies is transaction-specific — UNCONFIRMED per transaction. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. GST treatment of the manufactured home itself (new vs resale, builder status) is UNCONFIRMED at topic level; for land, GST is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only (most individuals' vacant-land sales kept for personal use are exempt, with taxable examples including land used in a business, land sold in the course of a business, and parcels created by subdividing into more than two parts). Financing rules (lender / mortgage-insurer treatment — chattel vs land-home, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in the research: UNVERIFIED — confirm financing terms for the specific home and tenure before removing subjects. (Research file, §§4, UNVERIFIED list; guide acreages sibling above; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html; https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: If I buy a mobile home in a park, do I own the land?** No. **A home on a rented pad is not land ownership — you own the home as a chattel and rent the pad.** The MHPTA applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on. (§§2–3.)
- **Q: The listing calls a park home a "half duplex" or "townhouse." Is it one?** No. A manufactured home is a factory-built chattel registered in the Manufactured Home Registry — it is neither legal form of half duplex (2-lot strata, or subdivided fee-simple pair), whatever the form field or listing copy says. If you own the home and rent the pad, the MHPTA governs. (§2.)
- **Q: Can the park owner refuse to let me buy a home in the park?** The sale needs the park owner's prior written consent to assign the tenancy (MHPTA s.28, on RTB Form 10) — but the owner must respond so you receive it by the 10th day, silence means consent is deemed given, no fee can be charged for consent, and if consent is unreasonably withheld the home owner can apply for dispute resolution (to the director). (§3.)
- **Q: What paperwork should I have before I write an offer?** The written tenancy agreement and the written park rules — the seller must give you both before consent to assign is even requested — plus the current pad rent and the date of the last increase, read off the park's own documents (no pad-rent dollar figure is stated at topic level). (§3.)
- **Q: How much can my pad rent go up each year?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the RTB cap: **2026 = 2.3% + proportional; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional** — inflation (BC CPI) plus the park's proportional levy/utility amount divided by the number of sites. Percentages only; no dollar figure is published as fact here. (§3.)
- **Q: What happens to me if the park is sold or redeveloped?** On a closure/conversion of all or a significant part of the park, the landlord must have all permits and approvals in place first, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date, with the settled additional assessed-value and $5,000 / 12-months-pad-rent amounts and a 15-day dispute window carried in §3. You may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. (§3.)
- **Q: How is the purchase actually registered?** Through the Manufactured Home Registry — the sale is only effective once registered. Your lawyer or notary files the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership, and the home must carry its CSA certification label at the point of sale. (§3.)
- **Q: Do I pay PTT when I buy a resale home on a rented pad?** UNCONFIRMED in this program — both stronger pad claims met elsewhere are flagged UNVERIFIED and neither is adopted. Confirm the specific transaction against BC PTT guidance / a lawyer before writing. The worked PTT tiers in §3 govern a registered land transfer (the owned-land form). (§3.)
- **Q: Does SSMUH give me rights over a pad?** No — SSMUH does not apply to pad tenancies. It is a municipal zoning framework for lots, not a tenancy right. (§2.)
- **Q: How do I check the park's future, not just the home?** Search the municipality's development applications for the park's address: a park with an active rezoning application is a different risk than a stable one, even though the s.42 closure terms (permits first, 12 months, $20,000) protect the tenant on an actual closure. (§3.)
