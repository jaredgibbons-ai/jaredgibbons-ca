## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **Zoning (New Westminster frame).** New Westminster Zoning Bylaw 6680 (2001) governs; the OCP governing is Bylaw 7925 (2017), with the City's Interim Development Review Framework (first endorsed 2024, updated June 2026) governing how applications are reviewed while policy changes are underway. **The RT districts are RT-2–RT-2C plus RT-3/3A/3B ONLY — RT-A/RT-B do NOT exist in the current bylaw, and the 836.10 m² figure is an RM apartment-district density threshold, never an RT figure** (the carrier in this slug's own 55-plus sibling is flagged in §5). The zone of a specific lot is UNCONFIRMED until read. (Research file above; townhomes sibling above; https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects, read via search result 2026-11-01.)
- **SSMUH / infill position (New Westminster frame).** In June 2026 Council adopted amendments to the OCP and Zoning Bylaw to permit **up to six units of infill housing (secondary suites, laneway and carriage houses, triplex, quadplex and other multiplex forms) on single detached and duplex properties across most of the city, under zones RGO-A (up to four units) and RGO-B (up to six units)**, removing the need for a site-specific rezoning; Bylaw No. 8453, 2024 had earlier rezoned the roughly 160 "restricted zone" duplex properties to RD-1 (§2). Queensborough SSMUH compliance runs on the extended provincial timeline to May 4, 2029. A duplex is the baseline two-unit form; infill potential does not by itself split a duplex into separately titled halves. (Research file above; https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects, read via search result 2026-11-01.)
- **Secondary suites inside a duplex — the city's own rule.** A secondary suite is a separate dwelling unit within a house, accessory to a single detached dwelling **or duplex** and contained within the same structure; most single detached dwellings and some duplexes in New Westminster are zoned to permit them; they must meet the Zoning Bylaw, Building Code and Tree Protection and Regulation Bylaw; **they cannot be strata-titled, sold or used for short-term rentals**, and all properties with suites are subject to annual secondary suite water and sewer charges. Suite size cap and owner-occupancy rule: UNVERIFIED — neither is stated in the City guide read for this slug, and no other city's figure is borrowed. (Research file above; https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Secondary_Suite_Guide_August_2025.pdf, read via search result 2026-11-01.)
- **Flood position — the CORRECTED ledger, own-only per ground.** **Queensborough = Floodplain Zone 1** (an earlier "Zone 3" for Queensborough was WRONG and is never carried — see the queensborough duplex file for its FCL and dyke record). **Quay + Downtown NW = Zone 3** (the Fraser River waterfront zone; the condos sibling notes many newer Quay buildings have floor space above the Flood Construction Level, while egress is the diligence point). **North Arm = Zone 2. - **Property taxes (New Westminster frame).** 2026 taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026; a 5% penalty applies to current taxes remaining (including unclaimed Home Owner Grants) on July 3, 2026, and a second 5% penalty applies to any unpaid balance on September 3, 2026. - **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan).** Form B diligence applies in full. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum contingency reserve fund (CRF) contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the City's Approving Officer process, against the current zoning minimums; no finer process detail is stated because no official source opened for this slug states it. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math.** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. A resale duplex or half duplex is not new housing for this rebate. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is there a named duplex development or half-duplex subdivision I can shop in New Westminster?** No named one is verified city-wide. What is documented is the form class plus the City's own record of roughly 160 "restricted zone" duplex properties rezoned to RD-1 in 2024 — a zoning fact, not a roster; no individual strata half or fee-simple half is named in this file. (§2.)
- **Q: What is the difference between buying a duplex and buying a half duplex here?** A duplex purchase is the whole building and lot on one title. A half duplex is one unit sold separately, which is only possible where the pair was strata-divided (a 2-lot strata plan — Form B diligence) or the parcel was subdivided fee-simple (party-wall title). If neither happened, there is no "half" to buy. (§2.)
- **Q: Are Portside, Jamieson Court or Clinton Place duplex options?** No — each is townhouse-form strata stock counted once in its sub-area townhomes home (Portside's duplex-form homes are documented in the queensborough duplex file with no count taken there either). Townhouse form is not duplex form. (§2.)
- **Q: How many units can a New Westminster duplex property hold now?** Since June 2026, up to six units of infill housing on single detached and duplex properties across most of the city, under RGO-A (up to four units) and RGO-B (up to six units), without a site-specific rezoning — and Queensborough runs on the extended provincial timeline to May 4, 2029. Verify the specific property's zone before writing either claim. (§3.)
- **Q: Can a suite inside a duplex be sold separately or rented short-term?** No — the City's guide states suites cannot be strata-titled, sold or used for short-term rentals, and properties with suites pay annual suite water and sewer charges. (§3.)
- **Q: Which flood zone is my duplex in?** It depends on the ground, and the zones are own-only: Queensborough Zone 1; Quay + Downtown Zone 3; North Arm Zone 2; Brunette Zone 4 (Brunette's own position only); Fraserview NW states its own position in its own files; Glenbrooke North and Queen's Park are UNVERIFIED — no zone is imported for them. (§3.)
- **Q: Does a 2-lot strata duplex need a depreciation report?** The 5-year cycle applies to stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; a 2-lot strata is below that threshold on lot count. Ask what the strata holds anyway, and run full Form B diligence. (§3.)
- **Q: What PTT do I budget on a New Westminster duplex purchase?** Run the tiers on your own price: $1,000,000 → $18,000; $1,500,000 → $28,000; $2,000,000 → $38,000; $3,500,000 → $93,000 including the further 2% tier (fully residential, no exemption). A half duplex is taxed on its own price as its own transfer. (§3.)
