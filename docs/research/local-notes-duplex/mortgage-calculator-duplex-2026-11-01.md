## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

**TOPIC — NO ROSTER at the top: this file names no duplex stock as its own and takes no count.** The stock this page actually serves is not a set of listings — it is every BC purchase a borrower might price into the tool, and the duplex-relevant branches are these, each a scenario class, never a property: **(i) a whole duplex on one title** — one purchase price, one mortgage, priced like any other single-title purchase against the branch points in §3; **(ii) a strata half duplex** — the half is its own strata lot with its own price, so the tool runs on the half's price alone, and 50% of the strata fees enter the GDS ratio (§3); **(iii) a fee-simple half duplex** — likewise its own lot and its own price, with no strata fee line. The price bands shape what the tool must branch on for any of the three: at $500,000 the down-payment tier changes; at $1,500,000 insurance disappears and 20% down becomes mandatory; at 20% down the insurance premium disappears and the amortization menu widens. (Research file above; https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained, read 2026-11-01.)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **How interest is calculated — semi-annual compounding.** Under the federal Interest Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. I-15, s.6, where principal and interest secured by a mortgage on real property are payable on a blended-payment plan, no interest is chargeable unless the mortgage contains a statement showing the principal and the rate of interest chargeable on it, "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" — the statutory basis for the Canadian convention that fixed mortgage rates are nominal rates compounded semi-annually, which is why a Canadian payment calculation converts the nominal rate using semi-annual compounding rather than dividing by 12. The live page states the same convention for fixed rates; its variable-rate monthly-compounding statement is lender practice, UNVERIFIED as a rule. (Research file above; https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html, read 2026-11-01; https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Minimum down payment (CMHC).** For homes $500,000 or less, a minimum 5% down payment; for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require a minimum down payment of 20% and mortgage loan insurance is not available. The Department of Finance confirms the same structure as the insured-mortgage rule from the December 15, 2024 cap increase. A half duplex is measured on its own price for these tiers. (Research file above; https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained, read 2026-11-01; https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Mortgage loan insurance (CMHC) — and THE DEFECT on this page.** Insurance is required when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the purchase price, on a home priced at $1.5 million or less; it protects the lender, not the buyer, and allows qualification with as little as 5% down; it is not available at all on homes priced over $1.5 million. **Premiums as published by CMHC range from 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount**, based on the down payment size and loan amount — the smaller the down payment, the higher the premium rate — and the premium can be paid in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. **The live page's stated range (2.80%–4.00% plus 0.20% for 30-year insured amortizations) does NOT match CMHC's published range and the surcharge was not confirmed on a CMHC or Finance page: the page's range is WRONG against CMHC and both figures are UNVERIFIED here (Jerry item, §5).** The other two approved insurers (Sagen and Canada Guaranty) are named on the live page; their premium schedules were not read in this pass and no figure from them is stated here. (Research file above; https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained, read 2026-11-01; https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **The stress test — OSFI Guideline B-20 / minimum qualifying rate.** OSFI's minimum qualifying rate (MQR) for uninsured mortgages is **the greater of the mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25%** (buffer currently 2%, floor currently 5.25%; OSFI reviews both at least annually). Federally regulated lenders must apply it, and FCAC states the same test applies to insured and uninsured mortgages. The renewal-switch exception: OSFI does not expect lenders to apply the MQR to an uninsured straight switch at renewal with no increase to the amortization period and no increase to the loan amount; a refinance or a new HELOC triggers the test again. Whether any given BC credit union (provincially regulated) applies it is lender-specific and UNVERIFIED here. Run the payment twice — at the contract rate (what you pay) and at the qualifying rate (what sizes the approval). (Research file above; https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages, read 2026-11-01; https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Amortization.** Insured mortgages are those with less than 20% down, available only on homes priced at $1.5 million or less (cap raised from $1 million effective December 15, 2024). Eligibility for 30-year insured amortizations was extended to **all first-time homebuyers** and to **all buyers of new builds**, effective December 15, 2024. The exact general insured maximum (25 years) for insured borrowers outside those two groups was NOT stated on a regulator page re-read in the research pass and is not asserted as verified here; the Department of Finance's detailed first-time-homebuyer criteria for the 30-year measure are likewise UNVERIFIED. A longer amortization lowers the payment at any given rate and raises total interest — arithmetic on the user's own inputs. (Research file above; https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Affordability ratios — GDS and TDS (FCAC).** Gross Debt Service: total monthly housing costs should not be more than **39%** of gross household income — housing costs are the mortgage payments, property taxes, heating costs and **50% of condo/strata fees** (the strata-half-duplex line a generic calculator misses). Total Debt Service: total debt load should not be more than **44%** of gross income, adding all other debts. FCAC frames both as guidelines, not hard approval cut-offs, and the ratios are calculated at the qualifying rate. A calculator computes payments and guideline ratios; it does not approve a mortgage — the underwriting decision sits with the lender under Guideline B-20. (Research file above; https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **STRATA block — applies where the purchase priced into the tool is a strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan).** Form B diligence applies in full on an ordinary purchase. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**. Minimum contingency reserve fund (CRF) contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. For the calculator itself, the strata point that changes the math is the fee line: 50% of strata fees sit inside GDS (above). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** Whether a "half duplex" price entered in the tool is a strata lot or a fee-simple lot is a title question, not a calculator setting: a fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the municipality's Approving Officer process, and party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (closing-cost cross-reference only; the live page defers PTT to the companion first-time buyer calculator).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000, on fair market value on the day of registration — separate from annual property taxes. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. GST on a newly built home is 5% in BC. A resale duplex or half duplex is not new housing for this rebate. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. The exemption programs themselves are researched in the companion first-time-buyer-calculator file and are not restated here. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What rate does the stress test use?** The greater of your mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25% — OSFI's minimum qualifying rate, applied by federally regulated lenders to insured and uninsured mortgages alike; OSFI reviews the buffer and floor at least annually. (§3.) https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **Q: What is the minimum down payment on a duplex or half duplex?** The same tiers as any home, measured on the price of what you are buying — the whole duplex's price, or the half's own price: 5% at $500,000 or less; 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 up to $1,499,999; 20% minimum at $1,500,000 or more, where insurance is not available. On CMHC's own $750,000 example the minimum is $50,000, not the $37,500 a flat 5% would suggest. (§3.) https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: When do I need mortgage loan insurance, and what does it cost — is it 2.80%–4.00%?** Whenever your down payment is under 20% on a home priced at $1.5 million or less. And no — that range, stated on the live page, is WRONG against CMHC's published **0.6% to 4.5%** of the total mortgage amount; the page's additional 0.20% for 30-year insured amortizations was not confirmed on any official page in this research: UNVERIFIED. The premium can be paid up front or added to the mortgage — CMHC's example prices a $690,000 mortgage at a 4% example rate as a $27,600 premium. (§3.) https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: Can I get a 30-year amortization?** On an insured mortgage, 30-year amortizations have been available to all first-time homebuyers and to all buyers of new builds since December 15, 2024; on an uninsured mortgage (20% or more down) 30 years is a lender offering rather than an insured-program rule, confirmed with the lender, not the calculator. (§3.)
- **Q: How do strata fees on a half duplex enter the affordability math?** Inside GDS: total monthly housing costs (mortgage payments, property taxes, heating, and 50% of strata fees) should stay within 39% of gross household income, and total debt within 44% (TDS) — guidelines, calculated at the qualifying rate, not hard cut-offs. (§3.) https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: Why does the Canadian calculation not just divide the rate by 12?** Because fixed rates are nominal rates compounded semi-annually — the Interest Act requires the mortgage to state the rate "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" for blended-payment mortgages, so the monthly conversion uses semi-annual compounding. The live tool's own compounding implementation is NOT CONFIRMED (the calculator body was not in the page text). (§1; §3.)
- **Q: If the premium is added to my mortgage, what changes?** Both the balance and the payment the stress test is applied to rise — budget the premium as part of the mortgage, not as an afterthought. (§3.)
- **Q: At renewal, do I face the stress test again?** A straight switch of an uninsured mortgage to another federally regulated lender, with no increase to the loan amount or amortization, is not expected to be stress-tested; a refinance or a new HELOC triggers the test again — model the two paths separately. (§3.)
- **Q: Does the calculator approve my mortgage?** No — it computes payments and guideline ratios. The underwriting decision sits with the federally regulated lender under OSFI Guideline B-20, and the insurer sets its own eligibility on insured loans; the output is an estimate to take to a lender or broker. (§3.)
- **Q: What closing costs sit outside the mortgage math?** BC property transfer tax on the tiers worked in §3 is cash needed at completion on top of the down payment; GST at 5% applies to new housing. Legal/notary fees, title insurance, inspection and tax adjustments are real categories named on the live page, but the page's legal/notary dollar range ($1,500–$2,500) is not an official figure: UNVERIFIED as a figure — take these as inputs, not page facts. (§3; research file above.)
