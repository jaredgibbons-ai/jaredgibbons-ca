## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **Open-house disclosure — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), carried as the settled treatment states it.** In British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and BCFSA states a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. The open-house exception: Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report, provides that **a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about their motivation, financial qualifications or needs**. The page's own due-diligence section makes the same point in its own words — the hosting agent represents the seller — which is consistent with the BCFSA statement. Practical consequence for a duplex open house: use the visit for facts, and save your motivation, finances and needs for your own agent — once those are discussed with the seller's licensee, the exception's ground is gone. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/abbotsford-open-houses.md; condos sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/condos/abbotsford-open-houses-condos-2026-11-01.md; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers; http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download)

- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (for transfers where PTT applies).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
