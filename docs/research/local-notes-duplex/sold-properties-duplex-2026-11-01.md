## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **Who owns and runs the MLS® system.** CREA, a trade association, owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service® ("MLS®") and REALTOR®, and comprises over 100,000 real estate brokers and agents across 100 local real estate boards and associations across Canada. Agents and brokers must become members of a local real estate board and CREA to access the MLS® system — described by the Competition Bureau as a comprehensive database of current and historical listing information crucial to residential real estate brokerage services — and must adhere to CREA's rules, such as the REALTOR® Code, and their local boards' rules. For this site, the local boards named in the page's own Reciprocity notice are GVR, FVREB and CADREB — the sold feed spans all three board territories. (Research file above; https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage, read 2026-11-01; https://jaredgibbons.ca/sold-properties.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Sold-data display is a governed, litigated question — and the documented precedent is Ontario's, not BC's.** The Competition Bureau's litigation against the Toronto Real Estate Board concerned exactly the display of sold data online: the Commissioner argued TREB's Virtual Office Website (VOW) policies prohibited VOWs from displaying sold and "pending sold" homes and other listing states; the Competition Tribunal agreed with the Commissioner, rejected TREB's privacy rationale as a pretext, and required TREB, among other things, to provide certain data fields in VOW feeds and allow display on any device. That is a Greater Toronto Area precedent about VOW display — it documents that sold-data display in Canada is contested and governed board by board; it is NOT a statement of any FVREB or GVR rule. The October 2010 CREA consent agreement likewise encompassed where a seller's information can be displayed; its 10-year term as described has run and what followed it is UNVERIFIED. **No BC board's sold-display rule is stated in this file, because neither board publishes its MLS® rules on a public page that could be read for this research**; whether sold listings specifically require a separate board permission or form in FVREB/GVR is UNVERIFIED. (Research file above; https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage, read 2026-11-01.)

- **The BC regulator's consumer rules around any sale on this page.** In BC, real estate trading services are regulated by BCFSA; a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure is required at the outset of dealings, and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. (Research file above; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers, read 2026-11-01.)
- **STRATA block — applies where a sold card in this feed is a strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan), as the diligence frame for reading that record.** Form B diligence applies in full on an ordinary purchase. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**. Minimum contingency reserve fund (CRF) contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the municipality's Approving Officer process; no finer process detail is stated. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles; a sold card answers neither question. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (the frame the sales behind this feed were taxed under).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. A resale duplex or half duplex — the ordinary content of a sold feed — is not new housing for this rebate. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
