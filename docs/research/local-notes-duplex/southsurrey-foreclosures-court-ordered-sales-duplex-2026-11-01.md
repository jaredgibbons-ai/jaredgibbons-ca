## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

**TOPIC — NO ROSTER at the top: this file names no duplex stock as its own and takes no count.** Court-ordered status attaches to a property and a court file, not to a complex — a standing roster would be stale on publication. In process terms, any mortgaged property can be the subject of a Rule 21-7 proceeding, which by its terms covers "mortgaged property" without a property-type limit — so a duplex (whole building, one title) or a strata half duplex (one strata lot) can each come to market this way, and each is a listing event for a property whose home is its own area file. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **The court process (settled treatment, carried at the settled level).** A foreclosure proceeding is started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7 (1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner who registers a certificate of pending litigation binds later-registered interests under Rule 21-7 (4); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). The court may make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7 (5)(a); order payment of what is due within a redemption period the court fixes (there is NO fixed length in the Rule text read), with foreclosure of the equity of redemption in default — Rule 21-7 (5)(b); and determine or have certified the amount due — Rule 21-7 (5)(c). Those subrules are the ones this slug's research re-confirmed verbatim on BC Laws. Beyond them, this file carries the SETTLED treatment at the settled level only: a party of record may apply for an order that the mortgaged property be sold, and the person having conduct of a sale may apply for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser, including a sale before the redemption period ends; conduct-of-sale mechanics (court directions, reserve/upset price, bid and offer rights) sit in Rule 13-5 and the Supreme Court's foreclosure practice direction PD-66, with Form 60 certification. **In this slug's own research those later texts were NOT re-read — it marks Rule 21-7 after (5)(c), Rule 13-5, PD-66 and Form 60 UNVERIFIED program-wide, and the live page's accounts of them (including sealed bids collected in the courtroom by 9:45 a.m. under PD-66, a pre-petition demand step and "order nisi" sequence, and a Law and Equity Act s. 21 filing-registry rule) are the page's own statements, NOT adopted here as verified process** — get the seller's counsel's bid instructions for the specific file in writing before spending. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can a duplex be sold through a court-ordered sale?** Yes — Rule 21-7 covers "mortgaged property" without a property-type limit, so a whole duplex on one title, or one strata half duplex as its own strata lot, can each be the subject property. Which title is being sold is read off the court file. (§2; §3.)
- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. (§3.) https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — it is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5)(b). Anyone quoting a standard period for a specific file without reading its order is guessing. (§3.)
- **Q: Is my accepted offer final?** No — under the settled treatment, the person with conduct of sale applies for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title, and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires. Until that order, the sale is not final. Note the research-stance position in §3: this slug's research marks the confirming-sale wording UNVERIFIED program-wide, so the file's own order and counsel's instructions are the check. (§3.)
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** The bid mechanics sit in the court's sale directions (Rule 13-5) and PD-66 under the settled treatment — and this slug's research marks both UNVERIFIED program-wide, including the live page's 9:45 a.m. sealed-bid account, which is NOT adopted here. Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for, and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing before spending on inspections. (§3.)
- **Q: Where is a South Surrey foreclosure filed?** UNVERIFIED — Surrey has NO Supreme Court registry, no registry mapping is filled in anywhere in this program, and this slug's research opened no registry source. The registry shown on the individual court file is the only check that settles where a specific file sits. (§1; §3.)
- **Q: Do I get a Form B if the court sale is a strata half duplex?** UNCONFIRMED — Form B treatment on court-ordered sales is an open item in the settled treatment. Confirm with your lawyer what strata documents the specific file provides before you spend. (§2; §3.)
- **Q: What PTT and GST do I pay on a court-ordered purchase?** UNCONFIRMED as a court-sale-specific treatment — have your lawyer cost both on the specific property before you set your ceiling. The ordinary tiers are worked in §3, and at a $3.5M input the governing figure is $93,000, not the $78,000 this slug's townhomes sibling states. (§3; §5.)
- **Q: Does a 2-lot strata need a depreciation report?** The 5-year cycle applies to stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; a 2-lot strata is below that threshold on lot count. (§3.)
- **Q: Are foreclosure duplexes cheaper in South Surrey?** No figure is stated — SnapStats publishes no foreclosure category and no official source publishes foreclosure counts or discounts, and no published source supports a standard foreclosure discount, because the court, not the market feed, confirms the sale.
