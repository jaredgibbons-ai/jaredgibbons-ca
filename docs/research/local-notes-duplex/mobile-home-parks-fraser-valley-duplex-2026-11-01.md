## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A manufactured home is none of these forms: it is a home built off-site and transported to its site for installation — on a rented pad it is a chattel on someone else's land, and even on owned land it is not a two-unit building. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; manufactured-home frame: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes, read 2026-11-01, via research file, §4.)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **Governing law (the topic's frame):** manufactured homes, sometimes called mobile homes, are built off-site and transported to a final location for installation, and BC's Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act covers most manufactured home tenancies. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Pad-rent increases (MHPTA / Residential Tenancy Branch):** a park landlord may increase rent only once every 12 months, only with 3 full months' written notice, and only up to the yearly limit; the landlord may not round up and may not carry forward an unused portion of a prior year's limit. The limits, re-confirmed in the research pass on the Province's manufactured-home rent-increase page: **2026 = 2.3% plus a proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% plus a proportional amount** — the proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park. These percentages are the only pad-rent figures stated in this file, stated exactly as the RTB source documents them. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes, read 2026-11-01.)
- **Assignment on a sale (MHPTA s.28):** assignment permanently transfers the site tenancy to the buyer; under s.28(1)(b) a tenant may assign only with the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it — the Act's word is "director," never "arbitrator"; under s.28(3) the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting to an assignment, and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf.)
- **Park closure / change of use (MHPTA s.42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to another use, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the effective date of the notice; a tenant may end the tenancy early on at least 10 days' written notice, the dispute window for a 12-month notice is 15 days, and additional compensation of the assessed value of the home minus the $20,000 may be ordered where the tenant could not obtain the permits needed to move the home or could not move it to another site within a reasonable distance, with a further $5,000 or 12 months' pad rent, whichever is greater, payable if the landlord does not proceed with the conversion purpose within a reasonable period. (Research file, §4; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes.)
- **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan) and, in its general form, to a bare-land strata manufactured-home lot at its home file; it does NOT apply to a pad-rental tenancy, which is an MHPTA tenancy, not a strata.** Form B diligence applies in full to a strata purchase. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through the Approving Officer process of the municipality the parcel sits in; no finer process detail is stated because no official source opened for this slug states it. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (own-land / strata-lot transfers only).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. Whether PTT applies at all to a given park-home purchase turns on whether a registrable land interest transfers — a pad-rental purchase generally transfers no land interest, as the townhomes sibling for this slug states; confirm per transaction. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. Have a lawyer or notary model the exact bill from the current Province pages before offering. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax (townhomes sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/townhomes/mobile-home-parks-fraser-valley-townhomes-2026-11-01.md.)
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Whether either rebate applies to a specific manufactured home purchase is a CRA-eligibility question on that home's facts; neither rebate is stated in the research file as applying to any park or home on this page, and none is claimed here. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html (condos sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/condos/mobile-home-parks-fraser-valley-condos-2026-11-01.md.)

- **Q: If I buy a home on a rented pad, what do I own?** The home itself, as a chattel — not the land. **A home on a rented pad is not land ownership: you own the home and rent the pad** under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement, and the pad tenancy transfers on a sale only by assignment under MHPTA s.28. - **Q: How much is pad rent in Fraser Valley parks?** No figure is stated in this file: this page publishes no pad rent for any park, no official source publishes a pad-rent figure, average or range, and the ranges on sibling pages of this site are unverified site copy — the figure that matters is the specific park's current rent in its written Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement. **No pad-rent dollar figure is published as fact here.** (§2.)
- **Q: How much can a park raise pad rent each year?** Up to the Residential Tenancy Branch limit for manufactured home site tenancies: **2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027**, on 3 full months' written notice, once every 12 months, with no rounding up and no carry-forward of an unused portion. (§3.)
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold or converted to another use?** Under MHPTA s.42 the landlord must first have all required government permits and approvals, give 12 months' notice, and pay $20,000 on or before the effective date; the tenant may leave early on 10 days' notice, has 15 days to dispute, and may be eligible for additional compensation of the assessed value minus $20,000 through dispute resolution. (§3.)
- **Q: Is a park home a duplex or half duplex?** No — in any tenure. A pad-rental home is a chattel on rented land; a co-op home is a share; a bare-land strata home is a strata lot in a manufactured-home community. None is a two-unit building, a 2-lot strata duplex, or a fee-simple half duplex. (§2.)
- **Q: Are there market statistics for Fraser Valley mobile homes?** No — SnapStats has no manufactured-home category. The only figures in this file are all-residential community context as of September 2026 (for example Langley Detached median $1,345,000 and Condos & Townhomes median $611,000), and neither describes park homes. (§1.)
- **Q: Do I pay PTT on a park-home purchase?** Only where a registrable land interest transfers — an own-land or bare-land strata purchase can attract PTT on the worked tiers in §3; a pad-rental purchase generally transfers no land interest. Confirm per transaction with a lawyer or notary. (§3.)
