## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

The Nation states the community is home to approximately 1,495 of its more than 2,275 citizens. ## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

**HONEST ZERO at the top — and it is structural: no duplex stock of any form exists at Mount Currie as market stock, because the tenure system the two half-duplex forms depend on does not exist on reserve land.** There is no fee-simple title to subdivide and no strata regime to strata-divide: the research states it directly — tenure is Nation-administered housing on reserve land (band housing, and member home ownership through the Nation's process), which is neither freehold nor strata, and homes do not come to the open market for general buyers. Every sibling that counts stock on this ground reaches the same structural zero for its own form (condos 0, townhomes 0, 55-plus none, mobile-homes zero as a market). No individual two-unit building is verified by name either — and none is looked for as a listing, because a two-unit Nation-administered home would still not be a duplex in the market sense this program documents. (Research file above; siblings listed in the FILE STATUS block.)

- **How housing actually works here (the defining fact, stated instead of a roster).** The Líl̓wat Nation's "Five Steps to Home Ownership on Reserve" runs through the Nation: a Housing Manager meeting; a land encumbrance check from the Lands Department confirming a serviced lot is available and registered to the person; a Band Council Resolution (the Band Council acts as guarantor on the loan); possible housing subsidies from Indigenous Services Canada; a Ministerial Loan Guarantee; and bank financing. The Nation is building new housing in both the Mount Currie and Xet̓ólacw communities, with a goal of 12 units per year under its Community Housing Plan (2018–2033). Typical types are single-detached family homes in the village neighbourhoods, including newer subdivision homes and modular/manufactured homes built to CSA Z-240. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf and https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf (Research file above.)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can anyone buy a house — or a half duplex — in Mount Currie?** No. Mount Currie is the main community of the Líl̓wat Nation on reserve land; housing is allocated and financed through the Nation's own process for its citizens — there is no fee-simple open market, and therefore no duplex or half duplex for a general buyer to purchase. Inquiries go to the Líl̓wat Nation (info@lilwat.ca, 604-894-6115). https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf (§2.)
- **Q: How does home ownership work for Líl̓wat citizens?** Through the Nation's Five Steps: Housing Manager first, then the Lands Department land encumbrance check, a Band Council Resolution (the Council acts as guarantor), Indigenous Services Canada subsidy and Ministerial Loan Guarantee checks, then a lender. Because the Band Council acts as guarantor and a Ministerial Loan Guarantee backs the loan, standard mortgage and foreclosure mechanics do not apply the way they do off reserve. (§2, §3.)
- **Q: Is Mount Currie housing freehold or strata?** Neither — the research states it directly: Nation-administered housing on reserve land. Do not present Mount Currie housing as freehold or strata in any page copy. (§2.)
- **Q: What does it cost to build a home there?** The Nation's own guidance: new construction of a modest home at about $250–$300 per square foot including water, sewer and hydro connections, and modular/pre-manufactured homes built to CSA Z-240 at about $160–$180 per square foot installed and hooked up; modulars for Mount Currie must meet zone 6 construction standards and a roof snow load rating of 136 lb per square foot. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf (§2.)
- **Q: Is there any strata duplex diligence (Form B) on a Mount Currie purchase?** No — there is no strata here and no Form B exists for a Nation-administered home. The diligence is the Nation's process itself: the Lands Department encumbrance check is the step that confirms the serviced lot and its registration. (§3.)
- **Q: Does PTT apply to a Mount Currie home?** Not the way it applies off reserve — standard municipal/regional taxes and provincial PTT do not apply to reserve land as they do to fee-simple land. The Nation's own taxation laws or service fees for a specific lot are UNVERIFIED. The PTT anchors in §3 are an off-reserve comparison frame only. (§3.)
- **Q: How is Mount Currie different from Pemberton next door?** Pemberton homes are fee-simple in a municipality, with standard taxes and MLS sales; Mount Currie homes are on reserve land under the Nation's system. Same valley, two different tenure worlds — never comp one against the other. (§1.)
- **Q: Which schools serve Mount Currie?** Xet̓ólacw Community School is the Nation's own school in the community (K4-12 as the Nation's site lists it, with an Ucwalmícwts immersion program K4 to Grade 7), and under the Local Education Agreement with School District 48, Líl̓wat students also attend Signal Hill Elementary and Pemberton Secondary School in Pemberton, with SD48 bus routes serving Mount Currie directly. (§5.)
- **Q: Is there transit?** Yes — BC Transit's Pemberton Valley Transit, cost-shared between BC Transit, the Village of Pemberton, the SLRD and Líl̓wat Nation: Route 100 Pemberton Local connects Xit'olacw and Mt Currie to the Village, and Route 99 Pemberton Commuter runs between Pemberton and Whistler. (§5.)
