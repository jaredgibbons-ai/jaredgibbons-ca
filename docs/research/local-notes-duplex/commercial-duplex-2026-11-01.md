## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

- **Where duplexes are permitted — NO frame asserted for this topic.** A duplex takes the zoning of the municipality and parcel where it sits; this file asserts no city's zoning, SSMUH, suite, servicing or short-term-rental rule for the topic, because the topic has no ground. - **Commercial tax treatment — UNCONFIRMED, stated plainly.** This slug's research documents NO property-transfer-tax or GST treatment for commercial transactions. The townhomes sibling states the position expressly: the residential PTT tiers, first-time and newly-built exemptions, GST rebates, HBP and FHSA figures are RESIDENTIAL rules, not asserted for a commercial purchase, a business sale, or a lease; a commercial buyer takes the transaction's actual tax treatment from a legal/tax professional. The condos sibling carries the general provincial tiers (1% on the first $200,000, 2% to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million) as published for property transfers generally, with how PTT applies to a specific commercial or mixed-use transfer (including any residential component) left as a conveyancing question for that deal. (Townhomes sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/townhomes/commercial-townhomes-2026-11-01.md; condos sibling: ~/workspace/local-notes/condos/commercial-condos-2026-11-01.md; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (general residential tier math ONLY, carried as the program's anchors; NOT a commercial treatment).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex, where a transfer is a residential transfer, is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer; whether and how PTT applies to any specific commercial or mixed-use duplex transfer is UNCONFIRMED here (see the bullet above). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan) wherever it sits.** Form B diligence applies in full. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. The condos sibling confirms the two Strata Property Act rules that DO apply to a strata corporation holding a commercial unit on the same terms as a residential one: depreciation reports (5-year cycle, 5+ lots, since July 1, 2024) and the minimum 10% CRF contribution (since November 1, 2023). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through that municipality's Approving Officer process, against its current zoning minimums; no finer process detail is stated because no official source opened for this slug states it. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. Neither program is a commercial-feed program: the lots-and-land sibling states the boundary expressly — the rebate applies to new HOUSING, not to bare land and not to commercial property. A resale duplex or half duplex is not new housing for this rebate. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Representation framework (from the research).** In BC, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensee must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients and of the risks of dealing unrepresented, and dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. This framework applies to commercial trading services as it does to residential. (Research file above; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers)
