## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

- **Regulator context for acting on what a map shows.** Real estate trading services in BC are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), which states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and work for only the buyer or the seller in a transaction — the frame within which map findings become advice to a client. - **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (the cost a buyer checking a parcel on GIS is budgeting around).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000, based on fair market value at registration unless an exemption applies. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000**; $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **STRATA block — applies to a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan) wherever it sits.** Form B diligence applies in full: depreciation report, financial statements, CRF balance, approved special levies still payable and the insurance summary. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; a buyer still asks what reports, if any, the strata holds. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** A fee-simple split of a duplex parcel requires subdivision approval through that municipality's Approving Officer process, against its current zoning minimums; a GIS lot-dimension readout settles none of that — it is a first look only, on this page's own framing. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief; research file above, §4.)
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. A resale duplex or half duplex is not new housing for this rebate. GST is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What is GIS, in plain language?** As this site's own page puts it, Geographic Information Systems are "digital maps that carry data behind them" — click a property and, on the page's description, you can pull up zoning, lot dimensions, flood plain status, ALR boundaries and easements. The one tool reference verified in the research is the Province's: iMapBC lets users view and analyze thousands of geographic datasets and upload, print or email their work. Which specific layers any one tool carries is UNVERIFIED in this file. (§1, §3; research file §7.)
- **Q: Do I need GIS skills to buy a house?** On the page's own answer: no — for a standard townhome or detached home in an established neighbourhood GIS is rarely critical, and it matters most for land, acreage, property near water or in a flood zone, and anything you plan to build on, subdivide or significantly modify. (§1; research file §7.)
- **Q: Does this topic file list duplexes I can check on a map?** No. It is a topic/utility file with no roster; GIS is a checking method, and a duplex parcel is documented in its home-area file, with its title form verified on title — never from a map alone. (§2.)
- **Q: Can a GIS map tell me if a half duplex is legally separate?** No — that is a title question. On the page's own hierarchy, GIS is a first look and the title is the source of truth: a half is separable only as a strata lot in a 2-lot strata plan or as its own fee-simple lot, and only the title (and, for a strata half, the Form B package) settles which. (§2, §3.)
- **Q: What is the difference between zoning and an OCP?** In the page's plain-language framing: zoning is the current rule for what may be built on a property today, while the Official Community Plan is the municipality's long-term direction for the area over the next 10–30 years. Those are the page's simplified definitions, recorded as site copy, not as bylaw-verified definitions. (§3; research file §7.)
- **Q: How do I find out if a property is in a flood plain?** The page's route: toggle the flood plain layer on a municipal GIS map — it names Chilliwack MapOnline, Abbotsford CityMap and Delta's map as the relevant regional tools — with BC iMap holding provincial flood data; and it warns that flood plain status affects insurance, lending and rebuilding rules. The current names, layers and mechanics of those municipal tools are UNVERIFIED in this file — none was opened in the research. (§3; research file §7.)
- **Q: Can a GIS map tell me about easements and rights-of-way?** Sometimes, on the page's own answer: GIS layers often show registered easements and obvious utility rights-of-way, but not every encumbrance appears on a map — use GIS as a first look and confirm with the title search, which the page calls the source of truth. The title-search mechanics behind that advice are UNVERIFIED in this file; no Land Title and Survey Authority source was opened. (§3; research file §7.)
- **Q: The map shows a big lot — can I assume I can build a duplex on it?** No. The page itself warns a "big lot" can be "actually mostly unbuildable" once flood, creek setbacks and other layers are applied, and zoning/OCP designations must be verified with the municipality for the specific parcel — the explainer's shorthand settles nothing. (§1, §3.)
