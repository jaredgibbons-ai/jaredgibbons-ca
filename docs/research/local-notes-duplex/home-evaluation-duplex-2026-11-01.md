## 2. THE DUPLEX STOCK

**Definitions, stated plainly.** A DUPLEX is one building containing two dwelling units, on one lot under one title unless it is strata-divided. A HALF DUPLEX is one of the two units sold separately — in BC that happens in only two legal forms: **(a) a STRATA duplex**, where the pair is a 2-lot strata plan, each half is a strata lot, and Form B diligence applies; or **(b) a FEE-SIMPLE half duplex**, where the parcel itself was subdivided so each half sits on its own fee-simple lot (the party-wall / fee-simple row form, where a source documents it). A duplex that was never strata-divided or subdivided cannot be bought "by the half" — the whole building and lot sell together. (Program definition, settled in `~/workspace/local-notes/duplex-D1-BRIEF.md`; strata form per the Province's strata housing pages: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing)

- **What a duplex evaluation works from, instead of a stock list.** A CMA "evaluates the prices of similar properties recently sold in your area" (the page's own terms). For a two-unit property, the most similar properties are other duplex sales in the same ground — and the evaluation has to settle the title form first, because the three forms price differently: a whole duplex on one title is valued as one property (both units, any suite/coach position, and the lot); a strata half duplex is valued as its own strata lot against other half-duplex and strata sales, with the 2-lot strata's Form B position in view; a fee-simple half duplex is valued as its own subdivided lot against other fee-simple halves, with the party-wall agreement on title in view. Which form a given seller's property takes is a title fact established in the evaluation itself — no form is asserted for any property in this file, and no comparable set is named. (Research file above; program definition above.)
- **The tenure traps an evaluation must not flatten.** Two duplexes on the same street can carry opposite added-unit positions under different cities' suite rules (each city's own cap governs its own ground — no figure is carried in this topic file; the figures live in the ground files), different flood, heritage or permit-area positions, and different strata positions where a 2-lot plan exists. An evaluation that prices a duplex from detached-house comparables alone misses the second unit's contribution entirely; an evaluation that prices a half duplex from whole-duplex sales double-counts the half it does not include. Those are method points for the CMA conversation, stated here because this page is where that conversation starts — they are not claims about any property. (Research file above; brief chain.)

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT DUPLEXES

- **Framing for this topic.** No municipal rule is applied to a property in this file, because this file holds no property. The rules below are (i) the regulator's framework that governs the evaluation service itself, and (ii) the provincial rules (strata, PTT, GST) that an evaluation of a duplex or half duplex will meet, stated at the provincial level only. A specific duplex takes the city frame of its home-area file — Surrey's, Abbotsford's, Langley's or Delta's figures are never blended into this topic file. (Brief chain; research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-11/home-evaluation.md.)
- **The service framework (BCFSA).** In British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed real estate professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings — whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA; dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. A CMA request through this page is, on the page's own text, a request for an evaluation — the page does not state when agency disclosure occurs in Jared's process: UNVERIFIED. (Research file above; https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers)
- **Mechanics the page does NOT state (all UNVERIFIED, never described from industry habit):** the cost of the evaluation (the page states no price and does not use the word "free" in the text read for the research); any turnaround time beyond Jared contacting the seller "as soon as I have the results"; what the delivered CMA contains (page count, comparable count, report format, whether a suggested list price or a home visit is included); any obligation created by requesting one; and how the submitted form data is stored or used. A duplex evaluation inherits every one of those open items unchanged — nothing about a two-unit property fills them in. (Research file above; https://jaredgibbons.ca/home-evaluation.html, read 2026-11-01.)
- **STRATA block — applies where the evaluated property is a strata duplex / strata half duplex (2-lot strata plan).** Form B diligence applies in full, and the strata position moves the evaluation number for a strata half just as it does for a townhouse: fees, levies, CRF balance and bylaws are part of value. Depreciation reports run on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024 — **a 2-lot strata duplex is BELOW that 5-lot threshold, so the 5+ depreciation-report cycle does not capture it on lot count alone**; what reports, if any, the strata holds is a Form B question. Minimum CRF contribution 10% since November 1, 2023. Rental-restriction bylaws are VOID since November 24, 2022. Only 55+ age bylaws are valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + Regulation 7.01, with the May 1, 2023 exemptions); a 45+ bylaw is VOID and never converts. A fee-simple duplex or fee-simple half carries NO strata and NO Form B — municipal rules plus any registered covenants govern instead, and an evaluation must not price the two forms as if they were the same product. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Subdivision / party-wall facts — only as official sources support.** Whether a whole duplex could be split into fee-simple halves turns on subdivision approval through the home municipality's Approving Officer process; no finer process detail is stated because no official source opened for this slug states it, and the answer belongs to the property's own city file in any case. Party-wall obligations between two fee-simple halves rest on the registered easement / party-wall agreement on the two titles — read both titles. (Program discipline, D1 brief.)
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs + tier math (the tax a buyer of an evaluated duplex will meet; province-wide).** BC property transfer tax: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Fully residential, no exemption: $500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = **$8,000**; $800,000 → **$14,000** (the figure this slug's townhomes sibling uses for a buyer's net-proceeds illustration: "$14,000 on an $800,000 resale with no exemption"); $1,000,000 → **$18,000**; $1,200,000 → **$22,000**; $1,500,000 → **$28,000**; $2,000,000 → **$38,000**; $3,500,000 → $38,000 + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$93,000 including the further 2% tier**. A half duplex is taxed on its own purchase price as its own transfer. The CMA speaks to market value; a seller's NET is a different number, and the buyer's PTT is part of the affordability the list price meets. First-Time Home Buyers' Program: full exemption on the first $500,000 at fair market value $835,000 or less, partial $835,000–$860,000; Newly Built Home Exemption: full to $1,100,000, eliminated at $1,150,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First-home / GST material = new HOUSING only.** The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent **March 13, 2026 per CRA** — never March 12) applies to new housing: 100% of GST to $1,000,000 (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1,500,000, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000. A resale duplex is not new housing for this rebate, and 5% GST applies to new construction, not resales — a distinction an evaluation's comparable set must respect, as this slug's condos sibling states for condos. GST on land / new housing is carried at the CRA GI-003 general level only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
