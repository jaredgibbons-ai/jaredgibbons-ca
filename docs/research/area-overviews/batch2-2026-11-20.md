Easy-to-read neighbourhood copy for Jared's area pages. No sales statistics. Official details (catchments, zoning, heritage rules, flood mapping) should still be confirmed with the City of White Rock, the City of Surrey, the Township of Langley, the City of Langley, or the school district for a specific address.

## White Rock

**Suggested H2:** Living in White Rock

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in White Rock; White Rock real estate; living in White Rock; White Rock beach homes; White Rock condos

White Rock is a small seaside city on Semiahmoo Bay, established in 1957 and bounded on three sides by South Surrey. The waterfront is the city's landmark: the 470-metre White Rock Pier, a 2.19-kilometre promenade, and the large white rock on the beach, known to the Semiahmoo First Nation as P'Quals. A BNSF railway line runs along the shore, and the historic train station houses the White Rock Museum and Archives.

Homes for sale in White Rock include houses on the hillside streets above the bay, along with townhouses and apartments closer to the waterfront and the uptown area. Everyday shopping and dining are grouped in three parts of the city: the Waterfront, Five Corners, and Uptown. Peace Arch Hospital is in White Rock. Parks include Centennial Park and Bayview Park on Marine Drive.

Buses, including routes along Marine Drive, connect to White Rock Centre at 152 Street and 16 Avenue, on the boundary with Surrey. This is not a SkyTrain community; the usual trip toward Vancouver is by bus or by car using the highways in South Surrey. Semiahmoo Town Centre, just north of the city, has a larger mall and services. Nearby public schools include White Rock Elementary and Peace Arch Elementary. Secondary catchments often include Semiahmoo Secondary or Earl Marriott Secondary in South Surrey. Catchments split by address, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator for the home you have in mind.

Living in White Rock suits buyers who want a compact seaside city, with the beach and pier close at hand, rather than a large suburban town centre.

## Grandview Heights (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Grandview Heights, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Grandview Heights; Grandview Heights Surrey; living in Grandview Heights; Grandview Heights real estate; Grandview Heights townhouses

Grandview Heights is a growing part of southeast Surrey, bordering the Township of Langley. The City of Surrey calls the wider community Grandview. It includes residential neighbourhoods, shopping along the main roads, some remaining agricultural land, and large parks. King George Boulevard, 24 Avenue, and 176 Street (Highway 15) are the usual ways in and out. Highway 99 is a short drive west.

If you are browsing homes for sale in Grandview Heights, you will mainly find newer family houses and townhouses in a part of South Surrey that is still being built out. The Grandview Heights General Land Use Plan guides that growth. Daily life often centres on 24 Avenue, including the shops at Grandview Corners near 160 Street.

Grandview Heights Aquatic Centre sits at 24 Avenue and 168 Street, with a pool, leisure pool, and weight room. Redwood Park and Darts Hill Garden Park are in the community as well. This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood; buses serve the main roads. Nearby public schools include Pacific Heights Elementary, Edgewood Elementary, and Grandview Heights Secondary, which is next to the aquatic centre. Catchments can include other nearby schools, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator for the exact address.

Grandview Heights Surrey real estate is a fit if you want a newer South Surrey neighbourhood with parks, a rec centre, and shopping close by, without living in the older beach communities to the west.

## Morgan Creek (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Morgan Creek, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Morgan Creek; Morgan Creek Surrey; living in Morgan Creek; Morgan Creek real estate; Morgan Creek golf

Morgan Creek is a residential neighbourhood in South Surrey, around Morgan Creek Golf Course and Morgan Creek Park, near 32 Avenue and 156A Street. It sits between Rosemary Heights and the growing Grandview Heights area, with Highway 99 a short drive west and 24 Avenue shopping a few minutes away.

Homes for sale in Morgan Creek are mostly houses, with some townhouses, on streets laid out around the golf course and the park. Living here often means a quieter residential pocket rather than a walk-to-everything town centre. Morgan Creek Park has a playground, basketball, tennis and pickleball courts, and a grass sports field. Grandview Heights Aquatic Centre is a short drive east.

This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood. Buses serve nearby main roads, and most residents drive to Highway 99 or 24 Avenue. Morgan Elementary is in the neighbourhood, beside the park. Secondary catchments can include Grandview Heights Secondary; always confirm with the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator for the exact address.

Morgan Creek real estate suits buyers who want a house-oriented South Surrey neighbourhood with a local park and golf course, close to the shops and recreation of Grandview Heights.

## Crescent Beach / Ocean Park (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Crescent Beach and Ocean Park, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Crescent Beach; Ocean Park Surrey homes; living in Crescent Beach; Ocean Park real estate; Crescent Beach Surrey

Crescent Beach and Ocean Park are neighbouring communities in west South Surrey. They share schools, bus routes, and a pull toward the water, but they do not feel the same on the ground. Crescent Beach is the low-lying historic resort community on the shoreline. Ocean Park sits up the hill, with a small village of shops and services.

-   **Crescent Beach.** The City of Surrey describes this as a historic resort community, first opened to the public after the railway arrived in 1909. Today it is a compact seaside neighbourhood with a walkable stretch of shops and restaurants, the beach, and a pier. The best public beach access is on Beecher, Sullivan, and Target Streets. Dykes built to keep the area dry now serve as a waterfront pathway. Next door, Blackie Spit Park has nature trails, picnic areas, and dog off-leash space, including Surrey's only dog off-leash swim area. Homes here are mainly houses on smaller lots. Because this is a low-lying, dyked shoreline, check flood mapping and insurance with the City before you buy.

-   **Ocean Park.** This is a more typical South Surrey residential area above the beach, with family houses, some townhouses, and a village centre around 128 Street and 16 Avenue. Surrey's Ocean Park Library is at 12854 17 Avenue, in the heart of that village, next to Ocean Park Shopping Centre. From here it is a short trip down to Crescent Beach, or along 16 Avenue toward White Rock.

The 350 bus runs between Crescent Beach and White Rock Centre. Ocean Park is also served by local buses along 16 Avenue and 128 Street, including routes 350, 352, 360, and 361. This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood. Nearby public schools include Crescent Park Elementary, Ocean Cliff Elementary, and Ray Shepherd Elementary, with Elgin Park Secondary close by. Catchments split by address, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator.

Living in Crescent Beach or Ocean Park, Surrey, is a good fit if you want to be near the water in South Surrey. Choose Crescent Beach for a walkable shoreline village, or Ocean Park if you want a hillside neighbourhood with local shops and a slightly easier daily drive.

## Walnut Grove (Langley Township)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Walnut Grove, Langley

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Walnut Grove; Walnut Grove Langley; living in Walnut Grove; Walnut Grove real estate; Walnut Grove townhouses

Walnut Grove is a residential neighbourhood in the northwest Township of Langley, north of Highway 1. It has shops, movie theatres, parks, and the Walnut Grove Community Centre in the neighbourhood, so daily needs are close at hand. The Golden Ears Bridge connects this part of the Township to Maple Ridge and Pitt Meadows, on the north side of the Fraser River.

Homes for sale in Walnut Grove are mainly family houses and townhouses. Everyday life often centres on the community centre at 8889 Walnut Grove Drive, which has a 50-metre pool, leisure pool, fitness centre, gymnasium, and the Dean Drysdale Library in the same building. Walnut Grove Community Park is next to the school and community centre area, with sports fields, a playground, a skateboard park, and a spray park.

Carvolth Exchange, at 202 Street and 86 Avenue, is the main transit hub at the south edge of Walnut Grove, next to Highway 1, with park-and-ride and bus connections. Highway 1 is the usual route toward Vancouver and the rest of the Fraser Valley. Nearby public schools include Walnut Grove Secondary, Alex Hope Elementary, Dorothy Peacock Elementary, and James Kennedy Elementary. Other elementary schools in the same family also serve parts of the area. Confirm the catchment for the exact address with the SD35 school locator.

Living in Walnut Grove, Langley, suits families who want parks, a pool, and daily shops close by, with quick access to the highway and the Golden Ears Bridge.

## Willoughby, including Yorkson (Langley Township)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Willoughby, Langley

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Willoughby; Willoughby Langley; living in Willoughby Langley; Yorkson Langley homes; Willoughby townhouses

Willoughby is a growing part of the northwest Township of Langley, south of Highway 1 and centred on the 200 Street corridor. The Township groups it with Willowbrook as Willoughby-Willowbrook. This is where you will find the Township's Civic Facility, the Langley Events Centre, and the Muriel Arnason Library. The Township describes it as a community of newer neighbourhoods with housing, parks, and plenty of shopping.

Homes for sale in Willoughby include houses and a large number of townhouses, in several planned neighbourhoods rather than one single street pattern. Willowbrook's shops and services sit along the south side of the community, near 200 Street and the Fraser Highway.

**Yorkson** is one of those Willoughby neighbourhoods, around Yorkson Community Park at 84 Avenue and 206 Street. It is a family-oriented, newer residential area with parks, schools, and a mix of houses and townhouses. If you are looking at a specific Willoughby address, it is worth asking whether it sits in Yorkson or in one of the other neighbourhoods, because they do not all feel the same.

Highway 1 and Carvolth Exchange are just to the north. This is not yet a SkyTrain neighbourhood, though the Surrey Langley SkyTrain project will add Willowbrook Station at 196 Street and Fraser Highway, on the south side. The Township is also updating plans for a future 200 Street bus rapid transit corridor. Nearby public schools include Willoughby Elementary, Lynn Fripps Elementary, Richard Bulpitt Elementary, Yorkson Creek Middle School, and RE Mountain Secondary. Catchments vary by address, so check the SD35 school locator.

Willoughby Langley real estate is a fit if you want a newer Township neighbourhood with rec facilities and shopping close by, and you are comfortable living in a community that is still growing.

## Fort Langley (Langley Township)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Fort Langley, Langley

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Fort Langley; Fort Langley real estate; living in Fort Langley; Fort Langley village homes; Fort Langley Langley

Fort Langley is a village in the Township of Langley, on the banks of the Fraser River. The Township calls it the birthplace of B.C. It is home to the Hudson's Bay Company's Fort Langley, now Fort Langley National Historic Site, as well as Salishan Place by the River. The village itself is a compact, walkable stretch of shops, restaurants, and art galleries along Glover Road, and it draws visitors as well as residents.

Homes for sale in Fort Langley include village houses, some older properties, and homes on larger lots toward the edge of the community. Fort Langley includes one of only two heritage conservation areas in the Township (the other is in Murrayville). That designation applies to the historic core and comes with rules for changes to properties. Confirm the details with the Township if you are buying or renovating there.

Parks, trails, and the river are part of daily life. Amenities include the Fort Langley Library on Glover Road, Marina Park on the Fraser, and the Langley Rowing and Paddling Centre. The closest community centre is the Walnut Grove Community Centre, a short drive west. Highway 1 is about six kilometres south. The 562 bus serves the village. Nearby public schools include Fort Langley Elementary. D.W. Poppy Secondary is the usual family secondary, though it is not in the village itself. Confirm the catchment for the exact address with the SD35 school locator.

Living in Fort Langley suits buyers who want a historic river village, with shops and the national historic site close by, rather than a newer suburban town centre.

## Clayton / East Clayton (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Clayton, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Clayton Surrey; East Clayton homes for sale; living in East Clayton; Clayton Surrey real estate; East Clayton townhouses

Clayton is a growing community in northeast Surrey, along Fraser Highway, east of Cloverdale and west of the Township of Langley. The City of Surrey describes a mix of residential neighbourhoods, parks, schools, and commercial areas. Some streets are older and more established; much of Clayton Heights has been built out more recently as a family neighbourhood.

Clayton Community Centre, at 7155 187A Street, is the main community hub, with recreation, arts programming, and a Surrey Libraries branch in the same building. Local parks include Hazelgrove Park, Clayton Park, and Katzie Park. Fraser Highway is the main route through the area. This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood today, but the Surrey Langley SkyTrain project will add Clayton Station at Fraser Highway and 190 Street.

**East Clayton** is the first urban neighbourhood that was developed in Clayton Heights. It sits north of Fraser Highway, along the Langley border. The City notes that the area was once rural acreage and farmland; it is now a compact residential community of houses and townhouses, with parks, schools, and local shops. Hazelgrove Elementary and Katzie Elementary are in this part of Clayton, and Hazelgrove Park is a central green space. Planning along Fraser Highway is intended to tie East Clayton to future rapid transit.

Nearby public schools also include Clayton Heights Secondary and École Salish Secondary. Catchments in this area split by address and have changed as new schools have opened, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator for the home you have in mind.

Clayton Surrey real estate, and East Clayton in particular, suits buyers who want a newer family neighbourhood with a community centre and parks, on the Surrey--Langley edge, and who are planning around future SkyTrain on Fraser Highway.

## Panorama Ridge (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Panorama Ridge, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Panorama Ridge; Panorama Ridge Surrey; living in Panorama Ridge; Panorama Ridge real estate; Panorama Ridge Secondary

Panorama Ridge is a residential neighbourhood in Surrey's Newton community, in the south-central part of the city. The City of Surrey refers to it as the Panorama neighbourhood. It is generally the area around 64 Avenue, west of 152 Street and north of Highway 10 (56 Avenue). Those edges are approximate, so ask if a specific address is included. Sullivan, often called Sullivan Station, sits immediately to the east.

Homes for sale in Panorama Ridge are mainly family houses, with some townhouses mixed in. It feels like an established suburban neighbourhood rather than a new town centre. Local parks include Joe Brown Park and Panorama Heights Park. For daily shopping, many residents use the shops along 152 Street and the Panorama Village shopping centre at 152 Street and Highway 10.

This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood. Buses serve the main roads, including 64 Avenue and 152 Street, and Highway 10 is the usual east-west drive toward Delta or Cloverdale. Nearby public schools include Panorama Park Elementary and École Panorama Ridge Secondary, at 13220 64 Avenue. Catchments can include other Newton schools, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator for the exact address.

Panorama Ridge real estate is a fit if you want an established Newton neighbourhood of mostly houses, with a local secondary school and a short drive to Highway 10, without living in the busier Newton Town Centre to the north-west.

## Sullivan Station (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Sullivan Station, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Sullivan Station; Sullivan Station Surrey; living in Sullivan Surrey; Sullivan Heights Surrey; Sullivan Park Surrey

Sullivan Station is the name many buyers use for the neighbourhood around Surrey's historic village of Sullivan, in the eastern part of Newton. The City of Surrey points to this historic village, anchored by the Sullivan Community Hall, a heritage site at 152 Street. The usual heart of the area is around 152 Street and 64 Avenue, next to the railway. Panorama Ridge sits immediately west; the edges are approximate, so ask if a specific address is included.

Homes for sale here include older family houses, some newer houses, and townhouses. Sullivan Park, at 6272 152 Street, is a large community park with sports fields, tennis and pickleball, a playground, trails, and a view over agricultural land. Shopping is along 152 Street, with the Panorama Village shopping centre a short drive south at 152 Street and Highway 10.

This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood. Buses run along 152 Street and 64 Avenue, and Highway 10 is close for driving. Nearby public schools include Sullivan Elementary and Sullivan Heights Secondary. Other elementary schools in the same family also serve nearby streets. Catchments split by address, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator.

Living in Sullivan, Surrey, suits buyers who want an established neighbourhood with a local park and a small historic village at its centre, on the east side of Newton, without being in Cloverdale or the Newton Town Centre.

## Cloverdale (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Cloverdale, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Cloverdale; Cloverdale Surrey real estate; living in Cloverdale; Cloverdale townhouses; Cloverdale main street

Cloverdale is the historic centre of Surrey, on the city's eastern side, bordering the Township of Langley. The City of Surrey describes a mix of historic and newer neighbourhoods, a recognizable main street, and a western and railroad character. It is home to the Museum of Surrey, the Surrey Archives, and a number of heritage buildings, including the 1912 Municipal Hall. Since 1945, Cloverdale has hosted the annual Cloverdale Rodeo at the Cloverdale Fairgrounds. The city also holds a large Canada Day celebration here.

Homes for sale in Cloverdale range from older houses near the town centre to newer houses and townhouses in the surrounding neighbourhoods. Everyday shopping includes the historic downtown as well as stores along the main roads. Recreation facilities include the Cloverdale Recreation Centre, Cloverdale Arena, and Don Christian Recreation Centre. Parks include Cloverdale Athletic Park and Hi-Knoll Park. Clayton Community Centre is just to the north, in Clayton.

Highway 10 (56 Avenue) and 176 Street (Highway 15) are the main routes. Fraser Highway runs along the north side of the wider community. This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood today. The Surrey Langley SkyTrain project will add stations along Fraser Highway, including Hillcrest--184 Street and Clayton, which will be closer to the north side of Cloverdale than to the historic downtown. Nearby public schools include Cloverdale Traditional Elementary and Lord Tweedsmuir Secondary. Other elementary schools in the Lord Tweedsmuir family also serve the area. Check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator for the exact address.

Cloverdale real estate is a good fit if you want a town-centre neighbourhood with a main street, community events, and a mix of older and newer homes, on the Surrey--Langley edge.

## Brookswood (Langley Township)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Brookswood, Langley

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Brookswood; Brookswood Langley; living in Brookswood; Brookswood Fernridge; Brookswood real estate

Brookswood is a residential community in the south of the Township of Langley. The Township's official name for the wider area is Brookswood-Fernridge. It is known for parkland, trees, natural spaces, and conservation areas. The Township says the community primarily features single-family homes. Fernridge, to the south, has a more rural feel; Brookswood itself is the more settled residential area, with local shops and services.

The George Preston Recreation Centre is the main rec facility, with the Brookswood Library nearby. The Township also notes a BMX bike track and a skateboard park in the community. 200 Street and 24 Avenue / 40 Avenue are the usual local routes. This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood; most residents drive, with bus service on the main roads.

Nearby public schools include Brookswood Secondary, along with elementary schools in the same family, such as Belmont Elementary, Glenwood Elementary, and Noel Booth Elementary. Some nearby streets, including on the Langley City edge, may go to a different school. Confirm the catchment for the exact address with the SD35 school locator.

Living in Brookswood, Langley, suits buyers who want a house in a treed, established part of the south Township, with a rec centre and library close by, rather than a newer high-density neighbourhood.

## Aldergrove (Langley Township)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Aldergrove, Langley

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Aldergrove; Aldergrove Langley; living in Aldergrove; Aldergrove real estate; Aldergrove Community Centre

Aldergrove sits on the eastern border of the Township of Langley, toward Abbotsford. The Township describes a mix of rural, residential, and business use, with parkland, shops, and the Gloucester Industrial Park. It feels more like a distinct town than a suburban neighbourhood of central Langley, with a local main street along the Fraser Highway.

Homes for sale in Aldergrove include family houses, some townhouses, and larger or more rural properties toward the edge of the community. The Aldergrove Community Centre, at 27032 Fraser Highway, is a full rec facility, with a 25-metre pool, leisure pool, wave pool, waterslides, ice arena, fitness centre, and an indoor walking track. The Aldergrove Library is nearby. South of the community, Aldergrove Regional Park, a Metro Vancouver park, has trails and open space that straddle the Langley--Abbotsford line.

Fraser Highway is the main street. Highway 13 (264 Street) runs south to the Aldergrove--Lynden border crossing, and Highway 1 is a short drive north. This is not a SkyTrain community. Nearby public schools include Parkside Centennial Elementary, Shortreed Elementary, Betty Gilbert Middle School, and Aldergrove Community Secondary. Confirm the catchment for the exact address with the SD35 school locator.

Aldergrove Langley real estate is a fit if you want a more stand-alone Township community, with its own rec centre and shops, closer to Abbotsford and the border than to Langley's west-side neighbourhoods.

## Langley City

**Suggested H2:** Living in Langley City

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Langley City; Langley City real estate; living in Langley City; downtown Langley homes; Langley City condos

Langley City is its own municipality, separate from the Township of Langley that surrounds it. It was incorporated in 1955 and is a compact urban centre in the middle of the Township. The City describes a pedestrian-oriented downtown, with shops, restaurants, and services, and notes that the Nicomekl River runs through the community. Kwantlen Polytechnic University's Langley campus is in the city.

Homes for sale in Langley City include older houses, townhouses, and a growing number of apartments near downtown and the main corridors. It feels more urban than most Township neighbourhoods, with daily needs often within a short drive or a walk, depending on the street. Parks and recreation include City Park, the Timms Community Centre, and the Douglas Recreation & Child Care Centre. Willowbrook's larger shopping area sits just west, in the Township.

Langley Centre is a major TransLink bus exchange. This is not a SkyTrain community today, but the Surrey Langley SkyTrain project will add Langley City Centre Station near 203 Street and Industrial Avenue, with a transit exchange. Public schools in the city include Douglas Park Community Elementary, Nicomekl Elementary, Simonds Elementary, Uplands Elementary, Blacklock Fine Arts, and HD Stafford Middle School. Secondary school is at Langley Secondary, just outside the city boundary in the Township. Some addresses on the edge may belong to another family of schools. Check the SD35 school locator for the exact address.

Living in Langley City suits buyers who want a walkable downtown, city services, and a smaller municipal footprint, while still being close to the Township's neighbourhoods and, in the coming years, SkyTrain.

## Fleetwood (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Fleetwood, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Fleetwood; Fleetwood Surrey real estate; living in Fleetwood Surrey; Fleetwood townhouses; Fleetwood Park Secondary

Fleetwood is a town centre in north-central Surrey, with Whalley to the west, Guildford to the east and north, and Newton and Cloverdale to the south. The City of Surrey calls it the smallest of Surrey's town centres and describes a mostly residential area of single-family homes, parks, and a growing business presence along Fraser Highway.

Fraser Highway is the main corridor. This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood today, but the Surrey Langley SkyTrain project will add Fleetwood Station at 160 Street and Fraser Highway, and Bakerview--166 Street Station near the Sport & Leisure Complex. Nearby public schools include Fleetwood Park Secondary, Coyote Creek Elementary, Walnut Road Elementary, and William Watson Elementary. Check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator for the exact address.

Fleetwood Surrey real estate is a fit if you want a central, mostly house-oriented Surrey community with a strong rec centre, and you are looking ahead to SkyTrain on Fraser Highway.

## Guildford (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Guildford, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Guildford; Guildford Surrey real estate; living in Guildford Surrey; Guildford Town Centre; Guildford townhouses

Guildford is in northeast Surrey, with the Fraser River to the north, Whalley to the west, and the Township of Langley to the east. The community grew up around Guildford Town Centre, which the City of Surrey describes as the region's largest shopping centre, at 152 Street and 104 Avenue. The City also includes Fraser Heights, to the north toward the river, in the broader Guildford community; that pocket has its own, more house-and-park feel and is described on the North Surrey page.

Homes for sale in Guildford include houses, townhouses, and apartments, especially closer to the mall and 104 Avenue. Daily shopping is unusually close for a Surrey neighbourhood, because so much retail is concentrated at Guildford Town Centre. The Guildford Recreation Centre and Guildford Library are in this same area. For larger parks, Tynehead Regional Park and Surrey Bend Regional Park, both managed by Metro Vancouver, are in the community. Green Timbers Urban Forest Park is just to the west, in Whalley.

Highway 1, 152 Street, and 104 Avenue are the main routes. Buses serve the town centre and the surrounding streets. SkyTrain is farther than it is in Whalley: it is a bus or short drive to Surrey City Centre, not a walk from most Guildford streets. Nearby public schools include Guildford Park Secondary, Hjorth Road Elementary, Holly Elementary, and Lena Shaw Elementary. Catchments split by address, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator.

Guildford real estate is a good fit if you want a mix of housing next to a major mall and rec centre, with Highway 1 close by, without living in the City Centre towers.

## Notes on sources and items left out

Nothing in the body text is marked UNCONFIRMED. Facts were taken from official pages of the City of White Rock, City of Surrey, Township of Langley, City of Langley, Surrey Schools (SD36), Langley Schools (SD35), TransLink, Parks Canada, Metro Vancouver, Fraser Health, and the Province's Surrey Langley SkyTrain project. The following were left out because they were not clearly confirmed on those official sources, or because they could mislead:

-   Population, land-area, and housing-count figures (even where a city publishes them).
-   For a standalone East Clayton page, use the East Clayton paragraph as the core: it is the Langley-border, north-of-Fraser-Highway pocket, distinct from older Clayton streets and from West Clayton.
-   Exact legal boundaries for Panorama Ridge, Sullivan Station, Morgan Creek, and Ocean Park; edges are described as approximate where needed.
-   "Sullivan Station" as a City of Surrey official community name. The City refers to the historic village of Sullivan in Newton; Sullivan Station is the commonly used neighbourhood name.
-   Green Timbers Urban Forest as being in Guildford. The City's park page places it in Whalley and City Centre; the Guildford overview only notes that it is just to the west.
-   The old East Clayton Neighbourhood Concept Plans as current policy. The City states those NCPs were rescinded and that development is now led by the Official Community Plan. The overview describes the built neighbourhood, not the repealed plans.
-   The Albion Ferry, which no longer operates.
-   Current open/closed status of the Fort-to-Fort Trail.
-   A golf course in Panorama Ridge, and the Greater Vancouver Zoo in Aldergrove, pending a clearer official community-page mention.
-   Named store tenants inside malls and shopping centres, other than the shopping centres themselves.
-   Specific bus route numbers, except Marine Drive routes serving White Rock, the 350 (and the Ocean Park Library's list of 350, 352, 360, and 361), and the 562 in Fort Langley.
