# Area overviews for the website --- batch 3

Easy-to-read neighbourhood copy for Jared's area pages. No sales statistics. Official details (catchments, zoning, heritage rules, flood mapping, ALR status) should still be confirmed with the City of Surrey, the Township of Langley, or the school district for a specific address.

## Sunnyside / Elgin-Chantrell (South Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Sunnyside and Elgin-Chantrell, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Sunnyside Surrey; Elgin Chantrell Surrey; living in Sunnyside Surrey; Elgin Park Surrey; Chantrell Creek Surrey

Sunnyside and Elgin-Chantrell are neighbouring parts of South Surrey, on either side of the hill between Highway 99 and Crescent Road. They share a quieter, residential feel and a short drive to the shops and recreation around 20 Avenue and 24 Avenue, but they do not sit on the same streets. The edges are approximate, so ask if a specific address is included.

-   **Sunnyside.** This is the neighbourhood around Sunnyside Park, near 26 Avenue and 154 Street, just west of Highway 99. Homes for sale here are mainly family houses, with some townhouses mixed in. Sunnyside Park has an outdoor pool, playgrounds, tennis courts, sports fields, and ball diamonds. South Surrey Athletic Park, the South Surrey Recreation & Arts Centre, and Semiahmoo Town Centre are a short drive west and south. Catchments in this pocket can split between nearby schools on both sides of Highway 99.

-   **Elgin-Chantrell.** This is the treed area around Crescent Road, Chantrell Park, and Elgin Heritage Park, toward the Nicomekl River and the hill down to Ocean Park and Crescent Beach. The City of Surrey describes Elgin as a historic community; Elgin Heritage Park includes wetlands, forest, a marina, and the Historic Stewart Farm, a Victorian farmhouse from 1894 that is open to visitors. Chantrell Park is a trail park and the gateway to a linear park corridor running through Elgin Park toward Ocean Park. Homes here are mainly houses, some on larger, well-treed lots. Nearby public schools include Chantrell Creek Elementary and Elgin Park Secondary.

This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood. Buses serve the main roads, and most residents drive to Highway 99 or King George Boulevard. Other nearby public schools can include Jessie Lee Elementary, Sunnyside Elementary, and Earl Marriott Secondary, depending on the address. Catchments split in this part of South Surrey, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator for the home you have in mind.

Living in Sunnyside or Elgin-Chantrell suits buyers who want an established South Surrey neighbourhood of mostly houses, with parks close by, without living in the busier town-centre streets to the south.

## Rosemary Heights / King George Corridor (South Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Rosemary Heights and the King George Corridor, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Rosemary Heights; King George Corridor Surrey; living in Rosemary Heights; Rosemary Heights townhouses; King George Boulevard South Surrey

Rosemary Heights and the King George Corridor sit on opposite sides of Highway 99 in South Surrey, meeting near the Nicomekl River. They are close on the map, but they feel different: Rosemary Heights is a planned residential neighbourhood east of the highway, while the King George Corridor is the long strip of housing, shops, and services along King George Boulevard, west of the highway.

-   **Rosemary Heights.** The City of Surrey uses this name for the neighbourhoods south of the Nicomekl River, east of Highway 99, and north of Titman Creek. The area is largely built out and includes houses and townhouses, with a business park along the highway. It is also home to the Nicomekl, fish-bearing creeks, and wetland habitat. Morgan Creek sits immediately to the east. The City notes that Rosemary Heights Elementary and Morgan Elementary serve the area. Grandview Heights Aquatic Centre and the shops along 24 Avenue, including Grandview Corners, are a short drive south and east.

-   **King George Corridor.** This is the City's plan area along King George Boulevard, from 8 Avenue north to the Nicomekl River, with Highway 99 along its eastern edge. Homes for sale here include a mix of houses, townhouses, and some apartments, with shops and services along the boulevard. Semiahmoo Town Centre sits toward the south end of the corridor. South Surrey Athletic Park and the South Surrey Recreation & Arts Centre, at 20 Avenue and 146 Street, are in this part of South Surrey, next to Sunnyside Acres Urban Forest, a protected second-growth forest with walking trails. Nearby public schools can include Semiahmoo Trail Elementary and Jessie Lee Elementary; secondary catchments vary along the length of the corridor.

Land-use rules along both the corridor and Rosemary Heights are now guided by Surrey's Official Community Plan. Confirm zoning with the City if you are buying or renovating. This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood. Buses run along King George Boulevard, and Highway 99 is the usual regional drive. Catchments split by address and can change from one block to the next, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator.

Rosemary Heights real estate suits buyers who want a newer, house-and-townhouse neighbourhood beside the river and Highway 99. The King George Corridor is a better fit if you want to be on or near the main South Surrey boulevard, with shops, parks, and the rec centre close at hand.

## Hazelmere and Campbell Valley (South Surrey / Langley Township)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Hazelmere and Campbell Valley

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Hazelmere; Campbell Valley Langley; living in Hazelmere Surrey; Campbell Valley Regional Park; Hazelmere acreages

Hazelmere and Campbell Valley sit on either side of the Surrey--Langley border, in the rural southeast of South Surrey and the southwest of the Township of Langley. They share the Little Campbell River watershed, larger properties, and a short trip to Campbell Valley Regional Park. They are not, though, the same municipality, and school districts change at the border.

-   **Hazelmere.** This is a rural and semi-rural part of southeast Surrey, near 8 Avenue and the Langley border, in the Little Campbell River area. The City of Surrey uses Hazelmere as a heritage-area name, including for the historic Hall's Prairie school site at 18035 8 Avenue. The City describes the wider Campbell River watershed as mainly rural residential and agricultural, with a large portion in the Agricultural Land Reserve. Homes for sale here are often acreages or houses on larger lots; some properties are in the ALR and some are not, and parts of the area are in the Campbell River floodplain, which does not have dykes like Surrey's other river floodplains. Check zoning, ALR status, and flood mapping with the City --- and with the Agricultural Land Commission if the land is in the ALR --- before you write an offer. Highway 15 (176 Street) and 8 Avenue are the usual routes. Nearby public schools include Douglas Elementary and Earl Marriott Secondary. Xw'epiteng Elementary, the former Hall's Prairie school on 8 Avenue, is a district land-based learning program of choice, not a regular catchment school. Confirm the catchment with the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator.

-   **Campbell Valley.** Buyers use this name for the rural area around Campbell Valley Regional Park, in the southwest Township of Langley. It is not one of the Township's official community names; the park's official plan places Brookswood-Fernridge immediately north and describes the setting as farms, agri-businesses, and homes on acreages. The park itself is managed by Metro Vancouver, lies entirely in the Agricultural Land Reserve, and is entered from 8 Avenue and 16 Avenue. It has walking, cycling, and equestrian trails, picnic areas, a nature house, and heritage buildings at the Rowlatt Farmstead. Homes around the park are mainly acreages and larger rural properties. This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood, and daily shopping is a drive toward Brookswood, Murrayville, or South Surrey. Nearby public schools are in the Township; confirm the catchment for the exact address with the SD35 school locator.

Living in Hazelmere or Campbell Valley suits buyers who want space, a rural setting, and the regional park close by, and who are prepared to check servicing, zoning, and flood mapping on a specific property.

## Fraser Heights (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in Fraser Heights, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in Fraser Heights; Fraser Heights Surrey; living in Fraser Heights; Fraser Heights real estate; Fraser Heights Secondary

Fraser Heights is a family neighbourhood in northeast Surrey, just north of Highway 1. The City of Surrey includes it in the broader Guildford community and describes it as a subdivision that slopes down toward the Fraser River, with views of the river and the Port Mann Bridge along the northern edge. It has a quieter, house-oriented feel than the streets around Guildford Town Centre, south of the highway.

Homes for sale in Fraser Heights are mainly family houses, with some townhouses. Everyday recreation centres on Fraser Heights Park and the Fraser Heights Recreation Centre, at 10588 160 Street, at the entrance to the neighbourhood just north of the freeway. The rec centre has a weight room, a fitness studio, and two gymnasiums. The park has tennis courts, sports fields, a skateboard plaza, a basketball court, and forest walking trails. Tynehead Regional Park is a short way south of Highway 1, and Guildford Town Centre is a short drive west for shopping.

Highway 1 is the usual commute. This is not a SkyTrain community; buses serve the main roads, and Surrey City Centre is a drive or a bus trip rather than a walk. Nearby public schools include Dogwood Elementary, Bothwell Elementary, Erma Stephenson Elementary, Fraser Wood Elementary, and Fraser Heights Secondary, at 16060 108 Avenue. Catchments are set by address, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator for the home you have in mind.

Fraser Heights real estate is a fit if you want a house in a park-oriented North Surrey neighbourhood, with a local rec centre and secondary school, and quick access to Highway 1, without living in the Guildford mall area or the City Centre towers.

## West Newton (Surrey)

**Suggested H2:** Living in West Newton, Surrey

**Keyword phrases:** homes for sale in West Newton; West Newton Surrey; living in West Newton; West Newton townhouses; Tamanawis Secondary

West Newton is the name the City of Surrey uses for the community north of Highway 10 and west of King George Boulevard, in the west part of Newton, toward the Delta border. The City says most of the area was built out gradually over the twentieth century, while the central part stayed as larger suburban lots and forest until more recently. The City's neighbourhood plans cover a large portion of the community, generally south of 68 Avenue and north of Highway 10. Those edges are a useful guide, but ask if a specific address is included.

Homes for sale in West Newton include older family houses, newer houses, and townhouses. It is a largely residential area, with parks and schools mixed in, rather than a town-centre core. West Newton Community Park, at 13008 59 Avenue, has a playground, picnic shelters, a walking loop, and a cricket pitch. Tamanawis Park, at 12601 64 Avenue, has sports fields, including field-hockey pitches, and connects to the Serpentine Greenway. Boundary Park, near the Delta line, has forest trails, a playground, and a pond. For a larger shopping and recreation trip, Newton Town Centre sits to the northeast, across King George Boulevard.

This is not a SkyTrain neighbourhood. Buses serve King George Boulevard, 64 Avenue, and other main roads, and Highway 10 is the usual east-west drive. The City notes J.T. Brown Elementary, Panorama Park Elementary, and Tamanawis Secondary in this part of Newton. Catchments can include other nearby schools and they split by address, so check the Surrey Schools (SD36) locator.

West Newton real estate suits buyers who want an established Newton neighbourhood of houses and townhouses, with local parks and a short drive to Highway 10 and King George Boulevard, without living in the busier town centre to the east.

## Notes on sources and items left out

Nothing in the body text is marked UNCONFIRMED. Facts were taken from official pages of the City of Surrey, the Township of Langley, Surrey Schools (SD36), Langley Schools (SD35), and Metro Vancouver. The following were left out because they were not clearly confirmed on those official sources, or because they could mislead:

-   Population, land-area, and housing-count figures (even where a city or park page publishes them).
-   Exact legal boundaries for Sunnyside, Elgin-Chantrell, Hazelmere, the residential area around Campbell Valley Regional Park, and parts of West Newton beyond the City's "north of Highway 10 and west of King George Boulevard" description; edges are described as approximate where needed.
-   Sunnyside Acres Urban Forest as being in the Sunnyside Park neighbourhood. The City's athletic-park page places the forest next to South Surrey Athletic Park, at 20 Avenue and 146 Street, so it is mentioned with the King George Corridor.
-   "Campbell Valley" as a Township of Langley official community name. The Township's official community to the north of the park is Brookswood-Fernridge; Campbell Valley is the commonly used name for the rural area around the regional park.
-   Hall's Prairie Elementary as a current catchment school. The school was renamed Xw'epiteng Elementary and operates as a Surrey Schools land-based learning program of choice; the overview states that clearly and points buyers to the SD36 locator for the catchment school.
-   An approved Hazelmere Neighbourhood Concept Plan. The City's South Surrey land-use plans list does not include one; the overview describes the built rural area, ALR, and floodplain from official City sources, not a repealed or unadopted plan.
-   Rosemary Heights NCP and King George Corridor land-use designations as current zoning policy. The City states those designations have been superseded by the Official Community Plan; the overview describes the built neighbourhoods and notes that the OCP now guides land use. The West Newton South NCP was likewise rescinded.
-   Newton Recreation Centre, the wave pool, and Kwantlen Polytechnic University as being in West Newton. Those are Newton amenities, but they are not listed on the City's West Newton plan page, so they are not placed inside this neighbourhood.
-   A golf course in Hazelmere, named store tenants, and specific bus route numbers.
