# Commercial real estate facts for jaredgibbons.ca

Research compiled November 20, 2026 (America/Vancouver). This is
read-only research: no site changes, no contacts, no forms, and no
sign-ups.

## How this was prepared

- Facts are taken from city, regional district, Metro Vancouver,
  economic development, or business-park owner pages and official PDFs.
  Each fact was opened and read on the source page or document, not
  taken from search snippets alone.
- There are no opinions, rankings, or marketing language in the facts
  below. Where a city page uses promotional wording, only the underlying
  factual statement is recorded.
- No listing prices are included. Licence fees and announced
  infrastructure investments are included only where the official source
  states them; the read date (or the document date) is given.
- Where two official sources give different figures, both are reported
  and labelled. In particular, the City of Surrey industrial-lands web
  page still states 2020-era inventory totals; the Metro Vancouver 2025
  Industrial Lands Inventory (effective July 1, 2025) is the later
  regional dataset.
- If something could not be confirmed on an official source, the bullet
  says so. A summary of those items is at the end.
- Every bullet ends with its source URL and the date it was read.

## Metro Vancouver — regional industrial land context

- The Metro Vancouver 2025 Industrial Lands Inventory has an effective
  date of July 1, 2025 and includes lands locally designated industrial,
  plus lands locally zoned industrial and developed for industrial,
  retail or commercial use. (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- As of July 1, 2025 the Inventory totalled 12,067 hectares (29,819
  acres). (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- Of that total, 77% (9,277 ha) were in industrial use, 17% (2,028 ha)
  were Other/Vacant (totally vacant or with non-industrial uses that may
  convert to industrial), and 6% (763 ha) were in retail or commercial
  use. (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- More than half of Inventory lands (54%) were in the southern and
  eastern parts of the region: 22% in Surrey, 17% in Delta/Tsawwassen
  First Nation, and 14% in Richmond. (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- Between the 2020 and 2025 inventories there was a net increase of 195
  ha (2%). New parcels totalling 238 ha were added and parcels totalling
  83 ha were removed. (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- Net absorption (lands going from vacant to developed) was 379 ha over
  the five years. Key areas of absorption included Surrey (Campbell
  Heights), Burnaby (Big Bend), and Pitt Meadows. (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The Inventory is updated every five years. The 2025 report and
  executive summary are posted on Metro Vancouver’s Industrial Lands and
  Employment page. (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/industrial-lands-and-employment,
  read Nov 20, 2026)

## South Surrey (City of Surrey)

### 1. Industrial

- The City of Surrey lists eight business parks and industrial areas,
  including these in South Surrey: Campbell Heights Business Park,
  Rosemary Heights Business Park, the Highway 99 Corridor, and Douglas
  Industrial Area. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/business-economy/business-data/industrial-lands-business-parks,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The Metro Vancouver 2025 Industrial Lands Inventory identifies
  Campbell Heights as Surrey’s largest industrial area, at 770 ha (1,902
  acres). (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The City’s Campbell Heights Local Area Plan (approved by Council in
  2000; the City says it is currently building out) covers the
  industrial and business park area generally east of 184 Street, south
  of 44 Avenue and west of 196 Street, along the Township of Langley
  border, about 798 hectares (1,971 acres). (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/campbell-heights,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The City’s South Surrey land-use plans page describes the Campbell
  Heights LAP as designating industrial and employment land uses along
  Surrey’s eastern border, between 16 Avenue and 44 Avenue. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- A City of Surrey Economic Investment Services overview dated 2026
  states that Campbell Heights Business Park covers 1,900 acres, is home
  to 495 businesses, and has more than 12,600 people employed in the
  area (12,638 total jobs in the City’s 2025 employment table, of which
  12,195 are classified as industrial). (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CampbellHeightsBusinessPark.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The same 2026 City overview, using 2025 business licence data, lists
  the main business counts in Campbell Heights as construction (183),
  transportation and warehousing (92), wholesale trade (89), and
  manufacturing (81). (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CampbellHeightsBusinessPark.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- Zoning in Campbell Heights is described in the 2026 City overview as
  primarily IB-1 and IB-2, intended for industrial business parks and
  light impact industry, including warehouse, office and limited service
  uses in enclosed buildings. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CampbellHeightsBusinessPark.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The 2026 City overview states that regional offsite servicing
  (sanitary sewer, water, drainage, and transportation) has been
  constructed to meet current needs and support future growth in
  Campbell Heights, and that BC Hydro identifies the area as a major
  growth area, with a \$450 million investment over the next decade to
  deliver a new 230 kV Brookswood substation providing about 100 MVA of
  25 kV distribution capacity by 2033. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CampbellHeightsBusinessPark.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- On December 16, 2024, Surrey Council approved the Stage 2 South
  Campbell Heights Local Area Plan. Development applications consistent
  with the approved plan may now be considered. The plan area includes
  Mixed Employment, Conservation and Recreation designated lands south
  of the existing Campbell Heights Business Park, near the Township of
  Langley border, about 249 hectares (615 acres). (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/south-campbell-heights,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The 2026 City Campbell Heights overview states the area is adjacent to
  the South Campbell Heights expansion, with 350 acres of employment
  land added, and that the South Campbell Heights Plan is expected to
  generate between 5,907 and 7,702 jobs and to increase available
  employment land in Campbell Heights by 25%. Complementary commercial
  uses that serve the business park are also supported on smaller sites
  in South Campbell Heights. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CampbellHeightsBusinessPark.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The 2025 Industrial Lands Inventory reports that 154.4 ha of new
  industrial parcels were added in Campbell Heights between 2020 and
  2025, the largest addition in the region, and that key absorption of
  vacant land also occurred in Campbell Heights. Surrey’s vacant lands
  include a large share at Campbell Heights. (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- A City of Surrey corporate report dated July 22, 2019 (R186) describes
  the Campbell Heights North industrial business park lands as
  approximately 305 acres, including 61 acres of parkland, and concerns
  the phased purchase of those lands. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2019-R186.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- Corporate Report R030 (Council date March 6, 2023) and its appended
  Surrey City Development Corporation materials refer to industrial
  projects in Campbell Heights North and Campbell Heights East,
  including Campbell Heights East Phase 1 remainder at 2750 194A Avenue
  and Campbell Heights East Phase 1 Walmart at 19525 24 Avenue. A
  current City land-use plan page named “Campbell Heights East” or a
  current acreage or servicing announcement for a remaining east or
  north expansion was not found on official sources beyond these
  corporate reports. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2023-R030.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The Highway 99 Corridor Local Area Plan (approved by Council on
  February 23, 2004) covers lands generally bounded by Highway 99 to the
  west, the B.C. Hydro right-of-way and 164 Street to the east, 8 Avenue
  to the south, and Rosemary Heights to the north, with a gross area of
  about 158 hectares (390 acres). The City’s South Surrey plans page
  says this LAP designates employment land uses along the eastern edge
  of Highway 99. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/highway-99-corridor-local-area-plan,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- A 2026 City overview describes the Highway 99 Corridor (390 acres) as
  located centrally in South Surrey, home to 253 businesses, mostly
  commercial, and says zoning includes a mix of commercial,
  comprehensive development and residential, and that zoning encourages
  business park, light industrial, commercial and tourism uses. The
  City’s 2025 employment table lists 3,988 jobs in the corridor (2,846
  non-office commercial, 1,134 office commercial, and 8 industrial).
  (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/Hwy99CorridorIndustrialArea.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The 2026 City overview of Rosemary Heights Business Park describes it
  as 77 acres, bounded by 32 Avenue to the north, 156 Street to the east
  and Highway 99 to the southwest, home to 213 businesses. It is zoned
  for Business Park use, with Comprehensive Development zones to the
  east. The current neighbourhood plan envisions a business park for
  low-impact businesses such as high-tech industries, research and
  development companies, light manufacturing and corporate head offices.
  The City’s 2025 employment table lists 1,432 jobs there (1,382 office
  commercial, 40 industrial, 9 non-office commercial). (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/RosemaryHeightsBusinessPark.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The City’s South Surrey plans page describes the Rosemary Heights
  Business Park Plan as designating land uses for the north extension of
  the Highway 99 Corridor Local Area Plan, extending employment lands
  north to 32 Avenue. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- A 2026 City overview describes the Douglas Industrial Area as 26 acres
  in central-south Surrey at the Pacific Border crossing, home to 113
  businesses, including customs and logistics companies and the Canada
  Border Services Agency. The City says it is encouraging development
  with flexible zoning that could accommodate business parks, office,
  mixed-use and local-serving commercial. Land use is designated for a
  mix of industrial, commercial and residential, including Comprehensive
  Development zones. The City’s 2025 employment table lists 1,387 jobs
  in Douglas (540 office commercial, 250 non-office commercial, 490
  institutional, 107 industrial). (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/DouglasIndustrialArea.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)

- A city-wide inventory of office buildings or office strata in South

### 3. Retail

- The Semiahmoo Town Centre Plan was approved by Surrey Council on
  January 31, 2022. On July 27, 2026, Council approved updates to align
  the plan with King George Boulevard Bus Rapid Transit and with Surrey
  2050 Official Community Plan designations. The plan area extends from
  16 Avenue (North Bluff Road) in the south to 24 Avenue in the north,
  and from around Martin Drive in the west to Earl Marriott Secondary
  School in the east, totalling 136 hectares (336 acres). The City
  describes the plan as guiding a compact, transit-supportive urban
  centre and the commercial heart of South Surrey for the next 30 years.
  (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/semiahmoo-town-centre-plan,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The 2026 City Highway 99 Corridor overview states that the corridor is
  served by commercial businesses at Morgan Crossing and Grandview
  Corners, and that those commercial nodes provide business services,
  dining and shopping. The City plans road improvements along 20 Avenue,
  including an overpass over Highway 99. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/Hwy99CorridorIndustrialArea.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- A City of Surrey corporate report dated 2005 (L007) describes
  Grandview Corners as the commercial development located in the four
  quadrants of the intersection of 24 Avenue and 160 Street. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2005-L007-.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- A City of Surrey corporate report dated 2007 (R096) states that the
  Morgan Crossing site lies north and west of the Grandview Corners
  site, and that the site lies within the Highway 99 Corridor Local Area
  Plan and Area A of the Grandview Heights NCP Area \#1 (Morgan
  Heights). The report describes a proposal to rezone the Morgan
  Crossing lands for a large mixed-use retail, office, and residential
  development. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2007_R096.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The Grandview Heights General Land Use Plan (approved by Council in
  June 2005) covers about 1,050 hectares (2,600 acres), extending from
  16 Avenue in the south to 28 Avenue in the north and the Agricultural
  Land Reserve in the east. Neighbourhood concept plans within it
  include Morgan Heights (Area 1). (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/grandview-heights-general-land-use-plan,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The 2026 Rosemary Heights overview also names South Point and
  Semiahmoo Town Centre, together with Morgan Crossing and Grandview
  Corners, as commercial nodes within a short drive of the business
  park. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/RosemaryHeightsBusinessPark.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The King George Corridor Local Area Plan designates land uses along
  King George Boulevard, from 8 Avenue north to the Nicomekl River.
  (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans,
  read Nov 20, 2026)

### 4. Commercial land

- The City of Surrey industrial lands page states that Surrey has a
  total of 2,534 ha of industrial land, vacant and developed, of which
  365 ha is vacant developable land, and that Surrey has the largest
  share of the Metro Vancouver region’s developed lands (20%) and vacant
  lands (29%). Those figures match the Metro Vancouver 2020 Inventory
  total for Surrey (2,534 ha) as restated in the 2025 Inventory report;
  they do not match the 2025 Inventory totals below. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/business-economy/business-data/industrial-lands-business-parks,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The Metro Vancouver 2025 Industrial Lands Inventory (effective July
  1, 2025) reports Surrey at 2,713 ha (6,704 acres), 22% of the regional
  Inventory and the largest jurisdiction in the region, up 179 ha (7%)
  from 2,534 ha in 2020. (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The 2025 Inventory lists Surrey’s largest industrial areas as Campbell
  Heights (770 ha), Newton (449 ha), Bridgeview (343 ha) and South
  Westminster (341 ha). Top land-use classifications in Surrey include
  General Industrial (493 ha), Distribution/Warehouse (347 ha) and
  Vacant Land (285 ha). Surrey has the largest share of the region’s
  Vacant lands (31%), with over half at Campbell Heights. (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The 2025 Inventory reports 635 ha of Other/Vacant lands in the Surrey
  sub-region (31% of regional Other/Vacant land). Region-wide, 60% of
  Other/Vacant land was completely vacant. (Source:
  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The City states that at 317.2 square kilometres Surrey is the largest
  city in Metro Vancouver and has approximately 46% of Metro Vancouver’s
  total mixed employment land. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/business-economy/business-data/industrial-lands-business-parks,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- Planned employment land in South Surrey includes the approved South
  Campbell Heights Local Area Plan (Council approval December 16, 2024;
  about 249 ha / 615 acres of Mixed Employment, Conservation and
  Recreation land south of the existing business park). (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/south-campbell-heights,
  read Nov 20, 2026)

### 5. Businesses

- The City of Surrey requires a valid business licence to conduct
  business in Surrey. Applications may be made through the Online
  Business Licensing Application Portal or by a Business Licence
  Application form emailed to license@surrey.ca or mailed to Surrey
  Operations Centre, Business Licencing, 6651 148 Street, Surrey, BC V3S
  3C7. The Licensing Office is at that address; phone 604-591-4370.
  (Source: https://www.surrey.ca/business-economy/business-licensing,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The City’s types-of-businesses page states that all businesses in
  Surrey must hold a valid business licence, including businesses that
  do not have a physical location in Surrey but perform services in the
  city. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/business-economy/business-licensing/types-of-businesses,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- For a change of owner, the City’s updating-your-licence page states
  there is a \$25 fee, the applicant must provide a copy of the purchase
  and sale agreement, and the applicant should contact the Business
  Licence team at 604-591-4370 to see if there are additional
  requirements, such as health permits and criminal record checks. A
  change of address, once processed, may require inspections based on
  the type of business. A change of name results in a new business
  licence being issued. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/business-economy/business-licensing/updating-your-business-licence,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- Business License By-law, 1999, No. 13680, sections 24–25, states that
  an applicant who purchases the interest of a licensed business must
  not carry on or continue the business without first obtaining a
  transfer of licence or a new licence, and that the transfer fee is
  \$25.00 plus applicable taxes (section 27). (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_reg_13680.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)

## Cloverdale (City of Surrey)

### 1. Industrial

- A 2026 City of Surrey overview describes the Cloverdale industrial
  area as 700 acres in central-east Surrey, made up of three areas:
  Cloverdale East (333 acres), Cloverdale South (268 acres) and
  Cloverdale North (98 acres). It is home to 731 companies, with high
  concentrations in construction, manufacturing and
  wholesale/distribution. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CloverdaleIndustrialArea.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The same overview states the industrial area is at the intersection of
  two provincial highways. Highway 15 provides access to the
  Trans-Canada Highway 1 to the north and the Pacific Border crossing to
  the south. Highway 10 is described as a major east-west trucking
  route. Campbell Heights Business Park is described as being to the
  south and the Port Kells industrial area to the north. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CloverdaleIndustrialArea.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- Cloverdale East and South are designated for a mix of light and heavy
  industrial uses. Cloverdale North is designated for a mix of business
  parks and light and heavy industrial uses. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CloverdaleIndustrialArea.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The City’s 2025 employment table lists 11,264 jobs in Cloverdale
  (7,241 industrial, 2,240 institutional, 1,166 non-office commercial,
  617 office commercial). 2025 licence data list construction (323
  businesses), manufacturing (89) and wholesale trade (75) as the
  largest groups among 731 businesses. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CloverdaleIndustrialArea.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The 2026 overview states that infrastructure and servicing (sanitary
  sewer, water, drainage, and transportation) have been constructed to
  meet current needs and support future growth. It states that 72 Avenue
  improvements and extensions started in May 2025, to become a
  continuous east-west route connecting Cloverdale with other
  communities. It also states that BC Hydro recognizes the area as a
  major growth zone, with a \$15 million expansion of the 25 kV McLellan
  substation by 2026 and an additional \$56 million project to increase
  the station’s capacity by about 200 MVA by 2030. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CloverdaleIndustrialArea.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- A separately named “Highway 15 employment area” or a current official
  plan for a new Cloverdale/Highway 15 employment-land expansion, other
  than the existing Cloverdale industrial areas and the Cloverdale Town
  Centre Plan (which is at Highway 10 and Highway 15), was not found on
  official sources. (Not found on official sources, checked Nov 20,
  2026)

### 2. Office

- The City’s 2025 employment table reports 617 office-commercial jobs in
  the Cloverdale industrial area. A named office-building or
  office-strata cluster, or an office-only zone, in Cloverdale was not

  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CloverdaleIndustrialArea.pdf,

### 3. Retail

- The Cloverdale Town Centre Plan area is at the intersection of Highway
  10 and Highway 15 (Pacific Highway). Council endorsed a new Cloverdale
  Town Centre Plan on November 18, 2019. On October 30, 2023, Council
  endorsed an update of the plan, focused on supporting the planned new
  hospital and BC Cancer Centre. The City describes Cloverdale Town
  Centre as the historic heart of Surrey and a centre for commercial,
  social, cultural, and recreational activities. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/cloverdale-land-use-plans/cloverdale-town-centre-plan,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- On July 22, 2024, Council endorsed a northward expansion of the plan
  boundary, bounded by 60 Avenue to the south, 175 and 175A Street to
  the west, 64 Avenue to the north and 180 Street to the east, plus a
  lot at 18120 56 Avenue. The expansion covers roughly 80.8 hectares and
  145 properties. The plan update is in progress, with a plan document
  targeted for Council consideration in spring–summer 2027. The
  Cloverdale Fairgrounds are a Special Study Area and are not within the
  scope of the Town Centre Plan. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/cloverdale-land-use-plans/cloverdale-town-centre-plan,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- The 2026 industrial-area overview states that the Cloverdale
  industrial area has business services, dining and shopping at
  commercial nodes and the Cloverdale Town Centre. (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CloverdaleIndustrialArea.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)
- An official City source stating that the main retail street is
  specifically 176 Street, as a named retail corridor, was not found.
  The Town Centre is officially described as being at Highway 10 and
  Highway 15; 176 Street is the municipal name for the Highway 15
  alignment through Cloverdale, but that street-name wording was not
  confirmed on the Town Centre plan web page. (Not found on official
  sources, checked Nov 20, 2026)

### 4. Commercial land

- Cloverdale industrial and commercial land sits within the City of
  Surrey totals in the Metro Vancouver 2025 Industrial Lands Inventory
  (Surrey 2,713 ha as of July 1, 2025). The Inventory’s published list

  https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-planning/Documents/mv-industrial-lands-inventory-summary-report-appendices-2025.pdf,

  (Source:
  https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/CloverdaleIndustrialArea.pdf,

### 5. Businesses

- Cloverdale businesses are licensed by the City of Surrey. The same
  city-wide rules apply as for South Surrey: a valid licence is
  required; a change of owner has a \$25 fee and requires a copy of the
  purchase and sale agreement; Business License By-law No. 13680

  https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/Industrial%20Land%20Strategy.pdf,

  land was reduced to 86 hectares (213 acres); after also netting out
  sites with active non-industrial uses, 43 hectares remained as the

  https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/Industrial%20Land%20Strategy.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)

  https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/Industrial%20Land%20Strategy.pdf,

  existing Gloucester Industrial Estates (in the Township of Langley).
  Special Study Area B is described as in close proximity to existing
  industrial areas to the north and to the airport, with access to Mt
  Lehman Road and Highway 1; a later section locates Area B between the
  Clearbrook and Peardonville Industrial Areas and the airport, with Mt
  Lehman Road along the west edge and Townline Road toward the east.

  https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/Industrial%20Land%20Strategy.pdf,
  read Nov 20, 2026)

  identified represented 8 to 14 years of supply, with caveats that

  https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/Industrial%20Land%20Strategy.pdf,
