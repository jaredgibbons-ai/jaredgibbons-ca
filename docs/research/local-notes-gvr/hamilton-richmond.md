# Hamilton, Richmond

## Location
- The MLS area is "Hamilton RI"; the MLS street boundary is published only on GVR's interactive map and is UNVERIFIED. [1]
- The City has a Hamilton Area Plan (Bylaw 9000, Schedule 2.14) bounded by Westminster Highway, Boundary Road, Gilley Road, Highway 91, the North and South Arms of the Fraser River and New Westminster. [2][3]
- The City's trails vision for the area names McLean Park, Queensborough, Dyke Road and River Road. [4]

## Housing and zoning
- The plan permits large and small lot single-family homes, townhouses and apartments up to four storeys. [3]
- Single-family designations carry floor-area ratios of 0.55, 0.60 and 0.75; townhouse areas 0.75 and stacked townhouse 1.00; village centre up to 1.50. [3]
- Industrial, mixed-employment and marine residential/industrial designations sit along the rivers. [3]
- Hamilton is not among the five transit-oriented areas, so the 2024 small-scale multi-unit housing (RSM) rules apply to its single-family lots. [5]

## Parks, community facilities and shopping
- Hamilton Community Centre is at 5140 Smith Drive. [6]
- City park inventory lists McLean Neighbourhood Park (10.7 acres), Hamilton Community Park (12.4 acres), Hamilton Highway Park (7.2 acres) and North East Bog Forest. [7]
- The plan says the existing shopping centre and nearby Gilley Road properties may be redeveloped as a village centre, up to four storeys. [3]

## Schools
- Hamilton Elementary is at 5180 Smith Drive. [8]
- The plan places the existing elementary school in its School designation. [3]
- Secondary catchment: UNVERIFIED; use the SD38 locator. [9]

## Transit
- No bus route or Canada Line station near Hamilton was confirmed from TransLink sources; UNVERIFIED. [10]

## Risks and rules a buyer should know
- The plan says much of Hamilton has deep peat soils that are unstable and requires geotechnical and structural reports before construction. [3]
- It sits on the Fraser River floodplain; the plan requires dike upgrades, setbacks and flood construction levels. [3]
- ALR land forms a buffer along the west boundary, and the City does not support removing land from the ALR. [3]
- The plan was amended in June 2024 (Land Use Map and policies); confirm current designations with the City. [3]
- Richmond Fire-Rescue says pockets of liquefaction are possible in a significant earthquake. [11]
