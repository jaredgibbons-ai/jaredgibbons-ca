# Marpole, Vancouver

## Location
- Marpole is both an MLS sub-area name and a City local area. The City's Marpole Community Plan area is bounded by Angus Drive, West 57th Avenue, Ontario Street and the Fraser River, about 1,386 acres. [1]
- The MLS sub-area boundary may differ from the plan area: UNVERIFIED.
- Five arterials cross Marpole: Granville Street, Oak Street, Cambie Street, SW Marine Drive and West 70th Avenue. [1]
- Granville Street is the neighbourhood's main shopping street and holds the Marpole Library. [1]

## Housing and zoning
- Council approved the Marpole Community Plan on April 2, 2014. [2]
- At the time of the plan, about 10,100 homes, 55% apartments and 56% rented. [1]
- Plan land-use designations range from townhouse and rowhouse (up to 3 storeys) through apartments of 4, 6 or 8 storeys to mixed use up to 12 storeys. [1]
- Council approved Marine Landing policy updates on December 8, 2021, guiding rezoning for affordable housing and employment sites near Marine Drive Station. [2]
- Which zone applies to a given lot: UNVERIFIED.

## Parks, community facilities and shopping
- The plan names parks including William Mackie, Oak, Winona, Riverview, Marpole, Eburne, Ash, Ebisu, Shannon and Fraser River Park. [1]
- Marpole-Oakridge Community Centre is at 59th Avenue and Oak Street, with a gym, fitness centre, tennis court and the water park at Oak Park. [3]
- The plan's public benefits strategy calls for a renewed community centre and library and new childcare spaces. [1]

## Schools
- The plan names Sexsmith Elementary, Laurier Elementary, Laurier Annex, David Lloyd George Elementary and Churchill Secondary. [1]

## Transit
- Canada Line Marine Drive Station opened in 2009, per the plan. [1] TransLink lists it between Langara-49th Avenue and Bridgeport. [4]
- TransLink's weekday peak 80 Marine Drive Express links River District with Marine Drive Station. [5]
- Other bus routes: UNVERIFIED.

## Risks and rules a buyer should know
- Marpole's southern edge is the Fraser River. [1] The City names the Fraser River foreshore as its most flood-exposed area and plans for about 50 cm of sea level rise by mid-century. [6] Parcel-level exposure: UNVERIFIED.
- Rental: the 2014 plan reported 97% of rental units covered by Rate of Change regulations and a goal of protecting affordable rental in the Hudson sub-area. [1] The City now states the Tenant Relocation and Protection Policy replaced the Rate of Change Guidelines for applications received from February 15, 2016. [7]
- Under that policy, redevelopment of purpose-built rental needs a tenant relocation plan, compensation of 4 to 24 months' rent depending on tenancy length, and a right to return at a 20% discount to starting market rent. [8]
- Empty Homes Tax: annual declaration; 3% of assessed value for properties deemed or declared empty in the 2025 reference year (page as retrieved Nov 2026). [9]
