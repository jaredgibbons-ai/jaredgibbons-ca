# Yaletown, Vancouver

## Location
- Yaletown is a Greater Vancouver REALTORS® sub-area and a district of Downtown on the north side of False Creek; it is not itself one of the City's 22 local areas, where Downtown is listed. [1][2]
- The City says the 1900 warehouse district was bounded by Nelson, Homer, Drake and Pacific Streets, and the revitalized section lies north of Pacific Boulevard between Nelson and Drake. [1]
- A second City page gives different bounds (Homer and Nelson to the north and east, Cambie, Mainland and Drake to the south and west), so the MLS boundary is UNVERIFIED. [3]

## Housing and zoning
- The Yaletown Historic Area (HA-3) schedule caps height at 21.4 m (or existing height if greater) and floor space ratio at 5.00, with dwellings limited to 3.0; there is no residential use on the ground floor. [4]
- HA-3 aims to convert old warehouses and fit new buildings to circa-1900 proportions. [4]
- Nearby Downtown South is planned as a high-density residential and mixed-use area under the Downtown Official Development Plan, amended by Council in 1991. [5]
- Which zone applies to a given building: UNVERIFIED.

## Parks, community facilities and shopping
- The City parks map labels Emery Barnes Park, David Lam Park and Yaletown Park; it gives no facility details. [6]
- Roundhouse Community Arts and Recreation Centre is in Yaletown, with a gymnasium, studios, a 200-seat theatre and the Engine 374 locomotive. [7]
- The City names Granville and Davie Streets as Downtown South's main retail strips and recognizes Yaletown's loading docks and canopies as a heritage feature. [1][5]

## Schools
- Elsie Roy Elementary, 150 Drake Street; VSB describes it as full, with about 430 students and some catchment students on a waitlist. [8]
- VSB began a downtown catchment review after a new school was planned for Coal Harbour; outcome: UNVERIFIED. [9]

## Transit
- TransLink lists Yaletown-Roundhouse on the Canada Line, between Vancouver City Centre and Olympic Village. [10]
- Bus routes: UNVERIFIED.

## Risks and rules a buyer should know
- The City lists portions of False Creek North, including Yaletown, as flood-exposed from sea level rise, with over $19 billion of assessed value in the False Creek floodplain. [11]
- The City lists Downtown, False Creek and the Port Lands as a primary flood zone. [12] Parcel-level exposure: UNVERIFIED.
- The Tenant Relocation and Protection Policy applies citywide to rezoning and development permit applications affecting renters. [13]
- Empty Homes Tax: annual declaration; 3% of assessed value for properties deemed or declared empty in the 2025 reference year (page as retrieved Nov 2026). [14]
