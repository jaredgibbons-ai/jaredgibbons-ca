## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Zoning bylaw — note the name change.** Research briefs for Burnaby sometimes still cite the "Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965". The City states the new **Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026** was adopted June 9, 2026, replaced the former Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965, and took effect July 1, 2026. Use the 2026 bylaw. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/bylaws/zoning-bylaw
- **R1 SSMUH zoning:** Burnaby consolidated its single- and two-family districts into the R1 Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing district in June 2024 — 3 units on lots up to 280 m², 4 units on larger lots, and 6 units on lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent-transit stop; SSMUH lots require a minimum lot width of 10 m and minimum lot area of 281 m², lots with two or more primary units may be stratified, and secondary suites count toward the unit total and cannot be stratified. On Central's detached streets, that infill — suites, duplexes, multiplexes on house lots — is the only mechanism adding smaller ground-oriented homes; none of it is age-restricted. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **OCP: the Burnaby 2050 Official Community Plan** was adopted December 9, 2025, replaces the 1998 OCP, and takes precedence over older community-plan land use maps where they conflict. The Royal Oak Community Plan covers the plan area to the south of this sub-area. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-12-11/city-burnaby-adopts-burnaby-2050-official-community-plan and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/strategies-and-plans/official-community-plan/community-plans
- **Streamside checks on the east side:** Deer Lake and Deer Lake Brook drain the area's east side, so parcel-level streamside setbacks on lake- and brook-side lots are property-specific checks — UNVERIFIED for individual lots. Sources: the area research file (sources read 2026-11-01).
- **Property taxes:** Burnaby's 2026 property taxes were due Friday, July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date; the City's 2026–2030 Financial Plan approved a 2.9% tax increase (about $61 on the average home). The detached single-family water utility charge is $652.33, with a 5% discount if paid by the due date. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Central?** None that any source verifies. There is no age-restricted strata building with an address inside this sub-area, and no seniors rental, care home or subsidized seniors building either. A 55+ buyer in Central is buying an ordinary detached house (or one of the few general-market attached homes) with no age bylaw. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Is "Central" the same area as Central Park?** No. Central is the Canada Way / Deer Lake civic area in mid-Burnaby; Central Park is a separate sub-area around the park on the Vancouver border, west of Metrotown. The two have different stock and different September 2026 numbers, and seniors buildings assigned to one do not belong to the other. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report)
- **Q: Aren't the Dania buildings in Central? They are on Canada Way.** The campus is at Canada Way and Norland Ave — west of Willingdon Avenue, toward Burnaby Hospital — and it is documented in the Burnaby Hospital 55+ notes. It is also a non-profit rental, assisted-living and long-term care campus: there is nothing to buy there regardless of which sub-area label is used. https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/affordable-homes-open-for-seniors-in-burnaby
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. If we buy a house here, does any age rule apply?** No — a detached freehold house carries no strata age bylaw at all. The 55+ bylaw rules (and the younger spouse/partner exemption) only matter inside a strata corporation. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building elsewhere as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Can I add a suite or a second dwelling for a parent or caregiver instead?** That is the realistic ageing-in-place route in this sub-area: Burnaby's R1 SSMUH zoning allows 3–4 units on most house lots (up to 6 near frequent transit), secondary suites are part of the permitted forms, and multi-unit lots may be stratified. What a specific lot can hold depends on its size, width and transit proximity — and on the streamside checks for lake-side lots. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **Q: Is there a SkyTrain station in Central?** No. Metrotown Station (Expo Line) is south-west and Sperling–Burnaby Lake Station (Millennium Line) is north-east of the area; day-to-day transit inside Central is Canada Way bus service. Market this area on its civic and park position, not on a station. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/about-burnaby/town-centres
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Burnaby Hospital, 3935 Kincaid St, south of the sub-area — the hospital-side pocket west of Willingdon is also where the nearest seniors campus (Dania) sits. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Burnaby/burnaby-hospital
- **Q: If I need help later, can I stay in Central?** Not inside the sub-area — no assisted-living residence or long-term care home was verified here. The nearest ladder is the Dania campus west toward the hospital (subsidized/market seniors rental at Dania Gardens, assisted living at Dania Manor, long-term care at Dania Home), accessed by application through the society, BC Housing or Fraser Health, not by purchase. https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/affordable-homes-open-for-seniors-in-burnaby

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The file name lies about the compass.** The site files this area as "central-bn," but the research file states the "-bn" is a filing convention only: the board name is "Central," and the Canada Way / Deer Lake civic area is mid-city, not North Burnaby. Any inherited copy calling this "Burnaby North" is wrong. Source: the area research file (sources read 2026-11-01).
- **Central also shares its school list with the hospital sub-area.** Douglas Road Elementary, Cascade Heights Elementary and Burnaby Central Secondary appear in both the Central and the Burnaby Hospital research files, because the two sub-areas meet around Willingdon Avenue and Canada Way — which is exactly why the hospital-side seniors buildings keep getting pulled into Central searches. The buildings did not move; the boundary is simply UNVERIFIED in any public source. Sources: the area research files for Central and Burnaby Hospital (sources read 2026-11-01).
- **Thin is the normal state of this market.** One detached sale on 14 listings and no attached sale on 8 listings in September 2026 is not a collapse — it is a small civic sub-area with little turnover. Price opinions here need a long window and wider central Burnaby comparables. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report)
- **Part of the Deer Lake boardwalk is closed.** A section of the Deer Lake Park boardwalk closed on September 21, 2026, with replacement expected by winter 2027 — the lakeside walking loop at this sub-area's east edge is interrupted in the meantime. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/parks/deer-lake-park
- **City Hall is in the sub-area.** Burnaby City Hall and the governmental and cultural precinct sit at the Deer Lake end of Central — property-tax, permit and plan questions for a Central house are answered in person inside the neighbourhood itself. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/parks/deer-lake-park
- **Burnaby replaced its entire zoning bylaw in 2026.** The Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026 (adopted June 9, 2026, in force July 1, 2026) repealed and replaced the Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965 — documents or summaries that still cite the 1965 bylaw are out of date. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **Burnaby taxes have two penalty steps.** 2026 property taxes were due July 3, 2026; a 5% penalty applies after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date, and the detached water charge ($652.33) carries a 5% early-payment discount. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees
