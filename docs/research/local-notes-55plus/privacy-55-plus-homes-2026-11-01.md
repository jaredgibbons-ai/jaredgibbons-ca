## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT THIS HOME TYPE

- **The governing framework (Province of BC):** to collect, use or disclose personal information, private sector organizations in BC must follow the personal information and privacy rules set out in the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), which are based on the 10 principles of privacy protection, and the rules generally apply to all private sector organizations including corporations, partnerships, unincorporated associations and not-for-profit organizations. The live privacy page names no statute — PIPA is the framework the rebuild must be written against, not a law the current page cites. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/managing-a-business/running-a-business/protect-personal-information
- **Privacy officer and policy duties (Province of BC):** every organization must designate at least one privacy officer to oversee privacy practices and ensure compliance with PIPA, with the privacy officer as the primary contact for privacy-related questions and complaints; every organization must have a privacy policy, available upon request, that explains how personal information is collected, used, disclosed, stored and disposed of; and an organization is responsible for all personal information it handles, including information shared with contractors. The live page names no designated privacy officer, and whether Jared or the brokerage has one whose name or title the rebuilt page should publish is UNVERIFIED. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/managing-a-business/running-a-business/protect-personal-information and https://jaredgibbons.ca/privacy.html
- **Oversight and safeguards (OIPC):** organizations in BC are subject to PIPA, which has rules for how and when organizations can collect, use and/or disclose personal information (information about identifiable individuals such as name, account numbers and photographs, including information about customers); organizations also need to make reasonable security arrangements to protect personal information; and the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner (OIPC) for BC oversees and enforces the administration of PIPA, including investigating privacy complaints and whether organizations have reasonable safeguards. https://www.oipc.bc.ca/documents/guidance-documents/3134
- **Breaches (OIPC):** when personal information is collected, used, disclosed or destroyed in contravention of PIPA it is called a privacy breach; the OIPC guidance sets out a four-step breach response (containment, risk evaluation, notification, prevention) and organizations can report breaches to the OIPC. The live page states no breach-notification practice — a rebuild drafting item, not a fact about current practice. https://www.oipc.bc.ca/documents/guidance-documents/3134 and https://jaredgibbons.ca/privacy.html
- **The age-bylaw frame behind the enquiries this page receives:** only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now (Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, since Nov 24, 2022); the bylaw restricts who may live in a unit, not who may own it, and Strata Property Regulation 7.01 carries the exemptions (legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, defined children). Stated here in one line because it is the subject of most 55+ enquiries the site's forms collect information about — the detail lives in the area files. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Retention, disposal and the statute text:** the live policy states no retention period, no destruction practice and no service-provider or cross-border storage detail anywhere in the text read — those topics are absent from the page, which is a rebuild gap rather than a page statement, and current practice on each is UNVERIFIED.
