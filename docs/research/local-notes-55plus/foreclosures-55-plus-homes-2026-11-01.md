## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **How a BC foreclosure starts — Rule 21-7 (1):** "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Parties — Rule 21-7 (2) and (3):** all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished and all persons against whom any relief is sought must be made respondents; a petitioner may also join a claim arising out of the mortgage or collateral security and a person liable to pay the mortgage debt. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Later interests — Rule 21-7 (4):** if the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation against the mortgaged property, a person who subsequently registers or files an interest in a land title office need not be served with the petition, is bound by an order made in the proceeding, and may file a response to petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Information notice — Rule 21-7 (4.1):** a petitioner who serves the petition must include with it an informational notice for foreclosure proceedings in Form 125. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Powers of the court — Rule 21-7 (5)(a)–(c):** the court may make a final order of foreclosure; order that a respondent must, within a redemption period that the court may fix, pay to the petitioner what is due under the mortgage and for costs, and that in default of payment the respondent is to be foreclosed of the respondent's equity of redemption; and determine summarily, or order that an account be taken of and that a registrar certify, the amount due. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Redemption period length:** the Rule text read states no fixed length — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5)(b). Any "usual" length quoted from non-official sources is NOT adopted here. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Everything after Rule 21-7 (5)(c) — UNCONFIRMED in this program.** That includes the final-order application, an application for an order that the property be sold, and an application to confirm a sale, direct the proceeds and vest title in the purchaser, including any sale before the redemption period ends. Do not publish those mechanics from this file. ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md
- **Rule 13-5 mechanics — UNCONFIRMED.** Conduct of sale, court directions, reserve or minimum price, sale by contract conditional on court approval, bid and offer rights, and Form 60 certification were not read from official text in this program. The live page's statement that competing offers can come in at the courthouse on the hearing day sits in this zone and is the page's own statement, not a verified rule. https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html
- **Practice Direction 66 — UNCONFIRMED,** including any sealed-bid timing or manner for competing bids. ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md
- **Law and Equity Act ss. 19/21 and Land Title Act s. 271 — UNCONFIRMED,** including any order nisi wording and any pre-petition demand step under the standard mortgage terms. ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md
- **"As-is, where-is" conditions, absence of a Property Disclosure Statement, absence of subjects at the court stage, and tenancy treatment on a court-ordered sale — UNCONFIRMED as stated process rules.** No official source opened in this program states a standard conditions form or its terms; the live page's as-is / no-subjects wording is its own editorial. https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html
- **The 55+ age-bylaw layer still applies to a court-ordered purchase of a strata unit.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older, and any age bylaw set below 55 has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022. A court sale does not remove a registered age bylaw from the strata a buyer is purchasing into. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Age-bylaw exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** legacy residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed; live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. Whether and how those exemptions interact with a specific court file: UNCONFIRMED — confirm with a lawyer on that file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide** as of Nov 24, 2022; stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Property transfer tax (general rates only):** 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion above $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% on the portion above $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion of value above $3,000,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure actually start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rule text read in this program — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5)(b). Do not rely on a "usual" figure from a non-official source for a specific file. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What can the court order at the first stage?** Under Rule 21-7 (5) the court may make a final order of foreclosure, order payment of what is due within a court-fixed redemption period with foreclosure in default, or determine or have certified the amount due. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer at the approval hearing?** UNCONFIRMED as a stated process rule in this file — the bid-rights wording in Rule 13-5 and the PD-66 practice direction remain UNVERIFIED program-wide, and the live page's statement that competing offers can come in at the courthouse on the hearing day is the page's own account, not adopted here. Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for, and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel. https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html
- **Q: Are foreclosures cheaper than regular listings?** No published source in this program supports a standard foreclosure discount, and SnapStats publishes no foreclosure category, so no foreclosure price or discount figure is stated in this file. The live page's own caution stands as editorial: "foreclosures aren't always cheap." https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html
- **Q: If a unit in a 55+ building is sold through the court, does the 55+ rule still apply to me?** The age bylaw is a strata bylaw under Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2, not a term of the individual sale — nothing in the Rule 21-7 text read removes it. You would need to meet the bylaw or an exemption under Regulation 7.01 to live in the unit, even though ownership itself is not age-restricted. Confirm on the specific file with a lawyer: UNCONFIRMED for any specific court file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Do I buy as-is, with no subjects and no disclosure statement?** UNCONFIRMED as a general rule in this file. The live page states that position as its own editorial, but no official source opened in this program states a standard conditions form. Expect the conditions in the form for that specific file, confirmed with a lawyer, not a standard residential contract. https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html
- **Q: What taxes do I budget on a court-ordered purchase?** The general PTT tiers are stated in Section 3 from the Province's PTT page. The GST treatment and the exact tax position for the specific property are UNVERIFIED here — cost them with your lawyer before setting a ceiling. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Which 55+ buildings should I watch for a court sale?** The verified universe is the 68 strata complexes counted in Section 2 — from the single Mayfair Terrace in Port Coquitlam to Abbotsford's 26 — plus the parks, co-operatives and life leases carried in the city files. No building on that list is claimed to be in foreclosure; a watch only pays off if a specific unit in a specific building enters a specific court file. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Can the owner still save the property after I find a listing?** The court may order payment of what is due within a redemption period the court fixes, with foreclosure of the equity of redemption in default — Rule 21-7 (5)(b) — so a file's stage matters before a buyer spends on inspections or legal review. Ask which stage the file is at. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The hub page's most quotable claims are exactly the ones this program could not verify.** Competing offers at the courthouse, as-is with no warranties, and no subjects at the court stage all appear on the live page — and all three sit in the Rule 13-5 / PD-66 zone that no official text was read for in this program. They are flagged UNCONFIRMED in Section 3 rather than repeated as mechanics. https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html
- **The verified law in this file stops mid-rule, on purpose.** Rule 21-7 subrules (1), (2), (3), (4), (4.1) and (5)(a)–(c) were confirmed verbatim on BC Laws in this program's batch-10 pass; everything after (5)(c) — sale applications, confirmation, proceeds, vesting — is UNVERIFIED program-wide. A guide that describes the full sale-and-vesting sequence smoothly is going past the verified text. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **There is no foreclosure discount statistic to quote, anywhere in the permitted sources.** SnapStats has no foreclosure category, and no official source publishes Fraser Valley foreclosure counts — so any page stating a typical court-sale discount for this region is stating an UNVERIFIED figure. ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-15/foreclosures.md
- **A 55+ court purchase carries two independent gates, not one.** The buyer must satisfy the court file's own process (whatever that file's verified instructions are) AND the strata's 55+ residency bylaw or a Regulation 7.01 exemption. Passing the first gate says nothing about the second. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The redemption order can already be final at the first stage.** The court's powers under Rule 21-7 (5) include making a final order of foreclosure — a buyer or a seller who assumes every file is at an early, reversible stage is assuming something the Rule does not say. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **The regional 55+ universe is thin outside two cities.** Abbotsford (26 strata) and Surrey (15 strata) hold 41 of the 68 counted strata complexes; Port Coquitlam holds one, Ladner three and Mission three — so a 55+ court-sale watch in those smaller communities is a watch on a handful of named buildings, not a market segment. Sources: Section 2 above.
