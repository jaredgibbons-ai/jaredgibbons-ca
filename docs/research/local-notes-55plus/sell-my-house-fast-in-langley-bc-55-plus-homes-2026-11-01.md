## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. A seller marketing a unit as "45+" or "19+" is marketing a bylaw form that cannot be valid today. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. For a seller, that means the buyer pool includes under-55 buyers who will not live in the unit — and excludes under-55 buyers who would. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. A seller can therefore offer a 55+ unit to an investor buyer, but the investor's tenant must still satisfy the 55+ bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **A park's 55+ rule can stop a sale outright.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Regulation (B.C. Reg. 481/2003) recognises a park where every site is reserved for a tenant who has reached 55 and allows a park landlord to refuse an assignment where the proposed purchaser does not meet the park's age requirement. Assignment also requires the landlord's prior written consent under MHPTA s. 28 (the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent), and on assignment the pad rent stays the same. Build the consent step into a "fast" timeline for any Brookswood park home. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Pad-rent increases (MHPTA):** only with 3 full months' written notice, only once every 12 months, and only up to the yearly limit — 2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026 (2.2% plus the proportional amount for 2027). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **The regulator's framework for the service itself:** in BC, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings; dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **What the page does NOT state — all UNVERIFIED:** any guaranteed timeline or guaranteed price; a cash-offer program or any named buyer; fees, commission, or who pays for preparation, photography or advertising; any committed time-to-offer; and what happens if a home does not sell on the seller's timeline. When agency disclosure occurs in the fast-sale process: UNVERIFIED — the page does not state it. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sell-my-house-fast-in-langley-bc.html
- **Two tax calendars, kept separate:** Township of Langley property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty at midnight July 2, 2026 and a further 5% at midnight September 3, 2026. City of Langley payments received after the July 2 due date incur a penalty whose 2026 percentage is UNVERIFIED (the City page states a penalty applies but not the rate). A seller closing around those dates needs the right municipality's calendar. https://www.tol.ca/en/services/property-taxes.aspx and https://www.langleycity.ca/PayPropertyTaxes
- **Two zoning bylaws, kept separate:** Township homes sit under Township of Langley Zoning Bylaw 1987 No. 2500 (its "seniors' housing" definition caps a seniors' housing unit at 70 m², per a Township council report); City homes sit under City of Langley Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300 (adoption date UNVERIFIED — do not state one). https://e-connect.tol.ca/OurCity/Prospero/FileDownload.aspx?fileId=866EA2E9-916E-485E-9075-F59C90961D19&folderId=47498C260508154601958591 and ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/rules-gaps-2026-11-01.md

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What does this page actually offer a Langley seller who needs to sell fast?** A fitted plan, not a guarantee: the seller weighs timeline, home condition and goals, then applies thoughtful pricing, focused preparation and a launch schedule, with routes to a free home evaluation and a direct conversation. The page names no cash-offer program, no guaranteed timeline and no fee — and it opens by disavowing "promises or guarantees." https://jaredgibbons.ca/sell-my-house-fast-in-langley-bc.html
- **Q: How fast can a Langley home actually sell?** UNVERIFIED as a general claim — the page's FAQ says a well-prepped, well-priced home in a desirable neighbourhood can have offers within seven to fourteen days and that closing usually adds 30 to 60 days, but those are the page's editorial figures with no official source, and no SnapStats or Paragon timeline for Langley fast sales is stated in this file. Your timeline depends on your home, neighbourhood and pricing, established from comparables. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sell-my-house-fast-in-langley-bc.html
- **Q: Will selling fast cost me money?** The page's own framing is a trade-off: it claims pricing 3–5% below comparables usually nets close to fair market through competing offers, while a seven-day cash-investor sale usually costs 15–25% — both figures are UNVERIFIED site copy, not sourced data. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sell-my-house-fast-in-langley-bc.html
- **Q: I own a unit in a 55+ complex. Does the age rule slow my sale?** Mechanically it narrows the resident buyer pool: the next resident must be 55+, be an exempt spouse/partner, caregiver or legacy case — though an under-55 buyer may still purchase without living in the unit, and may rent it out (long-term rental bylaws are void), provided the tenant satisfies the age bylaw. In Langley the verified 55+ stock is only 15 ownership complexes, so a listed unit also faces little direct same-complex competition. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm selling a home in a 55+ park. What extra steps apply?** The park's written consent to assignment under MHPTA s. 28 — and in a 55+ park the landlord may refuse an assignment where the proposed purchaser does not meet the park's age requirement. The pad rent carries to the buyer unchanged. At Pioneer Park the extra step is co-op acceptance plus a share purchase. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://murphyrealestategroup.ca/recip.html/1272399.search/SearchResults.form?_pg=3
- **Q: Can I market my building as 55+ if its records just say "age restrictions"?** Not on that wording alone. Highgate Terrace's building record says "age restrictions" with no age stated, and Morningside Estates' 55+ status is UNCONFIRMED — and since Nov 24, 2022 only a 55+ bylaw is valid in any case. Market the age rule from the registered bylaws, not from a building-data line. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/highgate-terrace-19645-64-avenue and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Do I have to fix everything up before selling?** On the page's editorial advice: for an MLS® listing, basic prep (declutter, deep clean, minor repairs, professional photos) is presented as worthwhile and major renovations as rarely paying back; for a cash sale, no preparation, with the trade-off in price. Those are the page's claims, stated here as site copy — what prep pays on a specific home is property-by-property. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sell-my-house-fast-in-langley-bc.html
- **Q: What does this service cost, and does requesting it make me Jared's client?** The page states no fee or commission, and does not say when client status begins. Under the BC framework BCFSA publishes, a licensee must give consumers information about commissions and fees and must disclose at the outset of dealings whether they can represent you as a client — ask for both in the first conversation. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Does "sold" mean my deadline is met?** No — separate "sold" from "sold and closed," as the page's FAQ itself does: even on the page's editorial numbers, an accepted offer and a completed sale are weeks apart, and completion dates are negotiated per deal. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sell-my-house-fast-in-langley-bc.html
- **Q: Which municipality's rules apply to my sale?** The one the home sits in: Township homes run under Township Zoning Bylaw 1987 No. 2500 and the Township tax calendar (5% penalties July 2 and September 3, 2026); City homes run under City Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300 and the City tax calendar. The page serves both, but the paperwork is not interchangeable. https://www.tol.ca/en/services/property-taxes.aspx and https://www.langleycity.ca/PayPropertyTaxes

- **The page disavows guarantees, then its FAQ prints numbers anyway.** The page opens by fitting strategy to the seller "rather than relying on promises or guarantees," but its FAQ then states offers in seven to fourteen days, closing in 30 to 60 days, "sold and closed" in six to ten weeks, a 3–5% underpricing strategy and a 15–25% cash-investor cost. - **Langley's 55+ sellers are concentrated in two pockets.** Nine of the fifteen verified 55+ ownership complexes stand in Langley City's downtown cluster and Walnut Grove's 208 St / 88 Ave belt; the rest are single complexes in Willoughby, Aldergrove and Fort Langley, plus the three Brookswood parks and the Aldergrove co-op. - **The three tenures sell on three different rule books.** A 55+ strata sale turns on the registered bylaw and Regulation 7.01 exemptions; a 55+ park sale turns on MHPTA assignment consent and the park's power to refuse a buyer who fails the age rule; a Pioneer Park sale turns on co-op acceptance and a share purchase. Timelines that ignore which book applies are not timelines. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

- **No Langley fast-sale result is published anywhere on the page.** No testimonial, sold example, result statistic or days-on-market claim for Jared's own Langley sales appears in the page's body copy — it argues from approach only. Any "how fast" figure a seller is quoted should be asked for against Paragon comparables for their own complex or street, not the page. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sell-my-house-fast-in-langley-bc.html
