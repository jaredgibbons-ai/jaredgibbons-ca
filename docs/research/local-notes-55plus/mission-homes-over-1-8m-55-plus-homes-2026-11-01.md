## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. This matters on this page in reverse: a general-market strata an over-55 buyer purchases cannot later adopt a 19+ or 40+ "adults only" bylaw — 55+ is the only age bylaw available to it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Zoning: Zoning Bylaw 5949-2020** is Mission's zoning bylaw; rural zones such as RU16 carry a 1.6 ha minimum lot size in City notices — the framework behind this band's acreage product. https://www.mission.ca/business-building/land-development-planning
- **Current plan: Official Community Plan Bylaw 6405-2026** replaces and repeals OCP Bylaw 6350-2025, keeping the same vision, land use framework, mapping and policy direction with a Regional Context Statement added; the OCP carries Urban Residential, Suburban Residential and Urban Residential Townhouse designations. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/public-hearing-notice-april-7-2026 and https://www.mission.ca/council-government/plans-strategies/official-community-plan
- **Suites and added units at the top of the market:** the City's SSMUH page states zones previously restricted to single family or duplex dwellings are subject to the provincial small-scale multi-unit housing legislation the City has adopted, and City planning pages set out how secondary suites and detached accessory units are handled by zone — the legal route for adding a caregiver suite or a family unit to a large house or acreage in this band. https://www.mission.ca/planning/small-scale-multi-unit-housing-ssmuh
- **ALR and flood checks scale with this band:** Agricultural Land Reserve land sits mainly on the city's rural edges, with status parcel-specific on Agricultural Land Commission mapping; the Fraser River floodplain runs along the city's south edge and low areas such as Hatzic Prairie are protected by the Dewdney Dyke system, and the City's tax rates page states additional taxes are levied for diking on top of the municipal, school, regional district and BC Assessment levies. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca and https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Bylaws/Planning~and~Land~Use/Area%20F%20-%20OCP%20Bylaw%20999%20-%20Hatzic%20Valley.pdf and https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-rates
- **Property taxes:** Mission's 2026 property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a flat 10% penalty added to any current balances outstanding on July 3, 2026 (Mission has no staged 5% + 5% scheme). https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes
- **First-home programs at this price point:** FHSA and Home Buyers' Plan dollar limits are UNVERIFIED in this file — they were not read off a live CRA page in this research, and no figure is stated.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is there a 55+ home in Mission at or over $1.8 million?** None was verified on any source checked. Mission's entire verified 55+ inventory — Cherry Lane and Luther Place (strata townhouses), Cedar Valley Manor (life lease), Mission Mobile Home Park and the adult side of Oasis Country Estates (manufactured homes on pads) — is product priced far below the band by type, and no attached property over $1,800,000 in Mission is verified at all (attached community median $430,000, September 2026). Sources: Section 2 above; SnapStats FVR September 2026 report.
- **Q: What 55+ homes CAN I buy in Mission, then?** Two verified age-restricted strata complexes: Cherry Lane (32165 7th Ave, 20 townhouses, 1989, NWS2971) and Luther Place (7525 Martin Place, NWS2729, 1988). Beyond strata, the buy-in products are a life lease at Cedar Valley Manor (8358 Cedar St — an occupancy interest, not ownership) and manufactured homes on rented pads at Mission Mobile Home Park (34519 Lougheed Hwy). Sources: the building entries in Section 2.
- **Q: I'm over 55 and buying an estate in this band. Does any age rule help or hurt me?** The estate itself carries no age bylaw — it is general-market freehold. The age-bylaw machinery only appears if the property is a strata: there, only a 55+ bylaw is legal, it restricts residency not ownership, and a younger spouse or partner is exempt. On a freehold acreage, who lives in the house is the owner's choice. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I add a suite or a second dwelling for a caregiver or family on a top-end acreage?** That runs through City zoning, not age rules: secondary suites and detached accessory units are handled by zone under the City's planning pages, the City has adopted the provincial SSMUH framework for eligible single-family and duplex zones, rural zones such as RU16 carry a 1.6 ha minimum in City notices, and ALR status on the rural edges is parcel-specific — confirm the zone and the ALR before pricing any added-unit potential. https://www.mission.ca/planning/small-scale-multi-unit-housing-ssmuh and https://www.mission.ca/business-building/land-development-planning and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in one of the city's 55+ strata complexes?** Yes — provincial rules exempt a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Cherry Lane's own listing text advertises "Family Exemptions." In a manufactured-home park the park's own rules govern instead — read them before writing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www.zolo.ca/mission-real-estate/32165-7-avenue/5
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ building, or rent out an estate while I decide?** In a strata, long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022; your tenant still has to meet the 55+ bylaw or an exemption, and short-term rentals can still be restricted. A freehold estate has no strata bylaw layer at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: How many Mission homes are actually over $1.8 million?** That count cannot be stated from the permitted source: SnapStats publishes Mission totals with no price-band cut (231 detached and 122 attached active listings in September 2026), so no band count, band share or band median is claimed in this file. The live page's feed displays its own listing count, which is a feed display, not a verified band statistic. Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report, and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mission-homes-over-1-8m.html
- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink my resale buyer pool (in the city complexes)?** Mechanically, yes: the next buyer must also be able to live there under the bylaw — 55+, an exempt spouse/partner, a caregiver, or a legacy case — or buy without living in the unit. The over-$1.8M band has the opposite profile: no age restriction at all, but a thin comparable set — the whole city recorded 28 detached sales in September 2026 across all prices. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report.
- **Q: Where is the hospital, and what care is in town if I need it later?** Mission Memorial Hospital is at 7324 Hurd St, with the Mission Community Health Centre at 7298 Hurd St and the Mission Urgent and Primary Care Centre at 304-32555 London Ave (open 7 days, 9:00 a.m.–8:00 p.m.). The in-town ladder runs: independent strata (Cherry Lane, Luther Place) → life lease (Cedar Valley Manor) → independent/assisted rental (Chartwell Cedarbrooke, Chartwell Carrington House, The Cedars) → long-term care. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Mission/mission-memorial-hospital and https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/About~the~FVRD/Documents/Homelessness/2020%20Mission%20Housing%20Inventory.pdf
- **Q: Does Mission flood at the high end too?** The city's south edge is Fraser River floodplain, and low areas such as Hatzic Prairie sit behind the Dewdney Dyke system — some of this band's acreage product sits in those rural sub-areas, so flood status is property-specific and must be checked against City and FVRD floodplain mapping before buying; taxes also carry a diking levy. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Bylaws/Planning~and~Land~Use/Area%20F%20-%20OCP%20Bylaw%20999%20-%20Hatzic%20Valley.pdf and https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-rates

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The $1.8M floor is more than double the city's typical detached sale.** Mission's September 2026 detached median was $814,000 and its Condos & Townhomes median was $430,000 — so this band is the top tail of a modest-priced city, not a luxury market in the Metro Vancouver sense, and over-$1.8M pricing has to be built from the specific estate and its acreage comparables, never from city medians. Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report.
- **One sub-area in Mission's own market table is not in Mission at all.** The "Hemlock" row in the SnapStats Mission table is FVRD Electoral Area C resort land at Sasquatch Mountain Resort, roughly 55 km from Mission — it is grouped under Mission for board reporting only. Anyone reading Mission sub-area tables (13 detached and 9 attached Hemlock listings in September 2026) is partly reading a ski village's numbers. Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report.
- **Mission's 55+ supply stopped growing as strata decades ago.** The strata 55+ supply is still the two late-1980s complexes — Cherry Lane (1989) and Luther Place (1988) — while the city's newest seniors product (The Bellevue, downtown) is rental, and its mid-tier buy-in (Cedar Valley Manor) is life lease. At no point on that ladder does a $1.8M product appear. https://thebellevue.ca/ and https://www.missionseniors.ca/our-communities/cedar-valley-manor/
- **Two buildings eight civic numbers apart on 7th Avenue are opposite products.** Chartwell Cedarbrooke (32331 7th Ave) is a rental retirement residence; Cedarbrook Estates (32339 7th Ave) is a general-market family townhouse complex with no age restriction in any source. Search results blur the names — check the civic number, not the name. https://chartwell.com/bc/mission/cedarbrooke and https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/cedarbrook-estates-32339-7-ave
- **Mission's tax penalty is a single flat step, not the staged pattern in some neighbouring cities.** Taxes were due July 2, 2026 and a flat 10% penalty landed on outstanding current balances on July 3, 2026 — a one-day miss costs the full 10%, and diking levies ride on top of the municipal rate. On a top-band assessment that flat step is a large absolute dollar figure. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes and https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-rates
- **Even the official-adjacent sources disagree about Mission's small seniors stock.** Chartwell's site puts Carrington House at 32679 6th Ave while the Assisted Living Registry puts it at 32700 7th Ave; Cherry Lane's feeds split between Freehold Strata (NWS2971) and "Freehold NonStrata," and between 1989 and 1991 build years. The Form B, the registered bylaws and the operator — not any single directory — are the settling documents. Sources: the flagged entries in Section 2.
