# First-Time Buyer Calculator — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Scope note:** this is a province-wide topic file, not a neighbourhood file. It covers how buying a 55+ home in British Columbia intersects the first-time-buyer tools and incentives the calculator page covers — BC property transfer tax, the GST rebates, and the First Home Savings Account. **The practical point, stated up front: a 55+ buyer is usually NOT a first-time buyer.** The BC first-time program requires the buyer to have never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world; a buyer who has owned before does not meet that condition as published, however long ago the ownership was. The sections below state only what the official pages state, and mark everything else UNVERIFIED — including two federal figures (the FHSA lifetime limit and the Home Buyers' Plan limit) that the research for the calculator page could not re-read on the CRA's detailed pages and that are therefore NOT stated as figures anywhere in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**No named complexes are listed in this file.** This is a province-wide topic file: named examples belong in the city and sub-area files of this series, and none is carried here. Section 2 instead records the types of 55+ product a buyer encounters province-wide, with the tenure consequence of each — because for the calculator's programs, the form of the purchase matters as much as the price.

### Verified 55+ strata (ownership) — as a product type

- The standard 55+ ownership product in BC is a strata lot (condo or townhouse) in a corporation whose bylaws restrict residents to age 55 or older under ss. 123.1/123.2 of the Strata Property Act — the only age bylaw a BC strata may have since Nov 24, 2022. The purchase is a registered interest in property, which is the kind of interest the BC property transfer tax and its exemptions are written around. Named, verified examples are in the city files of this series (Surrey, Chilliwack, Port Coquitlam and others) and are not repeated here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

### Verified 55+ manufactured-home park (own the home, rent the pad — NOT strata, NOT freehold land) — as a product type

- The second product type is a manufactured home in an age-restricted park: the buyer owns the home and rents the pad under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, and a sale is an assignment of the pad tenancy requiring the park owner's consent. Because the buyer is not acquiring the land, a calculator built on registered-title transfer tax cannot simply be run on a park purchase price — whether and how BC property transfer tax applies to a specific manufactured-home purchase is a question for the buyer's legal professional on that transaction, and no official page read for this file states a park-specific calculator rule: UNCONFIRMED as a tool treatment. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo108/loo108/161_2000_pit

### Life-lease — NOT verified from a primary source in this pass

- Life-lease housing (a leasehold interest in a seniors project, bought for a lump sum rather than as a strata lot) is a form a BC buyer may encounter in the market, but no primary source verifying a specific BC life-lease 55+ project, its legal structure, or its treatment under the BC exemptions or the federal rebates was read for this file — so life-lease is recorded here as UNCONFIRMED, with no named project and no tax treatment stated. Do not put a life-lease claim on the calculator page from this file.

### Verified 55+ rental (not ownership)

- Not applicable to the calculator as a purchase tool: a rental involves no purchase, no transfer tax and no buyer's rebate. Stated in one line, per the topic framing — the rental, care and subsidized categories are covered in the city files of this series.

### Retirement residences and care (rental/care — NOT strata ownership)

- Not applicable as a purchase: retirement residences registered in BC's Assisted Living Registry are rental/care, not ownership, so none of the calculator's purchase programs applies to moving into one. The registry is the verification route for any named residence in the city files. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence

### Subsidized seniors rental (non-profit, income-based — not ownership)

- Not applicable as a purchase, for the same reason: subsidized seniors housing through BC Housing's Housing Registry is income-based rental, not a purchase the calculator prices. https://www.bchousing.org/housing-assistance/rental-housing/subsidized-housing

### Checked and NOT 55+ (do not put these on a 55+ page)

- Not applicable at province-wide topic level — the checked-and-not-55+ lists are address-specific and live in the city and sub-area files of this series. Stated in one line rather than padded with other cities' examples.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **BC property transfer tax — the base calculation the calculator starts from:** when a person purchases or gains an interest in property registered at the Land Title Office, the person or their legal professional must file a property transfer tax return and pay the tax unless an exemption applies. PTT is based on the fair market value of the property (land and improvements) on the day it is registered, at 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; if a property has residential property worth over $3,000,000, a further 2% applies to the residential value greater than $3,000,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **BC first time home buyers' program — the conditions that decide the 55+ question:** the buyer must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident; must have lived in BC for at least a year immediately before registration, or filed at least 2 income tax returns as a BC resident in the last 6 taxation years; must never have owned a registered interest in a property that was the buyer's principal residence anywhere in the world; and must never have received a first time home buyers' exemption or refund. Foreign entities and taxable trustees are not eligible, and where only some purchasers qualify, only the qualifying purchasers' percentage of interest is eligible. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **BC first-time program — the property conditions and amounts:** for a full exemption the property must only be used as the buyer's principal residence, have a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), be 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) or smaller, and contain only residential improvements; where all conditions are met the purchaser is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price. A partial exemption may apply where the fair market value is more than $835,000 but less than $860,000, the property is larger than 0.5 hectares, and/or another building sits on the property other than the principal residence. Verified on the live provincial page for this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Keeping the BC exemption:** the buyer must move into the home within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy it as a principal residence up to the first anniversary of the registration date (part may be kept if the buyer moves out early); the buyer's legal professional applies the exemption in the property transfer tax return, and a buyer who qualified but did not apply at registration may apply for a refund from the first anniversary to 18 months after registration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **BC newly built home exemption — the one purchase exemption a repeat buyer CAN reach:** it reduces or eliminates PTT on qualifying purchases of a principal residence regardless of first-time status as published; effective April 1, 2024, the fair market value threshold for a full exemption is $1,100,000, a partial exemption phases out over the $50,000 range above the threshold, with complete elimination at $1,150,000. A newly built home includes a house constructed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since construction, a unit in a newly built condo building not occupied since construction, a manufactured home placed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since placement, and a building converted from non-residential to residential use not occupied as a residence since the conversion began. Qualification also requires Canadian citizenship or permanent residency, the property to be in BC, used only as the buyer's principal residence, and 0.5 hectares or smaller, with the same move-in-within-92-days occupancy pattern. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Federal First Home Savings Account (FHSA):** the Canada Revenue Agency describes the FHSA as a registered plan that allows a first-time home buyer to save to buy or build a qualifying first home tax-free, up to certain limits; FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000, contributions are generally deductible and can be used to reduce tax, and transfers from RRSPs to FHSAs are not deductible. Verified on the live CRA page for this file. The FHSA lifetime contribution limit and maximum participation period could NOT be re-read on the CRA's detailed pages in the underlying research, so both are UNVERIFIED here and are not stated as figures. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Federal Home Buyers' Plan (HBP):** the CRA describes the HBP as allowing a withdrawal from an RRSP to buy or build a qualifying home, with the withdrawn amount to be repaid to the plan within the published repayment period, and states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the HBP for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. The current HBP withdrawal limit and repayment-period length could NOT be re-read on the CRA's HBP page in the underlying research, so both figures are UNVERIFIED here and are deliberately not stated from memory or from secondary sources. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **GST on new homes and the federal rebates:** the CRA describes the GST/HST new housing rebate as helping a buyer recover some of the GST, or the federal part of the HST, paid on a new or substantially renovated house where all requirements are met, with the individual or a related person using the housing as a primary place of residence and being the first individual to live in it. The newer first-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate provides eligible first-time home buyers with a full or partial rebate of the GST (or the federal part of the HST) on newly constructed or substantially renovated homes, up to $50,000; the application time limit is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction, a builder can credit the rebate against the amount payable when ownership transfers, and where the builder does not credit it the buyer can apply directly to the CRA through a CRA account or by filing Form GST190 (houses purchased from a builder) or Form GST191 (owner-built houses); builders may tell buyers they may be eligible if they entered a purchase agreement on or after March 20, 2025, and an owner-builder who started construction on or after March 20, 2025 can apply directly. Both items verified on the live CRA pages for this file. The value thresholds and phase-out ranges for both GST rebates could NOT be re-read on the CRA's detailed rebate pages in the underlying research, so no threshold figure for either rebate is stated here. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
- **Down payment and mortgage loan insurance (CMHC):** CMHC states mortgage loan insurance is required in Canada when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the purchase price, that the insurance protects the lender rather than the buyer, and that it allows a buyer to qualify with a smaller down payment, as low as 5%; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, and for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999 it is 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require a minimum down payment of 20% and mortgage loan insurance is not available; premium rates range from 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount, and the premium can be paid in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **BC home owner grant is not closing money:** the grant reduces property taxes paid each year on a principal residence; as published on the Province's page, effective January 1, 2027 the regular grant amount is $570, the grant threshold for 2026 is $2,075,000 for a full grant, the grant is reduced by $5 for each $1,000 of assessed value over $2,075,000, and the regular grant is $0 if the value is over $2,189,000; the claimant must be the registered owner, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, live in BC and occupy the residence as a principal residence, and must apply each year. A calculator should not net it against purchase taxes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I am 58 and buying my first home — a 55+ strata. Do I get the first-time exemption?** Age is not one of the program's conditions. On the conditions as published, what decides it is that you are a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, meet the BC residence test, have never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world, and have never received the exemption before — and that the property is $835,000 or less in fair market value, 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as your principal residence. A genuine first-time buyer at 58 who meets all of that is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: I owned a house 20 years ago, sold it, and have rented since. Am I a "first-time buyer" again for the BC program?** Not on the wording as published: the condition is that the buyer must never have owned a registered interest in a property that was the buyer's principal residence anywhere in the world at any time. Prior ownership, however long ago, is stated as disqualifying for the BC program — confirm your specific facts with your legal professional before budgeting any exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: My spouse has never owned, but I have. We are buying a 55+ unit together. Does any exemption apply?** Partly, on the BC program's own rule: if one or more purchasers do not qualify, only the percentage of interest that the qualifying first-time buyer(s) hold in the property is eligible — the Province's own example is a 60% qualifying interest receiving the exemption on that 60%. The property conditions ($835,000 or less, size, residential improvements, principal residence) still have to be met. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Does buying in a 55+ building change the property transfer tax itself?** No rule read for this file makes the tax or the exemptions turn on the age bylaw. The general tiers (1% / 2% / 3%, plus the further 2% on residential value over $3,000,000) and the exemption conditions turn on fair market value, property size, use as a principal residence, and the buyer's own history and status — the 55+ bylaw governs who may live in the unit, which is a residency question, not a tax rate. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I am downsizing into a NEW 55+ unit. Is there any exemption left for me?** Potentially the newly built home exemption, which is not a first-time program: full exemption threshold $1,100,000, phasing out to complete elimination at $1,150,000, for a qualifying principal-residence purchase by a Canadian citizen or permanent resident on 0.5 hectares or smaller — including a unit in a newly built condo building not occupied since construction. Whether any given new 55+ project qualifies is a property-by-property check against those conditions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: Can I open an FHSA at 55+ to save for this purchase?** The CRA describes the FHSA as a plan for a first-time home buyer saving to buy or build a qualifying first home tax-free up to certain limits, with $8,000 of participation room in the first year the account is opened and generally deductible contributions. The account's own eligibility conditions are on the CRA's FHSA pages and turn on first-time-buyer status as the CRA defines it — a buyer who has previously owned and lived in a home should not assume eligibility, and the lifetime limit is UNVERIFIED in this file because it could not be re-read on the CRA's detailed pages. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Q: Can the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan be combined for the same 55+ purchase?** The CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. The HBP withdrawal limit itself is UNVERIFIED in this file — it could not be re-read on the CRA's HBP page in the underlying research, and no figure is stated here from secondary sources. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Q: Does GST apply to a resale 55+ strata unit? What about a new one?** The federal rebates in this file are written around new housing: the GST/HST new housing rebate concerns GST paid on a new or substantially renovated house, and the first-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate (up to $50,000 for eligible first-time buyers) concerns newly constructed or substantially renovated homes. No source read for this file states a GST rebate for a resale purchase — a resale 55+ strata is not the rebate's subject, and a first-time buyer of a NEW 55+ unit is the buyer these rebates are written for. Confirm the GST treatment of a specific new unit with the builder and your legal professional. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
- **Q: I am buying a manufactured home in a 55+ park with the calculator's numbers. Do they apply?** Not directly, and this is the calculator's sharpest edge for 55+ buyers: a park purchase is the home plus an assignment of a pad tenancy under the MHPTA, not a purchase of the land, and no official page read for this file states how the live tool treats a park purchase — UNCONFIRMED as a tool treatment. The newly built home exemption's own definition does include a manufactured home placed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since placement, which is a different fact pattern from buying an existing home in an existing park. Get the tax treatment of the specific park transaction from your legal professional. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo108/loo108/161_2000_pit and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: What do I have to do AFTER closing to keep an exemption I claimed?** For the BC first-time program: move in within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy the home as your principal residence up to the first anniversary of the registration date, or part of the exemption may be repayable; if you qualified but did not apply at registration, the refund window runs from the first anniversary to 18 months after registration. The newly built home exemption carries the same move-in and first-year occupancy pattern. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **"First-time" means three different things in the three programs, and a 55+ buyer can pass one test and fail another.** The BC program's test is never having owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world, ever. The FHSA is described by the CRA as a plan for a first-time home buyer, with its own eligibility conditions on its own pages. The FTHB GST/HST rebate is for "eligible first-time home buyers" as the CRA defines them for that rebate. No source read for this file states that the three definitions are identical — check each program on its own page rather than carrying one answer across all three. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
- **The exemption thresholds were re-cut on April 1, 2024, and old advice is stale.** The BC first-time full-exemption value line is $835,000 (was $500,000 for a full exemption before April 1, 2024, on the Province's own note), with a partial band to under $860,000; the newly built threshold is $1,100,000 with elimination at $1,150,000. A 55+ buyer working from a relative's or a website's older figures will misprice the closing by the full exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **The one program with no first-time condition is the one that fits downsizers.** The newly built home exemption as published turns on the property being newly built, the buyer's citizenship/permanent residency, principal-residence use and the $1,100,000 / $1,150,000 thresholds — not on never having owned. For a 55+ buyer who has owned for decades, it is the only purchase exemption in this file that is even in play, and only on a new unit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Two federal figures are deliberately missing from this file, and that absence is the honest state of the research.** The FHSA lifetime contribution limit and the Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal limit could not be re-read on the CRA's detailed pages during the calculator research, so neither is stated here — secondary websites state figures for both, but this series does not publish a tax figure it has not read on the official page. Any version of the calculator page that displays those limits should be checked against the CRA pages directly before it is relied on. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **The 0.5-hectare line is where acreage-seeking 55+ buyers lose the full exemption.** Both BC programs set 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) as the size line for a full exemption, with a partial exemption possible above it under the first-time program's stated conditions — a large-lot or farm-edge first purchase prices differently in the tool than a strata purchase of the same value, which is not obvious from the headline $835,000 figure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **The home owner grant is an annual item, not a closing credit — and it has its own threshold story.** The regular grant is $570 effective January 1, 2027 as published, with the 2026 full-grant threshold at $2,075,000 of assessed value and the grant reaching $0 over $2,189,000 — a first-time buyer who nets the grant against purchase taxes in a spreadsheet has double-counted money that arrives, if at all, on the annual tax bill. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
