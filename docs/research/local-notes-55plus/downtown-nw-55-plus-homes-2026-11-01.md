# Downtown (New Westminster) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Downtown is New Westminster's historic Columbia Street core and Fraser River waterfront, between the river and the Royal Avenue slope, planned through the Downtown Community Plan (Schedule C of the OCP). Precise Downtown Community Plan boundary roads: UNVERIFIED. Sources: the area research file (sources read 2026-11-01).
- "Downtown" is the GVR board sub-area name in the SnapStats GVR report for New Westminster. NAMING CONFLICT: the City's OCP residential-neighbourhoods map names one area "Downtown (including Quayside)", while the board report lists Downtown and Quay as two separate sub-areas — these notes cover the board's Downtown only, not the Quay waterfront pocket. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/OUR_CITY_2041_Official_Community_Plan_New_Westminsters_Residential_Neighbourhoods_Map.pdf
- Main streets: Columbia Street (the historic retail main street), Carnarvon Street, Royal Avenue, Agnes Street, 8th Street and McBride Boulevard; Stewardson Way / Marine Way links west toward Burnaby and to the Queensborough Bridge (Highway 91A). https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf
- Transit: New Westminster Station and Columbia Station, both on the Expo Line, are in Downtown; TransLink routes listed for New Westminster Station include 102 (Victoria Hill), 103 (Quayside), 105 (Uptown), 106 (Edmonds Station), 109 (Lougheed Station), 112 (Edmonds Station) and 123 (Brentwood Station). https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/schedules-by-region/2024-schedules/september/burnaby-new-west-p155.pdf
- Civic anchors: the Anvil Centre, 777 Columbia Street, is Downtown's civic and cultural centre (with a library kiosk and holds locker); Westminster Pier Park, 600 Quayside Drive, and the Waterfront Esplanade boardwalk run along the waterfront at the foot of Columbia Street. https://www.newwestcity.ca/facilities and http://www.newwestcity.ca/parks-and-recreation/parks/community-parks and https://www.nwpl.ca/
- Schools: School District 40 (New Westminster) runs a three-tier system (elementary K–5, middle 6–8, secondary 9–12) with address-based catchments. École Qayqayt Elementary, 85 Merivale Street, is the elementary school physically in Downtown; the SD40 locator result recorded for that address is Qayqayt Elementary, Fraser River Middle and New Westminster Secondary. https://newwestschools.ca/our-schools/ and https://newwestschools.ca/our-schools/registration/
- Where the seniors housing actually is: not in Downtown. New Westminster's seniors buildings cluster OUTSIDE this sub-area — Uptown and Moody Park (Legion Manor, Lions Moody Park Tower, Thornebridge Gardens), Brow of the Hill and the West End (Dunwood Place, Ross Towers, Rotary Tower), Victoria Hill (Victoria Heights) and Sapperton (Cedar Manor, AgeCare Royal City). Inside Downtown itself, no 55+ strata, seniors rental, retirement residence, care home or subsidized seniors building was verified (Section 2).

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Zoning: Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001** carries Downtown's Columbia Street historic and high-rise commercial districts; minimum lot sizes are zone-specific in the bylaw text, and a Downtown-specific minimum is UNVERIFIED. Townhouse form elsewhere in the city is regulated under Townhouse Residential Districts RT-A and RT-B where designated. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Zoning_Bylaw_No_6680_2001_as_of_June_30_2022_(Complete).pdf
- **Plans:** the Downtown Community Plan (Schedule C of the OCP) governs Downtown under Our City 2041, the Official Community Plan (Bylaw No. 7925, 2017); Downtown is designated a Regional City Centre, and the OCP is under a 2025–2026 update process (OCP Amendment Bylaw No. 8574, 2026). https://www.newwestcity.ca/ocp/past-event-materials-and-council-reports
- **Adaptable housing rule (the City's actual seniors-housing lever):** since a Zoning Bylaw amendment adopted June 28, 2011 (Bylaw No. 7464, 2011), a minimum of 40% of all single-storey dwelling units in multiple-unit residential buildings city-wide must be built as adaptable dwelling units under the BC Building Code — the City states the policy is a proactive means of planning for its aging population. Adaptable is not age-restricted: any buyer can purchase these units. http://newwestcity.ca/housing/adaptable-housing
- **Transit-Oriented Areas:** provincial rules introduced in 2024 enable density within 800 metres of the city's five SkyTrain stations — 22nd Street, New Westminster, Columbia, Sapperton and Braid — two of which (New Westminster and Columbia) are in Downtown. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/2026_02_17_APC___Regional_Planning_and_Transit_Oriented_Development_Area_Extensions___2026_78.pdf
- **Flood:** the City's floodplain study places the Downtown waterfront in Floodplain Zone 3 — the Fraser River waterfront zone — where many newer buildings have floor space above the Flood Construction Level, but egress and the waterfront path sit below it. Confirm the specific building's compliance and insurance position lot by lot. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf and http://www.newwestcity.ca/services/public-safety/flood-management
- **Property taxes and utilities:** City of New Westminster 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on current taxes remaining unpaid on July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on any unpaid balance on September 3, 2026. New Westminster operates its own electrical utility alongside City water / sewer services. https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Downtown New Westminster?** None that any source verifies. No Downtown strata carries a verified 55+ bylaw — the towers on Carnarvon, Agnes and Royal are general-market buildings marketed to families, students and investors. A 55+ buyer downtown is buying a regular condo with no age bylaw; the city's verified 55+ and seniors stock is in other sub-areas (Section 2). Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink my resale buyer pool?** Mechanically, yes, in a true 55+ building: the next buyer must also be able to live there under the bylaw — 55+, an exempt spouse/partner, caregiver or legacy case — or buy without living in the unit. Downtown's general-market buildings carry no such limit, which is part of their liquidity: 102 attached listings and 15 solds in September 2026. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: What does "adaptable" mean in the newer downtown buildings?** Under the City's 2011 zoning amendment, at least 40% of single-storey units in multiple-unit residential buildings must be built as adaptable dwelling units to BC Building Code standards — wider clearances and features that can be adjusted for limited mobility due to age, disability or illness. It is a construction standard, not an age restriction. http://newwestcity.ca/housing/adaptable-housing
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Royal Columbian Hospital, 330 East Columbia Street in Sapperton (Fraser Health) — east of Downtown, at Sapperton Station on the Expo Line. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/New-Westminster/royal-columbian-hospital
- **Q: What transit can I actually use day to day?** Two Expo Line stations sit in Downtown — New Westminster and Columbia — plus the station bus loop (routes 102, 103, 105, 106, 109, 112, 123). This is the most transit-served core in the city; no car is required for daily life in the way it is in the house neighbourhoods. https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/schedules-by-region/2024-schedules/september/burnaby-new-west-p155.pdf
- **Q: Is Downtown in the Fraser River floodplain?** The waterfront is — Floodplain Zone 3. Many newer buildings have floor space above the Flood Construction Level while egress and the waterfront path sit below it, so the building's flood construction level compliance, strata insurance and deductible position all need confirming before subject removal. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf
- **Q: If I need help later, can I stay downtown?** Not at the assisted-living or subsidized steps — both require leaving the sub-area: Thornebridge Gardens (649 8th Ave, independent + assisted living) is up the hill, the subsidized towers (Dunwood Place, Legion Manor, Ross Towers) are in Brow of the Hill, Uptown and the West End, and long-term care is in Sapperton and the West End. Independent condo living downtown, with services brought in, is the only in-sub-area step. https://www.verveseniorliving.com/thornebridge-gardens/ and https://www.readkong.com/page/affordable-housing-bc-housing-9785076

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Downtown and the Quay are one neighbourhood to the City and two to the board.** The OCP map says "Downtown (including Quayside)"; SnapStats splits them, and they behaved differently in September 2026 — the Quay sold 16 of 59 listings (27%) against Downtown's 15 of 102 (15%). A Carnarvon / Columbia seller should not be quoted Quayside momentum, and a Quayside buyer should not be quoted Downtown supply. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/OUR_CITY_2041_Official_Community_Plan_New_Westminsters_Residential_Neighbourhoods_Map.pdf
- **The seniors infrastructure is up the hill, a pattern set decades ago.** The City's non-market inventory classes exactly three buildings "seniors specific" at scale — Dunwood Place (193 units, 901 Colborne St), Legion Manor (123 units, 505 6th Ave) and Ross Towers (100 units, 45 Clute St) — and all three sit west or uphill of Downtown, which is why no amount of downtown tower supply has produced a downtown seniors building. https://newwestcity.ca/database/rte/Housing%20web%20update%20-%20Non-Market%20details.pdf
- **Two of BC Housing's directory names collide with downtown buildings.** The subsidized "Hillside Place" is at 1025 Queens Ave (Uptown); the market condo also called Hillside Place is at 509 Carnarvon St (Downtown). Confusing them puts a subsidized Uptown tower on a downtown page. https://www.readkong.com/page/affordable-housing-bc-housing-9785076 and https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing/r3102163-509-carnarvon-street-105-new-westminster-bc-v3l-5s4/brochure
- **The city's biggest recreation build is a short climb away.** The təməsew̓txʷ Aquatic and Community Centre, 65 East Sixth Avenue — fully opened May 2024, at 10,644 sq. m. the City's largest capital project to date, with an eight-lane 50-metre pool, leisure pool, fitness centre and gymnasia — is up the hill in Uptown, and the Main Branch of the public library is at 716 6th Avenue; Downtown itself has the Anvil Centre and a library kiosk. https://www.newwestcity.ca/facilities and https://www.nwpl.ca/
- **New Westminster runs its own electrical utility** — unlike the BC Hydro service in neighbouring cities, a downtown strata's electrical costs run through the City's own utility. https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php
- **New West taxes have two penalty dates.** 2026 taxes were due July 2, 2026; a 5% penalty applies to balances unpaid on July 3, 2026 and a second 5% on balances still unpaid on September 3, 2026. https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php
