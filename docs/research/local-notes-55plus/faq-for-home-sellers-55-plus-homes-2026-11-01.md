## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT THIS HOME TYPE

- **The buyer pool for a 55+ home is set by provincial law.** Only 55+ age bylaws are valid in BC strata (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2, Regulation 7.01, since Nov 24, 2022); the bylaw restricts who may reside, not who may own, and required exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and — expanded May 1, 2023 — younger spouses/partners, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children cared for before 19. A seller's marketing must describe that pool accurately. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide** since Nov 24, 2022 — a seller cannot market a 55+ unit as "no rentals allowed" for long-term rentals, though short-term rental bylaws remain allowed and any tenant must still meet the age bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Selling without a licence, verified at the regulator:** a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves — for example, by selling or renting out their own property — while a person must not provide real estate services to or on behalf of another, for or in expectation of remuneration, unless licensed or exempt. That supports the FAQ page's "not legally required to use a REALTOR®" answer. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information
- **The regulator's consumer framework (BCFSA):** a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; the representation, duties and BCFSA complaint-route disclosures must happen at the outset of dealings. The FAQ page states no commission rate or amount — consistent with that framework, which places commission information in the listing conversation. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Cooling-off / rescission (BCFSA):** where a buyer rescinds an accepted offer under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining deposit balance to the buyer, with no separate release form. The live FAQ page does not itself raise the rescission period; the sibling selling page does. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **Deposits generally (BCFSA):** a buyer's deposit is held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder, and is not automatically returned if subjects are not removed — both parties must sign a release, except where the buyer exercises the right of rescission. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **Property transfer tax is the buyer's tax:** PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office. The FAQ page's repeated statement — "property transfer tax is paid by the buyer, not the seller" — matches that frame; it becomes the seller's tax when they buy their next home. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Counters, as the page states them:** an offer can be accepted as-is, countered or rejected, and a counter voids the previous offer and is itself a new offer the other side can accept, counter or walk away from. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Selling a park home is an assignment (MHPTA s.28):** the site tenancy transfers to the buyer with the park owner's prior written consent (or an order of the director, or authorization in the tenancy agreement); the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting; and the pad rent and the date of the next increase carry over unchanged to the buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Why isn't my 55+ home selling?** The FAQ page's answer, in its three reasons: it is overpriced relative to comparable recent sales and current competition, it is not marketed well enough online and offline, or it does not show well — and for a 55+ home there is a fourth, structural factor the page does not name: the resident pool is limited by the age bylaw itself (Section 3), so the comparable set and the pricing have to be drawn from 55+ stock, not from the unrestricted building next door. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Do I need a REALTOR® to sell my 55+ home in BC?** No — BCFSA's licensing guidance confirms a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves, such as selling their own property; a licence (or an exemption) is required to provide real estate services to or on behalf of another person for remuneration. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information
- **Q: What are the costs of selling a 55+ home in BC?** The page lists real estate commission (split between the listing agent and the buyer's agent), cleaning, staging and preparation, legal fees and disbursements, and a possible mortgage penalty if the mortgage is broken early — and states no commission figure, which BCFSA's framework places in the listing conversation, where a licensee must give consumers information about commissions and fees. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Do I have to accept an offer on my home?** The page's answer: no — on receiving an offer you can accept it as-is, counter, or reject it altogether; a counter voids the previous offer. The page's further claim — that a seller "may be required to accept" a cash offer at list price matching the listed terms — is UNVERIFIED as a legal statement in the research file; read your own listing contract's stated terms and take that question to your agent or lawyer before relying on any general statement. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html and ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/faq-for-home-sellers.md
- **Q: Can a buyer back out after I accept their offer?** For most homes, yes, inside the Home Buyer Rescission Period: the buyer may rescind within three business days after acceptance, and the brokerage then pays you the 0.25% rescission fee directly from the deposit and returns the balance to the buyer, with no release form required. Otherwise a deposit is held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder, and is not automatically returned if subjects are not removed. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **Q: Who pays the property transfer tax when I sell?** The buyer: PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office — when you buy your next home, you pay it as the buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Should I price high and lower later?** The page's position: no — pricing high and reducing later fails because the first two weeks on market are critical and a reduced listing reads as stale; price from sold evidence and price it right at launch. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: Will renovations increase my 55+ home's value?** The page's position is that renovations rarely return 100% of their cost, so the small jobs come first. Its specific return ranges, attributed on the page to experience and the Appraisal Institute of Canada, are UNVERIFIED — no Appraisal Institute of Canada source was opened in the research pass — so do not quote the percentages as sourced figures. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: How is selling a park home or a co-op share different from selling my strata unit?** A park home sale is an assignment of the site tenancy with the park owner's prior written consent, at no consent fee, with the pad rent unchanged for the buyer — there is no land to convey. A co-op or membership sale (St. George's Village, Sullivan Mews, Pioneer Park) is a share/membership transfer under the community's own documents. Neither is a strata Form B sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf and ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/selling-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **Q: When is the best time to sell a 55+ home?** The page's answer is that timing depends on home type, local and macro conditions, time of year and personal goals, with a REALTOR® tour and a sold/expired/active comparison as the recommended route. Its specific seasonality pattern (slowest November–January, spring pick-up, softer August, September rebound) is UNVERIFIED — no board or SnapStats source for that pattern was opened in the research pass. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The FAQ page repeats itself, by design or by accident.** Its structured eight-question FAQ block substantially repeats the five themed sections above it on the same page (pricing, timing, costs, renovations, acceptance) — recorded in the research file as a rebuild consideration, so a seller reading the whole page reads most answers twice. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **A seller in a one-building city prices differently from a seller in Abbotsford.** Port Coquitlam has exactly one verified 55+ strata building, while an Abbotsford seller competes inside a 26-complex market — scarcity of the type itself is part of the listing story in the thin markets and irrelevant in the deep ones. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Surrey's count cannot be quoted clean.** Two complexes verified in later passes (Camberley Green, The Greens at Douglas) sit outside the consolidated roster of 15, and a third (Sea Ridge Bays) fits neither the strata-townhouse nor the pad-park box — a Surrey 55+ seller's comparable search built on a bare "15" or a bare "18" is ahead of the evidence. The honest form is "15 + flagged additions pending re-consolidation." Source: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/surrey-homes-for-sale-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **The service-plan building hides its real monthly cost in plain sight.** At Mayfair Terrace, listing information states a mandatory base service plan on top of strata fees — a seller there who quotes only the strata fee will lose the buyer's trust at the documents stage; the service agreement is part of the price conversation from day one. Source: ~/workspace/local-notes/port-coquitlam-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **Similar names poison comparable searches on both sides of Boundary Drive.** Lakewood Heights, Lakewood Place and Lakeview Estates are three separate Surrey stratas on one drive; Glenwood Village (55+) and Glenwood Village Heights (no age restriction) are neighbours in Newton. A 55+ seller's "comparables" pulled by name alone will quietly include the wrong buildings. Source: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/surrey-homes-for-sale-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **The cooling-off fee needs no one's signature.** If a buyer rescinds in the three-business-day window, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% fee directly from the deposit and returns the balance — no release form is signed by either party, unlike a failed subject removal, where the deposit is not automatically returned. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **The page's board name is a generation old.** Its listing-contract answer names the "Real Estate Board of Greater Vancouver" — the board's current name, as used in this site's own footer notices, is the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® (GVR). The claimed 60-day minimum in the same answer is UNVERIFIED. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **A park home's buyer inherits the pad rent exactly.** On assignment the pad rent and the next increase date carry over unchanged and the park cannot charge a consent fee — so a park seller cannot offer a "new-tenant" rent deal, and a below-market pad rent is a genuine, documentable selling feature. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
