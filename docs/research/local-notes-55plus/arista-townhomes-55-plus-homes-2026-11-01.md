## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. Arista has no age bylaw in any source checked — this rule matters here only because a buyer searching "Arista 55+" needs to know the complex was never verified as carrying one. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. This applies at Arista as at every BC strata: any older rental wording in its bylaws no longer restricts long-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Plans and zoning:** the current Official Community Plan is the Surrey 2050 OCP, Bylaw No. 21900 (finally adopted June 15, 2026) — never cite the replaced Bylaw No. 18020 as current. Surrey's zoning bylaw is Zoning By-law No. 12000. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/r133-plansurrey-2013-official-community-plan-surrey-2050-official-community-plan-alignment-related-amendments and https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/12000
- **Secondary suites in Surrey:** the City defines a secondary suite as an accessory dwelling unit whose habitable floor area does not exceed 40% of the principal dwelling unit, within a single family, semi-detached or duplex building, and not a separate strata lot; only one suite is permitted per dwelling unit, and a suite is not permitted within a coach house or garden suite. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- **Suites in townhouses:** Surrey Council approved bylaw changes to permit secondary (lock-off) suites in townhouses in the RM-15, RM-23 and RM-30 zones for new developments in transit-oriented areas and frequent bus stop areas, with a suite minimum of 30 sq. m and less than 40% of the habitable floor area of the larger principal unit. That permission is for new developments in specified zones — not a blanket right in a 2011-era complex like Arista. https://www.Surrey.ca/news-events/news/surrey-creating-more-housing-options-secondary-suites-townhouses
- **Small-scale multi-unit housing in Surrey:** the City adopted its SSMUH zoning changes on July 8, 2024 — a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop. SSMUH addresses single-residential lots; it does not convert a strata townhome complex into anything else. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **Property taxes (city-wide):** City of Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes

- **Q: Can I rent out an Arista townhome?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. A strata can still restrict short-term rentals, so check Arista's current bylaws for any short-term rule before counting on that use. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Does any age rule affect us at Arista?** No — because Arista carries no verified age bylaw, the provincial 55+ exemptions are not engaged here at all. They would matter only if you bought into a verified 55+ strata elsewhere in Surrey, where a younger spouse or partner of a qualifying resident is expressly exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I add a lock-off suite or caregiver suite inside an Arista townhome?** Not as a blanket right. Surrey's townhouse-suite permissions are for new developments in the RM-15, RM-23 and RM-30 zones in transit-oriented and frequent bus stop areas, and the City's secondary-suite definition excludes a separate strata lot. Whether any in-unit alteration is permitted is a strata-bylaw and City-permit question for the specific unit: UNVERIFIED for Arista in any source checked. https://www.Surrey.ca/news-events/news/surrey-creating-more-housing-options-secondary-suites-townhouses and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- **Q: What should I review before buying at Arista?** The strata package the live page itself directs buyers to: the Form B information certificate, bylaws, depreciation report and minutes, and what the monthly fees include — plus the pet bylaws for the specific unit, since the page states most Arista bylaws permit pets with size and quantity restrictions. https://jaredgibbons.ca/arista-townhomes.html
