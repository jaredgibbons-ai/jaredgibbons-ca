## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. This applies to a residential strata lot wherever it is bought — it does not turn a commercial listing into a 55+ property, and a commercial strata's own bylaws are a separate, property-specific check: UNVERIFIED for any property in this feed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **The BC regulator's consumer framework applies to commercial trading services as it does to residential:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed real estate professional in BC must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings:** whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA; dual agency — representing two or more clients in the same transaction whose interests conflict — was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **At the national level,** the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA), a trade association, owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service® ("MLS®") and REALTOR®, and agents and brokers must become members of a local real estate board and CREA in order to access the MLS® system and must adhere to CREA's rules and their boards' rules — the framework behind the C-prefixed commercial MLS® numbers on this feed. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Check the permitted use, not just the building:** the page's editorial text itself names zoning as essential to commercial investing; whether a specific business use — including any seniors-housing, care or residential use — is permitted at a specific address is a municipal zoning question for that property, decided by that municipality's zoning bylaw and OCP, and no zoning conclusion for any listing in the feed is stated in this file: UNVERIFIED. https://jaredgibbons.ca/commercial.html
- **Treat the feed as a finding aid, not the record:** the page's own notice says the representation is based on board-generated data the boards do not warrant for accuracy, and the materials may not be reproduced without board consent — the authoritative record for a specific property is the Paragon/MLS® listing and the municipality's zoning confirmation for the intended use. https://jaredgibbons.ca/commercial.html
- **First-home programs do not apply to this feed as stated here:** the First Home Savings Account and Home Buyers' Plan dollar limits are UNVERIFIED in this file — no live CRA page stating the current limits was read in this pass, so no figure is published here — and in any case those are home-buying programs for a principal residence, not commercial premises programs. (No CRA source is cited because none was read; that is the point of the UNVERIFIED label.)
