## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** legacy residents lawfully living in the unit when the bylaw passed; live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide** since Nov 24, 2022; stratas may still restrict SHORT-TERM rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Does a 55+ bylaw affect lender or insurer appetite? UNCONFIRMED.** No official or primary source read in this program states that a strata's age-restriction bylaw changes a lender's or a mortgage insurer's eligibility, pricing or approval decision. Do not put any such claim in calculator copy; a specific lender's policy on a specific building is a broker question, not a published rule in these notes.
- **Strata fees enter qualification through the debt-service ratios — this part is official.** FCAC: Gross Debt Service (GDS) — total monthly housing costs should not be more than 39% of gross household income, where monthly housing costs are the mortgage payments, property taxes, heating costs and 50% of condo fees (if applicable). Total Debt Service (TDS) — total debt load should not be more than 44% of gross income, being total monthly housing costs plus all other debts. FCAC frames 39% and 44% as guidelines, not hard approval cut-offs. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **A second mandatory service charge is not a "condo fee" in FCAC's wording — how a lender treats it is UNCONFIRMED.** FCAC's GDS composition counts 50% of condo fees; whether a building's mandatory service plan (for example Mayfair Terrace's, Section 2) is counted by a given lender, and at what fraction, is not stated on any official page read here. Flag it to the broker rather than assuming the calculator captures it. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **The stress test (OSFI minimum qualifying rate):** the greater of the mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25%, applied by federally regulated lenders to insured and uninsured mortgages alike; OSFI reviews the 2% buffer and the 5.25% floor at least annually. OSFI does not expect the test on an uninsured straight switch at renewal with no increase to the loan amount or amortization; a refinance or a new HELOC triggers it again. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Minimum down payment and CMHC insurance:** 5% on homes of $500,000 or less; on homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000; at $1,500,000 or more a minimum of 20% and insurance is not available. Insurance is required under 20% down; CMHC publishes premium rates of 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount, payable up front or added to the mortgage. (The live calculator page states a different range — 2.80% to 4.00% plus 0.20% for 30-year insured amortizations — which does not match CMHC's published range and was not confirmed on an official page: UNVERIFIED.) https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **30-year insured amortization:** available to all first-time homebuyers and to all buyers of new builds since December 15, 2024, per the Department of Finance Canada. The general insured maximum for other insured borrowers, and Finance's detailed first-time-buyer criteria for this measure, were not stated on a regulator page re-read in the research pass: UNVERIFIED here. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Canadian compounding:** under the federal Interest Act, s. 6, a blended-payment mortgage must state the rate "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" — the statutory basis for fixed rates being compounded semi-annually, which is why a Canadian payment calculation does not simply divide the nominal rate by 12. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html
- **BC property transfer tax (general rates):** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% on the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000; 3% above $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on residential property value over $3,000,000. PTT is based on fair market value on the day of registration and is separate from annual property taxes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **PTT first-time-buyer exemption (BC Gov):** where all conditions are met, exemption on the first $500,000 of the purchase price; full exemption requires fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), a property of 0.5 hectares or smaller containing only residential improvements and used only as the buyer's principal residence; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **PTT newly built home exemption (BC Gov):** effective April 1, 2024, full exemption threshold is a fair market value of $1,100,000, with a partial exemption phasing out over the $50,000 above it and complete elimination at $1,150,000 for qualifying purchasers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Reverse mortgage / CHIP (lender-stated — HomeEquity Bank's own pages):** HomeEquity Bank states the CHIP Reverse Mortgage is for Canadian seniors age 55 and older; the borrower (and spouse) must be 55 or older, the home must be the primary residence, any existing mortgage or lien balance must be paid off from the proceeds (or other funds), and the home must be of a type and in a location the bank typically lends on. The bank states homeowners can access up to 55% of the home's value, with no health check or minimum income required, and no required payments while living in the home provided the property is maintained, insured and property taxes are kept up to date. These are the lender's own published terms, not a government rule — confirm current terms with the lender. https://www.chip.ca/reverse-mortgage-resources/reverse-mortgage/reverse-mortgage-for-canadian-seniors/
- **FHSA and Home Buyers' Plan amounts: UNVERIFIED in these notes.** No dollar limit for either program is stated here, because neither was read off a live CRA page in this pass. Do not copy a limit from calculator marketing copy.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What rate does the stress test use on my 55+ purchase?** The greater of your mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25% — OSFI's minimum qualifying rate, applied by federally regulated lenders to insured and uninsured mortgages alike. The 55+ bylaw itself does not change the test on any official page read here. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: How do strata fees affect how much I can qualify for?** Directly, through FCAC's ratios: housing costs for GDS are the mortgage payment, property taxes, heating and 50% of condo/strata fees, against a 39%-of-gross-income guideline; TDS adds all other debts against 44%. The ratios are calculated at the qualifying (stress-test) rate, not your contract rate — so a high strata fee shrinks the mortgage the ratios support even before the rate does. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: My building charges a mandatory service plan on top of strata fees. Does the calculator capture it?** Not on FCAC's published composition: GDS counts 50% of condo fees, and no official page read here states how a lender treats a separate mandatory service plan (for example Mayfair Terrace's listing-stated $715/mo first-resident plan). Whether a given lender counts it is UNCONFIRMED — disclose it to the broker and do not treat a calculator pass as an approval. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html and ~/workspace/local-notes/port-coquitlam-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **Q: Will a lender or insurer refuse a 55+ age-restricted building?** UNCONFIRMED — no official or primary source read in this program states that a 55+ bylaw affects lender or insurer appetite, eligibility or pricing. Any answer is a specific lender's underwriting policy on a specific building; ask the broker to confirm with the lender before removing subjects, and do not rely on a general claim in either direction.
- **Q: What is the minimum down payment, and when do I pay CMHC insurance?** 5% to $500,000; 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion above it up to $1,499,999; 20% minimum at $1,500,000 or more, where insurance is unavailable. Insurance is required under 20% down, at CMHC-published premium rates of 0.6% to 4.5% of the mortgage amount, paid up front or added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: Can I get a 30-year amortization?** On an insured mortgage, 30-year amortizations have been available to all first-time homebuyers and to all buyers of new builds since December 15, 2024. On an uninsured mortgage (20% or more down), 30 years is a lender offering rather than an insured-program rule — confirmed with the lender, not the calculator. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Q: I'm 55+ and own my home. How does a reverse mortgage work instead of a regular mortgage?** On HomeEquity Bank's own published terms (lender-stated): CHIP is for homeowners 55 and older on a primary residence, accesses up to 55% of the home's value, requires no health check or minimum income, requires any existing mortgage or lien to be paid off from the proceeds, and requires no payments while you live in the home if you maintain the property, keep insurance in place and keep property taxes up to date. It is a loan against the home you already own — it does not finance a new purchase in the way a purchase mortgage does, on the terms the bank publishes. https://www.chip.ca/reverse-mortgage-resources/reverse-mortgage/reverse-mortgage-for-canadian-seniors/
- **Q: How much property transfer tax do I add on top of the mortgage math?** On the general BC tiers: 1% to $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above that, on fair market value — cash needed at completion on top of the down payment, before any exemption. A qualifying first-time buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 (full exemption to $835,000 fair market value); a qualifying newly built purchase has a full exemption to $1,100,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: At renewal, do I face the stress test again?** Not on a qualifying straight switch: OSFI does not expect lenders to apply the minimum qualifying rate to an uninsured straight switch to another federally regulated lender with no increase to the loan amount or amortization. A refinance, or taking out a home equity line of credit, triggers the test again. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: Does the calculator approve me?** No. A calculator computes payments and guideline ratios; OSFI's framework leaves the underwriting decision with the federally regulated lender applying Guideline B-20, and the insurer sets its own eligibility on insured loans. The output is an estimate to take to a lender or broker. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The live calculator page's insurance premium range does not match CMHC's.** The page states premiums of 2.80% to 4.00% (plus 0.20% for 30-year insured amortizations); CMHC's own page publishes 0.6% to 4.5%, re-read for the research file, and the 0.20% surcharge was not confirmed on a CMHC or Finance page. Use the CMHC range in any calculation you rely on, and treat the page's range as UNVERIFIED until it is corrected or sourced. https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **In the older Abbotsford 55+ buildings, the strata fee often includes utilities** — electricity, heat and water at Elmwood Manor; heat and hot water at Fernwood Manor and Alameda Court; gas, heat and hot water at Lakewood Gardens. FCAC's GDS counts heating costs separately from the 50%-of-fees line, so entering both the full fee and a full heating estimate for these buildings double-counts heat. Sources: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/abbotsford-west-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md and ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/central-abbotsford-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md; https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Canadian fixed rates are not divided by 12.** The Interest Act's "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" wording is why a correct Canadian calculator converts a fixed nominal rate using semi-annual compounding — a tool that skips this overstates or understates the payment. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html
- **The two $ figures a 55+ buyer most often conflates are both "55".** The strata bylaw age (55+, Strata Property Act) and CHIP's maximum share of home value (up to 55%, HomeEquity Bank's published term) are unrelated numbers from unrelated sources — one is provincial strata law, the other is one lender's product limit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www.chip.ca/reverse-mortgage-resources/reverse-mortgage/reverse-mortgage-for-canadian-seniors/
- **Provincially regulated lenders sit outside OSFI's test as a rule.** FCAC notes lenders that are not federally regulated may also ask a borrower to pass a stress test — whether any given BC credit union applies it is lender-specific and UNVERIFIED here. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
