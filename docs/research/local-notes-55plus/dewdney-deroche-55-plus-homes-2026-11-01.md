## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. No Dewdney-Deroche strata was found for this to attach to — and note it does NOT govern manufactured-home parks: a park's age rule is a park rule under tenancy law, not a strata bylaw, which is why Oasis's adult side cannot be settled from strata law. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** People under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; exemptions attach to a "specified resident." https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** legacy residents; live-in caregivers; and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. These are strata exemptions — they do not automatically apply inside a manufactured-home park. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide** since Nov 24, 2022 (strata rule); SHORT-TERM rental bylaws are still allowed in stratas. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Manufactured-home parks in unincorporated areas run on the BC Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, province-wide.** Pad tenancies at Oasis Country Estates and Nicomen Park/Village fall under that Act regardless of FVRD jurisdiction: the resident owns the home and rents the pad; pad rent may be raised only once in 12 months on at least three full months' written notice, capped at the provincial limit (2.3% for 2026) plus a proportional share of changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees. Park rules, approval on resale/assignment, and any age policy are park documents — read them before writing. http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Land use runs through the FVRD, not a city hall:** FVRD official community plan and zoning bylaws govern Electoral Area G (with the Area C plan area beginning east of Deroche, including the eastern Deroche Bench). The specific bylaw numbers and zone names for a given lot: UNVERIFIED in this research — confirm with the FVRD for the PID. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/areacocp?sort_by=recent&tool=forum_topic
- **ALR:** farmland parcels in the corridor that sit in the Agricultural Land Reserve follow the ALC housing frame of one principal residence at a maximum of 500 m² plus at most one additional residence of 90 m² (parcels ≤40 ha) or 186 m² (parcels >40 ha). Whether a specific parcel is in the ALR: UNVERIFIED at parcel level — check the ALC maps for the PID. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Servicing overlays:** FVRD community water systems exist for Deroche and for Dewdney in Area G, but not every lot is inside a service area — confirm water source, sewer vs septic and any system fees for the specific parcel; wells are in the provincial GWELLS database and onsite sewerage must meet the Sewerage System Regulation (including the 30 m setback from a well unless professional advice is obtained). https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Bylaws/Sewer~and~Water/CONSOLIDATED%20Water%20Systems%20Regulations%20and%20Fees.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo104/loo104/22_326_2004
- **Property taxes are rural-area taxes, paid to the Province:** a property not in a city, town, district or village is in a rural area; its owner receives the tax notice from the Province's Surveyor of Taxes Office and pays the Province, not the FVRD and not the City of Mission. Under the Taxation (Rural Area) Act, s.11, rural taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty on any portion unpaid on July 2 and a further 5% on any portion unpaid on October 31. (This is the rural regime — do NOT apply Mission's flat 10% city penalty here.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/rural-area and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The mailing address lies about who governs you.** Every listing here carries a Mission mailing address, yet permits, zoning, the OCP and servicing are FVRD Electoral Area G (Area C on the eastern Deroche Bench), and taxes go to the Province's Surveyor of Taxes. A buyer who budgets Mission's flat 10% tax penalty has the wrong regime — here it is 5% after July 2 and a further 5% after October 31. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Document~Library/EA%20Housing%20Needs%20Report.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01
- **One park, two names.** The 43201 Lougheed Highway park is "Nicomen Park" in one current listing and "Nicomen Village" in another — search both names, and do not assume either listing's silence on age means an age rule exists or does not; only the park rules settle it. https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing/r3098330-43201-lougheed-highway-59-mission-bc-v0m-1g0/brochure and https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r3047274-43201-lougheed-highway-111-mission-bc-v0m-1g0/brochure
- **"Adult side" is not a number.** Oasis Country Estates is the corridor's only age-oriented product, and its marketing language stops at "adult" — no source gives 55, or any other age, for that side, while the park simultaneously runs a family section. In BC strata law only 55+ bylaws survive, but a manufactured-home park is not a strata, so that law does not fill in the blank. https://www.zolo.ca/mission-real-estate/41168-lougheed-highway/13 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **School district and municipality part company here.** Children in unincorporated Area G attend SD75 (Mission) schools — Dewdney and Deroche each have their own elementary — and Area G voters elect an SD75 rural trustee, even though the City of Mission has no jurisdiction over the land those schools sit on. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Elections/Notice of Election - SD75.pdf
- **The G/C line runs through the Deroche Bench.** The FVRD's Area C plan area includes "the eastern portions of the Deroche Bench," so two neighbouring Bench lots can sit in different electoral areas under different plan documents — a servicing and governance question to ask by PID, not by street name. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/areacocp?sort_by=recent&tool=forum_topic
- **Four parks, one corridor, four different age stories.** The Ruskin-to-Deroche parks overview groups Ruskin Place (family, no age restrictions — in Stave Falls), Triple Creek Estates (no age rule stated — Stave Falls), Nicomen Village (no age rule stated) and Oasis Country Estates (adult side + family section) as one affordable-living corridor — a buyer shopping "the parks" is really shopping four separate rule books. https://www.rodrhearyan.com/blog/affordable-manufactured-homes-ruskin-deroche-oasis-country-triple-creek-nicoman/
