# Downtown Vancouver — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Downtown is the City of Vancouver's official local area name, one of its 22 distinct areas — the downtown peninsula, the primary business district between Burrard Inlet and False Creek. The City describes it as bounded by Burrard Inlet on the north, False Creek and Yaletown to the south, the West End and Stanley Park to the west, and Gastown, Chinatown and Strathcona to the east. The West End is its own separate official local area and is NOT part of this file — West End seniors buildings are flagged as such below, not filed as Downtown stock. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/downtown.aspx and https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/areas-of-the-city.aspx
- Inside the Downtown local area sit Yaletown and Gastown. The Downtown Eastside and Chinatown need care: the City's Strathcona page states Strathcona includes the Downtown Eastside and False Creek Flats, so those districts sit at the east side of Downtown and overlap into Strathcona, a separate local area — Downtown Eastside buildings are not claimed as Downtown stock in this file. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/areas-of-the-city.aspx
- Main streets named in City and TransLink sources: West Georgia, Granville, Burrard, Howe, Hastings, Pender and Cordova Streets; the City's history names Georgia and Granville as the city-centre streets and Water and Cordova as the earlier centre. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/downtown.aspx
- Schools located in the area (VSB, SD39; catchments are address-based — check the VSB locator for any specific home): Elsie Roy Elementary, 150 Drake Street, and Crosstown šxʷwəq̓ʷəθət Elementary, 55 Expo Boulevard. A public secondary school located inside the Downtown local area: UNVERIFIED — the VSB neighbourhood list names only the two elementaries under Downtown. SD39 has no middle-school tier (K–7 / 8–12). https://vsb.bc.ca/list-of-vsb-schools-by-city-neighborhoods and https://vsb.bc.ca/catchment-boundaries
- Library and civic anchor: Vancouver Public Library Central Library, 350 West Georgia Street. A park, community centre or pool located inside the Downtown local area by name on a City page: UNVERIFIED in the area research — the Coal Harbour Community Centre is listed on the City's West End page and is not claimed here. https://www.vpl.ca/
- Health: a hospital located inside the Downtown local area on a Vancouver Coastal Health page: UNVERIFIED. Vancouver General Hospital, 899 West 12th Avenue, is in Fairview, across False Creek. The one care home verified inside Downtown is Yaletown House in Yaletown (Section 2). https://www.vch.ca/en/location/vancouver-general-hospital
- Transit: Downtown is the hub where the Expo Line, Millennium Line and Canada Line meet — Waterfront, Burrard, Granville and Stadium-Chinatown stations serve the local area, and Yaletown–Roundhouse Station serves the Yaletown blocks where the seniors stock below sits. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/skytrain/?page=1
- Where the seniors housing actually is: almost all of it is in the Downtown South / Yaletown band — the Seymour Street, Davie Street, Granville Street and Pacific Boulevard addresses in BC Housing's seniors directory — plus one long-term care home on Cambie Street in Yaletown. There is no 55+ ownership building anywhere downtown (Section 2). The better-known downtown seniors names — Twin Arms and Haro Park Centre — are in the West End, a different local area. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 below.

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**No verified 55+ strata (ownership) stock exists in Downtown Vancouver.** No age-restricted ownership building appears in any source checked — the area is almost entirely general-market strata apartment form, and its verified seniors supply is one long-term care home and a band of non-profit subsidized seniors rental buildings in Downtown South and Yaletown, all below.

### Verified 55+ strata (ownership)

- None verified in Downtown. If a listing calls a Downtown or Yaletown building "55+," treat the age claim as UNCONFIRMED until the registered strata bylaws say so.

### Verified 55+ rental (not ownership)

- No stand-alone market-rental 55+ apartment building was verified in Downtown — the rental-form seniors stock is the subsidized directory buildings and the care home below.

### Retirement residences and care (rental/care — NOT strata ownership)

- **Yaletown House — 1099 Cambie Street (Yaletown).** The Downtown local area's verified care home: Vancouver Coastal Health describes Yaletown House as a non-profit care home operated by the Yaletown House Society, built in 1985 in Vancouver's False Creek North neighbourhood, a four-storey, crescent-shaped building with a secure central courtyard garden, offering 127 subsidized long-term care beds. The operator describes it as a non-profit residential care facility for seniors with complex care needs. Admission is through the long-term care system — you cannot buy or rent a suite here. https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location/yaletown-house and https://www.yaletown.org/

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw — the live issue in Downtown towers, where short-term rental bylaws and the City's licensing rules, not age bylaws, are what a buyer will actually meet in the strata documents. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **How the City creates new 55+ rental:** Vancouver's Seniors Housing Rezoning Policy (approved July 23, 2024; last amended May 19, 2026) treats "Social Housing (where occupancy is restricted to older adults 55+)" as social housing secured by a housing agreement restricting occupancy to residents aged 55 or older, owned and operated by a non-profit or government, and secures seniors supportive or independent living housing as rental — no stratification, no separate sales covenants — for 60 years or the life of the building, whichever is greater. https://guidelines.vancouver.ca/policy-rezoning-seniors-housing.pdf
- **Downtown zoning:** the Downtown District (DD) schedule of Zoning and Development By-law No. 3575 governs much of the area — the only permitted uses are those in the Downtown District Official Development Plan, including commercial, residential, institutional and light industrial uses, parks and open spaces and public uses; CD-1 and C-3A districts also apply in parts of the area. There is no house-lot minimum to work with downtown; every seniors project here is a site-specific rezoning or a non-profit building inside those districts. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/zoning/zoning-by-law-district-schedule-DD.pdf
- **Heritage controls at the area's east and south edges:** the City's heritage areas work gives regulations for Gastown, Chinatown and Yaletown — Yaletown is bordered by Homer and Nelson Streets to the north and east and Cambie, Mainland and Drake Streets to the south and west — which affects renovation and alteration plans for the older Yaletown stock near the seniors buildings. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/vancouvers-heritage-areas.aspx
- **Tax calendar and the extra Downtown taxes:** City of Vancouver 2026 advance taxes were due February 3, 2026 and main taxes July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty after each due date; the Empty Homes Tax requires an annual property status declaration, with the 2025 payment due April 16, 2026 — a standing check on any Downtown purchase, where investor-held units are common. At Downtown price points the Province's additional school tax can also apply: 0.2% on the residential portion of assessed value between $3 million and $4 million and 0.4% above $4 million for 2026, rising to 0.3% and 0.6% on January 1, 2027. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/deadlines-and-penalties.aspx and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/school-tax/additional-school-tax-rate

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Downtown Vancouver?** None that any source verifies. No age-restricted strata building was found in the Downtown local area — a downtown buyer who wants an age-restricted community buys in another neighbourhood, and a buyer set on downtown buys a general-market strata unit in a tower whose bylaws to check are its short-term rental and pet bylaws, not an age bylaw. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Isn't Twin Arms a downtown 55+ building?** It is a real 55+ building — 217 suites for low-income seniors 55+ — but at 1030 Burnaby Street it sits in the West End, a separate official City local area. The same applies to Haro Park Centre on Bute Street. Filing either as "Downtown" misstates the geography a buyer is searching on. https://www.seniorcareaccess.com/retirement-communities/BC/Vancouver-seniors-buildings and https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/areas-of-the-city.aspx
- **Q: What seniors housing does exist downtown?** One care home and one band of subsidized rental: Yaletown House (1099 Cambie Street, 127 subsidized long-term care beds, built 1985), and the BC Housing Zone 6 directory's Downtown South/Yaletown non-profit buildings on Seymour Street (1105 Seymour, Karis Place, Seymour Place, New Continental), Davie Street (Bridgeview Place), Granville Street (Granville House, Granville Residence, Candela Place), Smithe Street (Gresham Residence), Richards Street (Kindred Place) and Pacific Boulevard (Quayside). All rental or care — nothing to buy. https://www.bchousing.org/publications/Zone-6-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf and https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location/yaletown-house
- **Q: How do I get into one of the subsidized buildings, or into Yaletown House?** The subsidized buildings are non-profit rentals listed in BC Housing's Zone 6 seniors directory (May 2025) — apply through BC Housing's Housing Registry or directly with the operator; the City points seniors to the Seniors Services Society, which maintains the Seniors Housing Directory of BC. Yaletown House is long-term care: admission runs through the Vancouver Coastal Health assessment process, not an application to the home for a suite. https://www.bchousing.org/publications/Zone-6-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf and https://vancouver.ca/people-programs/find-supportive-housing-for-seniors.aspx
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a BC 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption); children under 19 in the resident's care and adult children cared for before 19 are also exempt in the defined cases. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. Under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a 55+ parent can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a downtown unit, or run it as a short-term rental?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Short-term rentals are the exception that survives: a strata can still restrict them by bylaw, which is the bylaw that matters most in downtown towers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: Does downtown work without a car for an older buyer?** On transit, yes, on the sourced facts: downtown is the meeting point of the Expo, Millennium and Canada Lines, with Waterfront, Burrard, Granville and Stadium-Chinatown stations serving the local area and Yaletown–Roundhouse Station at the seniors band — the practical question is the specific tower's walk to its station, not the area's service level. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/skytrain/?page=1
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** No hospital inside the Downtown local area was verified on a health-authority page; Vancouver General Hospital, 899 West 12th Avenue, is across False Creek in Fairview. The in-area care anchor is Yaletown House, a long-term care home, not a hospital. https://www.vch.ca/en/location/vancouver-general-hospital
- **Q: Is a waterfront downtown unit in the floodplain?** The City's designated floodplain includes the False Creek coastal floodplain, and the Downtown shoreline on Burrard Inlet and False Creek is the part of the area to check against that dataset; land in the floodplain is subject to Vancouver Building By-law setbacks and flood construction levels. Whether a specific parcel is in it: UNVERIFIED — check the parcel in the City dataset. https://opendata.vancouver.ca/explore/dataset/designated-floodplain/information/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **BC Housing's own directory merges Downtown with the West End — and that is how West End buildings end up marketed as "downtown":** the Zone 6 seniors directory (May 2025) runs one combined "Vancouver - Downtown and West End" table, mixing Seymour and Davie Street buildings with Burnaby, Nelson and Haro Street buildings in a single list. Any "downtown seniors" list built straight off that table without address-checking will import West End stock. https://www.bchousing.org/publications/Zone-6-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf
- **The downtown seniors band is a Downtown South artefact, not a tower amenity:** the Seymour/Granville/Davie non-profit buildings sit in the same few blocks as the Granville entertainment district — the 1105 Seymour profile records the 15-storey City-owned building was funded through Community Amenity Contributions, with non-profit service space on its lower four floors. This stock was negotiated out of downtown development, which is why it clusters where the towers are. https://www.greenbuildingaudiotours.com/buildings/1105_seymour_street
- **Yaletown House is older than most of Yaletown's condos:** VCH records the care home was built in 1985 in False Creek North — a four-storey crescent around a secure courtyard garden with 127 subsidized beds — predating the Expo-lands residential build-out around it. It is the downtown peninsula's one verified long-term care home in this pass. https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location/yaletown-house
- **Even the City's own area links blur this geography:** the area research file records that on the City's areas page the Coal Harbour locality link resolves to the West End page, the Yaletown locality link resolves to the Downtown page, and the Gastown and Chinatown locality links resolve to the Strathcona page — three different answers for three waterfront districts a buyer will treat as one "downtown." Name the sub-neighbourhood and the station in any listing copy, not just "downtown." https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/areas-of-the-city.aspx
- **1105 Seymour shows why "in the seniors directory" does not mean "seniors only":** the same address the BC Housing seniors directory lists is described in its building profile as 81 units including 19 two-bedroom family apartments. Directory inclusion is a starting point for verification, not the verification — the pattern this file applies to every subsidized entry above. https://www.greenbuildingaudiotours.com/buildings/1105_seymour_street
- **The Empty Homes Tax declaration is the standing annual chore downtown owners actually face:** every Vancouver owner must file the yearly property status declaration, and downtown's investor-held stock makes the seller's declaration status a standard due-diligence check on any purchase here — the 2025 payment was due April 16, 2026, with a 5% penalty after that date. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/deadlines-and-penalties.aspx
