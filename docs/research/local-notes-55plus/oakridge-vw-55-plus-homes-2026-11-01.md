# Oakridge VW — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Oakridge VW is the City of Vancouver area around West 41st Avenue and Cambie Street in south-central Vancouver, centred on the Oakridge Municipal Town Centre and the Oakridge–41st Avenue Canada Line station. "VW" is the GVR board's Vancouver West suffix. Nearby areas in the research file: South Cambie, Langara, Marpole, Riley Park, Shaughnessy and Kerrisdale. (Area research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/oakridge-vw.md, Section 1.)
- Oakridge is planned as a Municipal Town Centre within the Cambie Corridor Plan, which Council approved May 9, 2011 to guide transit-oriented growth along Cambie Street around the Canada Line stations, including Oakridge–41st Avenue; Phase 3 (approved 2018) extended policy into the neighbourhood streets around the station, adding townhouse and low-rise forms off the arterials. https://www.cip-icu.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cambie-Corridor-Plan-FINAL-Aug-2011_WEB.pdf and https://council.vancouver.ca/20180417/documents/rr2_presentation.pdf
- The Oakridge Centre redevelopment (Oakridge Park, developers QuadReal Property Group and Westbank) is a 28-acre project at West 41st and Cambie with 3,000+ homes for 6,000+ residents, a new 9-acre park, roughly 700,000 sq ft of office space and a civic centre with a Vancouver Public Library branch; retail's first phase opened in 2026 and the first residential towers were expected ready in early 2027 (developer-sourced figures). https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/28/3303201/0/en/Oakridge-Park-Celebrates-Grand-Opening-Unveiling-Canada-s-Largest-Redevelopment-and-New-Global-Retail-Destination.html and http://vancouver.ca/files/cov/oakridge-park-open-house-boards-december-2017.pdf
- The first five Oakridge Park residential towers were reported as roughly 700 strata condos plus 180 social housing units, with further social housing completing in the first half of 2027 — the town centre's early resale market sits alongside a substantial non-market population from day one. None of the sources checked describes any Oakridge Park residential tower as age-restricted. https://burnabyhouse.com/news/oakridge-park-developers-ask-city-ease/
- Transit: the Canada Line's Oakridge–41st Avenue Station (being rebuilt with the Oakridge Park development, with direct underground access into the mall) and the R4 41st Ave RapidBus along West 41st Avenue between Joyce–Collingwood Station and UBC. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects and https://buzzer.translink.ca/2026/01/oakridge-41st-avenue-station-to-close-at-11-p-m-starting-jan-26-until-march/
- Schools verified in the research file: Sir William Osler Elementary (5970 Selkirk St) and Van Horne Elementary (5855 Ontario St) inside Oakridge, Sir Wilfrid Laurier Elementary (7350 Laurel St) serving the south side, and Eric Hamber Secondary as the regular secondary for both sample addresses run in the SD39 locator. Catchments for other addresses: UNCONFIRMED. https://vsb.bc.ca/list-of-vsb-schools-by-city-neighborhoods
- Where 55+ housing actually concentrates: on and just west of the West 41st Avenue corridor — Legacy Senior Living at 611 West 41st Ave (Cambie side) and the Louis Brier / Weinberg campus at West 41st and Oak Street (1055 West 41st Ave / 5650 Osler St). There is no seniors building at Oakridge Park itself in any source checked; the development's own materials list a seniors' centre among the civic amenities, which is a centre, not housing. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 below, and the Oakridge Park leasing material listing "a senior's centre and a daycare" among its components. https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blt110dde6cf7865bcb/bltc875dc5e87fe3ac3/68dff56a4306506b1d0b570c/6597-Oakridge-Leasing-Brochure-Redesign_Digital.pdf

The BC Seniors Advocate's facility record lists 215 total beds, all publicly funded, accredited, and records that it is co-located with a publicly subsidized assisted living residence (the Weinberg Residence). - **The Harry and Jeanette Weinberg (of Baltimore) Residence — 5650 Osler St (west end of the same Snider Campus).** Registered with BC's Assisted Living Registry as a seniors (class SN) residence, legal operator The Louis Brier Jewish Residence Society. The residence's own site describes a private boutique residence for Jewish seniors on the Dr. Irving & Phyllis Snider Campus for Jewish Seniors, next to Louis Brier Home. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **City zoning is the Zoning and Development By-law No. 3575.** For detached lots outside the Cambie Corridor designations, By-law No. 13817 replaced the RS districts with the R1-1 district, allowing up to 6 strata units (or 8 secured rental units) on former single-family lots. Corridor lots near Oakridge–41st Station may carry Cambie Corridor Plan designations instead — check which framework governs the specific lot. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/consolidated/13817.pdf
- **The Cambie Corridor Plan is the area framework** (approved May 9, 2011; Phase 3 in 2018): it designates Oakridge as a Municipal Town Centre and supports townhouse and low-rise forms on many streets within walking distance of Oakridge–41st Station, implemented through rezoning. https://www.cip-icu.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Cambie-Corridor-Plan-FINAL-Aug-2011_WEB.pdf and https://council.vancouver.ca/20180417/documents/rr2_presentation.pdf
- **City of Vancouver Seniors Housing Strategy (approved July 23, 2024):** the City's first seniors housing strategy, with a Seniors Housing Rezoning Policy (last amended May 19, 2026) to enable more seniors housing across all neighbourhoods and a Resident Relocation Policy adding protections for seniors in community care and assisted living residences in the event of a redevelopment. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/seniors-housing-strategy-address-needs-of-older-adults-july-2024.aspx and https://guidelines.vancouver.ca/policy-rezoning-seniors-housing.pdf
- **Citywide land-use direction now comes from the Vancouver Official Development Plan**, which replaced the Vancouver Plan on March 31, 2026. https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/vancouver-plan.pdf
- **Tax calendar:** City of Vancouver 2026 advance taxes were due February 3, 2026 and main taxes July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty on unpaid balances; the City's Empty Homes Tax charges 3% of assessed value on homes declared or deemed empty, with the annual declaration due February 3, 2026 and payment due April 16, 2026. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/deadlines-and-penalties.aspx

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Oakridge?** None that any source verifies. There is no age-restricted strata building in Oakridge VW — the new Oakridge Park towers are general-market strata with no age bylaw in any source checked. A 55+ buyer here is buying a regular town-centre condo, a corridor townhouse or a side-street house. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Can I buy a suite at Legacy Senior Living on West 41st?** No. It is a private retirement residence (independent living plus registered assisted living) at 611 West 41st Avenue — you rent a suite with services; there is no strata lot to purchase. https://legacyseniorliving.com/ and https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?name=&address=&order=alr_legal_operator_name&sort=desc&f%5B0%5D=city%3AVancouver&page=1
- **Q: Is the Louis Brier / Weinberg campus a place I apply to rent on the open market?** Louis Brier Home is long-term care (215 publicly funded beds) — admission is a health-system placement through Vancouver Coastal Health. The co-located Weinberg Residence is registered assisted living recorded as publicly subsidized; it is a care residence for Jewish seniors on the Snider Campus, not a market rental building. https://www.seniorsadvocatebc.ca/quickfacts/location/75923/ and http://weinbergresidence.com/
- **Q: How many homes are in the Weinberg Residence?** The operator's own sources disagree: one description says 40 assisted living and multi-level care rooms in total, while a campus posting and the redevelopment newsletter say 40 assisted living plus 20 multi-level care (60). This file flags the conflict rather than picking one — ask the residence which figure is current. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere in Vancouver?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out an Oakridge Park condo I buy?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022 — and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. Note the town centre's tenure mix: the first Oakridge Park towers sit alongside about 180 social housing units, so a purchase here is in a deliberately mixed-tenure development. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://burnabyhouse.com/news/oakridge-park-developers-ask-city-ease/
- **Q: Is the Oakridge–41st station upgrade finished?** TransLink expected the upgrade — new accessible escalators, a new elevator, a renovated weather-protected entrance and direct underground access into Oakridge Park — complete in summer 2026, after temporary 11 p.m. station closures through winter/spring 2026. Confirm the final handover state before relying on the direct station-to-mall connection as a completed feature. https://buzzer.translink.ca/2026/01/oakridge-41st-avenue-station-to-close-at-11-p-m-starting-jan-26-until-march/
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** The BC Children's Hospital and BC Women's Hospital & Health Centre campus is at 4500 Oak Street, a short distance north of West 41st Avenue on Oak Street — a major health-care trip generator on the area's west side. (Area research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/south-cambie.md, Section 4, for the campus description at http://www.bcchildrens.ca/.)
- **Q: Does Oakridge Park include seniors housing I can wait-list for?** The development includes a seniors' centre among its civic amenities and about 180 social housing units in its first towers, but no source checked designates any residential unit at Oakridge Park as seniors-only or 55+. Seniors designation for the social housing: UNCONFIRMED. https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blt110dde6cf7865bcb/bltc875dc5e87fe3ac3/68dff56a4306506b1d0b570c/6597-Oakridge-Leasing-Brochure-Redesign_Digital.pdf and https://burnabyhouse.com/news/oakridge-park-developers-ask-city-ease/
- **Q: If I hold a home here vacant, what does it cost me?** Budget for the City's Empty Homes Tax: an annual declaration is required, and homes declared or deemed vacant are taxed at 3% of assessed taxable value, on top of the ordinary tax calendar (advance taxes February 3, 2026; main taxes July 3, 2026, 5% penalty after each due date). https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/deadlines-and-penalties.aspx

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Oakridge's seniors care sits on one campus with two different doors.** At West 41st and Oak, Louis Brier Home (215 publicly funded long-term care beds) and the Weinberg Residence (registered, publicly subsidized assisted living) share the Dr. Irving & Phyllis Snider Campus for Jewish Seniors — the operator itself locates the campus "in the Oakridge area of Vancouver," on the corner of West 41st Avenue and Oak Street, with the Weinberg Residence at the campus's west end on Osler Street. A buyer who searches only "Oakridge retirement" finds Legacy Senior Living and misses this campus entirely. https://www.seniorsadvocatebc.ca/quickfacts/location/75923/ and http://louisbrier.com/students
- **The town centre's "seniors" component is a centre, not homes.** Oakridge Park's own leasing material lists "a senior's centre and a daycare" among the civic components alongside the community centre and library — amenity space inside a 3,000+ home development in which no source identifies a single age-restricted unit. https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blt110dde6cf7865bcb/bltc875dc5e87fe3ac3/68dff56a4306506b1d0b570c/6597-Oakridge-Leasing-Brochure-Redesign_Digital.pdf
- **The newest supply changes what "downsizing in Oakridge" means.** Before Oakridge Park, the side-street stock was post-war detached bungalows and older low-rise condos near 41st and Oak; the research file records the first five new towers as roughly 700 strata condos plus 180 social housing units. A 55+ buyer comparing a resale bungalow-area condo against a new tower is comparing two different products in the same few blocks, neither of them age-restricted. https://burnabyhouse.com/news/oakridge-park-developers-ask-city-ease/
- **The west side of the sub-area has a second master plan running.** The former Oakridge Transit Centre bus depot (closed by TransLink in 2016) west of Oak Street is being redeveloped as Mayfair West by Grosvenor under a 2015 policy statement and a 2020 rezoning, with a denser revised scheme advanced in 2026 — years of construction next to the West 41st/Oak seniors campus, and no seniors housing component in any source checked. https://www.grosvenor.com/property/property-america/oakridge and https://www.shapeyourcity.ca/929-989-w-41-ave-otc
- **Two of the retirement names that surface for "Oakridge" are not here.** Sunrise of Vancouver markets its closeness to Oakridge Shopping Centre from West 57th Avenue and Oak Street, and Shannon Oaks markets as a south Vancouver retirement home from Waverley Avenue in east Vancouver. Both are outside this sub-area — see the checked list in Section 2. https://uat.ca.sunriseseniorliving.com/en/communities/bc/sunrise-of-vancouver and https://www.baptisthousing.org/communities/lower-mainland/shannon-oaks
- **The station itself was a construction site into 2026.** TransLink ran temporary early (11 p.m.) closures of Oakridge–41st Avenue Station through winter/spring 2026 for the rebuild tied to Oakridge Park, expecting completion in summer 2026 — listings written during that window that describe the direct underground mall connection as finished were ahead of the work. https://buzzer.translink.ca/2026/01/oakridge-41st-avenue-station-to-close-at-11-p-m-starting-jan-26-until-march/
