## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. No building in this district could carry such a bylaw for homes, because there are no homes here — the law matters to a Lake City reader only for a 55+ purchase made in a residential sub-area. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **The rules that actually govern this district are employment-land rules:** the Burnaby 2050 OCP policy directions aim to protect existing industrial land, intensify employment lands and use them efficiently; the City defines industrial lands as lands intended for heavy and light industrial activities including distribution, warehousing, repair, construction yards, infrastructure, outdoor storage, wholesale, manufacturing, trade and e-commerce. Nothing in that framework designates Lake City land for housing. https://yourvoice.burnaby.ca/burnaby-2050-planning-our-city-together/burnaby-2050-policy-guide-share-your-feedback/economic-development-employment-and-industrial-lands
- **Zoning bylaw — note the name change, and verify the district in writing.** The new **Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026** was adopted June 9, 2026, replaced the former Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965, and took effect July 1, 2026; properties previously zoned under repealed districts were reassigned to the closest comparable new district, following the Burnaby 2050 OCP and Metro Vancouver designations for employment and industrial lands. The specific new district and permitted-use list for an individual Lake City parcel are UNVERIFIED — confirm in writing with the Burnaby Planning and Building Department, exactly as the Lake City plan map itself instructs. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite and https://burnaby.widen.net/s/7grv6g7v62/lum---lake-city
- **Plan hierarchy:** Burnaby's community plans are policy guidelines for rezoning and development applications, and the Burnaby 2050 Official Community Plan (adopted December 9, 2025) replaces the 1998 OCP and takes precedence over older community-plan land use maps where they conflict — so the 2000 Lake City Community Plan must be read under the 2050 OCP, not on its own. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-12-11/city-burnaby-adopts-burnaby-2050-official-community-plan
- **Strata law still applies to industrial strata purchases:** an industrial strata unit in this district is bought and governed under the provincial strata framework, so review the strata documents early; BC strata disputes can go to the Civil Resolution Tribunal. That is a commercial-purchase rule — it does not create any residential or 55+ product in the district. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing
- **Property taxes:** Burnaby's 2026 property taxes were due Friday, July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date, and a 2.9% increase in the 2026–2030 Financial Plan. Commercial/industrial class rates for a specific Lake City property are UNVERIFIED here — read them off the property's own tax notice. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Lake City?** None — there are no homes in Lake City at all. The district is industrial and business-centre land along Lake City Way and Enterprise Street under a community plan adopted in 2000 for commercial/office and industrial/business centre uses. A 55+ buyer searching this name is looking for the residential areas around it — Montecito, Government Road, Cariboo and Lougheed — not for anything inside the district. https://burnaby.widen.net/s/7grv6g7v62/lum---lake-city
- **Q: Why are there no Lake City sales numbers anywhere?** Because SnapStats publishes no Lake City sub-area row for Burnaby in its September 2026 report — there is no sub-area inventory, solds figure or sales ratio to quote. Any "Lake City housing market" figure would have to be invented, and the Burnaby community medians are city-wide context, not Lake City figures. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report)
- **Q: Is housing planned for the district under the new city plan?** No source checked states a residential designation or rezoning for Lake City: the Burnaby 2050 OCP's employment-lands direction is to protect existing industrial land and intensify it for employment use. Treat any claim of future homes in the district as UNCONFIRMED unless a City rezoning document names the parcel. https://yourvoice.burnaby.ca/burnaby-2050-planning-our-city-together/burnaby-2050-policy-guide-share-your-feedback/economic-development-employment-and-industrial-lands
- **Q: Where is the nearest seniors housing to Lake City?** The nearest registered assisted-living residence in the neighbouring notes is the Swedish Assisted Living Residence (7230 Halifax St) in Montecito, south-west across Burnaby Lake — 64 suites, most of them Fraser Health-subsidized, accessed through the operator and Fraser Health. The nearest City seniors recreation is the Temporary Cameron 55+ Centre in the Lougheed Town Centre (110-9855 Austin Ave). Neither is in Lake City, and neither is something to buy. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-At-Location/0/A/assisted-living-at-swedish-assisted-living-residence and https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/cameron-recreation-complex
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata in a neighbouring area?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. (Relevant to residential sub-areas; there is no such building in Lake City.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: There is a SkyTrain station called Lake City Way — doesn't that mean homes are coming?** The station is the district's worker transit link on the Millennium Line, between Sperling–Burnaby Lake and Production Way–University stations. A station inside an industrial district serves the employment lands; it does not change the district's industrial designation, and no residential rezoning for the district was verified in any source checked. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/about-burnaby/town-centres
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Burnaby Hospital, 3935 Kincaid St, in central Burnaby — west across the lake side of the city from this district. There is no health facility in Lake City itself. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Burnaby/burnaby-hospital
- **Q: What should a buyer actually check on a Lake City parcel?** The land file, not a housing file: confirm the parcel's current Zoning Bylaw 2026 district and permitted-use list in writing with the City, read the 2000 Lake City plan under the 2050 OCP, and confirm floodplain status, streamside setbacks and any environmental conditions for the specific parcel — the district sits on the Still Creek / Burnaby Lake lowland, and those checks are UNVERIFIED for individual parcels in the sources read. https://burnaby.widen.net/s/7grv6g7v62/lum---lake-city and https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park
