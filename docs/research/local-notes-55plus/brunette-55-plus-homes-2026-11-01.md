## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **City zoning: Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001** is New Westminster's zoning bylaw. Single-detached homes sit in the Single Detached Residential Districts (RS series, s.310) — RS-1 minimum site 557.40 sq. m. (6,000 sq. ft.), floor space ratio not over 0.5; secondary suite and laneway / carriage house rules are set in the same bylaw; townhouse form is regulated under Townhouse Residential Districts RT-A and RT-B where designated. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Zoning_Bylaw_No_6680_2001_as_of_June_30_2022_(Complete).pdf
- **OCP: Our City 2041, Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 7925, 2017** governs the area; the Sapperton and Braid stations area is one of the City's Frequent Transit Development Areas, which is the growth framework for this edge of the city — station-proximate lots may carry different expectations than interior RS-1 streets, so confirm the designation for the specific parcel at the City. https://www.newwestcity.ca/ocp/past-event-materials-and-council-reports
- **Flood rules are a live diligence item here:** the City's floodplain study places the industrial land along the Brunette River in Floodplain Zone 4, below the Flood Construction Level, and the City's flood management page places sections of the mainland within the provincial floodplain — any lot on the creek / river corridor needs its flood construction level confirmed before building or insuring; lot-level status is property-specific. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf and http://www.newwestcity.ca/services/public-safety/flood-management
- **Property taxes and utilities:** New Westminster 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on current taxes remaining unpaid on July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on any unpaid balance on September 3, 2026. New Westminster also operates its own electrical utility, plus City water / sewer services — a utility structure unlike Coquitlam's, which is one more reason the city line matters on a Brunette file. https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Brunette?** None that any source verifies — and in September 2026 there was nothing to buy under this sub-area name at all: 0 detached listings, 0 condos & townhomes listings, 0 solds in either segment. The sub-area's housing is detached houses under the RS districts with townhouse form only where RT-A / RT-B designated, and no named complex carries an age restriction in any source. A senior who wants a legal 55+ purchase is looking at other New Westminster or Tri-Cities sub-areas. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report, via the area research file.)
- **Q: Is Brunette in New Westminster or Coquitlam?** New Westminster. SnapStats carries Brunette as a New Westminster sub-area with no Coquitlam row of that name, and City sources place it on the east side of mainland New Westminster along the Brunette Creek corridor by Hume Park. The creek and Brunette Avenue continue into Coquitlam, but that side is a different municipality — different taxes, different school district (SD40 vs SD43), different zoning bylaw. Confirm the municipality before anything else on a "Brunette" lead. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/rte/2_4 Parks and Open Space.pdf
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Can a Brunette house take a suite or a laneway home for family instead?** The framework exists in the same bylaw that zones the houses: Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001 sets the secondary suite and laneway / carriage house rules for the RS districts (RS-1 minimum site 557.40 sq. m., FSR 0.5). Confirm the specific lot's zone, suite eligibility and — on creek-side lots — its flood construction level before pricing family-suite potential in. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Zoning_Bylaw_No_6680_2001_as_of_June_30_2022_(Complete).pdf
- **Q: Is the creek corridor a flood risk for a house purchase?** The corridor is the documented risk: the City floodplain study places the industrial land along the Brunette River in Floodplain Zone 4, below the Flood Construction Level. Pull the property's flood construction level and an insurance quote before subjects come off on any creek-adjacent lot; interior streets are a separate, lot-by-lot question. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Royal Columbian Hospital, 330 East Columbia Street, in Sapperton next to this area — one of the region's major hospitals, reached along the East Columbia Street corridor that also carries the route 109 bus. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/New-Westminster/royal-columbian-hospital
- **Q: What transit can I actually use day to day?** No station is in Brunette; the nearest are Sapperton and Braid on the Expo Line, and route 109 (New Westminster Station / Lougheed Station) serves East Columbia Street. A car-free senior here is bus-and-station dependent — check the exact address's walk to a stop before buying on a no-car plan. https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/schedules-by-region/2024-schedules/september/burnaby-new-west-p155.pdf
- **Q: If I need help later, can I stay in Brunette?** Not in a seniors building — there is none in the sub-area. The in-city ladder sits elsewhere in New Westminster: assisted living at Victoria Heights on Victoria Hill (59 units) and Thornebridge Gardens in Uptown, and hospital care next door at Royal Columbian in Sapperton. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ANew%20Westminster&f%5B1%5D=city%3ANorth%20Vancouver&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&name=&address=&order=alr_legal_operator_name&sort=asc
