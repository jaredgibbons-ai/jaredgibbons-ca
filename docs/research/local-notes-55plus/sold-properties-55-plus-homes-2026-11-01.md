## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. This is the rule that decides whether a sold record in a complex is a true 55+ record at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Who can access the MLS system at all:** the Competition Bureau (summarising CREA's position) states agents and brokers must become members of a local real estate board and CREA in order to access the MLS system, which it describes as a comprehensive database of current and historical listing information; members must adhere to both CREA's rules, such as the REALTOR Code, and the rules of their respective local real estate boards. CREA owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service ("MLS") and REALTOR. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **How this site's sold feed is authorised, on its own face:** the page's Reciprocity notice states the data comes in part from the MLS Reciprocity program of GVR, FVREB or CADREB; that listings held by participating firms are marked with the MLS logo and include the listing agent's name; that the representation is based on data generated by the boards, which assume no responsibility for its accuracy; and that the materials may not be reproduced without the boards' express written consent. Whether sold listings specifically require a separate board permission or form in FVREB/GVR is UNVERIFIED — neither board publishes its MLS rules on a public page that could be read for this research. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sold-properties.html
- **BC Assessment's own sales-history service (official source):** BC Assessment states that with a free registered account on its "Find your property assessment" service, a property owner can access the property's annual assessed value, 10-year historical values and sales history, and check recent sales of homes in the neighbourhood (sales updated monthly). Through BC OnLine, BC Assessment's database can also be searched for detailed assessment roll information including recent sales, and its Assessment Roll Query Report includes the five most recent sales. https://info.bcassessment.ca/nho and https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/look-up-property-assessment/BC-Online
- **The BC regulator's consumer rules around any sale:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party; dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is the price on each sold card the price the home sold for?** The page does not say. Each card shows a price alongside Status: Sold and the MLS number, with no label stating whether it is the sold price or the final list price, and no sold date — ask the REALTOR for the sold price and completion details on any specific property before using a card as a comparable. No sold price is stated anywhere in this file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sold-properties.html
- **Q: Where does the sold data on this page come from?** Per the page's own footer, in part from the MLS Reciprocity program of the Greater Vancouver REALTORS (GVR), the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board (FVREB) or the Chilliwack and District Real Estate Board (CADREB); the boards assume no responsibility for its accuracy and the materials may not be reproduced without their express written consent. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sold-properties.html
- **Q: Can I look up a home's sales history myself, without a REALTOR?** Partly, through BC Assessment's official service: BC Assessment states a free registered account gives access to your own property's sales history and to recent sales of homes in your neighbourhood, updated monthly, and its public property pages show a sales history for the last 3 full calendar years where sales exist. That is an assessment service, not the MLS record — the full MLS listing history (including a 55+ complex's bylaw position on the Form B) comes through a board member. https://info.bcassessment.ca/nho and https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/look-up-property-assessment/BC-Online
- **Q: Why do I need a REALTOR for sold data at all?** Because MLS access is member access: the Competition Bureau states agents and brokers must be members of a local board and CREA to access the MLS system — the comprehensive database of current and historical listing information — and must comply with both CREA's rules and their local board's rules. A member REALTOR can pull the Paragon/MLS listing history for a specific property; the public Reciprocity feed on a website is a display, not that database. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Q: Are these all homes Jared Gibbons sold?** The page presents them that way — heading "Recently Sold Homes by Jared Gibbons," every first-page card showing Status: Sold and "Listed by Royal LePage Little Oak Realty" — but the list-view cards name the brokerage rather than the agent's role on each transaction, so whether every entry is a Jared listing (versus a buyer-side sale) is UNVERIFIED from the page text. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sold-properties.html
- **Q: How far back does the sold record go?** UNVERIFIED — the feed is paginated, but no date range, sold dates or year filter appears in the page text read for the underlying research; the start of the record can only be confirmed from the REALTOR's Paragon sold-history export. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sold-properties.html
- **Q: If a home in a 55+ complex has sold, can I buy the next one that comes up if I'm under 55?** Ownership is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a resident must be 55+, hold a legacy exemption, or fit an exemption (younger spouse/partner, live-in caregiver, defined children cases). A past sale in the complex does not change that. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Does a 55+ restriction affect what a home sells for or how fast?** No sourced figure answers that in this research, and none is stated here: the next buyer must also be able to live in the unit under the bylaw, which mechanically narrows the resident buyer pool, but any price or pace effect for a specific complex is UNVERIFIED without that complex's Paragon comparables. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit I buy in a 55+ complex?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. The tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: What should I check before using a sold home as a comparable for my 55+ home?** That the comparable is in the same legal product: a strata sale carries the complex's bylaws (including its 55+ bylaw position, proved on the Form B), a manufactured-home park sale is a home-plus-pad-tenancy assignment, and a care or subsidized rental unit is never sold at all. The sibling files in this series keep those three categories separate for exactly this reason. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 above.

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The sold feed's own notice disclaims its accuracy.** The page states the representation is based in whole or part on data generated by the boards, which assume no responsibility for its accuracy, and that the materials may not be reproduced without the boards' express written consent — treat the feed as a finding aid, and get the authoritative record (the Paragon/MLS listing history) from the REALTOR. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sold-properties.html
- **Sold-data display in Canada has been litigated — in Ontario, not BC.** The Competition Bureau's case against the Toronto Real Estate Board concerned exactly the display of sold data online: the Tribunal rejected TREB's privacy argument as a pretext and required sold-data fields to be provided in Virtual Office Website (VOW) feeds, displayable on any device. That is a Greater Toronto Area precedent about password-protected client websites — it is NOT a statement of any FVREB or GVR rule, and no BC board's sold-display rule is stated in this file because neither board publishes its rules publicly. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **The 2010 CREA consent agreement was about listing services, not BC sold display.** In October 2010 a consent agreement between the Commissioner of Competition and CREA, registered with the Tribunal for a 10-year period, required CREA not to adopt or enforce rules denying agents the ability to provide mere postings or discriminating against agents who offer them. What followed or replaced it after that term is UNVERIFIED in this research. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **BC Assessment's "sample sold properties" come with their own caution.** BC Assessment states sample sold properties on its property pages are for general information only, are not necessarily the properties it would use as comparisons, may be time-adjusted to the July 1 valuation date, and sales within the past 60 days may not yet be verified. An assessed value is a tax figure, not a sold price for your home. https://www.bcassessment.ca/Property/Info/QTAwMDBSMFVZVw==
- **Freshness is stamped on the feed itself.** The sold-properties page stamps its own data update time in its footer, and sold feeds change as sales complete and records age — check the stamp before citing anything from the page, and re-confirm the specific record with the REALTOR. https://jaredgibbons.ca/sold-properties.html
- **In the 55+ market, the "sold" category silently excludes most seniors housing.** Of the seniors product verified across this series, only the strata complexes and the manufactured homes in parks are ever bought and sold; the 55+ rental building (Kew Lifestyles at Montrose), every care residence, and every subsidized building in Section 2 never appears in a sold feed at all — a sold-properties search is a search of the ownership slice only. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 above.
