- **Feed mechanics** — how listings are selected into this site's feed, how often it refreshes, why the count is 310 at reading, and whether sold or expired listings ever appear in it: UNVERIFIED — the page text states no filter, no refresh interval beyond "real time"/"continuously" marketing language, and no inclusion rules. https://jaredgibbons.ca/recip.html
- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. A "55+" claim on any card in this feed is proved by the building's registered bylaws on the Form B, not by the card text. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption (Strata Property Regulation 7.01: legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and since May 1, 2023 a younger spouse/partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children cared for before 19). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Manufactured-home pads follow tenancy law, not strata law,** wherever a 55+ park from Section 2 appears in search results: the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act covers most manufactured home tenancies in BC, pad rent can be increased only once every 12 months with at least three full months' written notice, capped at the provincial limit (2.3% plus a proportional amount for increases taking effect in 2026; 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027), and assignment under s. 28 requires the landlord's prior written consent or an order of the director, with the pad rent unchanged on assignment. http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Who the visitor is dealing with, per the regulator:** in BC, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and BCFSA states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, must disclose at the outset whether they can represent the consumer and how to complain to BCFSA, and can only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction — dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. That frame matters on this page precisely because the homes shown are other firms' listings: the agent whose contact block sits beside each card is not the listing agent. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://jaredgibbons.ca/recip.html
