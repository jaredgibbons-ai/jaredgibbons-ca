# Surrey Lots & Land — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Scope note: this is a topic file, not an area file. The live page is a City of Surrey-wide feed of building lots and vacant land. Vacant land has no 55+ product as such: a bare lot carries no strata corporation, no age bylaw and no park rule, so Surrey has ZERO verified 55+ ownership complexes on the lots-and-land page itself. Section 1 defines the topic's two land systems (serviced urban infill lots under Surrey Zoning By-law No. 12000 versus agricultural parcels governed by Agricultural Land Reserve rules), Section 2 covers Surrey's verified 55+ stock city-wide — the built product a 55+ buyer is weighing against building — and Sections 3–5 are angled to what a 55+ buyer needs to know about buying land and building in Surrey: SSMUH added units, reserve housing limits, the Serpentine–Nicomekl floodplain, servicing, and the single-level / caregiver-suite questions that drive most 55+ land purchases. No land market figures are stated: SnapStats publishes no vacant-land category.**

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. This rule does not attach to a freehold lot — it is the rule a 55+ buyer who builds and later strata-titles, or who buys into a bare-land strata, will live under. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. The live-in caregiver exemption is the strata-side mechanism a 55+ buyer planning ahead for care should know; on a freehold lot there is no bylaw to exempt anyone from, and the caregiver-suite question is a zoning and ALR question instead (below). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Establish which land system you are buying in before anything else.** An urban lot runs on Surrey Zoning By-law No. 12000 and the SSMUH added-unit framework, while a reserve parcel also runs on Agricultural Land Commission rules — the same "land" search spans both. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_Zoning_12000.pdf
- **Zoning instrument:** Surrey Zoning By-law, 1993, No. 12000 divides the city into zones and regulates use, density, siting and subdivision; the consolidated bylaw text is the source for any zone's minimum subdivision lot area, and zoning is confirmed parcel by parcel in COSMOS. A complete Surrey zone-minimum table was not re-extracted in the research pass: UNVERIFIED as a table in this file. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_Zoning_12000.pdf
- **Added units on urban lots (SSMUH):** Surrey's framework, adopted July 8, 2024, consolidated 20 existing single-family and duplex zones into 9 zones (RA, R1, R2, R2-O, R3, R4, R5, R5-S, R6); the City states a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop, applying to eligible residential lots under 4,050 m² within the Urban Containment Boundary, with exemptions including certain hazard lands and properties not connected to community water and sewer. For a 55+ buyer, this is the urban route to a rancher plus a suite or added dwelling on one lot. Added-unit permission is not the same as permission to subdivide. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **Secondary suites on urban lots:** Surrey permits only one suite per single-family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit, at most 40% of the principal unit's habitable floor area, and never inside a coach house or garden suite. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- **Reserve subdivision:** subdivision of Agricultural Land Reserve land requires permission from the Agricultural Land Commission, apart from the limited cases in the Agricultural Land Reserve General Regulation where an approving officer may approve without the Commission. A specific parcel's reserve status is checked on the Agricultural Land Commission maps by PID or civic address. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/application-and-notice-process/applications/required-documents/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Housing on reserve land:** a principal residence built without an Agricultural Land Commission application must have a total floor area of 500 m² or less, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill placed for its construction, and a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence; one additional detached residence is permitted without an application at 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller and 186 m² or less on larger parcels, where Surrey bylaws also permit it. That additional-residence route is the ALR version of the caregiver-dwelling question. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Floodplain on the lowland edges:** Surrey's coastal floodplain runs along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers and the Serpentine–Nicomekl lowlands, so a lowland parcel can carry flood-construction levels and diking-related requirements set by the City — confirm the parcel's position and levels with the City before writing. A single-level rancher design does not remove a flood-construction level; it has to be designed to it. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands
- **Riparian and creek constraints:** Surrey plan areas record fish-bearing creeks whose riparian rules can affect parcels — for example, the South Newton plan area includes Archibald Creek headwaters, Sullivan Heights Creek and Hyland Creek tributaries. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/newton-land-use-plans/south-newton
- **Manufactured-home parks run on a different statute.** Pad tenancies are governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act — age rules in a park are park rules/policies, not strata bylaws. Surrey's own policy brochure states the property owner must give 12 months' notice of a final moving date using a Notice to End Tenancy, which can only be given after the owner receives all their permits, and the City's Manufactured Home Park Policy requires an affordable housing program for residents when a park is redeveloped. Surrey's Tenant Protection Bylaw, 2025, No. 21788 does NOT apply to property governed by the MHPTA. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_reg_21788.pdf
- **Development Cost Charges and tree protection:** current DCC rates from the City's DCC bylaw, and the Surrey Tree Protection Bylaw's own thresholds (protected-tree definitions, permit triggers, replacement ratios, Tree Fund amounts), are UNVERIFIED in this file — direct opening of the City's pages was stopped by a 429 rate-limit response in the research pass and was not retried. Do not rely on the live page's summaries of either. https://jaredgibbons.ca/surrey-lots-land.html
- **Water and septic on unserviced parcels:** the provincial GWELLS registry holds recorded wells (older wells may not appear); domestic groundwater use needs no licence, while non-domestic use, including irrigation, requires a water licence under the Water Sustainability Act; and Fraser Health requires a home not connected to a community sewer to use an onsite sewage system complying with the Sewerage System Regulation, designed and installed by an Authorized Person. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/health-topics-a-to-z/onsite-sewerage-systems/regulatory-requirements-for-onsite-sewerage-systems
- **Taxes on a land purchase:** City of Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2. The Province's general property transfer tax rates are 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. GST treatment of a specific lot sale is UNVERIFIED in these notes — the live page's summary was not verified against a Canada Revenue Agency source; confirm with a CPA per transaction. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Tax relief that matters at 55+:** the Province's property tax deferment regular program is for people 55 or older (plus surviving spouses and persons with disabilities), and the additional home owner grant (for seniors 65+) is a separate item — both are principal-residence programs, so they speak to the finished home, not to the vacant lot purchase itself. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/property-tax-deferment-program/eligibility

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can I buy a "55+ lot" in Surrey?** No such product exists as land. A vacant lot carries no age restriction — the 55+ rule, where one exists, lives in a strata's registered bylaws (Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1/123.2) or in a manufactured-home park's rules, and it attaches only when a strata is created or a park operates. A lot marketed to downsizers is still a general-market lot. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: What 55+ homes CAN I buy in Surrey instead of building?** Fifteen verified strata complexes city-wide (five condo-form: Fleetwood Gardens, Carriage House, Semiahmoo Estates, the building at 13965 16 Ave, and Chelsea Gardens' apartment section; ten townhouse complexes), plus the Sullivan Mews membership complex in East Newton and three 55+ manufactured-home parks (Westwood Court, Westwood Estates, Newton Mobile Home Park). No verified 55+ strata complex exists in Guildford, City Centre or Whalley. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: If I buy land and build a rancher, can I make my own home "55+"?** A single freehold house has no strata corporation, so there is no age-bylaw mechanism for it. If the build is later strata-titled (for example a bare-land strata), only a 55+ bylaw is legal in BC, and it would restrict who may live there, not who may own. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I add a caregiver suite or a second dwelling on an urban lot?** Two sourced routes: a secondary suite inside the home — Surrey permits one per dwelling unit, at most 40% of the principal unit's habitable floor area, and never inside a coach house or garden suite — and the SSMUH added-unit framework, which allows 3 units on lots under 280 m², 4 units on larger lots and up to 6 on eligible lots within 400 m of a frequent bus stop. Which route a specific lot supports is a COSMOS and zoning check on that parcel. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **Q: How big a house can go on a reserve parcel, and can I add a caregiver dwelling?** A principal residence built without an Agricultural Land Commission application must be 500 m² or less in total floor area, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill for its construction; a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence. One additional detached residence is permitted without an application at 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller and 186 m² or less on larger parcels, where Surrey bylaws also permit it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The two-system split is the defining fact of this topic.** An urban lot's potential is set by its Bylaw No. 12000 zone and the SSMUH added-unit framework (3 units under 280 m², 4 units above it, up to 6 near a frequent bus stop), while a reserve parcel's potential is set by the Agricultural Land Commission's housing and subdivision rules (a 500 m² principal-residence cap, a 90 m² / 186 m² additional-residence limit, Commission permission to subdivide). The same search word — "land" — returns parcels whose buildable future differs by orders of magnitude. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **There is no 21900-era shortcut around the old plan documents.** The Surrey 2050 OCP (Bylaw No. 21900, adopted June 15, 2026) replaced Bylaw No. 18020, and older neighbourhood concept plan designations have been rescinded or superseded into it in areas across the city — a land document or listing that grounds a density claim in a superseded plan, or cites "OCP Bylaw 18020" as current, is citing a dead document. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/r133-plansurrey-2013-official-community-plan-surrey-2050-official-community-plan-alignment-related-amendments
- **Surrey's 55+ supply comes in four different legal forms, and none of them is land.** Strata (15 complexes), membership (Sullivan Mews — no strata lot, no conventional mortgage per its own listings), pad-rent manufactured homes (three parks — MHPTA, not strata law), and care/subsidized rental (Amica, Arbourside, Bethshan, PICS). A 55+ buyer on this page is choosing between building a single-level home on a general-market lot and buying into one of those four forms — the form decides the financing, the documents and the exit, before the floor plan matters. https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r3058951-15020-66a-avenue-49-surrey-bc-v3s-2a5/brochure and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
- **Surrey's 55+ strata supply is old, with one exception.** Fourteen of the fifteen verified complexes are 1986–2000 stock in the listings checked (Lakeview Estates 1986, Carriage House 1987, Charleston Green and Glenwood Village 1988, Manor Ridge 1989, the South Surrey corridor and Boundary Park complexes 1990s, The Crescent 2000) — Fleetwood Gardens, built 2005, is the only verified 55+ strata building in the city newer than 2000 in this research. That age profile is the practical reason a 55+ buyer ends up on a land page at all: the built stock rarely offers a new single-level home. Sources: Section 2 above.

An alert set on one name does not cover the others. https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/8670-156-street/9 and https://www.mhbo.com/browse/mobile_home_parks/bc/surrey
