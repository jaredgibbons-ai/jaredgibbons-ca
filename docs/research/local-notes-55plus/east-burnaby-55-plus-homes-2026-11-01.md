# East Burnaby — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- "East Burnaby" is a broad quadrant label rather than one small neighbourhood: it covers Burnaby's eastern side, from the Edmonds town centre and the Kingsway / Canada Way corridors east to the City of New Westminster border, taking in the Cariboo-Armstrong streets, the Second Street and Twelfth Avenue school neighbourhoods and the Robert Burnaby Park lands. The exact MLS sub-area boundary streets are UNVERIFIED — no City source checked publishes them, and several smaller sub-areas (Edmonds, Cariboo, The Crest and others) sit inside the geography a buyer would call "east Burnaby," so buildings documented under those sub-area names are cross-referenced here, not silently re-counted. Sources: the area research file (sources read 2026-11-01), and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/about-burnaby/town-centres
- Main roads: Canada Way, Kingsway, Edmonds Street and 10th Avenue run east–west; 6th Street, 2nd Street and Cariboo Road run north–south toward Lougheed Highway and New Westminster. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/ecosystem-restoration-robert-burnaby
- Schools: School District 41 (Burnaby) — 41 elementary and 8 secondary schools city-wide, no middle-school tier (K–7 / 8–12), address-based catchments. SD41 schools on the Province's list in or beside this broad area include Second Street Community Elementary (K–7), 7502 2nd St, Twelfth Avenue Elementary (K–7), 7622 12th Ave, Taylor Park Elementary (K–7), 7590 Mission Ave, and Cariboo Hill Secondary (8–12), 8580 16th Ave; which of these fall inside the MLS East Burnaby line, versus the neighbouring Edmonds and Cariboo sub-areas, is UNVERIFIED. https://burnabyschools.ca/about-sd41-overview/ and https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/041
- Parks and recreation: Robert Burnaby Park is the east side's big park — the City describes many creeks and streams running through it and over half its area as critical wildlife habitat, and lists its courts at 3 tennis and 10 pickleball, with a practice wall. Rosemary Brown Recreation Centre, 7789 18th Street, at the area's south side, is a year-round multi-sport facility with 2 NHL-sized rinks; Edmonds Community Centre, 7433 Edmonds Street, at the west edge, houses the Edmonds Centre for 55+ (recreation, not housing). https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/ecosystem-restoration-robert-burnaby and https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/outdoor-courts and https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/rosemary-brown-recreation-centre and https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/edmonds-community-centre
- Transit: Edmonds Station, on the Expo Line, serves the area's west side; route 116 runs between Metrotown Station and Edmonds Station via Griffiths Drive, 18th Avenue and Marine Way, serving the southern streets. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/about-burnaby/town-centres and https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt116.pdf
- Health: the Edmonds Urgent and Primary Care Centre is at #201-7315 Edmonds St, at the area's west side; the city's hospital is Burnaby Hospital, 3935 Kincaid St, north-west across the city. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/service-directory/locations/burnaby/edmonds-urgent-and-primary-care-centre and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Burnaby/burnaby-hospital
- Where the seniors housing actually is: in a band across the east side's southern half — the George Derby campus (Cumberland St / 16th Ave), the Canada Way retirement campus at Edmonds (7195 / 7197 Canada Way), and a string of BC Housing non-profit seniors buildings on the 11th–17th Avenue streets — plus the Edmonds town-centre cluster documented in the Edmonds BE notes. ALL of it is rental, care or subsidized housing. None of it is strata ownership (see Section 2).

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**No verified 55+ strata ownership complex was found in this sub-area.** East Burnaby's housing stock in the MLS row is detached-heavy (26 detached listings against only 5 condos and townhomes in September 2026, SnapStats GVR September 2026 report), and every verified seniors building in the broad quadrant is a rental residence, a care home or subsidized non-profit housing. A buyer cannot purchase in any of them — do not let a 55+ page imply otherwise.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **East Burnaby's 55+ rules are tenancy rules, not strata bylaws.** The verified 55+ buildings here are non-profit rentals: Derby Manor's 55+ test is the operator's rental eligibility for its 122 affordable non-profit units (monthly Occupancy Agreement, no lease), and New Sakura-so's "55 & older" note is a non-profit society's application rule in BC Housing's directory. Neither involves a Strata Property Act bylaw, a purchase, or a resale. https://derbymanor.ca/ and https://www.readkong.com/page/affordable-housing-bc-housing-9785076
- **Subsidized seniors housing here runs through The Housing Registry and the societies.** BC Housing's Zone 5 directory routes most Southeast-section applications through The Housing Registry, and Section 2 buildings (such as New Sakura-so) take applications directly from the non-profit society or co-op; the long-term care homes (George Derby, Chénchenstway, Harmony Court) are publicly subsidized beds accessed through Fraser Health, and the assisted-living residences are registered with BC's Assisted Living Registry — none of these routes involves a listing or a purchase. https://www.readkong.com/page/affordable-housing-bc-housing-9785076 and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Services/ltc-al/long-term-care
- **Zoning bylaw — note the name change.** Research briefs for Burnaby sometimes still cite the "Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965". The City states the new **Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026** was adopted June 9, 2026, replaced the former Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965, and took effect July 1, 2026. Use the 2026 bylaw. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/bylaws/zoning-bylaw
- **R1 SSMUH zoning:** Burnaby consolidated its single- and two-family districts into the R1 Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing district in June 2024 — 3 units on lots up to 280 m², 4 units on larger lots, and 6 units on lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent-transit stop; multi-unit lots may be stratified. On East Burnaby's detached streets, that is the realistic new ground-oriented supply — none of it age-restricted. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **OCP and town centre plan: the Burnaby 2050 Official Community Plan** was adopted December 9, 2025, replaces the 1998 OCP, and takes precedence over older community-plan maps; the Edmonds Community Plan, for the town-centre part of the east side, was adopted in March 2025. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/official-community-plan and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-12-11/city-burnaby-adopts-burnaby-2050-official-community-plan
- **Property taxes:** Burnaby's 2026 property taxes were due Friday, July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date; the City's 2026–2030 Financial Plan approved a 2.9% tax increase (about $61 on the average home). The detached single-family water utility charge is $652.33, with a 5% discount if paid by the due date. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in East Burnaby?** None that any source verifies as an age-restricted strata. The quadrant's verified seniors buildings are rental and care: Derby Manor (55+ non-profit rental), Marigold Gardens (rental retirement residence), George Derby Centre, Harmony Court and Chénchenstway (long-term care), and the BC Housing subsidized buildings in Section 2. A buyer here is purchasing a regular house, townhome or condo with no age bylaw — the MLS row for this sub-area is detached-heavy (26 detached listings vs 5 attached in September 2026, SnapStats GVR September 2026 report). Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: What is Derby Manor, and how is it different from George Derby Centre?** They are two halves of one society's campus: Derby Manor (8601 16th Ave) is independent living — 122 affordable non-profit rental units for individuals 55+, built 2017, on a monthly Occupancy Agreement with no lease — while George Derby Centre (7550 Cumberland St) is the long-term care home, with 300 publicly subsidized beds under contract with Fraser Health. The George Derby Care Society owns and manages both. Neither can be bought. https://derbymanor.ca/ and https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2015MNGD0084-002065 and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-At-Location/3/A/george-derby-centre
- **Q: Is the Canada Way retirement campus still called Harmony Court?** Partly — and the names have split. The retirement-living residence at 7197 Canada Way is now Marigold Gardens by Optima Living; the long-term care centre at 7195 Canada Way is recorded in care directories as AgeCare Harmony Court, with 80 publicly funded beds, opened 1976. Whether the care centre has also been rebranded is UNCONFIRMED — check the current operator name when you enquire. https://marigoldgardensseniors.ca/ and https://goodcaring.ca/agecare-harmony-court-care-centre-burnaby/
- **Q: How do I get into the subsidized seniors buildings on the east side?** By application, not purchase: most of the BC Housing Zone 5 Southeast-section buildings (Byrne Creek Manor, Catherine Anne Court, Douglas Manor, Grace MacInnis Place, J.S. Woodsworth Court, Lions Kingsway Terrace) take applications through The Housing Registry, while New Sakura-so takes applications directly from the Nikkei Seniors Health Care and Housing Society and states its own "55 & older" rule. https://www.readkong.com/page/affordable-housing-bc-housing-9785076
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere in Burnaby?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. (Note this is the strata rule; a non-profit rental like Derby Manor sets its own occupancy terms — the operator states an additional monthly charge for a second person in a suite.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://derbymanor.ca/
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. There is no such building to buy in East Burnaby, but the rule is the same across Burnaby. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: Is there culturally specific seniors housing on the east side?** Yes: New Sakura-so (6677 Southoaks Crescent, "55 & older," 34 one-bedroom homes) and Nikkei Home (assisted living, 6680 Southoaks Crescent) are run by the Nikkei Seniors Health Care and Housing Society at Nikkei Place, the Japanese Canadian community's campus at Kingsway and Southoaks — New Sakura-so opened in 1998 and Nikkei Home next door in 2002, on land purchased with Japanese Canadian Redress Foundation money. https://www.readkong.com/page/affordable-housing-bc-housing-9785076 and https://centre.nikkeiplace.org/research/japanese-canadian-history-in-brief/
- **Q: Where is the hospital and urgent care?** The Edmonds Urgent and Primary Care Centre is at #201-7315 Edmonds St for urgent and primary care; the city's hospital is Burnaby Hospital, 3935 Kincaid St, north-west across the city — there is no hospital on the east side itself. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/service-directory/locations/burnaby/edmonds-urgent-and-primary-care-centre and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Burnaby/burnaby-hospital
- **Q: If I need care later, can I stay in East Burnaby?** This is the quadrant's real strength — as rental and publicly funded care, not ownership: independent 55+ rental at Derby Manor and subsidized rental through the Housing Registry buildings, a rental retirement residence at Marigold Gardens, assisted living at the Edmonds edge (Nikkei Home, Rosewood by Nikoniko, Liberty Place), and long-term care at George Derby Centre (300 beds), Chénchenstway (216 beds) and Harmony Court (80 publicly funded beds). Every step is by application or Fraser Health assessment. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-At-Location/3/A/george-derby-centre and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-At-Location/3/E/chenchenstway and https://goodcaring.ca/agecare-harmony-court-care-centre-burnaby/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The east side's flagship 55+ building has no lease.** Derby Manor's operator states residents sign a monthly Occupancy Agreement rather than a lease, the monthly fee is set and includes a basic package of services (renting without the package is not offered), and a second person in a suite carries an additional monthly charge. That is a tenancy structure closer to a retirement residence than to an ordinary apartment rental — read the Occupancy Agreement, not a standard lease, before comparing costs. https://derbymanor.ca/
- **Derby Manor was a provincial project before it was a building.** The Province's Dec 10, 2015 release announced construction financing of up to $29.3 million arranged by the BC government, on land valued at $3.5 million provided by the George Derby Care Society, with construction estimated to complete by spring 2017 — the operator confirms the building was built in 2017. The 55+ supply on the east side exists because a care society and the Province built it as affordable rental, which is why none of it ever came to market as strata. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2015MNGD0084-002065 and https://derbymanor.ca/
- **The New Vista care home changed its name, address and bed count in 2023.** Fraser Health states Chénchenstway, at 7550 Rosewood St, is a renovated 216-bed long-term care community that opened in October 2023, formerly the New Vista long-term care home, operated jointly by Fraser Health (106 beds) and Providence Health Care (110 beds); the name is a Squamish word meaning "to lift each other up," gifted by Squamish Nation Hereditary Chief Ian Campbell. Older directories and the Edmonds notes still show the home as New Vista at 7232 New Vista Place with 240 residents — use the Fraser Health record. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/capital-projects/projects/chenchenstway-long-term-care-community and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/news/2023/Oct/seniors-in-the-lower-mainland-to-receive-more-dignified-care-with-private-long-term-care-home-rooms
- **One Canada Way campus, three names in the records.** The campus at Edmonds and Canada Way appears as Harmony Court Estate (AgeCare retirement living, in the Burnaby Seniors Resource Guide 2025 at 7197 Canada Way), as AgeCare Harmony Court (the care centre at 7195 Canada Way in care directories), and now as Marigold Gardens by Optima Living (the operator's current site for 7197 Canada Way, also listed by Fraser Health). A search under only one name will miss the others — and the care centre's current operator name is UNCONFIRMED. https://www.bbyservices.ca/pdfs/BBY%20NW%20As%20We%20Age%202025.pdf and https://goodcaring.ca/agecare-harmony-court-care-centre-burnaby/ and https://marigoldgardensseniors.ca/
- **The Rosewood Street cluster is a full ladder on one street.** Douglas Manor (7530 Rosewood St, 51 subsidized one-bedroom homes) and Grace MacInnis Place (7531 Rosewood St, 24 homes) face each other, the Chénchenstway long-term care home is at 7550 Rosewood St, and Rosewood by Nikoniko assisted living is at 7868 Rosewood St — subsidized rental, assisted living and long-term care within a few blocks on a single residential street. https://www.readkong.com/page/affordable-housing-bc-housing-9785076 and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-At-Location/3/E/chenchenstway and https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ABurnaby&f%5B1%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASupportive%20Recovery&name=&address=&order=alr_residence_type&sort=asc
- **Robert Burnaby Park is mid-restoration, with closures.** The City's ecosystem restoration project in the park — removing invasive species and planting 3,000 new trees, in a park where over half the area is critical wildlife habitat — has some trails and areas closed during the work. Park-edge buyers get the restoration upside and the construction-period closures together. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/ecosystem-restoration-robert-burnaby
- **New rental supply is planned at the New Westminster border — but it is not seniors housing.** The Burnaby Housing Authority's rezoning application for 10th Avenue and Newcombe Street (204 affordable and market rental homes in two six-storey buildings, reported Oct 23, 2025) states no seniors designation; the City's release for the same announcement totals 387 new rental homes across the Authority's first two projects. Do not let a future "affordable rental" headline become a "55+" claim. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-10-23/burnaby-housing-authoritys-first-two-projects-will-create-387-new-rental
- **Burnaby replaced its entire zoning bylaw in 2026.** The Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026 (adopted June 9, 2026, in force July 1, 2026) repealed and replaced the Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965 — this matters here in a specific way: the Derby Manor rezoning bylaw (No. 13388) was an amendment to the 1965 bylaw, so older documents for the George Derby campus cite a bylaw that no longer exists. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite and https://search.heritageburnaby.ca/media/hpo/_Data/_Bylaws/Unrestricted/Bylaws/13000/13388.pdf
