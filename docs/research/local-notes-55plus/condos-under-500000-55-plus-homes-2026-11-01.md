## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT THIS HOME TYPE

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. This is why older directory lines recording "rentals not allowed" against buildings in Section 2 (for example Semiahmoo Estates, Parkway Estates and The Lexington in their sibling files) cannot be read as a current long-term rental ban. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **The $500,000 line is a federal down-payment tier boundary.** CMHC states that homes $500,000 or less require a minimum 5% down payment, while homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999 require 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000 — every home on this page sits in the simpler flat-5% tier. Homes $1,500,000 or more require a minimum 20% down payment and mortgage loan insurance is not available. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Mortgage loan insurance in this band:** CMHC states mortgage loan insurance is required when a buyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the purchase price, that it protects the lender rather than the buyer, and that premium rates range from 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount, payable in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **BC property transfer tax across this band:** PTT is payable on a purchase registered at the Land Title Office unless an exemption applies, based on fair market value on the day of registration, at 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000 and 2% on the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000 — so a purchase under $500,000 meets only those two tiers, and never the 3% tier or the further 2% on residential property value over $3,000,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **First time home buyers' program (BC Gov):** where all conditions are met, the purchaser is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price; a full exemption requires a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), a property of 0.5 hectares or smaller containing only residential improvements and used only as the buyer's principal residence, and a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000. On price alone, every home in this band is inside the full-exemption value range — the program's other conditions still have to be met. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Newly built home exemption (BC Gov):** effective April 1, 2024, the fair market value threshold for a full exemption is $1,100,000, with a partial exemption phasing out over the $50,000 above it and complete elimination at $1,150,000 for qualifying purchasers — on price alone, a qualifying newly built condo in this band is also inside that threshold. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Strata paperwork is where a condo in this band is actually priced.** The Province's strata buying guidance directs buyers to the Form B information certificate, the Form F, strata bylaws and rules, fees and special levies — age restrictions, pet rules and rental restrictions live in those documents, not in the listing price. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/first-time-owners
- **Manufactured-home pads and membership/co-op complexes follow their own law and documents, not strata law:** pad tenancies are governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, and a membership complex's financing and transfer rules come from its membership documents — neither the strata exemptions above nor a strata Form B answers an occupancy or financing question in those products. Sources: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/surrey-condos-under-800k-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md and ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/mission-homes-for-sale-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **FHSA and Home Buyers' Plan dollar limits: UNVERIFIED in these notes.** No dollar limit for either program is stated here, because neither was read off a live CRA page in this pass. Do not copy a limit from calculator or listing marketing copy.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Why does this page stop at $500,000 — is that just an asking-price filter?** It is a price filter, but the line also matches a federal financing tier: CMHC states homes $500,000 or less require a minimum 5% down payment, while homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999 require 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000. One dollar over the ceiling, the portion above $500,000 needs 10% down. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: How many condos under $500,000 are 55+ right now?** No sourced number exists. No source publishes a cross-region under-$500,000 condo count, SnapStats publishes no price-band cut and combines condos with townhomes in a single category, and no pagination count appeared in the live page text read for the research file — so no band count, share or median is stated in these notes. What can be stated is the building list in Section 2: a unit in one of those 54 named condo-form buildings is in a verified 55+ building; a unit anywhere else in the feed needs its own age check in its strata documents. Source: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/condos-under-500000.md (Section 2) and Section 2 above.
- **Q: Is a specific 55+ complex under $500,000 right now?** UNCONFIRMED in these notes by design — no source in this program states a current price for a specific complex, prices change, and the live feed is the only place to check a current asking price for a specific unit. No complex in Section 2 is claimed to be inside the band. https://jaredgibbons.ca/condos-under-500000.html
- **Q: Which areas does the under-$500K condo feed cover?** The communities confirmed in the visible feed are Langley City and White Rock (in the S Surrey White Rock community). The live page's editorial claims a wider scope — Surrey, Mission, Vancouver's outer neighbourhoods and beyond — which is UNVERIFIED as feed scope, because no card from those communities appeared in the page text read for the research file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/condos-under-500000.html
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ condo?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. Several buildings in Section 2 state the same point in their own listing words — for example Royal Court and Regency Park in Abbotsford and Peninsula Village in Surrey state only one owner / occupant must be 55+ — but the building's registered bylaw, read on the Form B, is the settling document. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy a condo in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. In a manufactured-home park or a membership complex, the equivalent question is answered by the park rules or membership documents, not the Strata Property Act. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out my unit, or run an Airbnb?** Long-term, yes in a strata: every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022, and the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption. Short-term rentals are different — a strata may still ban or limit them by bylaw, so check the building's own bylaws before counting on short-term income. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Besides the strata fee, can a 55+ condo building charge a second mandatory monthly amount?** At least one verified building in this band's form does: listing information for Mayfair Terrace in Port Coquitlam states a mandatory base service plan on top of strata fees — $715/mo for the first resident, $400 for a second (MLS R3179064, Oct 2026) — covering meals, housekeeping, emergency response, activities and transportation. Treat the figures as listing-stated and confirm the current amounts in the strata documents and service agreement. Source: ~/workspace/local-notes/port-coquitlam-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **Q: How much property transfer tax would I pay inside this band, and can a first-time buyer avoid it?** On the general tiers: 1% on the first $200,000 plus 2% on the portion from $200,000 to the purchase price — nothing in this band reaches the 3% tier or the further 2% over $3,000,000. A qualifying first-time buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 of the purchase price and, on price alone, every home in this band is inside the $835,000 full-exemption value range, subject to the program's other conditions; a qualifying newly built purchase is likewise inside its $1,100,000 full threshold on price alone. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: Are pets allowed in 55+ condos?** Building by building, with no region-wide rule: Carriage House in Surrey records no pets permitted; Fleetwood Gardens in Surrey records pets allowed with restrictions; Ivy Green in Chilliwack records no pets while Spadina Regency, ten civic numbers away on the same street, records pets permitted; The Lexington in Tsawwassen allows one pet but no dogs. Confirm the specific building's pet bylaw on the Form B. Sources: Section 2 above.

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **$500,000 is a financing line, not just a budget line.** Just under it, the minimum down payment is a flat 5% and, for a qualifying first-time buyer, the BC transfer-tax exemption covers the first $500,000 of price; one dollar over it, the down payment on the portion above $500,000 doubles to 10%. The same ceiling therefore does two jobs at once for the buyers this page serves. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **The visible feed is much narrower than the page's own editorial.** Three cards were visible in the page text read for the research file, in only two communities (Langley City and White Rock), while the editorial claims Surrey, Mission, Vancouver and beyond — and one of those three visible cards was itself a 55+ building, described in its listing text as a secure 55+ community with only one occupant required to be 55+, whose maintenance fee was stated to include electricity, gas and hot water. A buyer who assumes the feed is region-wide, or that no age-restricted stock sits in this band, is wrong on both counts on the feed's own evidence. https://jaredgibbons.ca/condos-under-500000.html
- **Form, not price, is why most 55+ stock never appears on this page.** Ten of Surrey's fifteen verified 55+ strata complexes are townhouse complexes, both of Mission's are townhouses, and Pitt Meadows' only one is rancher townhomes — a condos-only feed excludes all of them by type before price is ever applied. The same filter excludes every manufactured-home park, the Surrey membership complex (whose listings state mortgages are not possible) and every life-lease building. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **In the older 55+ condo buildings, the strata fee often buys utilities, not just maintenance.** Elmwood Manor's fee is recorded as covering electricity, heat and water; Fernwood Manor's and Alameda Court's as covering heat and hot water; Lakewood Gardens' as covering gas, heat and hot water; Parkway II's as including heat, hot water and electricity. Two condos at the same price in this band can therefore carry very different monthly totals, and a comparison that treats every strata fee as maintenance-only misleads. Sources: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/abbotsford-west-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md; ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/central-abbotsford-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md; ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/langley-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **Whole-community medians sit on both sides of the ceiling, and that proves nothing about the band.** Mission's combined Condos & Townhomes median was $430,000 in September 2026, under the ceiling, while Langley's was $611,000, S Surrey White Rock's $700,000 and Surrey's $512,944, all above it — community context only; SnapStats publishes no price-band cut and no condo-only split, so how much of any of those markets the band holds cannot be quantified and is not estimated here. Source: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/condos-under-500000.md (Section 2)

The civic address and the strata plan, not the name or the street, settle which building a listing is in. Sources: Section 2 above.
