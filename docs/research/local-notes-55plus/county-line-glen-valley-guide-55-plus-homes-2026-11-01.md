## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **There is no age bylaw on a freehold acreage house.** Strata age law only bites inside a strata corporation, and this sub-area has no verified strata stock (Section 2). A senior buying a detached acreage home here faces zoning and, where it applies, Agricultural Land Reserve rules — not a 55+ residency bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **If the same buyer instead buys 55+ strata elsewhere in the region, the provincial frame is this:** the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1/123.2, has allowed only 55-and-over residency bylaws since Nov 24, 2022 (Bill 44); every younger age bylaw (19+, 40+, 45+) became invalid that day. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **That bylaw restricts residing, not owning, wherever it applies:** a person under 55 may own a lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in it, and Strata Property Regulation 7.01 exempts legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and — since May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the resident's care, and adult children cared for before 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rentals, if a strata is involved elsewhere:** strata rental-restriction bylaws have been void province-wide since Nov 24, 2022 (short-term rental bylaws still allowed), and a tenant in a 55+ building must still meet the age bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Manufactured-home parks, if a park purchase is involved elsewhere:** tenancies fall under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act — the resident owns the home and rents the pad, pad rent may be raised only once in 12 months on at least three full months' written notice, capped at the provincial limit (2.3% for 2026) plus a proportional share of changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees. A park's age rule is a park rule, not a strata bylaw. No park is verified in this sub-area (Section 2). http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **The ALR frame is the first check on this sub-area's acreage stock.** Land in the Agricultural Land Reserve may generally have no more than one residence per parcel; a principal residence may be up to 500 m² total floor area without an ALC application; one additional detached residence may be permitted of up to 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less, or up to 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha; a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence and only where local bylaws allow it. Whether any specific parcel here is in the ALR is UNVERIFIED — parcel-specific on ALC mapping. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Township suites:** under Township of Langley Zoning Bylaw No. 2500, s.102 (as amended July 2025), a secondary suite may not exceed 120 m² maximum gross floor area, each principal dwelling unit may have up to one secondary suite, and on ALR lands a suite must comply with Agricultural Land Commission regulations. Source: Township bylaw, as recorded in the research program's rules-gaps pass and the batch-12 / batch-17 research files for this area.
- **⚠️ SOURCE CONFLICT, flagged and not resolved:** the Township's January 2025 secondary-suites guide states suites are NOT permitted in townhouses/rowhouses and describes suites as within detached single-family dwellings, while the current s.102 definition (July 2025) says a suite may be located in a townhouse, rowhouse or manorhome. The bylaw definition is the later source. For a detached acreage home the point is academic, but cite the bylaw, not the guide, where the two differ. Source: both Township documents, as recorded in the research files for this area.
- **Township coach houses:** in Zoning Bylaw No. 2500, s.102, a "coach house" is an accessory dwelling unit located only within the second storey of a detached garage; no size cap for a coach house appears in s.100 as read in the rules-gaps pass — a coach-house size cap is UNVERIFIED in this file. Source: Township bylaw, as recorded in the research files for this area.
- **Do not apply SSMUH math to these acreages.** The Township adopted Bylaw No. 6020 on November 18, 2024, allowing 3 units on lots of 280 m² or less and 4 units on lots between 280 m² and 4,050 m², subject to eligibility (inside the Urban Containment Boundary, connected to municipal water and sewer, no heritage designation, not in a Transit Oriented Area); lots over 4,050 m² are not eligible, and the parcels in this guide's feed exceed that ceiling by a wide margin. Source: Township bylaw, as recorded in the research files for this area.
- **Township short-term rentals:** the Township does not issue business licences for short-term rental accommodations; the permitted-use status of short-term rentals under Zoning Bylaw No. 2500 was not readable on a Township page in the rules-gaps pass — short-term rental status in the Township is UNVERIFIED beyond the licensing point. Source: Township licence-application page, as recorded in the research files for this area.

- **Q: So how does a senior actually live in Glen Valley?** In a regular freehold acreage house, on the sources checked — the guide's own stock is ranchers and farm homes on multi-acre parcels, including a log rancher on just under 5 acres. There is no strata age bylaw on a detached parcel, so there is also nothing to qualify for — and nothing restricting who can buy the home from you later. The seniors-specific questions here are zoning questions: a suite for a caregiver or family member, and a second dwelling where the rules allow one. Sources: Sections 2 and 3 above.
- **Q: Can an acreage here have a second home or a suite for family or a caregiver?** Parcel by parcel. On ALR land, the ALC frame allows one principal residence up to 500 m² plus at most one additional detached residence of 90 m² (parcels 40 ha or less) or 186 m² (over 40 ha), with a suite only inside the principal residence; under Township Zoning Bylaw No. 2500 a secondary suite is capped at 120 m² gross floor area, one per principal unit. Confirm the parcel's ALR status and permits before valuing any second dwelling. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: A listing mentions a legal loft over a barn, and another mentions approved plans for a second home. Can I rely on that?** No — treat both as document claims, not rights. The guide feed itself contains the cautionary version: a court-ordered sale card disclosing a suite the Township had decommissioned. Verify approved plans, suite legality and any second-dwelling status against Township records and the ALC frame before subjects come off. https://jaredgibbons.ca/county-line-glen-valley-guide.html
- **Q: Does provincial small-scale multi-unit housing law let me add units to a Glen Valley acreage?** Generally no, on size alone: Township SSMUH eligibility under Bylaw No. 6020 ends at 4,050 m² and requires Urban Containment Boundary land on municipal water and sewer.
