## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT THIS HOME TYPE

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it, and Strata Property Regulation 7.01 exempts legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children cared for before 19. Rental-restriction bylaws have been void since Nov 24, 2022, while short-term restrictions remain allowed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **BC property transfer tax — the base calculation:** PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office, at 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **BC first time home buyers' program — the conditions that decide the 55+ question:** the buyer must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident; must have lived in BC for at least a year immediately before registration, or filed at least 2 income tax returns as a BC resident in the last 6 taxation years; must never have owned a registered interest in a property that was the buyer's principal residence anywhere in the world; and must never have received the exemption or refund before. For a full exemption the property must only be used as the buyer's principal residence, have a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), be 0.5 hectares or smaller, and contain only residential improvements; the purchaser is then exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price. A partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000, and where only some purchasers qualify, only the qualifying purchasers' percentage of interest is eligible. The buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and occupy the home as a principal residence to the first anniversary of registration to keep the full exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **BC newly built home exemption — the one purchase exemption a repeat buyer CAN reach:** it reduces or eliminates PTT on qualifying purchases of a principal residence regardless of first-time status as published; effective April 1, 2024, the fair market value threshold for a full exemption is $1,100,000, with a partial exemption phasing out over the $50,000 range above it and complete elimination at $1,150,000. A newly built home includes a unit in a newly built condo building not occupied since construction, and a manufactured home placed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since placement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **FTHB GST/HST rebate — VERIFIED from live CRA pages:** Bill C-4, which introduced the rebate, received royal assent on March 13, 2026. Together with the GST/HST new housing rebate it provides 100% of the GST (or the federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more — a maximum of up to $50,000. Eligibility turns on a builder agreement entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031 (owner-built construction beginning on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031), construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036, and the buyer being at least 18, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and a first-time buyer under the four-calendar-year look-back. Claiming is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction, by builder credit at closing or directly to the CRA (Form GST190; Form GST191 for owner-built homes). Any straight-line formula for the reduction between $1 million and $1.5 million is UNVERIFIED — the CRA publication says only that the rebate is "gradually reduced." https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
- **New housing rebate (the pre-existing rebate):** generally 36% of the GST paid, to a maximum rebate of $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and not available at $450,000 or more. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **FHSA:** the CRA describes the First Home Savings Account as a registered plan letting a first-time home buyer save to buy or build a qualifying first home tax-free up to certain limits; FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000, and contributions are generally deductible. The FHSA lifetime limit as stated on the live page is UNVERIFIED in the research file, because the lifetime limit was not stated on the CRA main page read in the sibling passes — no lifetime figure is adopted here. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **FHSA + HBP together:** the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. The Home Buyers' Plan figures as stated on the live page (a $60,000 withdrawal limit and its repayment-start years) are UNVERIFIED: the CRA's Home Buyers' Plan page returned HTTP 403 in the sibling passes, and no HBP figure is adopted anywhere in this file. The first-time home buyer's tax credit figures as stated on the live page are likewise UNVERIFIED as figures. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html and ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md
- **Down payment and mortgage insurance (CMHC):** mortgage loan insurance is required when a down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more (where insurance is not available); CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount, and the premium can be paid in full or added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Home owner grant is not closing money:** the grant reduces annual property tax on a principal residence and must be applied for each year; as published, the regular grant amount is $570 effective January 1, 2027 and the 2026 full-grant threshold is $2,075,000 assessed value. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I am 58 and buying my first home — a 55+ strata. Do I get the first-time exemption?** Age is not one of the program's conditions. On the conditions as published, what decides it is that you are a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, meet the BC residence test, have never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world, and have never received the exemption before — and that the property is $835,000 or less in fair market value, 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as your principal residence. A genuine first-time buyer at 58 who meets all of that is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: I owned a house 20 years ago, sold it, and have rented since. Am I a "first-time buyer" again for the BC program?** Not on the wording as published: the condition is that the buyer must never have owned a registered interest in a property that was the buyer's principal residence anywhere in the world at any time. Prior ownership, however long ago, is stated as disqualifying for the BC program — confirm your specific facts with your legal professional before budgeting any exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: How much GST can a first-time buyer get back on a new home in BC?** Up to $50,000: together with the new housing rebate, the FTHB GST/HST rebate returns 100% of the GST on a new or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more, for builder agreements entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. Bill C-4 received royal assent on March 13, 2026. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Does that GST rebate help me buy a resale 55+ strata unit?** No source read for this file states a GST rebate for a resale purchase: both federal rebates are written around new housing — the new housing rebate (36% of the GST paid, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) concerns GST paid on a new or substantially renovated house, and the FTHB rebate concerns newly constructed or substantially renovated homes. Almost all of the market's verified 55+ stock in Section 2 is resale, so a first-time buyer of a NEW 55+ unit is the buyer these rebates are written for. Confirm the GST treatment of a specific new unit with the builder and your legal professional. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: I am downsizing into a NEW 55+ unit. Is there any exemption left for me?** Potentially the newly built home exemption, which is not a first-time program: full exemption threshold $1,100,000, phasing out to complete elimination at $1,150,000, for a qualifying principal-residence purchase by a Canadian citizen or permanent resident on 0.5 hectares or smaller — including a unit in a newly built condo building not occupied since construction. Whether any given new 55+ project qualifies is a property-by-property check against those conditions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: My spouse has never owned, but I have. We are buying a 55+ unit together. Does any exemption apply?** Partly, on the BC program's own rule: if one or more purchasers do not qualify, only the percentage of interest that the qualifying first-time buyer(s) hold in the property is eligible. The property conditions ($835,000 or less, size, residential improvements, principal residence) still have to be met. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same 55+ purchase?** The CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal; FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000. The HBP withdrawal limit itself is UNVERIFIED in this file — the CRA HBP page failed to load in the sibling passes, and no figure is stated here from secondary sources. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Q: Does buying in a 55+ building change the property transfer tax itself?** No rule read for this file makes the tax or the exemptions turn on the age bylaw. The general tiers and the exemption conditions turn on fair market value, property size, use as a principal residence, and the buyer's own history and status — the 55+ bylaw governs who may live in the unit, which is a residency question, not a tax rate. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I am buying a manufactured home in a 55+ park with the program page's numbers. Do they apply?** Not directly, and this is the program page's sharpest edge for 55+ buyers: a park purchase is the home plus an assignment of a pad tenancy under the MHPTA, not a purchase of the land, and no official page read for this file states how the live tool treats a park purchase — UNCONFIRMED as a tool treatment. The newly built home exemption's own definition does include a manufactured home placed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since placement, which is a different fact pattern from buying an existing home in an existing park. Get the tax treatment of the specific park transaction from your legal professional. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption and ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/first-time-buyer-calculator-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **Q: What do I have to do AFTER closing to keep an exemption I claimed?** For the BC first-time program: move in within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy the home as your principal residence up to the first anniversary of the registration date, or part of the exemption may be repayable; if you qualified but did not apply at registration, the refund window runs from the first anniversary to 18 months after registration. The newly built home exemption carries the same move-in and first-year occupancy pattern. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **"First-time" means different things in the different programs, and a 55+ buyer can pass one test and fail another.** The BC program's test is never having owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world, ever. The FTHB GST rebate's test, per the CRA, is a first-time buyer under a four-calendar-year look-back. The FHSA is described by the CRA as a plan for a first-time home buyer, with its own eligibility conditions on its own pages. No source read for this file states that the definitions are identical — check each program on its own page rather than carrying one answer across all of them. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **The live program page's premium range conflicts with CMHC's, and its own worked example uses the page's range.** The page states the mortgage-insurance premium "runs from 2.80% to 4.00%" with a 0.20% surcharge over 25 years; CMHC's published range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount. The page's $700,000 worked example is carried in the research file as page content, with its premium leg flagged UNVERIFIED for exactly this reason. https://jaredgibbons.ca/first-time-home-buyer-program.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **The exemption thresholds were re-cut on April 1, 2024, and old advice is stale.** The BC first-time full-exemption value line is $835,000, with a partial band to under $860,000; the newly built threshold is $1,100,000 with elimination at $1,150,000. A 55+ buyer working from a relative's or a website's older figures will misprice the closing by the full exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **The one program with no first-time condition is the one that fits downsizers.** The newly built home exemption as published turns on the property being newly built, the buyer's citizenship/permanent residency, principal-residence use and the $1,100,000 / $1,150,000 thresholds — not on never having owned. For a 55+ buyer who has owned for decades, it is the only purchase exemption in this file that is even in play, and only on a new unit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Two federal figures are deliberately missing from this file, and that absence is the honest state of the research.** The FHSA lifetime contribution limit and the Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal limit could not be re-read on the CRA's detailed pages during the sibling research (the HBP page returned HTTP 403), so neither is stated here as a figure — secondary websites state figures for both, but this series does not publish a tax figure it has not read on the official page. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html and ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-17/first-time-home-buyer-program.md
- **The 0.5-hectare line is where acreage-seeking 55+ buyers lose the full exemption.** Both BC programs set 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) as the size line for a full exemption, with a partial exemption possible above it under the first-time program's stated conditions — a large-lot first purchase prices differently than a strata purchase of the same value, which is not obvious from the headline $835,000 figure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
