# Knight (Vancouver) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- "Knight" is the Knight Street corridor in Vancouver East — the major north–south arterial from the Clark Drive / Great Northern Way area south through the Kensington-Cedar Cottage, Sunset and Victoria-Fraserview local areas to the Knight Street Bridge over the Fraser River's north arm to Richmond. The City lists "Knight" as a recognised smaller area, but it is not one of the City's 22 distinct local areas. A block-by-block board mapping of how far east and west of the street the sub-area reaches is UNVERIFIED — that matters here, because every seniors building verified for this file sits on a side street east of Knight, not on the arterial itself. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/areas-of-the-city.aspx and https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/victoria-fraserview.aspx
- Knight Street is crossed by East Broadway, Kingsway, East 33rd Avenue, East 41st Avenue, East 49th Avenue and SE Marine Drive, and carries Highway 99 traffic south over the bridge to Richmond. It is one of the city's heaviest truck and commuter arterials. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/victoria-fraserview.aspx
- The corridor's civic health anchor is on the street itself: the South Community Health Centre, 6405 Knight Street at East 49th Avenue, which provides public and community health services for South Vancouver. It is a health centre, not a hospital and not housing. https://www.vch.ca/en/location-service/home-health-south-community-health-centre
- Schools in the local areas the corridor runs through, on the SD39 list: Sir Alexander Mackenzie Elementary, 960 East 39th Avenue, and Sir Richard McBride Elementary, 1300 East 29th Avenue (Kensington-Cedar Cottage); Henderson Elementary, 451 East 53rd Avenue, Moberly Elementary, 1000 East 59th Avenue, and Trudeau Elementary, 449 East 62nd Avenue (Sunset). Catchments are address-based; SD39 has no middle-school tier. https://vsb.bc.ca/list-of-vsb-schools-by-city-neighborhoods and https://vsb.bc.ca/catchment-boundaries
- Transit: the corridor is crossed by the R4 41st Ave RapidBus and the 41 at East 41st Avenue, the 49 at East 49th Avenue (Metrotown Station to UBC) and the 9 at East Broadway at the north end; no SkyTrain station sits on the corridor. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt049.pdf
- Where the seniors housing actually is: two side-street clusters east of the arterial. At the south end, three culturally founded care homes line Harrison Drive where it runs down to SE Marine Drive — the German-Canadian Care Home (2010 Harrison Drive), Icelandic Harbour (2020 Harrison Drive) and Finnish Home (2288 Harrison Drive). Further north, the Baptist Housing campus pairs Clarendon Court assisted living (6404 Clarendon Street) with Shannon Oaks independent living (2526 Waverley Avenue). Nothing seniors-specific was verified on Knight Street's own frontage. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 below.

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**No verified 55+ strata ownership complex was found on the Knight corridor.** All of the corridor's verified seniors housing is rental, assisted living or long-term care on the side streets east of the arterial — a stronger care ladder than most Vancouver areas have, with nothing on it that can be bought.

### Verified 55+ strata (ownership)

- None verified in this area.

### Verified 55+ rental (not ownership)

- None verified as a purpose-built market 55+ rental apartment building on the corridor. The independent-living rental that does exist — Shannon Oaks — is a serviced seniors residence and is listed under Retirement residences below.

### Retirement residences and care (rental/care — NOT strata ownership)

- **Shannon Oaks Vancouver — 2526 Waverley Avenue.** All-inclusive independent-living seniors residence operated by the non-profit Baptist Housing: private one-bedroom, one-bedroom-plus-den and two-bedroom suites with dining, housekeeping and activities, opened in 2001 next to Clarendon Court. Rental residence — nothing to buy. The operator does not state a 55+ age rule for this residence in the sources read, so its minimum age is UNCONFIRMED; it is a seniors residence by operator description, not a verified age-bylaw building. https://www.baptisthousing.org/communities/lower-mainland/shannon-oaks and https://www.baptisthousing.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/Corporate%20Brochure.pdf
- **Clarendon Court — 6404 Clarendon Street, V5S 4X9.** Assisted living, registered in BC's Assisted Living Registry (class SN), legal operator The Baptist Housing Society of B.C. Vancouver Coastal Health describes it as a four-storey wood-frame building completed in 2006, operated by Baptist Housing, providing assisted-living housing, hospitality and personal care services. Rental/care — admission runs through the operator and, for subsidized suites, the health authority. https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location/clarendon-court and https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?bcgovtm=May5&field_residence_name_value=&field_residence_type_tid=41&f%5B0%5D=city%3AVancouver
- **Icelandic Harbour (Höfn) — 2020 Harrison Drive, V5P 2P6.** Assisted living, registered in BC's Assisted Living Registry (class SN), legal operator Icelandic Care Home Hofn Society. A Province backgrounder on the redevelopment of the site describes 77 one-bedroom assisted-living apartments built under the Independent Living BC program — two meals a day, weekly housekeeping and linen services, 24-hour emergency response, personal care services and social and recreational opportunities, with Vancouver Coastal Health funding personal care and part of the hospitality services. That backgrounder is the project's opening-era source (construction completion was scheduled for fall 2006); the current suite count is UNCONFIRMED. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?bcgovtm=Cowichan%20Valley%20Newsletter&field_residence_name_value=&field_residence_type_tid=40&name=&address=&order=alr_legal_operator_name&sort=asc&f%5B0%5D=city%3AVancouver and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2005-2009/2005for0105-000972-attachment1.htm
- **German-Canadian Care Home — 2010 Harrison Drive, V5P 2P6.** Long-term (complex) care home operated by the German-Canadian Benevolent Society of BC, with 24-hour professional nursing care, social work and pastoral care, rehabilitation and recreation, meals, housekeeping and laundry; room rates are set by Vancouver Coastal Health and are income-based. SOURCE CONFLICT, flagged: the operator's current site states a 132-bed home, while Vancouver Coastal Health's page describes a subsidized 129-bed complex care home — the bed count differs by source and is not resolved here. Care only — nothing to buy. https://www.gcch.ca/ and https://www.vch.ca/en/location-service/long-term-care-german-canadian-care-home
- **Finnish Home — 2288 Harrison Drive, V5P 2P6.** Long-term care home operated by the Finnish Canadian Rest Home association. Vancouver Coastal Health states it was rebuilt in 1995 and features 64 subsidized private rooms, 11 of them in a secure special care unit for residents with dementia, with common areas and dining areas on both floors. Care only — access is through the health authority's long-term care process. https://www.vch.ca/en/location/finnish-home/about and https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location-service/long-term-care-finnish-home

### Subsidized seniors rental (non-profit, income-based — not ownership)

- None verified as an independent-living rental building on the corridor. The subsidized seniors housing that does exist here is inside the care buildings above: Icelandic Harbour's Independent Living BC assisted-living suites (VCH-funded personal care and part hospitality), Finnish Home's 64 subsidized long-term care rooms, and the German-Canadian Care Home's VCH-set income-based room rates. There is no subsidized independent seniors apartment building verified at a corridor address. https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2005-2009/2005for0105-000972-attachment1.htm and https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location-service/long-term-care-finnish-home

A Knight Street address in the registry is not evidence of a 55+ building. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?bcgovtm=May5&field_residence_name_value=&field_residence_type_tid=41&f%5B0%5D=city%3AVancouver

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Assisted living and long-term care here are health-system products, not strata products.** Clarendon Court and Icelandic Harbour are registered under BC's Assisted Living Registry; the Harrison Drive care homes' subsidized beds and Finnish Home's subsidized rooms are allocated through Vancouver Coastal Health eligibility, and the German-Canadian Care Home's room rates are set by VCH and are income-based. None of that is governed by the Strata Property Act age-bylaw rules above. https://www.gcch.ca/ and https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location/clarendon-court
- **City of Vancouver Seniors Housing Strategy (approved July 23, 2024):** the City's first seniors housing strategy, with a Seniors Housing Rezoning Policy to enable more seniors housing across all neighbourhoods and a Resident Relocation Policy for seniors in community care and assisted living residences facing redevelopment. https://vancouver.ca/people-programs/seniors-housing-strategy.aspx
- **The City does not run seniors housing.** Council encourages supportive, assisted and affordable rental housing for seniors, but City staff do not manage the facilities or their rental processes; the City directs seniors to the Seniors Services Society and its Seniors Housing Directory of BC. https://vancouver.ca/people-programs/find-supportive-housing-for-seniors.aspx
- **Zoning splits frontage from side streets.** The residential side streets sit under the R1-1 District Schedule of Zoning and Development By-law No. 3575 (secondary suite and laneway house with a house; multiplex up to 6 units or up to 8 rental units; minimum site areas 306 m² to 557 m² by form). The district schedule for the Knight Street arterial frontage itself — commercial, mixed-use and industrial stretches — is UNVERIFIED. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/zoning/zoning-by-law-district-schedule-R1-1.pdf
- **Floodplain at the south end:** the City's designated floodplain includes the coastal floodplain along the Fraser River in South Vancouver, and land in it is subject to the Vancouver Building By-law Floodplain Standards and Requirements for setbacks and flood construction levels. Whether a specific corridor parcel is in the floodplain is UNVERIFIED until checked in the City dataset — relevant to the blocks below SE Marine Drive, where the Harrison Drive care cluster sits. https://opendata.vancouver.ca/explore/dataset/designated-floodplain/information/
- **Tax calendar:** City of Vancouver 2026 advance taxes were due February 3, 2026 and main taxes July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty on the unpaid balance after each due date; owners must also file the annual Empty Homes Tax declaration — 3% of assessed taxable value for homes declared or deemed vacant for the 2025 reference year. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/deadlines-and-penalties.aspx and https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/empty-homes-tax.aspx

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy on the Knight corridor?** None that any source verifies. There is no age-restricted strata complex on the corridor; its seniors housing is a Baptist Housing independent-living and assisted-living campus and a Harrison Drive cluster of assisted living and long-term care, all rental or care. A 55+ buyer here is buying a regular side-street house or frontage strata unit with no age bylaw. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Can I buy into Shannon Oaks or Clarendon Court?** No. Both are Baptist Housing seniors residences on the same campus east of Knight — Shannon Oaks (2526 Waverley Avenue) is all-inclusive independent living and Clarendon Court (6404 Clarendon Street) is registered assisted living. You rent and receive services; there is no strata lot to purchase in either. https://www.baptisthousing.org/communities/lower-mainland/shannon-oaks and https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location/clarendon-court
- **Q: What are the three care homes on Harrison Drive?** A cultural-society cluster at the corridor's south end: the German-Canadian Care Home (2010 Harrison Drive, complex care, operator-stated 132 beds), Icelandic Harbour (2020 Harrison Drive, registered assisted living, 77 one-bedroom Independent Living BC apartments in the Province's opening-era description) and Finnish Home (2288 Harrison Drive, long-term care, 64 subsidized private rooms including an 11-room secure dementia unit). All three are rental/care, allocated through their operators and Vancouver Coastal Health — none is a purchase. https://www.gcch.ca/ and https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location-service/long-term-care-finnish-home
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere in Vancouver?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Can a corridor side-street lot hold a suite, a laneway house or a multiplex for family or a caregiver?** Yes, where the R1-1 schedule is met — a single detached house may include a secondary suite and a laneway house, and a multiplex may have up to 6 units or up to 8 rental units, with minimum site areas from 306 m² to 557 m² by form. Knight Street frontage parcels sit under their own commercial / mixed-use districts, so do not price frontage land on the R1-1 math. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/zoning/zoning-by-law-district-schedule-R1-1.pdf
- **Q: Is the South Community Health Centre on Knight Street a hospital?** No. It is a Vancouver Coastal Health community health centre at 6405 Knight Street providing public and community health services for South Vancouver. There is no hospital on the corridor in the sources read. https://www.vch.ca/en/location-service/home-health-south-community-health-centre
- **Q: What transit can I use day to day?** Buses, including two strong east–west crossings: the R4 RapidBus at East 41st Avenue and the 49 at East 49th Avenue (Metrotown Station to UBC), plus the 9 at East Broadway. There is no SkyTrain station on the corridor — the east–west buses are how corridor residents reach the SkyTrain network. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt049.pdf
- **Q: If I need help later, can I stay near the corridor?** This corridor has the fullest in-area ladder of the four areas in this batch: independent living (Shannon Oaks), assisted living (Clarendon Court, Icelandic Harbour), then long-term care (German-Canadian Care Home, Finnish Home) — all east of the arterial, all rental or health-authority-allocated, and none of it buyable. https://www.baptisthousing.org/communities/lower-mainland/shannon-oaks and https://www.gcch.ca/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Harrison Drive is a care-home street built by immigrant benevolent societies.** The German-Canadian Benevolent Society was founded in 1965 and built its care home on its present Harrison Drive site in 1969; the Icelandic society's Harrison Drive home opened in 1963 before being redeveloped as assisted living; the Finnish Home at the drive's south end was rebuilt in 1995. Three language-and-culture-founded homes ended up within a few blocks of each other at the foot of Knight Street — but all three now serve the general public through the provincial care system, not only their founding communities, on the sources read. https://www.gcch.ca/wp3/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/ResidentHandbook_rev-June2024.pdf and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2005-2009/2005for0105-000972-attachment1.htm
- **The German-Canadian Care Home is about to be rebuilt bigger.** The operator announced on May 28, 2025 that a new care home on the current site will accommodate 178 residents in individual rooms — 46 more than its current stated capacity — in 15 households of 12 residents each, each with a private room and ensuite. Expect construction disruption on Harrison Drive and a capacity change that will supersede both bed counts flagged in Section 2. https://www.gcch.ca/
- **The Baptist Housing campus is the corridor's hidden seniors anchor.** Baptist Housing began at Clarendon Court — a secondary source on the society's 60th anniversary describes Clarendon Court launching in the Killarney area in 1964 — then added Shannon Oaks independent living next door in 2001, and redeveloped Clarendon Court into assisted living, completed in 2006 per Vancouver Coastal Health. One non-profit campus east of Knight quietly provides the corridor's only independent-living step. https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location/clarendon-court and https://www.baptisthousing.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/Corporate%20Brochure.pdf
- **Frontage and side-street homes are different products on this corridor.** Knight Street frontage lots carry arterial exposure — truck traffic, noise and corridor expectations — and price differently from side-street houses one block in; the underlying research file warns never to comp one against the other without an explicit frontage adjustment. Every seniors building in Section 2 sits on the quiet side streets, not the frontage. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/victoria-fraserview.aspx
- **The registry entry actually on Knight Street is a recovery residence.** Giving Back Support Recovery at 3608 Knight Street (class SU, substance-use supportive recovery) is the only Assisted Living Registry entry with a Knight Street address found in this research — a useful reminder that a registry hit on the street name proves a service exists there, not that it is seniors housing. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?bcgovtm=May5&field_residence_name_value=&field_residence_type_tid=41&f%5B0%5D=city%3AVancouver
