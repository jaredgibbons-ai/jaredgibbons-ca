# Buying a Chicken Farm in BC — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Scope note:** this is a province-wide topic file, not a neighbourhood file, and it states its 55+ position plainly up front: **there is no 55+ ownership stock in this topic.** No age-restricted strata, park or co-op product is a chicken farm, and no source checked for this file verifies any 55+ complex on a broiler-farm parcel. The substance below is the purchase framework a buyer of any age faces — the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) housing frame, BC Chicken Marketing Board quota as verified from the Board's own pages in this pass, BC Assessment farm classification, and what a farm purchase means for housing on the land. A 55+ buyer retiring into, or out of, a farm operation meets exactly these rules; there is no seniors variant of them.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES — THE TOPIC'S SCOPE AND JURISDICTION

- There is no neighbourhood here: the jurisdiction is British Columbia. Broiler chicken production in BC is a supply-managed commodity, and a key aspect of a supply-managed commodity, in the BC Chicken Marketing Board's own words, is reasonable access to quota holdings — quota is the means of production. https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/
- The live page this file supports is a buying guide, headed "Buying a Chicken Farm in BC: Quota, BCCMB Transfers & Barn Values" — quota explainer, three routes into the industry, transfer process, cost decomposition (quota / barns and equipment / land / residence) and barn diligence — with no listing feed on the page as read; it links onward to the site's chicken-farms and poultry-farms listing pages. https://jaredgibbons.ca/buying-a-chicken-farm-bc.html
- The governing frames that apply province-wide, from official sources: the Agricultural Land Commission's ALR housing frame, BC Assessment's farm classification regime, the Board's quota system under the BC Chicken Marketing Scheme, and the general property transfer tax tiers. Sources: the rule entries in Section 3 below. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- Where BC chicken production is concentrated, as an official-source statement: UNCONFIRMED in this file. The live guide's statement that the vast majority of BC poultry production sits in the Fraser Valley, with Abbotsford and Chilliwack holding the bulk, is site copy only — no Province agricultural-plan or land-use-inventory source stating it was opened for this file, and it is not adopted here. https://jaredgibbons.ca/buying-a-chicken-farm-bc.html

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**No named complexes are listed in this file, because there are none for this topic.** Verified 55+ OWNERSHIP complexes for this topic file: **0** — stated plainly, not padded. Section 2 instead records the assets a chicken-farm purchase actually combines, because that decomposition is what a buyer is really buying.

### The product types that intersect this topic — as asset types, not complexes

- **The complete operating broiler farm** — the live guide's own decomposition: four priced-together assets, quota, barns and equipment, ALR land, and the residence (often a family home, sometimes two, per the page). Only the land, buildings and residence are real property in the ordinary conveyancing sense; quota is a Board-controlled production right, dealt with in Section 3. https://jaredgibbons.ca/buying-a-chicken-farm-bc.html and https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/
- **Quota bought separately onto an approved site** — one of the guide's three routes in, alongside buying a complete farm and the new entrant program. The Board's verified position (Section 3) is that there is no quota exchange to buy from: transfers happen privately and the Board's role is to approve the transaction. https://jaredgibbons.ca/buying-a-chicken-farm-bc.html and https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/
- **The farm residence** — housing on ALR land is capped by the ALC frame in Section 3 (principal residence, suite only inside it, one additional residence at a size set by parcel area). A farm purchase is therefore also a constrained housing purchase: the residence the buyer imagines adding for family or retirement is limited before any local bylaw is read. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **55+ housing near farm country, for contrast only:** age-restricted strata and parks exist in the Fraser Valley cities this series covers in its city files, but none is on, or part of, a chicken-farm parcel in any source checked — UNCONFIRMED as any farm-parcel 55+ product, and none is carried into this file.

- Not applicable to this topic: a chicken-farm purchase is a farm-business and ALR-land purchase. No care, rental or subsidized seniors product is part of the transaction, and none is listed here rather than padded in.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT THIS HOME TYPE

- **ALR housing frame (ALC):** land in the ALR may generally have no more than one residence per parcel unless the parcel qualifies for an additional residence, and local or First Nation governments may further regulate or restrict residences through zoning. A principal residence is permitted without an ALC application at a maximum total floor area of 500 m², with no more than 1,000 m² of fill placed for its construction. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Suite rule (ALC):** a secondary suite is permitted in the ALR only when it is located within the principal residence — entirely contained within it, not in a separate or detached dwelling — and only where local bylaws permit secondary suites; a local government may further restrict or prohibit suites or limit their size. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Additional residence (ALC):** at most one additional residence is permitted, of up to 90 m² total floor area on parcels 40 ha or less, or up to 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. Building more than the permitted number, or larger than the size limits, requires ALC approval. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Subdivision on ALR land (ALC):** strata subdivision is prohibited on ALR land under the frame addressed in ALC Information Bulletin 09, s. 6, and subdivision of ALR land generally requires ALC approval — a farm parcel cannot be split into strata homesites, including a seniors homesite, on the buyer's plan alone. Position as settled in this program. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Quota — what the Board itself verifies:** Section 4.01(c.1) of the BC Chicken Marketing Scheme, 1961 gives the Board the power to establish, issue, permit transfer, revoke or reduce quotas, on terms the Board sets, and quotas confer no property interest and remain at all times within the exclusive control of the Board — as quoted in the Board's Quota Use and Access Consultation discussion paper. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf
- **Quota — there is no exchange, and the Board approves rather than sells:** the Board states there is currently no centralized platform (or "quota exchange") for growers to transfer chicken quota in BC, that quota is transferred privately through hatchery-processor-grower connections, advertising, or word-of-mouth, and that the Board's only role currently is to approve quota transactions, which ensures growers are in good standing and production is allocated appropriately for each growing cycle; the Board has never had a quota exchange. Verified on the Board's own pages in this pass. https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/ and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf
- **New entrant program — status as the Board states it:** the Board's January 2024 discussion paper records that the New Entrant Grower program has been closed to applications since 2016, that the Board consulted February 1 – April 30, 2024 on a renewed program, and that on June 18, 2024 the Board put the final policy and program decision for the new New Entrant program in abeyance until it completed the Quota Use and Access Consultation; the Board's September 2025 Policy Directions document records staff directed to develop a new merit-based program, with feedback on those directions invited until October 1, 2025. A buyer should therefore treat "the new entrant program" as a program under redesign, not an open application route, and confirm its live status with the Board. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/New-Entrant-Program-Discussion-Paper-January-2024.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/ and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Policy-Directions-for-Public-Feedback-Sep2025.pdf
- **Land and quota in the same name — a rule the Board says is changing:** the Board's January 2024 paper states that, at that date, all land and quota must be owned in the same name to be a licensed chicken grower, and that following a 2023 consultation the Board was working to allow leasing of land/barns for licensed chicken production, expected to be reflected in the General Orders by Spring 2024. Whether that leasing change is now in the General Orders in final form is UNCONFIRMED in this file — check the current General Orders before structuring a purchase around leased barns. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/New-Entrant-Program-Discussion-Paper-January-2024.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/
- **Small-lot production, as far as an official-adjacent source verifies it:** BC residents can apply to grow up to 2,000 chickens per year for farm direct marketing under a Board permit, and the Board provides permits to approximately 200 small-lot holders who grow 2,000 birds or less — stated on a page reproducing the Board's own description; the federal Canadian Chicken Marketing Levies Order separately does not apply to a BC producer who produces and markets no more than 200 chickens per year. The live guide's further small-lot details (a $20 annual fee, one permit per site, yearly placement and processing reports) were NOT re-verified on a Board page opened in this pass — UNCONFIRMED at that detail level; confirm the current permit terms with the Board. https://www.laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2002-35/section-2-20170928.html and https://jaredgibbons.ca/buying-a-chicken-farm-bc.html
- **BC Assessment farm classification:** farm classification is a voluntary program; the gross-income thresholds are $2,500 if the total area of the farm operation is between 0.8 ha and 4 ha, $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if the operation is greater than 4 ha, and $10,000 if the operation is less than 0.8 ha; applications must be submitted to BC Assessment by October 31 for consideration for the next assessment and tax year, and a buyer who plans to continue farming a farm-classified property must submit a General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Property transfer tax — general tiers only:** 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion above $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% on the portion above $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion of value above $3,000,000. How PTT and GST are apportioned between land, buildings, residence and quota on a specific chicken-farm purchase is UNVERIFIED in this file — no Province farm-transfer tax source was opened for it; model it with an agricultural lawyer and accountant before writing an offer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Strata age law — why it does not create a farm product:** the BC strata age regime (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 — only 55+ bylaws valid since Nov 24, 2022; residency restricted, not ownership; rental-restriction bylaws void since the same date) applies to strata corporations, and strata subdivision is prohibited on ALR land, so the two regimes do not combine into a 55+ farm community. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Still UNVERIFIED in this file, recorded plainly:** that quota never transfers automatically with a sale and that a share sale is still treated as a quota transaction, in the live guide's wording — the Board pages opened in this pass verify Board control and Board approval of transfers, not the guide's automatic-transfer or share-sale sentences, so those sentences stay UNVERIFIED here; any quota price or valuation basis; barn construction, ventilation, manure-storage, mortality, groundwater-licensing and biosecurity requirements as stated rules; the BC Premises Identification registration requirement as stated on the guide; and any avian-influenza effect on a purchase. No source stating those items was opened for this file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/buying-a-chicken-farm-bc.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is quota included when I buy a chicken farm?** Quota does not come from an exchange and does not move on a private timetable: the Board states quota transfers are facilitated privately through hatchery/processor and grower connections, and the Board's role is to approve quota transactions. Whether a specific sale includes quota is therefore a contract question plus a Board-approval question — read the contract for an express quota term and confirm the transfer with the Board before subjects are removed. The guide's stronger wording (quota never transfers automatically; a share sale is still a quota transaction) is UNVERIFIED in this file. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf and https://jaredgibbons.ca/buying-a-chicken-farm-bc.html
- **Q: Is quota property I own outright once I have it?** No, on the Scheme wording the Board quotes: the Board's quota terms "shall not confer any property interest in quotas, and such quotas shall remain at all times within the exclusive control of the board." Price a quota holding as a Board-controlled production right, not as land. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf
- **Q: Can I get into the industry through the new entrant program instead of buying a farm?** Not as an open application route on the Board's own record: the program has been closed to applications since 2016, the final decision on a renewed program was put in abeyance in June 2024 pending the quota-access consultation, and the Board's September 2025 directions describe a new merit-based program still being developed. Check the program's live status with the Board before planning an entry around it. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/New-Entrant-Program-Discussion-Paper-January-2024.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Policy-Directions-for-Public-Feedback-Sep2025.pdf
- **Q: What house can I have on the farm, and can I add a suite or a second home for family?** Under the ALC frame: one principal residence up to 500 m² (fill ≤1,000 m²), a secondary suite only within that principal residence, and at most one additional residence of up to 90 m² (parcels ≤40 ha) or 186 m² (>40 ha), with local bylaws possibly more restrictive. Anything larger or more numerous needs ALC approval. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Can I split off a homesite — or build a small 55+ enclave — on part of the farm?** Strata subdivision is prohibited on ALR land (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s. 6, as settled in this program), and subdivision generally requires ALC approval. A plan that depends on carving residential lots out of a farm parcel is not a plan the ALR frame supports. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: What income does the operation have to show to keep farm class, and what do I file after I buy?** BC Assessment's thresholds are $2,500 (operation of 0.8–4 ha), $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha (larger operations), or $10,000 (under 0.8 ha); a buyer continuing to farm must submit the General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase, and applications are due by October 31 for the next tax year. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Q: Can I raise a small flock without quota while I look for a farm?** Within limits stated by official-adjacent sources: the federal levies order does not apply to a BC producer producing and marketing no more than 200 chickens per year, and BC residents can apply for a Board permit to grow up to 2,000 chickens per year for farm direct marketing. The permit's current fee and reporting detail on the live guide ($20, yearly reports) is UNVERIFIED in this file — confirm current permit terms with the Board. https://www.laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2002-35/section-2-20170928.html and https://jaredgibbons.ca/buying-a-chicken-farm-bc.html
- **Q: Do I have to own the land and the quota in the same name?** That was the Board's stated rule at January 2024 — all land and quota owned in the same name to be a licensed grower — with a Board project underway to allow leasing of land/barns, expected in the General Orders by Spring 2024. Whether the leasing change is in force in its final form is UNCONFIRMED here; it changes how a purchase can be structured, so verify it in the current General Orders. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/New-Entrant-Program-Discussion-Paper-January-2024.pdf
- **Q: What does a chicken farm cost, and what is quota worth?** UNVERIFIED in this file, deliberately: no official source opened for this file publishes a BC chicken-farm price, a price range, or a quota price, and the live guide's statement that operating Fraser Valley poultry farms "generally trade in the multi-million dollar range" is site copy only, not adopted here. A valuation stands on the specific land, barns, residence and any quota included, checked part by part. https://jaredgibbons.ca/buying-a-chicken-farm-bc.html
- **Q: I am over 55 and buying the farm as my last operation / retirement property. Do any seniors rules change the purchase?** None that any source read for this file states: the ALR housing caps, the farm-class thresholds and deadlines, the Board's quota control and the general PTT tiers apply the same way at any age, and there is no 55+ ownership product, exemption or bylaw attached to a farm parcel in this file — UNCONFIRMED as any age-specific farm rule, because none was found. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The Board has never run a quota exchange — access is relational, and the Board knows it is a problem.** Its consultation was launched precisely because quota moves privately through hatchery-processor-grower connections, advertising or word-of-mouth, with about 10% of its 118 consultation survey respondents self-identifying as people seeking to enter the industry. A buyer without industry connections is not missing a website; the website does not exist. https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/
- **The new entrant file has been open far longer than a purchase timeline.** Closed to applications since 2016, consulted on in early 2024, put in abeyance in June 2024, and carried into September 2025 Policy Directions that direct staff to develop a new merit-based program including at least one non-transferable quota option — a buyer who waits for the program is waiting on a multi-year policy process, not a queue. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/New-Entrant-Program-Discussion-Paper-January-2024.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Policy-Directions-for-Public-Feedback-Sep2025.pdf
- **The residence rules were written to keep the farm farmable, and they bite on ordinary family plans.** A suite must live inside the principal residence, a detached cottage counts as the one additional residence and is capped at 90 m² on most parcels, and even the fill for an additional residence engages ALC authorization processes that a principal residence's fill (up to 1,000 m²) does not in the same way — check the current ALC guidance before costing a second dwelling. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class is kept year by year, not bought once.** The classification a seller holds does not simply ride through a sale in this file's sources: the buyer continuing to farm files their own General Application by October 31 after purchase, and the operation must keep meeting the income threshold for its size band. Miss the deadline or the threshold and the tax position the price assumed is gone. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **FLAG — live-page claims this file does not adopt:** the guide page states a "multi-million dollar range" for operating farms, a new-entrant status, small-lot fees and a Premises Identification requirement as current fact. Of those, this pass verified from the Board only the quota-system frame (Board control, no exchange, approval role, program closed since 2016 / decision in abeyance / redesign directed in 2025). The price range, the $20 small-lot fee detail and the Premises ID statement remain UNVERIFIED here and should not be republished from this file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/buying-a-chicken-farm-bc.html and https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/
- **The 55+ connection is a housing question beside the farm, not on it.** A buyer who wants the farm operation and a 55+ home is really making two purchases in two regimes — ALR farm rules for the operation, and the strata/park rules in this series' city files for the home — because strata subdivision is prohibited on ALR land and no 55+ product was verified on a farm parcel. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
