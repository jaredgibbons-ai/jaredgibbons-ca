# Glenbrooke North (New Westminster) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Glenbrooke North is a mainland residential neighbourhood in the City of New Westminster, north of Queen's Park, around Glenbrook Ravine, Jamieson Court and Park Crescent. The City spells the name "Glenbrooke North" and lists it among its Historic Neighbourhood Context Statements; SnapStats capitalises the B ("GlenBrooke") in its GVR report for the same sub-area. Precise OCP boundary roads: UNCONFIRMED — no City page read states a surveyed boundary for this sub-area. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-research-and-resources/sb_expander_articles/683.php
- Identity note that matters for buyers: Glenbrooke North is a real City-recognised neighbourhood name, but it is NOT a Heritage Conservation Area — the Queen's Park HCA adjoins it to the south and its permit rules do not extend into Glenbrooke North on any source read. Nearby areas: Queens Park to the south, Sapperton to the east across McBride Boulevard, Massey Victory Heights beyond Sapperton, and Downtown / Uptown to the south-west. https://www.newwestcity.ca/ocp/past-event-materials-and-council-reports
- Main streets: 8th Avenue, 8th Street and McBride Boulevard serve the area, linking south to Downtown and the Pattullo Bridge approach and north to East Columbia Street in Sapperton. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf

The city's main aquatic centre, the təməsew̓txʷ Aquatic and Community Centre, 65 East Sixth Avenue, is in Uptown. The nearest library branch is the Main Branch, New Westminster Public Library, 716 6th Avenue. http://www.newwestcity.ca/parks-and-recreation/parks/community-parks and https://www.newwestcity.ca/facilities and https://www.nwpl.ca/

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Zoning: Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001** (consolidated as of June 8, 2026 on the City's current consolidation) carries the area's districts: houses sit under the Single Detached Residential Districts (RS series, s.310) — RS-1 minimum site 557.40 sq. m. (6,000 sq. ft.), floor space ratio 0.5 — and the RM-1 multiple unit residential district (s.420) caps site coverage at 40% and density at 35 units per net acre / FSR 1.2 where it applies. Townhouse form elsewhere in the city is regulated under Townhouse Residential Districts RT-A and RT-B where designated. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Zoning_Bylaw_No_6680_2001_as_of_June_30_2022_(Complete).pdf and https://www.newwestcity.ca/zoning-bylaw/sb_expander_articles/1618.php
- **Secondary suites and laneway / carriage houses (the in-place options in a house neighbourhood):** the City's Secondary Suites guide (updated August 2025) states only one secondary suite is permitted per house, though a property may have both a secondary suite and a laneway or carriage house, and a suite must meet Zoning Bylaw s.190.29, the Building Code and the Tree Protection Bylaw. The RS-1 district's stated intent is to allow single detached dwellings, secondary suites, and laneway or carriage houses in residential neighbourhoods. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Secondary_Suite_Guide_August_2025.pdf and https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/300_One_and_Two_Unit_Residential___As_of_June_8_2026.pdf
- **Adaptable housing rule (the City's actual seniors-housing lever):** since a Zoning Bylaw amendment adopted June 28, 2011 (Bylaw No. 7464, 2011), a minimum of 40% of all single-storey dwelling units in multiple-unit residential buildings city-wide must be built as adaptable dwelling units under the BC Building Code — the City states the policy is a proactive means of planning for its aging population. Adaptable is not age-restricted: any buyer can purchase these units. http://newwestcity.ca/housing/adaptable-housing
- **Plans:** Our City 2041, the Official Community Plan (Bylaw No. 7925, 2017), governs the area; the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area adjoins it to the south but does not cover it. Glenbrook Ravine is a watercourse corridor in the City Green Zone; streamside setback detail for a specific lot: UNCONFIRMED. https://www.newwestcity.ca/ocp/past-event-materials-and-council-reports and https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/rte/2_4%20Parks%20and%20Open%20Space.pdf
- **Property taxes and utilities:** City of New Westminster 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on current taxes remaining unpaid on July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on any unpaid balance on September 3, 2026. New Westminster operates its own electrical utility alongside City water / sewer services. https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Glenbrooke North?** None that any source verifies. No strata in the sub-area carries a verified 55+ bylaw — the area is detached houses plus a small number of general-market strata units. A 55+ buyer here is buying a regular house or condo with no age bylaw; the city's verified seniors stock is in other sub-areas (Section 2). Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Is Glenbrooke North inside the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area?** No — the HCA covers the Queen's Park neighbourhood to the south; Glenbrooke North adjoins it, is documented in the City's Historic Neighbourhood Context Statements, and carries no HCA permit layer on the sources read. For an older owner that means exterior renovations here do not face the Heritage Alteration Permit review a Queens Park Protected property faces — confirm any specific address with the City. https://www.newwestcity.ca/ocp/past-event-materials-and-council-reports
- **Q: Can an older owner add a suite or a carriage house here instead of moving?** The RS-1 district's intent allows single detached dwellings with secondary suites and laneway or carriage houses; the City's suite guide allows one secondary suite per house, and a property may have both a suite and a laneway or carriage house (Zoning Bylaw s.190.29, Building Code and Tree Protection Bylaw compliance required). That is the in-place family / caregiver housing route in a sub-area with no 55+ strata to move into. The zone and setbacks of a specific lot — especially a Glenbrook Ravine–edge lot — are UNCONFIRMED parcel by parcel. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Secondary_Suite_Guide_August_2025.pdf
- **Q: What does "adaptable" mean in the newer buildings?** Under the City's 2011 zoning amendment, at least 40% of single-storey units in multiple-unit residential buildings must be built as adaptable dwelling units to BC Building Code standards — wider clearances and features that can be adjusted for limited mobility due to age, disability or illness. It is a construction standard, not an age restriction. http://newwestcity.ca/housing/adaptable-housing
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Royal Columbian Hospital, 330 East Columbia Street in Sapperton (Fraser Health) — east of the area, at Sapperton Station on the Expo Line. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/New-Westminster/royal-columbian-hospital
- **Q: What transit can I actually use day to day?** No station is in the sub-area; the nearest are Columbia and Sapperton Stations (Expo Line), with TransLink routes 123 and 102 serving the north mainland corridors. Unlike the downtown core, daily life here is car-or-bus, not station-based. https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/schedules-by-region/2024-schedules/september/burnaby-new-west-p155.pdf
- **Q: If I need help later, can I stay in Glenbrooke North?** At the long-term care step, yes — AgeCare Royal City (77 Jamieson Court, 165 licensed beds) is inside the sub-area, with funded admissions through a Fraser Health assessment. At the assisted-living and subsidized-rental steps, no: Thornebridge Gardens (649 8th Ave) is up the hill in Moody Park / Uptown, and the subsidized towers (Dunwood Place, Legion Manor, Ross Towers) are in Brow of the Hill, Uptown and the West End. https://www.agecare.ca/community/agecare-royalcity/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Draw the HCA line precisely in every Glenbrooke North file:** the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area adjoins the area to the south, but no source read extends its Heritage Alteration Permit rules into Glenbrooke North — do not let a buyer price in HCA constraints (or a seller market HCA "protection") without a City confirmation for the specific address. The combined Glenbrooke North / Queen's Park context work documents some of the city's oldest dwellings in this area — context documentation is not HCA protection. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Queens_Pk_NHood_Ctxt_Stmt_14_April_2009%281%29.pdf
- **The sub-area's biggest apartment building is the one most likely to be mis-filed as seniors housing:** Glenview Apartments (700 Park Crescent, 89 units) sits on the middle school's street and reads as an institutional, CMHC-era building — but the City's own inventory classes it "mixed," not "seniors specific," and the City classes exactly three buildings "seniors specific" at scale (Dunwood Place, Legion Manor, Ross Towers), all outside this sub-area. https://newwestcity.ca/database/rte/Housing%20web%20update%20-%20Non-Market%20details.pdf
- **Two official spellings, one sub-area:** the City writes "Glenbrooke North" in its heritage context statements; SnapStats writes "GlenBrooke North" in the board-side report. Same place — a search or a listing that uses only one spelling will miss records filed under the other. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-research-and-resources/sb_expander_articles/683.php
- **Thin trading is the pricing fact here:** 2 detached solds (12% ratio on 17 listings) and 2 strata solds (20% on 10 listings) in September 2026 is one month of thin evidence — anchor on the New Westminster community medians (detached $1,365,000; condos & townhomes $560,000, as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report) and widen the comparable search to Queens Park and Sapperton.
- **Ravine-edge lots carry a setback file interior lots do not:** Glenbrook Ravine is a watercourse corridor in the City Green Zone, and streamside setbacks that reduce the buildable area are lot-specific — check them before promising additions, a carriage house or a suite on a ravine-edge property. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/rte/2_4%20Parks%20and%20Open%20Space.pdf
- **New Westminster runs its own electrical utility** — unlike the BC Hydro service in neighbouring cities, a Glenbrooke North home's electrical costs run through the City's own utility, and New West taxes have two penalty dates (July 3 and September 3, 2026). https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php
