# Fraserview VE (Vancouver) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- This is Fraserview, **Vancouver East** — the residential district on Vancouver's south slope to the Fraser River, inside the Victoria-Fraserview local area, between the Knight Street Bridge corridor and Killarney. It is NOT Fraserview, New Westminster, and NOT the "Fraserview" care home in Richmond (both traps are documented in Section 2). Fraserview is not one of the City's 22 distinct local areas; the City publishes no street-by-street written boundary for the district, and exact board boundary roads are UNVERIFIED. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/victoria-fraserview.aspx and https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/areas-of-the-city.aspx
- A real-estate source frames the board sub-area as running from the Fraser River up to East 54th Avenue, bordered by Knight Street to the west and Vivian Drive and the Fraserview Golf Course to the east — a listing-industry framing, not a City boundary. https://www.bilashandcharron.com/listings/city-Vancouver+East/area-Fraserview+VE/page-1R2363682
- The Fraserview Golf Course lands are the district's defining green space; the City's Victoria-Fraserview page lists "Parks in Fraserview" and the golf course off-leash dog area among the local area's facilities. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/victoria-fraserview.aspx
- Victoria Drive and Fraser Street are the north–south spines at the district's edges, crossed by East 41st, East 49th and East 54th Avenues, with SE Marine Drive along the river slope below. The City describes the local area's Victoria Drive and Fraser Street (South Hill) strips as a destination for fresh produce, sarees, fabrics, imported goods and international cuisine. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/victoria-fraserview.aspx
- Schools (SD39; catchments are address-based): the VSB's schools-by-neighbourhood list returned no school rows under its Victoria-Fraserview heading in the version read for the area research, so the elementary schools physically inside the district are UNVERIFIED; John Oliver Secondary, 530 East 41st Avenue, sits at Fraser Street and East 41st Avenue at the district's north-west edge. Run every address in the VSB locator before marketing a catchment. https://vsb.bc.ca/list-of-vsb-schools-by-city-neighborhoods and https://vsb.bc.ca/catchment-boundaries
- Libraries: the Fraserview Branch and the South Hill Branch of Vancouver Public Library are the two branches the City lists for the Victoria-Fraserview local area; their civic addresses were not confirmed on VPL pages in the area research pass: UNVERIFIED. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/victoria-fraserview.aspx
- Recreation for the local area is the Killarney Community Centre, 6260 Killarney Street (indoor pool, ice rink, fitness centre), east of the district. https://vancouver.ca/parks-recreation-culture/killarney-community-centre.aspx
- Transit is bus only — no SkyTrain station sits inside Fraserview VE. Route 8 runs along the west side via Fraser Street, route 49 runs on East 49th Avenue, and route 100 runs on Marine Drive. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/stop/50849/schedule
- Where the seniors housing actually is: in two clusters. (1) One non-profit campus just north of East 49th Avenue on the Elliott Street / Clarendon Street / Waverley Avenue block — Baptist Housing's Shannon Oaks (independent living) and Clarendon Court (assisted living), side by side. (2) Standalone care and social-housing sites south of East 49th: the German Canadian Care Home at 2010 Harrison Drive, and the Kopernik Foundation seniors site at 3132–3150 Rosemont Drive in Fraserlands at the district's south edge. All of it is rental or care — there is no seniors product here that a buyer can purchase. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 below.

**No verified 55+ strata ownership complex was found in this sub-area.** Fraserview VE's verified seniors housing is one Baptist Housing rental/care campus, one long-term care home and one foundation-owned seniors rental/care site — all rental or care, nothing to buy.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **House-street zoning: R1-1 District Schedule of Zoning and Development By-law No. 3575** — minimum site area 306 m² for a single detached house or duplex and for a multiple dwelling of 3 to 4 units (minimum frontage 10.0 m), 464 m² for 5 units, 557 m² for 6 to 8 units; a single detached house may include a secondary suite and a laneway house, and a multiplex may have up to 6 units or up to 8 rental units. Parcel-level zone: UNVERIFIED. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/zoning/zoning-by-law-district-schedule-R1-1.pdf
- **The seniors campus exists because of a site-specific CD-1, not the standard zoning.** The Elliott/Clarendon/Waverley block was rezoned CD-1 in April 1961 (By-law No. 3914) limiting the use to CMHC-assisted rental dwellings for seniors and a personal care home; the 1997 draft amending provisions recommended a non-stratification covenant on the non-market portion and a housing agreement so the seniors rental dwellings would keep qualifying for assistance under BC's SAFER (Shelter Assistance For Elderly Renters) program. That is why Shannon Oaks and Clarendon Court are rental for life — the zoning framework was written that way decades before the current buildings. https://council.vancouver.ca/previous_years/970729/p1.htm
- **The City's own definition:** the Zoning and Development By-law defines congregate housing as a facility providing residential units "for six or more persons aged 55 or over" with shared kitchen and dining areas — the use category the Clarendon site's 1999 amendment used for seniors housing with meals, laundry, housekeeping and recreation but no care. https://council.vancouver.ca/previous_years/990727/p4.htm
- **Finding seniors housing runs through directories, not the City:** the City states it encourages supportive, assisted and affordable rental housing for seniors but does not manage the facilities or their rental processes, and points to the Seniors Services Society's Seniors Housing Directory of BC. https://vancouver.ca/people-programs/find-supportive-housing-for-seniors.aspx
- **Floodplain:** the district is on the south slope to the Fraser River; the City's designated floodplain includes the coastal floodplain along the Fraser River in South Vancouver, and land in it is subject to the Building By-law Floodplain Standards and Requirements (setbacks, flood construction levels) — this matters most for the lowest streets above the river. A specific parcel's status: UNVERIFIED. https://opendata.vancouver.ca/explore/dataset/designated-floodplain/information/
- **Tax calendar:** City of Vancouver 2026 advance taxes were due February 3, 2026 and main taxes July 3, 2026 (5% penalty on the unpaid balance after each date); the Empty Homes Tax is 3% of assessed taxable value for homes vacant for the 2025 reference year, with the 2025 payment due April 16, 2026. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/deadlines-and-penalties.aspx and https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/empty-homes-tax.aspx

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Fraserview VE?** None that any source verifies. There is no age-restricted strata building in the district; the verified seniors addresses (Shannon Oaks, Clarendon Court, the German Canadian Care Home, the Kopernik site) are independent-living rental, assisted living, long-term care and social housing. A 55+ buyer here buys a regular house, townhome or condo with no age bylaw. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Can I buy into Shannon Oaks or Clarendon Court?** No. Both are Baptist Housing rental communities on the same Waverley/Clarendon block — Shannon Oaks is all-inclusive independent living (opened 2001) and Clarendon Court is assisted living and independent living (opened 1964). You apply to rent; there is nothing to purchase, and the site's CD-1 framework was built around non-market seniors rental from 1961 onward. https://www.baptisthousing.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/Corporate%20Brochure.pdf and https://council.vancouver.ca/previous_years/970729/p1.htm
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere in Vancouver?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: How does assisted living at Clarendon Court work — is it the same as buying a condo with services?** No. Clarendon Court is a registered assisted-living residence (class SN) run by a non-profit housing society: residents rent a fully accessible suite and receive hospitality services and personal care as required. It is a housing-and-care tenancy under the assisted-living framework, not a strata purchase. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?bcgovtm=homepage&name=&address=&order=alr_title&sort=desc&f%5B0%5D=city%3AVancouver&page=1 and https://www.baptisthousing.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/Corporate%20Brochure.pdf
- **Q: Is the German Canadian Care Home only for German speakers?** The Society's founding vision was care with services available in the German language if residents wish, and the home is a general long-term care home in the provincial program (it joined the BC Long Term Care Program in 1978) — HealthLink BC lists it among Vancouver Coastal Health long-term care homes at 2010 Harrison Drive. Current admission criteria beyond the long-term care system: UNCONFIRMED. https://www.gcch.ca/wp3/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/ResidentHandbook_rev-June2024.pdf and https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/find-care/find-health-services/program/long-term-care-1?location_nid=18842
- **Q: What transit can I use day to day?** Buses: the 8 down Fraser Street, the 49 along East 49th Avenue (Metrotown to UBC) and the 100 along Marine Drive. There is no SkyTrain station inside the district — the pitch is the golf course, the slope and the Victoria Drive/Fraser Street strips, not rapid transit. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/stop/50849/schedule
- **Q: Is the Fraser River floodplain a factor?** At the lowest streets, yes — the designated floodplain covers the Fraser River frontage in South Vancouver, and parcels in it carry Building By-law setback and flood construction level requirements. Check the specific parcel in the City dataset before pricing. https://opendata.vancouver.ca/explore/dataset/designated-floodplain/information/
- **Q: If I need help later, can I stay in the district?** The in-district ladder is unusually complete for Vancouver East: independent living rental (Shannon Oaks), assisted living (Clarendon Court, same campus), then long-term care (German Canadian Care Home on Harrison Drive). What it lacks is any ownership rung — every step is rental or care. https://www.baptisthousing.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/Corporate%20Brochure.pdf and https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/find-care/find-health-services/program/long-term-care-1?location_nid=18842

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Baptist Housing started on this block in 1964.** Clarendon Court was the society's first community — a church-history account says it opened in 1964 with eight staff and 172 residents — and Shannon Oaks, its first independent-living community, followed in 2001 next door. The society now runs 21 communities across BC, but its entire operation traces to this one Fraserview campus. https://churchforvancouver.ca/baptist-housing-has-welcomed-seniors-for-60-years-now-has-21-communities/ and https://www.baptisthousing.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/Corporate%20Brochure.pdf
- **The campus has been seniors-only by zoning since 1961** — five years before the first buildings (seniors rental dwellings and the Waverley Manor boarding home) were developed in 1966–67 under CD-1 By-law No. 3914. The "new" seniors buildings buyers see are the third generation on a site that has never been anything else. https://council.vancouver.ca/previous_years/970729/p1.htm
- **There are three "Fraserviews" in the housing system and only one is this district:** Vancouver's Fraserview VE (this file), New Westminster's Fraserview sub-area (where the SnapStats rows live), and the Fraserview Retirement Community long-term care home in Richmond. Any search that just says "Fraserview" will mix all three. https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/location/fraserview-retirement-community
- **The German Canadian Care Home was an accreditation first.** The operator's handbook records that after joining the BC Long Term Care Program in 1978, the home in 1979 was the first long-term care home in British Columbia to receive the Accreditation award from Accreditation Canada — and it still operates from the Harrison Drive site it was built on in 1969. https://www.gcch.ca/wp3/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/ResidentHandbook_rev-June2024.pdf
- **More seniors social housing is approved on paper at the district's south edge.** The 2023 Kopernik Foundation rezoning on Rosemont Drive contemplates 94 new social housing units in a six-storey building beside the existing 33-unit seniors rental building and care facility — the largest seniors-housing pipeline item in the district, and easy to miss because the site carries no prominent operating name in the City report. https://council.vancouver.ca/20230530/documents/rr2.pdf
- **One campus, two civic addresses.** Anything addressed to the Baptist campus may show 6404 Clarendon Street (operator/Registry) or 6475 Elliott Street (City rezoning documents) — same block, different frontages. Title and permit searches need both. https://council.vancouver.ca/previous_years/990727/p4.htm
