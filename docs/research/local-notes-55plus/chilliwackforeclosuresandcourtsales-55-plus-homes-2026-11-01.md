# Chilliwack Foreclosures & Court-Ordered Sales — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Scope note: this is a foreclosure / court-sales topic page for the City of Chilliwack (slug: chilliwackforeclosuresandcourtsales, no hyphens), so these notes use city-wide Chilliwack framing. Section 2 covers Chilliwack's verified 55+ complexes city-wide; Sections 3 and 4 add the BC court-ordered sale process, which applies to a 55+ strata unit in exactly the same way as to any other mortgaged property. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw, and any tenant in a 55+ building must still satisfy the 55+ bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **A foreclosure starts by petition.** "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7 (1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished and all persons against whom any relief is sought must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner who registers a certificate of pending litigation binds later-registered interests — Rule 21-7 (4); and a petitioner who serves the petition must include the informational notice for foreclosure proceedings in Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **The court's powers at the first stage, and the redemption period.** In a Rule 21-7 proceeding the court may make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7 (5) (a); order that a respondent must, within a redemption period that the court may fix, pay to the petitioner what is due under the mortgage and for costs, and that in default of payment the respondent is to be foreclosed of the respondent's equity of redemption — Rule 21-7 (5) (b); and determine summarily, or order that an account be taken of and that a registrar certify, the amount due — Rule 21-7 (5) (c). The Rule text states no set length for the redemption period — it is whatever the court fixes. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rule text read in the Chilliwack pass — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5) (b). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What can the court order at the first stage of a Chilliwack foreclosure?** Under Rule 21-7 (5) the court may make a final order of foreclosure, order payment of what is due within a court-fixed redemption period with foreclosure in default, or determine or have certified the amount due. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer at the approval hearing?** UNVERIFIED as a stated process rule in this file — the bid-rights wording in Rule 13-5 and the PD-66 practice direction could not be opened in the Chilliwack research pass. Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for, and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing before spending on inspections. https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwackforeclosuresandcourtsales.html
- **Q: Does buying through the court change the 55+ age bylaw?** No source states any such effect. The strata's registered 55+ bylaw (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2) governs who may live in the unit regardless of how title was acquired — a buyer who cannot reside under the bylaw or an exemption should not assume a court purchase cures that. UNCONFIRMED that any exception exists; none is stated in the sources. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a court-bought 55+ unit?** Yes, if the purchase completes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a court-bought unit in a 55+ building?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Do I pay property transfer tax on a court-ordered purchase?** On the Province's general rules, the general PTT tiers in Section 3 apply to a purchase in this band of value; the exact tax position and any exemption on a specific court-ordered purchase are UNVERIFIED in this file — have your lawyer cost it on the specific property before you set your ceiling. GST treatment for a court-ordered purchase is also UNVERIFIED here — do not assume a court sale is GST-free or GST-taxable. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Which Chilliwack 55+ buildings could a court-ordered unit even be in?** Only the five verified strata complexes in Section 2 (Ivy Green and Spadina Regency on Spadina Avenue, Knight Lodge on Knight Road, Mountainview Estates on Yale Road, Cottage Grove on Young Road) are strata-title 55+ product a foreclosure proceeding can attach to in the ordinary way. Cottonwood Village homes sit on rented pads on First Nations leased land — a different tenure, reviewed under the park's lease and tenancy documents, not a strata Form B. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Why are there no market numbers on this page?** Chilliwack is a CADREB market that SnapStats does not cover, CADREB publishes statistics board-wide only with no foreclosure category, and no official source publishes Chilliwack foreclosure counts or discount-to-market statistics — so any count or "foreclosure discount" figure is UNVERIFIED here; a figure would require a Paragon pull. https://www.cadreb.com/market/

- **The board's labels name the same ground differently.** Both Spadina 55+ buildings are labelled "Chilliwack Downtown" in board data while a house on the same street is labelled "Chilliwack Proper West," and Knight Lodge is labelled "Sardis West Vedder" — a foreclosure feed filtered by sub-area label alone will miss real 55+ stock and surface stock that belongs to another page. https://www.zolo.ca/chilliwack-real-estate/45660-knight-road/203
- **A park home is not a strata lot in a foreclosure.** Cottonwood Village sells a lifetime-style park tenure in practice — you own the manufactured home, rent the pad, and the land is First Nations leased land with a listing-stated lease expiry in 2076 plus an option to extend — so the due-diligence documents are the head lease summary and park rules, not a Form B, depreciation report or strata minutes. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/index.php/listing/r3069101-50-7610-evans-road-chilliwack-bc-v2r-2y8
- **Chilliwack taxes have two penalty steps and a dyking add-on:** a 5% penalty on taxes outstanding after July 2, 2026 and an additional 5% after August 4, 2026, plus separate drainage and dyking levies on top of the municipal rates — unpaid tax steps are part of what a buyer inherits at completion if taxes are outstanding. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2522 and https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2527
