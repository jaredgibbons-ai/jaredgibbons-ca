# BC Landlord Tenant Resources — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Scope note:** this is a province-wide topic file, not a neighbourhood file. Its 55+ angle is three situations that come up late in life: renting in retirement, renting out a suite or a 55+ unit you own, and holding a manufactured-home pad tenancy in retirement. The rules core is the Residential Tenancy Act, the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and the Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB) dispute process. General information only — not legal advice, matching the disclaimer on the live resource page. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES — THE TOPIC'S SCOPE AND JURISDICTION

- There is no neighbourhood here: tenancy law in British Columbia is provincial. The Residential Tenancy Act and the RTB govern residential tenancies across BC, and the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA) separately governs a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on. Which Act applies turns on the form of the housing, not the municipality. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/rent-rtb/rent-increases and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- The live page this file supports is a utility guide, headed "BC Tenancy & Landlord Forms: RTB Guide," built as a form-by-form explainer (RTB-1 agreement, RTB-27 condition inspection, RTB-7 rent increase, RTB-32L/32P landlord's / purchaser's use, RTB-30, RTB-33, RTB-29, RTB-8 mutual agreement, RTB-47 forwarding address and the RTB-12 dispute series), with a closing FAQ and an RTB contact block — last reviewed October 2, 2026 on the page as read. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- Where the 55+ angle actually sits: in a rental building reserved for 55+ tenants (a human-rights exception, not a strata bylaw — see Section 3); in a strata unit in a 55+ corporation that the owner rents to a 55+ tenant (strata rental-restriction bylaws are void, but the age bylaw still governs who may reside — see Section 3); in a secondary suite in the owner's own home; and in a manufactured-home park pad tenancy. Sources: the rule entries in Section 3 below.
- RTB contact as stated on the live page: Lower Mainland 604-660-1020, Victoria 250-387-1602, elsewhere in BC 1-800-665-8779, email HSRTO@gov.bc.ca, hours Monday to Friday 9 am to 4 pm except statutory holidays (11 am to 4 pm on the last Wednesday of the month); RTB information officers cannot give legal advice. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- No province-wide map of 55+ rental buildings exists in the sources checked for this file — UNCONFIRMED as any geographic concentration statement. Named 55+ rental buildings belong in the city files of this series.

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**No 55+ ownership stock is listed in this file, and that is the honest position for this topic: tenancy resources describe a legal relationship, not a building you can buy.** Verified 55+ OWNERSHIP complexes for this topic file: **0**. Section 2 instead records the housing forms tenancy law actually governs, with the consequence of each for a 55+ reader.

### The housing forms tenancy law governs — as product types

- **A rental unit under the Residential Tenancy Act** — an apartment, a house, or a secondary suite rented to a tenant. This is the live page's whole subject: agreement, deposits, rent increases, repairs, ending a tenancy and disputes all run through the RTB forms and rules in Section 3. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **A 55+ rental building (rental only — nothing to buy).** BC's Human Rights Code, s. 10(2)(b), provides that the ban on age and family-status discrimination in tenancy does not apply, as it relates to age or family status, if the space is a rental unit in residential premises in which every rental unit is reserved for rental to a person who has reached 55 years of age, or to 2 or more persons at least one of whom has reached 55. That is the legal basis for an all-55+ rental building — a Code exception, not a strata bylaw and not an RTB rule. https://www.bchrt.bc.ca/law-library/leading-cases/tenancy/
- **A strata unit the owner rents out.** The unit itself may sit in a 55+ strata corporation (ownership is covered in the city files of this series), but the tenancy inside it is a Residential Tenancy Act tenancy between owner-landlord and tenant, and the RTB rules in Section 3 apply to that tenancy. The strata layer is separate: see the age-bylaw and rental-bylaw entries in Section 3. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **A manufactured home on a rented pad (own the home, rent the site — MHPTA, not the Residential Tenancy Act).** Pad rent, pad-tenancy assignment on a sale, and park closure are MHPTA matters. The settled pad-rent position is in Section 3; the full park-buying treatment is in this series' how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **A suite in the owner's own home, and a live-in caregiver arrangement.** Renting a secondary suite makes the owner a landlord under the Residential Tenancy Act as the live page frames tenancies generally. Whether a specific live-in caregiver arrangement is a tenancy, employment, or neither is fact-specific and no source read for this file states a general rule for it — UNCONFIRMED; do not assume the RTB forms settle a caregiver's occupancy.

### Retirement residences and care (rental/care — NOT strata ownership)

- Not a tenancy-law purchase topic: retirement residences and assisted living are rental/care, covered building-by-building in the city files of this series through BC's Assisted Living Registry. Whether a particular residence's occupancy agreement is a Residential Tenancy Act tenancy is a per-residence question — UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence

### Subsidized seniors rental (non-profit, income-based — not ownership)

- Not priced by this file: subsidized seniors housing runs through BC Housing's Housing Registry, income-based rental rather than a market tenancy a buyer prices from RTB rules. https://www.bchousing.org/housing-assistance/rental-housing/subsidized-housing

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Rent increases — Residential Tenancy Act:** the 2026 rent increase limit for residential tenancies is 2.3% and the 2027 limit is 2.2%; rent can be increased only once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice on form RTB-7, with no carry-forward and no rounding up; a larger increase requires an arbitrator application (RTB-52 on the live page's index) or the tenant's voluntary written agreement. Verified on the Province's rent increases page in this pass. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/rent-rtb/rent-increases
- **Pad rent increases — MHPTA, a different formula:** for a manufactured home site tenancy the limit is the same inflation figure **plus a proportional amount** for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees — 2.3% plus the proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus the proportional amount for 2027 — on 3 full months' notice, once every 12 months. Do not apply the flat residential cap to a pad. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes and https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026HMA0085-000982
- **MHPTA wording — settled verbatim position in this program:** where the MHPTA speaks of compensation on a park tenancy, the trigger recorded in this program is **"an order of the director"** (MHPTA s. 28(1)(b) compensation trigger). MHPTA rules beyond the pad-rent limits and that wording were not re-opened source-by-source for this file — UNCONFIRMED beyond those two positions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Deposits:** a security deposit is capped at half the first month's rent, and a pet damage deposit at the same cap no matter how many pets, with no pet damage deposit for a certified guide or service dog; a landlord cannot keep a deposit without the tenant's written permission or an RTB order; once the landlord has the tenant's forwarding address in writing (RTB-47), deposits must be returned with any interest within 15 days if there are no issues, and deposit interest for 2026 is stated on the live page as 0%. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Agreements and inspections:** a landlord must prepare a written tenancy agreement on the RTB-1 template or an equivalent containing all standard terms the Act requires, and give the tenant a copy within 21 days of signing; landlord and tenant inspect together at move-in and move-out on the Condition Inspection Report (RTB-27), the landlord must offer at least 2 inspection opportunities (RTB-22 if the tenant was unavailable) to keep the right to claim against a deposit, and must give the tenant the move-in report copy within 7 days. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Ending a tenancy — landlord's notices, as indexed on the live page:** RTB-30 (10 Day Notice for Unpaid Rent or Utilities — tenant has 5 days to pay or dispute); RTB-33 (One Month Notice for Cause — 10 days to dispute); RTB-32L / RTB-32P (Three Month Notice for landlord's use / purchaser's use — these must be generated through the RTB web portal and a non-generated notice is legally unenforceable; 21 days to dispute; one month's rent compensation; the person moving in must live in the unit at least 12 months; personal-occupancy notices are not allowed in some buildings with 5 or more rental units); RTB-29 (Four Month Notice for Demolition or Conversion — permits/approvals required, 30 days to dispute, one month's rent compensation). Eviction notices cannot be served by text message. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Ending a tenancy — tenant's side:** no RTB form is required for a tenant's own notice, which must be in writing and, for a month-to-month tenancy, be received at least one full rental month before the effective date and before the day rent is due; on form RTB-8 (Mutual Agreement to End a Tenancy) neither side can be required to sign, and a text message is not a legal way to end a tenancy by mutual agreement. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Repairs:** landlords are responsible for most repairs including heating, plumbing, common areas and normal wear and tear; tenants are responsible for minor upkeep and damage caused by them, their pets or their guests; emergency repairs (health/safety — major leaks, blocked sewers, primary heating, locks, electrical) can be arranged by the tenant at reasonable cost after 2 phone attempts and a reasonable wait to reach the landlord's emergency contact. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Dispute resolution:** the RTB processes named on the live page are direct request (written submissions only, narrow situations), facilitation, participatory hearing (binding arbitrator decision) and expedited hearing (the RTB tries to schedule within 12 days); monetary claims must generally be $35,000 or less, with a limited exception for certain 12-month compensation claims; applications are online with a Basic BCeID or on paper (RTB-12 series); tenants must keep paying rent during the process; orders are enforced through the courts — orders of possession through the Supreme Court of BC and monetary orders through Small Claims Court. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Buying or selling a tenanted property:** a landlord cannot end a tenancy just because they want to sell; on a sale the buyer becomes the new landlord and the tenancy continues on the same terms; showings and open houses need written notice to the tenant or the tenant's permission; the owner at the time the tenant moves out is responsible for returning the deposits, so buyer and seller should deal with deposits in their contract or at closing. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **55+ in rental housing — the Human Rights Code position, verified in this pass:** the Code, s. 10(1), bars denying tenancy or discriminating on a term of tenancy because of age or family status (among other grounds); s. 10(2)(b) then provides the subsection does not apply, as it relates to family status or age, if every rental unit in the residential premises is reserved for rental to a person who has reached 55, or to 2 or more persons at least one of whom has reached 55, or the unit is in a prescribed class of residential premises. The Province's Age Discrimination fact sheet puts it in plain terms: generally landlords can't refuse to rent because of age, and a landlord can restrict rentals to people 55 and older in an all-seniors building. https://www.bchrt.bc.ca/law-library/leading-cases/tenancy/ and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/law-crime-and-justice/human-rights/human-rights-protection/age-discrimination.pdf
- **55+ in strata — a separate regime, do not blend it with rental law:** under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw below 55 has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022. The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it. Exemptions in Strata Property Regulation 7.01 include legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Strata rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide:** as of Nov 24, 2022 no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. That is why an owner can rent out a 55+ unit at all; the tenant must still satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Form currency flag:** the RTB updates its forms, and the live page directs readers to download the current version from the official RTB forms page. Whether every form number and version described on the live page is still the current published version is UNCONFIRMED in this file — it is a per-form check at rebuild time. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I am retired and renting. How much can my rent go up?** For a residential tenancy, at most 2.3% for an increase taking effect in 2026 and 2.2% in 2027, only once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' written notice on form RTB-7 — an increase above the limit or without proper notice does not have to be paid as stated on the live page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/rent-rtb/rent-increases and https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Q: Is a 55+ rental building legal, or is that age discrimination?** It is provided for in the Human Rights Code itself: s. 10(2)(b) takes a building out of the age/family-status tenancy ban where every rental unit is reserved for a person who has reached 55, or for 2 or more persons at least one of whom has reached 55. The building must be all-55+ in that sense — the exception is written for premises where every unit is so reserved. https://www.bchrt.bc.ca/law-library/leading-cases/tenancy/
- **Q: My partner is under 55. Can we rent together in a 55+ rental building?** On the Code wording, yes in the form the exception contemplates: a unit reserved for 2 or more persons, at least one of whom has reached 55. That is a rental-housing rule; do not carry the strata spouse exemption across — a 55+ strata has its own exemption list in Regulation 7.01. https://www.bchrt.bc.ca/law-library/leading-cases/tenancy/ and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I own a 55+ strata unit. Can I rent it out, and to whom?** Renting it out is not barred by a strata rental bylaw — every residential rental-restriction bylaw in BC strata became invalid on Nov 24, 2022 (short-term rentals can still be restricted). The tenancy itself runs under the Residential Tenancy Act, and the tenant still has to meet the corporation's 55+ age bylaw or hold an exemption, because that bylaw governs who may reside. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: I am renting out the suite in my house. What do I owe a tenant at the start?** A written agreement on RTB-1 or an equivalent with all standard terms, a copy to the tenant within 21 days of signing, a joint move-in condition inspection on RTB-27 with at least 2 inspection opportunities offered, and deposits capped at half the first month's rent each for security and (if pets are allowed) pet damage. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Q: When do I get my deposit back after I move out?** Once the landlord has your forwarding address in writing (RTB-47), the deposits with any interest must be returned within 15 days if there are no issues; a landlord cannot keep any of a deposit without your written permission or an RTB order, and failing return the tenant can apply through the direct request process and the landlord may be ordered to pay double, as the live page states it. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Q: I am buying a tenanted property to live in. Can the tenant be required to leave?** Not because of the sale alone — the tenancy continues and you become the landlord. Only once all conditions of sale are met can a purchaser who genuinely intends to live in the unit (or have a close family member live there, for at least 12 months) ask the seller in writing to give a Three Month Notice on RTB-32P, generated through the RTB web portal, with one month's rent compensation. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Q: My home is in a manufactured-home park. Does the 2.3% residential cap cap my pad rent?** Not on its own: a pad tenancy is under the MHPTA, whose limit is 2.3% **plus a proportional amount** for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees for 2026 (2.2% plus the proportional amount for 2027), on 3 full months' notice once every 12 months. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: We disagree about a deposit / a notice / repairs. Where does that get decided?** Through the RTB dispute process the live page indexes — direct request, facilitation, participatory hearing or expedited hearing — with applications online (Basic BCeID) or on paper in the RTB-12 series, rent kept paid during the process, and orders enforced through the courts. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **Q: Can a landlord end my tenancy by text message, or make me sign a mutual agreement?** No on both as the live page states them: eviction notices cannot be served by text message, a text is not a legal way to end a tenancy by mutual agreement, and on RTB-8 neither side can be required to sign. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **There are three different 55+ mechanisms in BC housing, and they are not interchangeable.** An all-55+ rental building rests on Human Rights Code s. 10(2)(b); a 55+ strata rests on Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 plus Regulation 7.01 exemptions; a 55+ manufactured-home park rests on a park designation under the MHPTA framework. A rule from one — the strata spouse exemption, for example — does not automatically apply in another. https://www.bchrt.bc.ca/law-library/leading-cases/tenancy/ and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The personal-use notice trap is a form-generation trap.** RTB-32L and RTB-32P must be generated through the RTB web portal, and the RTB position carried on the live page is that a notice other than a generated one is legally unenforceable — a downloaded PDF that looks identical is not the same instrument. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **The rent cap follows the effective date, not the serve date, and unused room does not carry forward.** The Province's page states landlords can't round up and can only use the current year's limit; an increase taking effect in 2027 is a 2.2% increase even if the RTB-7 was served in 2026. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/rent-rtb/rent-increases
- **Deposit interest is a year-by-year item, and only the current year is stated here.** The live page states the 2026 deposit interest rate as 0% and points to the RTB's Deposit Interest Calculator for earlier years; a year-by-year table for years before 2026 was not opened for this file — UNVERIFIED at that level, so a long tenancy's interest is a calculator check, not a guess. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **A sale does not reset a tenancy, and it does not reset the rent clock either.** The buyer becomes landlord on the same terms, showings need written notice or the tenant's permission, and the deposit obligation lands on whoever owns the property when the tenant moves out — which is why the live page's advice is to settle deposits in the contract, not at closing. https://jaredgibbons.ca/bc-landlord-tenant-resources.html
- **The research-file gap carried forward honestly:** the underlying research for this topic verified the Residential Tenancy Act side from the live page and its cited RTB sources, and the MHPTA side only to the pad-rent limits and the settled "an order of the director" wording. MHPTA rules beyond those two positions are UNVERIFIED in this file — do not publish a park-tenancy rule from this file alone; the park-buying file in this series is the MHPTA source file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
