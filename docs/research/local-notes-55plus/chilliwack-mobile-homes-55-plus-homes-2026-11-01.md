## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Manufactured-home parks run on tenancy law, not strata law.** In a manufactured home park, the tenant owns the manufactured home and rents the site (pad) under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act; where the home sits on land the owner also owns, the transfer runs through the Land Title Office instead. A park's age rule is a park rule under tenancy law, not a strata bylaw — the strata age-bylaw machinery below does not govern a pad tenancy. http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Pad-rent increases are capped by the Province.** Landlords in manufactured home parks can only increase rent with 3 full months' notice, can increase rent once every 12 months, and the increase cannot exceed the yearly rent increase limit; the 2026 limit for manufactured home site tenancies is 2.3% plus a proportional amount for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees, and the 2027 limit is 2.2% plus the same proportional amount. Standard increases are notified on RTB Form 11a, and a landlord may apply to the RTB (Form 52) for an additional increase above the limit for qualifying expense or capital reasons. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment on a sale needs park consent.** Under s.28 of the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, a tenant may assign a manufactured home site tenancy agreement, or sublet the site, only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed obtained under the Regulation), an order of the director authorizing it, or authorization in the tenancy agreement itself; consent may be withheld only in prescribed circumstances, and under s.28(3) the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting. The consent request runs on RTB-10, Request for Consent to Assign a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement. A home owner must also provide a purchaser with the written tenancy agreement and the written park rules. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/calculators-and-resources/tenancy-forms/forms-listed-number and https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwack-mobile-homes.html
- **Park closure / conversion has a statutory price.** Where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute the notice within 15 days, and may in defined circumstances apply for additional compensation. The conversion notice is issued on RTB-31. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Registration is what makes a manufactured-home sale effective.** Manufactured homes include modular and mobile homes, typically built in a factory off-site; mobile homes are built under the CSA Z-240 standards and modular homes under the CSA A-277 standards; in B.C., the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered, and the Manufactured Home Registry maintains the central register of manufactured home ownership details and controls the movement of manufactured homes in B.C. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **The CSA label is a sale gate.** The manufacturer attaches a CSA label (A-277 series for modular homes, Z-240 series for mobile homes, with a unique identifier, usually near the front door or on the electrical panel); the home may not be sold unless it has the original and legible CSA decal attached, and at buying/selling the seller must make sure the CSA certification label is attached at the point of sale, with the transfer filed on a Bill of Sale and a Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership through a qualified supplier (lawyer or notary). If a CSA certificate label is removed or lost, the home must be re-certified — Technical Safety BC has a licensed electrical contractor inspect the home's wiring and an electrical approval label is prepared, which can be used in lieu of a CSA label to list the home for sale (the route behind the "Silver Label" shorthand). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Moving a home runs on a 30-day permit.** A permit to move a manufactured home is issued by the Manufactured Home Registry, supported by an Application to Transport and a tax certificate for transport purposes; the transport permit expires 30 days after issue and the move must take place within that window. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **For contrast — the strata rules that do NOT apply inside a pad-rent park.** Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now (Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2; any age bylaw below 55 has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, under Bill 44); the bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it; Strata Property Regulation 7.01 exempts legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children cared for before 19; and residential rental-restriction bylaws have been void since Nov 24, 2022, while short-term restrictions remain allowed. Those exemptions do not automatically apply inside a manufactured-home park — read the park rules instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **No pad-rent figure is stated as a Chilliwack fact in this file.** The live page states monthly pad fees in Chilliwack and Sardis parks "typically range from $550 to $950 per month" — no official source publishes Chilliwack pad rents and no park's rent roll was opened in this research, so that range is UNVERIFIED. The only pad figure that matters is the specific site's tenancy agreement figure. https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwack-mobile-homes.html
- **Tax and financing claims stay UNVERIFIED until a professional confirms them per purchase.** The live page's statement that BC Property Transfer Tax generally does not apply when buying only a manufactured home on a rented pad, its financing split (standard mortgages on owned lots vs specialized chattel/leasehold financing on rented pads), and its First Nations lease-park framing were not confirmed on a Province, lender or park source opened in this research: UNVERIFIED as stated. City property taxes generally are due the first working day after July 1, with a 5% penalty on taxes outstanding after July 2, 2026 and an additional 5% after August 4, 2026 — how a specific park home is assessed depends on its tenure and registration, a per-property check. https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwack-mobile-homes.html and https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2522

The city-wide rules that are confirmed are the SSMUH amendment (Bylaw 5395, adopted June 18, 2024, allowing 3 to 4 units on urban-growth-boundary parcels zoned for single-detached and duplex housing) and the suite caps (secondary suite at the lesser of 90 m² and 40%; 1 ADU per lot; garden suites to 100 m²). https://chilliwack.com/ocp

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Which Chilliwack park is actually 55+?** One verified park: Cottonwood Village (7610 Evans Rd — 143 units, clubhouse, First Nations leased land, listing-stated lease expiry 2076 plus an option to extend), where you own the home and rent the pad. Every other park checked by name is below 55+ or all-ages: Rainbow Estates (40+ on the specialist list, with the feed itself conflicting 40+ vs 45+), Country Park Village (45+), Baker Trail Village (no age restrictions), Mt Cheam (families allowed). Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: What is the difference between a pad-rental park home and a manufactured home on owned land?** On a rented pad, the buyer purchases the manufactured home itself and holds a manufactured home site tenancy under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, paying monthly site rent to the park; on owned land (freehold or bare-land strata), the buyer also acquires the land interest, which transfers through the Land Title Office. In both cases, in B.C. the sale of the manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered in the Manufactured Home Registry. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up each year?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, and no more than the yearly limit set by the Residential Tenancy Branch: for manufactured home site tenancies the 2026 limit is 2.3% plus a proportional amount for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees, and the 2027 limit is 2.2% plus the proportional amount; increases above the limit require an RTB order. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: What pad rent should I expect in a Chilliwack park?** No figure is stated in this file: no official source publishes Chilliwack pad rents, and the live page's $550–$950 range is UNVERIFIED here. The figure that governs is the specific site's tenancy agreement — confirm the current pad rent, what it includes, and its increase history with the park for the exact site before offering. https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwack-mobile-homes.html
- **Q: Can the park refuse to let me buy — or refuse my buyer later?** Assignment of a pad tenancy needs the park owner's prior written consent under MHPTA s.28, and consent may be withheld only in prescribed circumstances, with no fee chargeable for consent under s.28(3). In a 55+ park, age is part of that consent picture through the park's own rules — get the park rules and the tenancy agreement in writing before writing an offer; the seller must provide them. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/calculators-and-resources/tenancy-forms/forms-listed-number
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in Cottonwood Village?** Park age rules operate through the park's own rules, not through the strata spouse exemption — the provincial younger-spouse exemption in Regulation 7.01 is a strata rule and does not automatically apply inside a park. Read Cottonwood Village's current park rules for how a younger spouse is treated before relying on it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Why does the CSA label matter when buying a manufactured home?** The CSA label is the home's certification decal — Z-240 series for mobile homes, A-277 series for modular homes, with a unique identifier usually near the front door or on the electrical panel. The Province states a manufactured home may not be sold unless the original legible CSA decal is attached; if it is lost, the home must be re-certified, with Technical Safety BC arranging an electrical inspection and an approval label that can be used in lieu of the CSA label to list the home for sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: What happens if a park is converted or closed?** A park conversion needs all government permits and approvals in place first, then 12 months' notice covering a full year and $20,000 compensation per home under MHPTA s.42, with defined additional-compensation routes where a home cannot be moved or no site is available within a reasonable distance. Confirm the park's ownership and any known redevelopment signals as part of due diligence. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Are pets allowed at Cottonwood Village?** The park specialist's list records 1 pet allowed at Cottonwood Village; an individual listing records 2 smaller pets permitted — CONFLICT, flagged: the park rules govern, so confirm the current rule with the park before relying on either figure. https://bentruscottrealestate.com/manufactured-homes.html?utm_medium=paid&utm_source=fb&utm_id=52609658579346&utm_content=52609658626346&utm_term=52609658585146&utm_campaign=52609658579346
- **Q: If I want 55+ but not a park, what else is in Chilliwack?** The city's 5 verified 55+ strata buildings — Ivy Green and Spadina Regency downtown, Mountainview Estates in Proper East, Knight Lodge and Cottage Grove on the Sardis side — are conventional strata purchases governed by a 55+ bylaw with provincial exemptions, not park tenancies. They are not manufactured homes; a buyer choosing between the two is choosing a tenure and a legal regime, not just a price. Sources: Section 2 above.
