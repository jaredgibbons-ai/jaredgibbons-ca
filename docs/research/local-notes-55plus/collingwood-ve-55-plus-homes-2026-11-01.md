# Renfrew-Collingwood (Vancouver) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- The City of Vancouver's official local area is "Renfrew-Collingwood," one of the City's 22 distinct areas; "Collingwood" on its own is the board/Paragon-style short name for this page, and City plans, visions and station work all use Renfrew-Collingwood. Boundary roads for the Collingwood portion on its own: UNVERIFIED — the City does not define Collingwood as a separate local area. - Kingsway, Joyce Street, Renfrew Street, Rupert Street and Nanaimo Street serve the area, with Boundary Road at the east boundary and Grandview Highway crossing its north side. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/renfrew.aspx
- Schools (Vancouver School Board, SD39; catchments are address-based — check the VSB locator for any specific home): Nootka Elementary, 3375 Nootka Street; John Norquay Elementary, 4710 Slocan Street; and Renfrew Elementary, 3315 East 22nd Avenue, are the elementaries listed under Renfrew-Collingwood, with Windermere Secondary at 3155 East 27th Avenue. - Community anchors: Collingwood Neighbourhood House, 5288 Joyce Street — its seniors programs serve older adults aged 55+ in Renfrew-Collingwood, including Better at Home non-medical home support — and the Renfrew Park Community Centre and Renfrew Pool at 2929 East 22nd Avenue. Libraries: Collingwood Branch, 2985 Kingsway, and Renfrew Branch, 2969 East 22nd Avenue. https://www.cnh.bc.ca/seniors/ and https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/renfrew.aspx

TransLink route 41 runs to Joyce Station via West 41st Avenue, Joyce Street and Vanness Avenue. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/joyce-collingwood-station-precinct-review.aspx and https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt041.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **A directory listing is not a covenant — the Collingwood Towers lesson.** BC Housing's directory can describe a building as seniors-restricted while the owner holds no age covenant at all: HFBC's own panel report records exactly that split for Collingwood Towers. For any non-profit seniors building here, the operative rule is the operator's current admission policy, not the directory line. https://ulidigitalmarketing.blob.core.windows.net/ulidcnc/2017/10/ULIBC_HFBC-TAP-Final-Report.pdf
- **How the City creates new 55+ rental:** Vancouver's Seniors Housing Rezoning Policy (approved July 23, 2024; last amended May 19, 2026) treats "Social Housing (where occupancy is restricted to older adults 55+)" as social housing secured by a housing agreement restricting occupancy to residents aged 55 or older, owned and operated by a non-profit or government, and secures seniors supportive or independent living housing as rental — no stratification, no separate sales — for 60 years or the life of the building, whichever is greater. New 55+ product in this area will be rental by design. https://guidelines.vancouver.ca/policy-rezoning-seniors-housing.pdf
- **House-street zoning: R1-1 District Schedule of Zoning and Development By-law No. 3575** — minimum site area 306 m² for a single detached house or duplex and for a 3–4-unit multiple dwelling, 464 m² for 5 units and 557 m² for 6 to 8 units; a multiplex may have up to 6 units or up to 8 rental units, and a single detached house may include a secondary suite and a laneway house. The C-2 District Schedule applies on Kingsway. Parcel-level zone: UNVERIFIED. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/zoning/zoning-by-law-district-schedule-R1-1.pdf
- **Station plans layer over the base zoning:** the Joyce-Collingwood Station Precinct Plan guides change around Joyce Station, and the Rupert and Renfrew Station Area Plan, approved by Council in July 2025, covers the Millennium Line station areas at the area's north side, on top of the Renfrew-Collingwood Community Vision. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/joyce-collingwood-station-precinct-review.aspx and https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/rupert-and-renfrew-station-area-plan-approved-july-2025.aspx
- **Tax calendar:** City of Vancouver 2026 advance taxes were due February 3, 2026 and main taxes July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty on the unpaid balance after each due date. Vancouver's Empty Homes Tax requires an annual property status declaration; the 2025 payment was due April 16, 2026, with a 5% penalty after that date. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/deadlines-and-penalties.aspx

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Renfrew-Collingwood?** None that any source verifies. There is no age-restricted strata building in the area; the verified seniors addresses (Collingwood Towers, the Lionsview buildings, Euclid Square and the other directory buildings) are non-profit subsidized rental, and Three Links and Renfrew Care Centre are long-term care. A 55+ buyer here buys a regular house, townhome or condo with no age bylaw. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Is Collingwood Towers actually restricted to 55+?** The sources conflict, and the file says so plainly: BC Housing's website/directory treats it as restricted to seniors 55+, but the owner's own 2017 panel report states HFBC "is not aware of any restrictive covenants on the property related to age." Treat it as a seniors rental building (66 studios and 12 one-bedroom units, 10 storeys, HFBC) whose formal age covenant is UNCONFIRMED — ask HFBC for its current admission rule. https://ulidigitalmarketing.blob.core.windows.net/ulidcnc/2017/10/ULIBC_HFBC-TAP-Final-Report.pdf
- **Q: How do I get into the Lionsview buildings or Euclid Square?** They are non-profit seniors rentals in BC Housing's Zone 6 directory (May 2025): subsidized seniors housing in the city is applied for through BC Housing's Housing Registry or directly with the non-profit operator, depending on the directory section. Lion's View III is run by Brightside Community Homes Foundation, which notes it was developed without government support for seniors who do not qualify for subsidized housing, with the SAFER grant available to lower-income seniors. Expect a waitlist process, not a viewing. https://www.bchousing.org/publications/Zone-6-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf and https://brightsidehomes.ca/properties/lions-view-iii/?org=map
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere in Vancouver?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: Can a house lot here hold a suite or laneway house for a caregiver or family?** In the forms the R1-1 schedule states: a single detached house may include a secondary suite and a laneway house, and a multiplex may have up to 6 units or up to 8 rental units — the practical aging-in-place route in an area with no 55+ strata to buy. Confirm the parcel's schedule first. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/zoning/zoning-by-law-district-schedule-R1-1.pdf
- **Q: What transit can I actually use day to day?** Joyce-Collingwood Station (Expo Line) with its bus exchange sits inside the area, the wider area reaches Nanaimo, 29th Avenue, Renfrew and Rupert Stations, and route 41 runs to Joyce Station via Joyce Street and Vanness Avenue — the Euclid Avenue seniors cluster sits south of Kingsway within the station's wider catchment, so check the specific building's walk to the station or a Kingsway bus. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/joyce-collingwood-station-precinct-review.aspx and https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt041.pdf
- **Q: Where is the hospital, and what seniors support exists locally?** No hospital sits inside the area; the in-area support layer is Collingwood Neighbourhood House at 5288 Joyce Street, whose Better at Home program provides non-medical home support for older adults 55+ in Renfrew-Collingwood, plus grocery shopping, light housekeeping and a walking club — services for aging in a regular home here, which is how most 55+ households in this area actually live. https://www.cnh.bc.ca/seniors/
- **Q: Is the Renfrew Ravine area in a floodplain?** Still Creek runs through Renfrew Ravine Park and its floodplain is part of the City's designated floodplain dataset; parcels in it carry Building By-law requirements. Whether a specific parcel is in the floodplain: UNVERIFIED — check the parcel in the City dataset. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/still-creek.aspx and https://opendata.vancouver.ca/explore/dataset/designated-floodplain/information/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The Lionsview seniors cluster began with a Lions Club land purchase in 1953:** Brightside records that in 1953 the Coordinating Council of Lions Clubs and the Central Lions Club raised and donated $18,000 to Brightside to purchase three acres of land for its first project, named Lion's View; two buildings were erected, 12 additional buildings were completed in 1960, and the property was later redeveloped, with Lion's View Building III reopening in 1995 — the reason three generations of "Lionsview" buildings share the Euclid Avenue / Horley Street site today. https://brightsidehomes.ca/properties/lions-view-iii/?org=map
- **Lion's View III was deliberately built outside the subsidy system:** Brightside states the 1995 building was developed without government support, using partial proceeds from the sale of part of the Lion's View land, specifically so it could house seniors who do not qualify for low-income government subsidized housing — a different admission door from its subsidized neighbours on the same site. https://brightsidehomes.ca/properties/lions-view-iii/?org=map
- **Collingwood Towers' owner bought the site in 2012 and was already studying its future in 2017:** the HFBC panel report records HFBC Housing Foundation purchased the Collingwood Tower site on July 16, 2012, that the site was occupied by single family dwellings between the mid-1910s and the late 1960s, and that the 2017 panel was convened on the future of the 40-year-old tower — a redevelopment file to watch for anyone relying on that building's 78 units of seniors rental stock. https://ulidigitalmarketing.blob.core.windows.net/ulidcnc/2017/10/ULIBC_HFBC-TAP-Final-Report.pdf
- **Two "Collingwood" housing worlds sit minutes apart and share nothing else:** Collingwood Village (2,700 general-market suites around Joyce Station, completed by 2006) and Collingwood Towers (a 78-unit non-profit seniors tower on Harold Street) — name searches return both, and only the second is seniors housing. https://docslib.org/download/5416602/collingwood-village-vancouver-bc and https://ulidigitalmarketing.blob.core.windows.net/ulidcnc/2017/10/ULIBC_HFBC-TAP-Final-Report.pdf
- **The area's newest plan layer landed in July 2025:** the Rupert and Renfrew Station Area Plan, approved by Council in July 2025, covers the Millennium Line station areas at the area's north side — station-area land expectations now sit on top of R1-1 on streets that also carry the area's older house stock, while the seniors stock itself stays in its two established pockets. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/rupert-and-renfrew-station-area-plan-approved-july-2025.aspx
- **The seniors services here are unusually organised for an area with no 55+ ownership:** Collingwood Neighbourhood House runs a dedicated seniors program out of 5288 Joyce Street — Better at Home home support, a supported grocery shopping program through a Save-On Foods partnership for Renfrew-Collingwood residents, light housekeeping and second-Friday shuttle trips for participants aged 55+ — the support infrastructure a buyer in a general-market home here would actually use. https://www.cnh.bc.ca/seniors/
