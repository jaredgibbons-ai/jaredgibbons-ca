# Langley (Township and City) — 55+ Homes, Court-Ordered Sale Framing: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Scope note: this slug is a city-wide Langley page, so these notes cover both Langley municipalities — the Township of Langley and the City of Langley — as one market, and they are framed for a buyer who may meet a 55+ home through a court-ordered (foreclosure) sale. The 55+ stock below is the Langley-wide stock; the court process in Section 3 is the BC process, and it applies to a strata unit, a manufactured home or a house in exactly the same way.**

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Langley is two municipalities: the Township of Langley (the larger, mostly suburban and rural municipality, governed by Zoning Bylaw No. 2500) and the City of Langley (the compact urban municipality around Douglas Crescent, 200 Street and the Fraser Highway downtown). A "Langley" 55+ search crosses both, and the complexes below are filed by community so the jurisdiction is not lost. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/about-the-township.aspx and https://www.langleycity.ca/PayPropertyTaxes
- Where the verified 55+ ownership stock concentrates: (1) downtown Langley City, around 205 / 206 Street and 53 / 54 Avenue — Huntsfield Green, Parkway Estates, Parkway II and Glenmont Park; (2) Walnut Grove, north of Highway 1 along 88 Avenue / 208 Street — Churchill Park, Spencer Green, Chartwell Green and Chelsea Green; (3) Willoughby's southwest edge — Willow Park Estates; (4) Brookswood's manufactured-home parks on and near 200 Street; (5) single complexes in Aldergrove (Creekside Villa) and Fort Langley (Waterfront at Bedford Landing). Sources: the building entries in Section 2 below.
- The rural sub-areas — Salmon River, Campbell Valley, County Line Glen Valley, Otter District — hold no verified 55+ complex; the Township states 235 square kilometres of the Township is in the Agricultural Land Reserve, and farm parcels are freehold, not strata. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/about-the-township.aspx
- Main corridors that connect the clusters: Fraser Highway (City downtown through Murrayville to Aldergrove), 200 Street (Willoughby, Brookswood, Langley City), 208 Street / 88 Avenue (Walnut Grove) and Highway 1 along the north. Transit and hospital access are community-specific; the shared hospital is Langley Memorial Hospital, 22051 Fraser Highway. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Langley/langley-memorial-hospital
- Schools are SD35 across both municipalities and catchments are address-based through the SD35 School Locator — relevant to a 55+ buyer only through the exemption cases in Section 3 (grandchildren in care). https://www.sd35.bc.ca/school-locator
- Court geography: a BC foreclosure proceeding is started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia, and unless the court otherwise orders it is commenced at the Supreme Court registry in the municipality where the land is located, if there is one there, and otherwise at a registry in the judicial district in which the land is located (Law and Equity Act, s. 21(2)). Which registry serves a given Langley parcel is a file-level check — the foreclosures research file records that no official source states a registry-to-Langley mapping. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. This matters in a foreclosure purchase exactly as in an ordinary one: winning the unit does not waive the residency rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. Several Langley buildings above still carry "rentals not allowed" in their building records (Parkway Estates, Parkway II, Willow Park Estates); for a long-term rental that line cannot be relied on, in a court-ordered purchase or any other. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **A BC "court-ordered sale" is a sale inside a foreclosure proceeding, not a bank sale.** The proceeding starts by petition in the Supreme Court of BC under Rule 21-7(1); the court may fix a redemption period in its order (Rule 21-7(5)(b)) — the Rules state no fixed length for it — and in default of payment a final order of foreclosure may be granted (Rule 21-7(6)). A party may instead apply at any time for an order that the property be sold (Rule 21-7(7)). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct and approval:** if the court orders a sale, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" (Rule 13-5(3)); the court's directions may fix the manner of sale, a reserve or minimum price, and bid and offer rights, and may direct sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" (Rule 13-5(4)). The person with conduct may apply, even before the redemption period expires, for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser (Rule 21-7(9)) — the vesting order is what cleans title for the buyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Redemption can end a deal:** interest on what is due is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem the property (Law and Equity Act, s. 19), so an owner who redeems before confirmation takes the property out of the sale. Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Check the file yourself:** Court Services Online is BC's electronic court registry; its eSearch is open to anyone with Internet access, shows file number, registry, party names and filed documents for Supreme Court civil files, and its file classes include "H - Foreclosure." No official source publishes Langley foreclosure counts or court-ordered sale statistics — any such number is UNVERIFIED. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Manufactured-home parks run on park rules, not strata law:** the age rules at Cedar Creek Estates, Fernridge Park and Pineridge are park rules attached to a leasehold pad, and Pioneer Park's are co-op rules attached to share ownership — the Strata Property Act exemptions in this section do not answer an occupancy question in any of them. Get the park's or co-op's current rules in writing.
- **Two tax systems, two due dates:** Township of Langley property taxes were due July 2, 2026 with 5% penalties at midnight July 2 and a further 5% at midnight September 3, 2026, and utility fees are billed separately; the City of Langley also sets a July 2 due date, with payments received after it incurring a penalty (the City's penalty rate is not stated on the page cited — UNCONFIRMED). A court-ordered purchase does not reset either calendar. https://www.tol.ca/en/services/property-taxes.aspx and https://www.langleycity.ca/PayPropertyTaxes

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Langley?** Fifteen verified ownership complexes in this pass: eleven strata (Huntsfield Green, Parkway Estates, Parkway II and Glenmont Park in Langley City; Willow Park Estates in Willoughby; Churchill Park, Spencer Green, Chartwell Green and Chelsea Green in Walnut Grove; Creekside Villa in Aldergrove; Waterfront at Bedford Landing in Fort Langley) and four manufactured-home/co-op communities (Cedar Creek Estates, Fernridge Park and Pineridge in Brookswood; Pioneer Park co-operative in Aldergrove). Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: If a unit in a 55+ building comes up as a court-ordered sale, is my accepted offer final?** No. Where the court has directed sale by contract conditional on the approval of the court, the person with conduct of sale must still apply under Rule 21-7(9) for an order confirming the sale and vesting title in you — until that order, the sale is not final, and the court's directions may allow others to bid, make offers or meet bids at that stage. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: Does buying through the court get me around the 55+ bylaw?** No — the age bylaw governs residency in the strata regardless of how the lot was acquired, and a vesting order vests title; it does not amend the strata's bylaws. An under-55 buyer can own, but may not be able to reside, and any tenant must also satisfy the bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a Langley 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. Note the manufactured-home parks and the Pioneer Park co-op set their own occupancy rules, which these strata exemptions do not govern. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can the owner still save the home after I've offered?** The owner may redeem by paying what is due plus interest to the date of payment (Law and Equity Act, s. 19), and the redemption period is whatever period the court fixed in its order — the Rules set no standard length. The person with conduct of sale may apply to confirm a sale even before that period expires, which is why the file's stage matters before you spend money. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How do I check the court file on a Langley foreclosure?** Court Services Online eSearch shows the file number, registry, parties and filed documents for Supreme Court civil files, class "H - Foreclosure," and is open to anyone with Internet access. The petition, the amount certified due and the orders made are the facts of the deal. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: Are court-ordered sales in Langley mostly houses?** No official source publishes a Langley foreclosure count by property type — UNVERIFIED as a statistic. The Langley foreclosure page itself is presented as including condos, townhomes and houses, which is why the 55+ strata and park stock above is in scope for this slug at all. https://jaredgibbons.ca/langley-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales.html
- **Q: Is Sunridge Gardens a condo I can buy in a foreclosure sale?** No — Sunridge Gardens, 22301 Fraser Hwy, is a Bria Communities rental retirement residence (independent living suites plus a licensed care centre). Rental residences do not sell unit by unit, in a foreclosure or otherwise. The same applies to Magnolia Gardens, Chartwell Langley Gardens and Langley Seniors Village. https://centurygroup.ca/seniors-living/
- **Q: Are pets allowed in Langley's 55+ complexes?** It is complex-by-complex and the records conflict in places: Willow Park Estates listings record cats and dogs OK to a limit of two in one source and a one-pet weight limit in another; Parkway Estates and Parkway II building sources record pets not allowed; Cedar Creek Estates records pets allowed with restrictions; Fernridge Park listings state no pets. The registered bylaw or park rule for the specific complex governs — never the listing that happens to be showing. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Does a court-ordered purchase change my tax or strata-fee obligations?** No. Strata fees, the Township's July 2 tax due date (5% + 5% penalties in 2026) and the City's July 2 due date all run on their normal calendars; arrears and how they are treated in a given file are a matter of the court's directions on the proceeds, which are file-specific. https://www.tol.ca/en/services/property-taxes.aspx and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Langley's 55+ map is two clusters and a fringe, not a spread.** Downtown Langley City holds four strata complexes within a few blocks of 205/206 Street and 53 Avenue; Walnut Grove holds four more along the 88 Avenue / 208 Street corridor; Brookswood holds three of the four manufactured-home parks. A buyer searching "Langley 55+" without a community name is really choosing between those clusters. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Three Langley seniors names are traps.** "The Maple" (subsidized rental, 5460 203rd St) is not "Maple Manor" (general-market rental, 20117/20119 56th Ave); "Sunridge Gardens" is a rental residence, not the "Sunridge" condos buyers imagine; and "Morningside Estates" (5365 205 St) could not be verified as 55+ in this pass, while its verified neighbour Glenmont Park (5375 205 St) could. https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/opening-more-seniors-housing-in-langley and https://www.zumper.com/apartment-buildings/p456764/maple-manor-apartments-nicomeki-langley-bc and https://www.zolo.ca/langley-real-estate/5375-205-street/110
- **The "rentals not allowed" lines in Langley building records are legally dead for long-term rentals.** Parkway Estates, Parkway II and Willow Park Estates all carry that line in public building data, and all such bylaws have been void since Nov 24, 2022 — the surviving restrictions are the age bylaw itself and any short-term rental rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Two of Langley's 55+ products are not strata at all, and a third is a co-op.** The Brookswood parks are leasehold pads under park rules, and Pioneer Park requires co-op acceptance and a share purchase — in a court-ordered sale context that difference is not cosmetic, because what is being sold, and whose approval a buyer needs, differs by tenure. https://www.estateblock.com/langley/5-3031-200-st-1968212 and https://www.estateblock.com/langley/134-27111-ave-1991100
