# Burnaby Lake — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Burnaby Lake wraps Burnaby Lake Regional Park in central-east Burnaby: the residential streets on the lake's edges — the Sperling and Kensington side to the west and north-west, the Government Road and Beaverbrook side to the east — around a large regional park and lake that are not themselves residential land. The exact MLS sub-area boundary streets are UNVERIFIED — no City or board source checked publishes them. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park
- Lougheed Highway runs along the north side, Highway 1 along the south side; the main roads around the lake are Lougheed Highway, Winston Street, Sperling Avenue, Kensington Avenue and Government Road. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2026-05-28/how-burnaby-funding-growth-through-responsible-use-reserves
- Transit: Sperling–Burnaby Lake Station, an elevated Millennium Line station at Sperling Avenue and Lougheed Highway, sits at the sub-area's north-west side, and you can walk into Burnaby Lake Regional Park directly from the station; bus routes 110, 134 and 144 connect at the station. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park and https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt110.pdf
- Schools: School District 41 (Burnaby) — 41 elementary and 8 secondary schools city-wide, no middle-school tier (K–7 / 8–12), address-based catchments. Schools on the Province's list beside the sub-area include Sperling Elementary (K–7), 2200 Sperling Ave, École Seaforth Elementary (K–7), 7881 Government Rd, and Burnaby Central Secondary (8–12), 6011 Deer Lake Parkway; no SD41 school sits on the regional park lands themselves. https://burnabyschools.ca/about-sd41-overview/ and https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/041
- Health: the city's hospital is Burnaby Hospital, 3935 Kincaid St, immediately south-west of the sub-area. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Burnaby/burnaby-hospital
- Recreation: Burnaby Lake Sports Complex, accessed from Kensington Avenue and Sperling Avenue, has 5 artificial turf fields with lights plus grass fields and diamonds; Bill Copeland Sports Centre, 3676 Kensington Avenue, is a 2,000-seat venue (1,984 permanent seats) with an NHL-sized rink inside the complex. https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/burnaby-lake-sports-complex and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/venue-rentals/bill-copeland-sports-centre
- Where the seniors housing actually is: nowhere verified inside this sub-area. Most of the "area" is parkland, the housing stock is detached houses on the edge streets (19 detached listings against only 3 condos and townhomes listed in September 2026), and no seniors building of any type was verified at a lake-edge address — see Section 2. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Zoning bylaw — note the name change.** Research briefs for Burnaby sometimes still cite the "Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965". The City states the new **Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026** was adopted June 9, 2026, replaced the former Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965, and took effect July 1, 2026. Use the 2026 bylaw. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/bylaws/zoning-bylaw
- **R1 SSMUH zoning on the edge streets:** Burnaby consolidated its single- and two-family districts into the R1 Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing district in June 2024 — 3 units on lots up to 280 m², 4 units on larger lots, and 6 units on lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent-transit stop; multi-unit lots may be stratified. The lake and park lands themselves are park and conservation land, not housing land. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **OCP: the Burnaby 2050 Official Community Plan** was adopted December 9, 2025, replaces the 1998 OCP, uses a parcel-based land use map, and takes precedence over older community-plan land use maps where they conflict. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/official-community-plan and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-12-11/city-burnaby-adopts-burnaby-2050-official-community-plan
- **Park tenure is settled for a generation:** the City of Burnaby and Metro Vancouver have signed a 25-year lease agreement for Burnaby Lake Regional Park; Metro Vancouver will continue to fund day-to-day maintenance, natural resource protection, regulation and enforcement, and capital improvements in the 360-acre park, which it has done since 1978, and will develop a Park Management Plan with the City within five years. That is the rule set a lake-edge home actually lives beside — park rules, not a strata. http://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-02-05/city-burnaby-and-metro-vancouver-sign-burnaby-lake-regional-park
- **Property taxes:** Burnaby's 2026 property taxes were due Friday, July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date; the City's 2026–2030 Financial Plan approved a 2.9% tax increase (about $61 on the average home). The detached single-family water utility charge is $652.33, with a 5% discount if paid by the due date. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy at Burnaby Lake?** None that any source verifies. There is no age-restricted strata building with an address inside this sub-area in any source checked, and almost no strata at all — only 3 condos and townhomes were listed in the whole sub-area in September 2026, against 19 detached listings (SnapStats GVR September 2026 report). A 55+ buyer here is buying an ordinary detached house on an edge street, with no age bylaw. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Is most of "Burnaby Lake" actually housing?** No. The centre of the sub-area is Burnaby Lake Regional Park — a 360-acre Metro Vancouver regional park and wildlife sanctuary around the lake, managed by Metro Vancouver since 1978 under a 25-year lease agreement with the City. The housing is only the streets around it. http://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-02-05/city-burnaby-and-metro-vancouver-sign-burnaby-lake-regional-park
- **Q: Does a lake-edge home come with lake frontage or water rights?** No source states any private lake frontage rights for the edge streets — the lake and shoreline are regional park land, with trails, viewing towers and docks, a Nature House, and a rowing course with a pavilion and public canoe launch at the Burnaby Lake Sports Complex. Treat any "lakefront" claim for a specific lot as UNCONFIRMED until title and park boundaries are checked. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere in Burnaby?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: Where do seniors in this area go for programs?** There is no City 55+ centre inside the sub-area. The nearest is the Temporary Cameron 55+ Centre inside The City of Lougheed Shopping Centre (110-9855 Austin Ave), serving north-east Burnaby while the Cameron Community Centre and Library is rebuilt — one of the City's four centres specializing in programs for adults 55+, with a 55+ single-centre membership at $22.60/year and a multi-centre membership at $56.45/year. It is a recreation centre, not housing. https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/cameron-55-plus-centre
- **Q: What transit can I actually use day to day?** Sperling–Burnaby Lake Station (Millennium Line) at Sperling Ave and Lougheed Hwy serves the west and north-west side, with buses 110, 134 and 144 connecting there; route 110 runs past the lake's north and west sides between Lougheed Station and Metrotown Station via Government, Winston and Sperling. The east (Government Road / Beaverbrook) side has no SkyTrain station in the sub-area. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt110.pdf and https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Burnaby Hospital, 3935 Kincaid St, immediately south-west of the sub-area — the closest hospital to both the Sperling and the Government Road edges. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Burnaby/burnaby-hospital
- **Q: If I need care later, can I stay at Burnaby Lake?** Not inside the sub-area — there is no assisted-living residence or care home here; BC's Assisted Living Registry lists eight seniors residences in Burnaby and every one is at an address outside this sub-area. The nearest seniors campus is the Dania campus on Norland Ave toward Burnaby Hospital (assisted living, long-term care and subsidized seniors rental), accessed by application through the society, BC Housing or Fraser Health — not by purchase. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ABurnaby&f%5B1%5D=city%3ANorth%20Vancouver&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&name=&address=&order=alr_legal_operator_name&sort=asc and https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/affordable-homes-open-for-seniors-in-burnaby

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The park's future was locked in on February 5, 2025.** The City and Metro Vancouver signed a 25-year lease for Burnaby Lake Regional Park that day, securing Metro Vancouver's management of the 360-acre park — funding maintenance, natural resource protection, enforcement and capital improvements, as it has since 1978 — with a Park Management Plan to be developed with the City within five years. A lake-edge buyer is buying beside a permanently managed regional park, not beside land awaiting a use decision. http://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-02-05/city-burnaby-and-metro-vancouver-sign-burnaby-lake-regional-park
- **A new recreation complex is being built at the lake right now.** The City reported on May 28, 2026 that the new Burnaby Lake Recreation Centre is one of four major facilities past the halfway mark in its capital program, and that the Burnaby Lake pedestrian and cycling overpass across Highway 1 has been delivered; programs at Bill Copeland Sports Centre (3676 Kensington Ave) continue during construction. Amenity improving, with construction in the middle of it. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2026-05-28/how-burnaby-funding-growth-through-responsible-use-reserves and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/venue-rentals/bill-copeland-sports-centre
- **The "33% sales ratio" for attached homes here is one sale.** September 2026's Burnaby Lake attached row was 3 listings and 1 sold (SnapStats GVR September 2026 report) — a small-numbers artefact, not a hot strata market. There is no 55+ strata cluster hiding in those three listings; the area research file could not confirm a single named condo or townhome complex inside the sub-area on a developer or strata source.
- **You can walk into the park straight from the SkyTrain.** Metro Vancouver notes you can walk into Burnaby Lake Regional Park directly from Sperling–Burnaby Lake Station — the west-side streets near Sperling Ave are the one part of this sub-area where a non-driving senior has station-plus-trails at the door. The Government Road east side has no equivalent. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park
- **The shoreline is protected habitat, and that travels with lake-edge lots.** Metro Vancouver describes the park as a wildlife sanctuary whose shoreline habitat the trails and boardwalks (including the Piper Spit boardwalk and viewing tower) are built around; the area research file flags that lake-edge and creek-edge lots need parcel-level flood, habitat and watercourse-setback checks, and that a floodplain designation for the residential streets was not confirmed in the sources read — check the specific lot, do not assume either way. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park
- **Burnaby replaced its entire zoning bylaw in 2026.** The Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026 (adopted June 9, 2026, in force July 1, 2026) repealed and replaced the Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965 — documents or summaries that still cite the 1965 bylaw for a lake-edge lot are out of date. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **Burnaby taxes have two penalty steps.** 2026 property taxes were due July 3, 2026; a 5% penalty applies after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date, and the detached water charge ($652.33) carries a 5% early-payment discount. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees
