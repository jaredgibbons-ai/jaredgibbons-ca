## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. This is directly live at Quilchena Green, where a 2016 MLS sheet records 45+ and current listings record 55+ (Section 2). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://choa.bc.ca/resources/strata-property-act/legislative-updates/
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. This voids the "rentals not allowed" lines still shown for Quilchena Green and Covenant Court in building databases. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **City zoning: Richmond Zoning Bylaw 8500.** Houses across Richmond's residential districts sit in Single Detached zones RS1 and RS2 (Section 8.1), with minimum lot areas by subzone including RS1/B 360.0 m², RS1/E 550.0 m², RS1/F 828.0 m² and RS1/K 315.0 m² and a 24.0 m minimum depth in most subzones; Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing zones RSM/S, RSM/M, RSM/L and RSM/XL also apply in the bylaw as amended. https://Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/RS1-RS2-RS324187.pdf
- **Suites and added units:** a secondary suite is a permitted secondary use in the RS1/RS2 zones (suite rules in Section 5.4 of Bylaw 8500: at least 33.0 m², at most the lesser of 40% of the dwelling floor area or 110 m²), and the City states it allows at least one secondary suite and/or accessory dwelling unit per lot, with Bill 44 small-scale multi-unit minimums of 3 units on lots of 280 m² or less and 4 units on larger lots. A suite is the main ageing-in-place mechanism in Richmond's house neighbourhoods, where no 55+ strata exists. https://Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/RS1-RS2-RS324187.pdf and https://RICHMOND.CA/business-development/planning/housing-legislation.htm
- **Flood — Richmond is a dike-protected island city:** Richmond is a low-lying island city in the Fraser River floodplain, protected by a perimeter dike system with drainage pump stations — 49 km of dikes and 39 pump stations city-wide, designed for a 1:500 flood event, with dikes being raised from 3.5 metres to 4.7 metres for sea level rise. The Flood Plain Designation and Protection Bylaw 8204 sets dike setbacks and flood construction level requirements, and all lands in Richmond are designated flood plain under it. https://www.Richmond.ca/services/water-sewer-flood/dikes.htm?PageMode=HTML and https://www.Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Bylaw_8204_0410201225280.pdf
- **Property taxes and utilities:** City of Richmond 2026 property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on any unpaid balance after that date and a further 5% penalty on balances still outstanding after September 1, 2026. Richmond's 2026 typical single-family metered utility total is $2,251 per year, including a $342 flood protection line. https://richmond.ca/city-hall/finance/rates/duedates-taxes.htm and https://RICHMOND.CA/city-hall/finance/utility-fees/utilityrates.htm
- **Subsidized seniors housing runs through BC Housing's Housing Registry** for this area (Zone 3: Richmond and West Delta directory, May 2025). https://learningondemand.bchousing.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Zone-3-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Richmond?** On the verified record for the nine consolidated sub-areas: Quilchena Green (5531 Cornwall Dr, Terra Nova) — 91 gated strata townhouses, built 1992, with a current-listing 55+ restriction and the three conflicts flagged in Section 2 — plus Covenant Court (9260 No. 2 Rd, Woodwards) on listing evidence only, pending the Form B. Everywhere else in those nine areas the verified answer is none: a 55+ buyer is buying a house, townhome or condo with no age bylaw, or using the suite rules in Section 3 to age in place. https://www.estateblock.com/richmond/72-5531-cornwall-dr-55264 and https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/listing/r2898499-1089260-no-2-road-richmond-bc-v7e-2c8
- **Q: Is Quilchena Green in Quilchena?** No. Board, listing and building sources all place 5531 Cornwall Drive in Terra Nova, and the separate Quilchena RI pocket has no 55+ housing at all. A buyer searching "Quilchena 55+" and a buyer searching "Terra Nova 55+" are looking for this same one complex. https://www.estateblock.com/richmond/72-5531-cornwall-dr-55264
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy a unit in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out my unit in a 55+ building?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022, whatever a building database or an old listing still shows for Quilchena Green or Covenant Court. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink my resale buyer pool?** Mechanically, yes: the next buyer must also be able to live there under the bylaw — i.e., be 55+, be an exempt spouse/partner, caregiver or legacy case, or buy without living in the unit. That is the trade-off built into every BC 55+ strata since the 2022 law change confined age bylaws to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Apple Greene has pools, a clubhouse and a library — isn't that a seniors complex?** No. No source checked records any age restriction for Apple Greene; building sources record pets and rental restrictions only, and listings market it to families and investors. Amenities are not an age bylaw. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/apple-greene-8860-no-1-road
- **Q: How do I get into The Remy seniors' apartments or Knightsbridge II?** Both are subsidized/non-profit rental, not purchase product: The Remy's 33 seniors' apartments are managed by S.U.C.C.E.S.S. inside a mixed rental building, and Knightsbridge II is a BC Housing Zone 3 directory building — applications run through BC Housing's Housing Registry. Expect a waitlist; neither building sells units. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2014MNGD0009-000204 and https://learningondemand.bchousing.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Zone-3-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf
- **Q: Is Richmond in a floodplain, and does that change what I pay?** Yes — the whole city sits in the Fraser River floodplain behind a 1:500-design dike system, Bylaw 8204 flood construction levels apply to new construction, and the typical metered utility bill carries a $342/year flood protection line. Flood status still has to be checked lot by lot against Bylaw 8204. https://www.Richmond.ca/services/water-sewer-flood/dikes.htm?PageMode=HTML and https://RICHMOND.CA/city-hall/finance/utility-fees/utilityrates.htm
- **Q: Where is the hospital, and what transit can I use day to day?** Richmond Hospital, 7000 Westminster Highway, is the city's hospital. Day to day, the Canada Line (Bridgeport, Aberdeen, Lansdowne, Richmond–Brighouse) plus local buses are the transit spine — there is no SkyTrain station in Terra Nova, Seafair, Boyd Park or Steveston; those areas ride buses to a Canada Line station. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/health/about-bc-s-health-care-system/heath-care-partners/hospitallist.pdf and https://www.translink.ca/news/2024/october/temporary%20service%20change%20on%20canada%20line
