# Deer Lake (Burnaby) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Deer Lake is the residential area around Deer Lake Park, 6450 Deer Lake Ave, in central Burnaby. Market data treats Deer Lake, Deer Lake Place and Upper Deer Lake as three distinct sub-areas — they are not interchangeable names. Sources: the area research file (SnapStats GVR September 2026, sources read 2026-11-01).
- The housing stock is almost entirely detached freehold houses. In September 2026 the sub-area recorded ZERO attached (condo/townhome) listings and zero attached sales — there is effectively no strata stock here at all, 55+ or otherwise. Sources: the area research file (SnapStats GVR September 2026).
- City Hall, 4949 Canada Way, sits just northwest of the sub-area. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/parks/deer-lake-park
- Schools: Buckingham Elementary, 6066 Buckingham Ave; the SD41 locator for that address returns Buckingham Elementary and Burnaby Central Secondary, 6011 Deer Lake Parkway. Burnaby is School District 41 — 41 elementary and 8 secondary schools, no middle-school tier (K–7 / 8–12), with address-based catchments. https://burnabyschools.ca/about-sd41-overview/ and https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/041
- Heritage: the Deer Lake Park Heritage Precinct runs along Sperling Ave (for example the Woodward House, 5195 Sperling Ave, built 1912), and the park's cultural precinct holds the Shadbolt Centre for the Arts, the Burnaby Art Gallery, the Burnaby Village Museum (6501 Deer Lake Ave) and Hart House restaurant. https://search.heritageburnaby.ca/list?objectType_facet=Landmark%20Description%20&objectType_facet=History%20Links&ps=50&q=&sort=title_sort%20asc&creator_facet=johnreid000000%7CJohn%20Reid&creator_facet=bernardrhill000000%7CBernard%20R.%20Hill&place_facet=sperlingavenue000000%7CSperling%20Avenue&p=1
- Transit and facilities: there is no SkyTrain station, no shopping mall and no City recreation facility verified inside this sub-area — it is a park-and-houses neighbourhood served by local buses. Sources: the area research file (sources read 2026-11-01).
- Where the seniors housing actually is: NOT here. The nearest seniors campus (Dania, on Norland Ave) and the nearest retirement/care cluster (Norfolk St / Kalyk Ave) sit west toward Burnaby Hospital, outside this park-centred sub-area as described in the area research — see the Checked section. Nothing seniors-specific was verified inside Deer Lake itself.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. (In Deer Lake this is mostly theoretical — there is almost no strata stock for a bylaw to attach to.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Zoning bylaw — note the name change.** Research briefs for Burnaby sometimes still cite the "Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965 (No. 4742)". The City states the new **Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026** was adopted June 9, 2026, replaced the former Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965, and took effect July 1, 2026. Use the 2026 bylaw. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/bylaws/zoning-bylaw
- **R1 SSMUH zoning:** Burnaby consolidated its single- and two-family districts into the R1 Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing district in June 2024 — 3 units on lots up to 280 m², 4 units on larger lots, and 6 units on lots of 281 m² or more within 400 m of a frequent-transit stop; multi-unit lots may be stratified. Around Deer Lake, that provincially driven infill — suites, duplexes, multiplexes on house lots — is the only mechanism adding smaller ground-oriented homes; none of it is age-restricted. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **OCP: the Burnaby 2050 Official Community Plan** was adopted December 9, 2025, replaces the 1998 OCP, and takes precedence over older community-plan maps. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-12-11/city-burnaby-adopts-burnaby-2050-official-community-plan
- **Heritage rules bite here:** the Deer Lake Park Heritage Precinct on Sperling Ave means older houses near the park can carry heritage status and the approvals that come with it — check a specific property's heritage designation before assuming a renovation or rebuild is straightforward. https://search.heritageburnaby.ca/list?objectType_facet=Landmark%20Description%20&objectType_facet=History%20Links&ps=50&q=&sort=title_sort%20asc&creator_facet=johnreid000000%7CJohn%20Reid&creator_facet=bernardrhill000000%7CBernard%20R.%20Hill&place_facet=sperlingavenue000000%7CSperling%20Avenue&p=1
- **Property taxes:** Burnaby's 2026 property taxes were due Friday, July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date; the City's 2026–2030 Financial Plan approved a 2.9% tax increase (about $61 on the average home). The detached single-family water utility charge is $652.33, with a 5% discount if paid by the due date. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Deer Lake?** None. There is no verified 55+ strata, rental, care or subsidized seniors building inside the Deer Lake sub-area in any source checked — and almost no strata stock of any kind (zero attached listings and zero attached sales in September 2026). A 55+ buyer here is buying a detached house with no age bylaw. Sources: Section 2 above and the area research file (SnapStats GVR September 2026).
- **Q: Aren't the Dania buildings by Deer Lake?** They are NEAR the park's west side, at Canada Way and Norland Ave — but they sit in the hospital-side pocket, are documented in the Burnaby Hospital 55+ notes, and are rental/care/subsidized housing in any case, not something to buy. "Close to Deer Lake Park" in a housing release is proximity marketing, not a sub-area assignment. https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/affordable-homes-open-for-seniors-in-burnaby
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. If we buy a house here, does any age rule apply?** No — a detached freehold house carries no strata age bylaw at all. The 55+ bylaw rules (and the spouse/partner exemption) only matter inside a strata corporation, and this sub-area has effectively none. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. (Relevant to other Burnaby sub-areas; there is no such building in Deer Lake.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I add a suite or a second dwelling for a parent or caregiver?** That is the realistic ageing-in-place route here: Burnaby's R1 SSMUH zoning allows 3–4 units on most house lots (up to 6 near frequent transit), secondary suites are part of the permitted forms, and multi-unit lots may be stratified. What a specific lot can hold depends on its size and transit proximity — check the lot against the City's R1 rules. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **Q: Can I rent out part of the house?** There is no strata to restrict you in a freehold house, and province-wide, strata rental-restriction bylaws have been void since Nov 24, 2022 in any case; short-term rentals are the category that can still be restricted. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Burnaby Hospital, 3935 Kincaid St, is immediately west of this sub-area — the closest hospital to any house around the lake. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Burnaby/burnaby-hospital
- **Q: What transit and daily services are in the sub-area itself?** Very little by design: no SkyTrain station, no mall and no City recreation facility was verified inside the sub-area; daily life runs on local buses and a car, with the park and its cultural precinct (Shadbolt Centre, Burnaby Village Museum, Burnaby Art Gallery) as the walkable amenity. Sources: the area research file (sources read 2026-11-01).
- **Q: If I need care later, can I stay in Deer Lake?** Not in the sub-area — there is no care home here. The nearest ladder is the Dania campus west toward the hospital (assisted living at Dania Manor; 102 long-term care beds at Dania Home), accessed through Fraser Health and the society, not by purchase. https://www.dania.bc.ca/residences/residential-care/ and https://www.dania.bc.ca/about-us/about-dania/
- **Q: Does the heritage precinct affect a purchase near the park?** It can: houses on Sperling Ave sit in the Deer Lake Park Heritage Precinct (the precinct includes homes such as the 1912 Woodward House at 5195 Sperling Ave), and heritage status affects what exterior work and redevelopment is permitted. Confirm the specific property's status with the City before writing an offer that assumes changes. https://search.heritageburnaby.ca/list?objectType_facet=Landmark%20Description%20&objectType_facet=History%20Links&ps=50&q=&sort=title_sort%20asc&creator_facet=johnreid000000%7CJohn%20Reid&creator_facet=bernardrhill000000%7CBernard%20R.%20Hill&place_facet=sperlingavenue000000%7CSperling%20Avenue&p=1

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **"Deer Lake" is three sub-areas, not one.** Market data separates Deer Lake from Deer Lake Place and Upper Deer Lake; a search filtered to one will miss or mislabel homes in the others, and none of the three has verified 55+ stock. Sources: the area research file (sources read 2026-11-01).
- **Part of the lake's boardwalk is closed.** A section of the Deer Lake Park boardwalk closed on September 21, 2026, with replacement expected by winter 2027 — the lakeside walking loop that sells this neighbourhood to downsizers is interrupted in the meantime. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/parks/deer-lake-park
- **The cultural precinct is the amenity — there is no commercial one.** Shadbolt Centre for the Arts, the Burnaby Art Gallery, Burnaby Village Museum (6501 Deer Lake Ave) and Hart House are all in or beside the park; the sub-area has no mall and no City recreation facility. Buyers picturing a walkable village centre are picturing a different Burnaby sub-area. Sources: the area research file (sources read 2026-11-01).
- **Burnaby replaced its entire zoning bylaw in 2026.** The Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026 (adopted June 9, 2026, in force July 1, 2026) repealed and replaced the Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965 — documents or summaries that still cite the 1965 bylaw are out of date. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **City Hall is the northwest neighbour.** Burnaby City Hall at 4949 Canada Way sits just outside the sub-area's northwest corner — property-tax, permit and heritage questions for a Deer Lake house are answered in person minutes away. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/parks/deer-lake-park
- **Burnaby taxes have two penalty steps.** 2026 property taxes were due July 3, 2026; a 5% penalty applies after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date, and the detached water charge ($652.33) carries a 5% early-payment discount. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees
