## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. This applies city-wide, at every price point. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may enforce a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit short-term rentals by bylaw, and a tenant in a 55+ building must still satisfy the age bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Manufactured-home parks run on tenancy law, not strata law.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act governs pad tenancies such as Paradise Park, Georgian Park and Maplewood Estates: the resident owns the home and rents the pad, pad rent may be raised only once in 12 months on at least three full months' written notice, and the raise is capped at the provincial limit (2.3% for 2026) plus a proportional share of changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees. A park's age rule is a park rule, not a strata bylaw. http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Zoning: Abbotsford Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014 governs city-wide; there is no Abbotsford Zoning Bylaw 3100.** The small-scale multi-unit housing amendment is Bylaw No. 3560-2024, adopted June 25, 2024. The Official Community Plan is Bylaw 2600-2016 (Abbotsforward 2050, as updated). Sources: the Abbotsford West, Central Abbotsford and Poplar files in this set, citing the City's bylaw framework.
- **On the rural side of the band, the ALC frame governs added homes.** The Agricultural Land Commission's housing guidance caps a principal residence on ALR land at a total floor area of 500 m² or less, permits a secondary suite only within the principal residence, and limits an additional residence to up to 90 m² on properties of 40 ha or less and up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **On the hillside side of the band, slope rules govern additions.** Most hillside subdivisions on McKee Peak and Eagle Mountain sit inside the Steep Slope Development Permit Area, and the McKee mountainside is guided by the McKee Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 3400-2023), Schedule E of the Official Community Plan. Whether a specific parcel carries a registered geotechnical covenant is a title-by-title check: UNVERIFIED for any individual property. Source: the Abbotsford East file in this set, citing the OCP schedules.
- **Property transfer tax bites hardest in this band.** The Province's general rates are 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion above $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% on the portion above $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion of value above $3,000,000. Have a lawyer or notary model the exact bill from the current Province pages before offering. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Property taxes:** Abbotsford taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty applied July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on September 16, 2026. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice
- **First-time-buyer programs:** FHSA and Home Buyers' Plan dollar limits are UNVERIFIED in this file — no live CRA page stating them was read for this file, so no limit figure is stated here.

- **Q: What does Abbotsford's verified 55+ ownership stock actually consist of?** Twenty-six strata complexes city-wide on the verified roster: 15 in Abbotsford West, 9 in Central Abbotsford and 2 in Poplar, plus three 55+ manufactured-home parks and two life lease residences. Most of the strata buildings were built between 1979 and 1996 along the Clearbrook and Mill Lake corridors. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules exempt a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Some Abbotsford buildings state the one-owner version themselves: Royal Court's listing data says only one owner must be at least 55, and Regency Park's says one owner must be 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www.zolo.ca/abbotsford-real-estate/31930-old-yale-road/215
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy a 55+ unit as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ building?** For a long-term tenancy, yes: strata rental-restriction bylaws have been void across BC since Nov 24, 2022. The tenant still has to meet the 55+ bylaw or an exemption, and bylaws can still restrict short-term rentals. Manufactured-home parks are different — Georgian Park's own rules, per listings, do not allow rentals of the homes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: If I want a newer luxury home and 55+ rules, what is the actual trade-off in Abbotsford?** On verified sources, you choose one or the other. The luxury band is detached hillside houses and rural estates with no age bylaw at all — a freehold house carries no strata age restriction — while the 55+ ownership stock is the older attached roster in Section 2. A detached house bought in the band can still house a caregiver or family member through a secondary suite where zoning permits one; that is a zoning question, not an age-bylaw question. Sources: Sections 2 and 3 above.
- **Q: What property transfer tax should I budget on a home over $1.8M?** Under the general provincial tiers — 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion above $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — a home in this band is taxed mostly in the 2% tier. Exemption positions are fact-specific and are UNVERIFIED in this file; have a lawyer or notary model the exact tax before offering. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Can a luxury acreage in this band add a second home for family?** Where the parcel is in the Agricultural Land Reserve, the ALC frame applies: one principal residence capped at 500 m² of floor area, a suite only inside the principal residence, and at most one additional detached residence of 90 m² (parcels of 40 ha or less) or 186 m² (over 40 ha). ALR status is parcel-specific — confirm it before valuing any second-dwelling plan. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink my resale buyer pool?** In a 55+ strata, yes by design: the next buyer must be able to reside under the bylaw — 55+, an exempt spouse/partner, caregiver or legacy case — or buy without living in the unit. A freehold house in the over-$1.8M band carries no such restriction, which is part of why the two markets behave differently. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
