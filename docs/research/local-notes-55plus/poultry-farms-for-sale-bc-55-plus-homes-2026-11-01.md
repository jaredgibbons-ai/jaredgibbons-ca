# Poultry Farms for Sale BC — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**The plain answer for this page type: the 55+ angle on poultry farms is thin to the point of not existing, and this file says so with sources rather than dressing it up.** A poultry farm is a working, supply-managed farm operation on Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) land, held fee-simple, not in a strata. No source checked states any age restriction — 55+ or otherwise — on any poultry farm, farm residence or farm parcel, and no verified 55+ ownership complex, park, rental or care product is a poultry farm or sits as part of one. What a 55+ buyer actually meets on this page is (1) freehold farm houses with no age rules, (2) ALR rules that restrict WHO may occupy an additional manufactured home on a farm (owner and family — the opposite direction from an age bylaw), and (3) the quota and board-approval system that governs the business side. Precedent for this treatment: the poultry-farms file in this series.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now — and most farms are not strata.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022 (Bill 44). A freehold farm parcel has no strata corporation and therefore no age bylaw at all — the mechanism simply does not exist for the typical poultry listing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Where the strata rule would bite, it restricts residency, not ownership,** with exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01) for legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and — since May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the resident's care, and adult children cared for before 19. Strata rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide since Nov 24, 2022, though short-term rental bans remain allowed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **The ALR rule that actually governs farm housing: one principal residence per parcel.** Section 20.1 of the Agricultural Land Commission Act provides that, unless permitted by the Commission or the regulations, an owner may have no more than one residence per parcel of agricultural land; an additional residence beyond what the ALR Use Regulation permits requires a Non-Adhering Residential Use (NARU) application, and the Commission must not grant permission for an additional residence unless it is necessary for a farm use. A 55+ buyer planning a second dwelling "for the kids" or a caregiver cottage is inside this rule, not strata law. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_policy_l-26_-_non-adhering_residential_use_applications.pdf
- **The ALC housing frame, stated whole:** on ALR land, one principal residence is permitted without an ALC application at a maximum total floor area of 500 m² (no more than 1,000 m² of fill), a secondary suite is permitted only when located within the principal residence, and at most one additional residence is permitted of up to 90 m² on properties 40 ha or less or up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha; local government or First Nation government bylaws may further regulate or restrict residences, and may not be more permissive than the ALC's requirements. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Additional manufactured home on a farm: family occupancy only.** Under the ALR Use Regulation's additional-residence provisions (s. 34(3) as summarised in ALC Policy L-25), an additional residence that is a manufactured home and not a pre-existing structure is permitted only if it is 9 m or less in width and is used only by the owner or specified relatives of the owner (parent, grandparent or great-grandparent, sibling, or child, within the regulation's meaning). ALC Policy L-25 adds that a manufactured home permitted as an additional residence may only be occupied by the property owner or the owner's immediate family, and if it is no longer occupied as permitted it must be demolished, removed, or converted to a non-residential use. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf
- **Barns are protected farm structures.** Under the Agricultural Land Reserve Use Regulation, B.C. Reg. 30/2019, s. 5, a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation is a designated farm use that a local government enactment may not prohibit (it may be regulated), provided it is required for the farm use on the property and in line with its level — the wording under which a poultry barn is treated as a farm building in the ALR. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019 and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/763/2025/10/Necessary-Farm-Use-Structures-in-the-ALR-Guidelines.pdf
- **Quota, not the house, is the regulated asset.** For chicken, quota is the amount of chicken, in kilograms of live weight, the BC Chicken Marketing Board allots to a grower; the board's General Orders name mainstream and specialty classes, deal separately with transfer of quota with registered premises and acquisition/disposition of quota without registered premises, and the board approves quota transactions, checking that growers are in good standing. No board source states any age criterion for holding quota or being licensed as a grower — age plays no documented role in the quota system. (Board-system facts verified from board sources in the poultry-farms precedent file in this series; the batch-13 research pass for this page opened no marketing-board source of its own, and the live page's board account — BC Chicken Marketing Board, BC Turkey Marketing Board, BC Egg Marketing Board, BC Broiler Hatching Egg Commission — is recorded there as site copy.) https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf and https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html
- **Municipal zoning still applies parcel by parcel.** This topic has no single municipality, so there is no one zoning bylaw for it; for example, Chilliwack's Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000 defines POULTRY as any domesticated fowl including chickens, turkeys, ducks, geese and swans, and lists General Agriculture as a permitted principal use in its AL, AU and AS zones. The bylaw to read is the one for the municipality the specific farm sits in. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- **Farm classification is a continuing obligation, province-wide.** BC Assessment's minimum gross farm income requirements at farm gate prices are $2,500 if the farm operation is between 0.8 ha (1.98 acres) and 4 ha (10 acres), $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if the operation is greater than 4 ha, and $10,000 if the operation is less than 0.8 ha; applications must be submitted by the legislated deadline of October 31 for the next assessment and tax year, late applications are not accepted, and a buyer who plans to continue farming a farm-classified property must submit a General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Property transfer tax (province-wide general tiers):** 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion above $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% on the portion above $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion of value above $3,000,000. How PTT and GST are apportioned between land, buildings, residence and quota on a specific poultry purchase: UNVERIFIED — no Province GST/PTT farm-transfer source was opened for this topic. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Tax schedules are local.** Whether a specific poultry parcel follows a municipal schedule or the provincial rural regime (Taxation (Rural Area) Act, s.11: due July 2, 5% penalty on any portion unpaid on July 2, further 5% on any portion unpaid on October 31) depends on where the parcel sits — a per-parcel check, not a province-wide statement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/rural-area and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01
- **Biosecurity is a sale condition, not a footnote.** Highly pathogenic avian influenza is a reportable disease in Canada; the CFIA establishes primary control zones where it has been detected, permits are required to move birds, products and by-products through a control zone, and on a quarantined farm nothing bird-related enters or leaves without CFIA permission — which can affect showings, bird placement and completion timing on any poultry purchase, whatever the buyer's age. https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is there a 55+ poultry farm or an age-restricted farm community in BC?** No source checked states one exists. Poultry listings carry no age restriction, and the age-bylaw mechanism in BC belongs to strata corporations, which freehold farm parcels do not have. If a listing is ever marketed as "55+ farm living," treat it as UNCONFIRMED until a registered bylaw or park rule is produced. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html
- **Q: Does this page list poultry farms across all of BC?** No — as read, its feed showed four cards, all in Burnaby or Abbotsford, and only one described an operating commercial poultry operation; the other cards described poultry potential or poultry barns among other uses. The feed count is a snapshot, not a BC inventory. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html
- **Q: I'm 65 and want to retire onto a small poultry operation. What am I actually buying?** Land (usually ALR), the barn package, the farm residence, and quota only where the seller includes it — the one operating farm in the current feed states quota is not included, which is a common structure. You would also be stepping into a licensed business: growers are licensed by the BC Chicken Marketing Board annually, and quota transfers need board approval. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html and https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf
- **Q: Is quota included when I buy a poultry farm in BC?** Not as a default. The live page states quota is not included unless expressly stated in the Contract of Purchase and Sale (site copy, recorded as such), and the board's General Orders treat transfer of quota with registered premises and acquisition of quota without registered premises as separate cases, both board-approved. Read the specific contract and verify the transfer position with the applicable board before subjects are removed. Any quota price or per-unit value: UNVERIFIED — no official source publishes one. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Q: Does my age help or hurt a quota purchase?** No board source states any age criterion for quota allotment, transfer approval or grower licensing; the board's stated checks are good standing and appropriate allocation of production for the growing cycle. Age is simply not a documented factor — anything beyond that is UNCONFIRMED. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Q: Can I put a manufactured home on the farm for my parent, or for myself while the kids run the operation?** Possibly — the ALR Use Regulation permits an additional manufactured-home residence only within its conditions: 9 m or less in width, and occupied only by the owner or specified relatives (the regulation's list includes a parent). Outside those conditions, an additional residence needs ALC permission through a Non-Adhering Residential Use application, and the Commission must not grant it unless the residence is necessary for a farm use. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_policy_l-26_-_non-adhering_residential_use_applications.pdf
- **Q: Can I build a second house or a caregiver suite on the farm parcel?** The default ALR position is one residence per parcel (ALCA s. 20.1): a suite only inside the principal residence, and at most one additional residence at 90 m² (≤40 ha) or 186 m² (>40 ha). Anything beyond what the ALR Use Regulation permits needs a NARU application with a farm-necessity case — plan on that application, not on a building permit alone. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_policy_l-26_-_non-adhering_residential_use_applications.pdf
- **Q: What income must a poultry operation show to keep farm classification?** BC Assessment's thresholds are $2,500 for an operation of 0.8 ha to 4 ha, $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha for a larger operation, and $10,000 for an operation under 0.8 ha; applications are due by October 31 for the next tax year, late applications are not accepted, and a buyer continuing to farm must apply by October 31 after purchase. A buyer scaling back production in semi-retirement has to keep the operation above its threshold or lose the classification. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Q: Can a municipality stop me adding a poultry barn in the ALR?** No — under B.C. Reg. 30/2019 a structure for an intensive livestock operation is a designated farm use a local enactment may not prohibit, though it may regulate it, and the structure must be required for the farm use and in line with its level. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019
- **Q: What happens to my purchase during an avian influenza outbreak?** HPAI is reportable to the CFIA; in a primary control zone, permits are required to move birds, eggs and by-products, and on a quarantined farm nothing bird-related moves without CFIA permission — placements, showings and completion dates can all be delayed. Confirm the property's control-zone status before fixing a completion date. https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected
- **Q: Are market statistics published for poultry farms in BC?** No figure is stated in this file: SnapStats has no farm category, and no official source publishes BC poultry-farm counts, farms-for-sale counts, quota prices or per-acre poultry values — each is UNVERIFIED, and a price opinion stands on the specific property's land, barns, residence and any quota included, checked part by part. The live page itself states there is no single price figure — price is driven by quota holdings, barn capacity and condition, land and location, and that many sales are off-market; that is the page's own framing, recorded as site copy. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The BC-wide title hides a four-card local feed.** At reading, a page titled "Poultry Farms for Sale BC" surfaced exactly four listings — in Burnaby and Abbotsford only — and three of the four were not operating poultry farms at all (two were "potential" acreages and one was a mixed-barn property). Anyone searching this page for an Okanagan or Vancouver Island operation is looking at an empty shelf wearing a province-wide label. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html
- **"Quota not included" is the single most important line on these pages.** The one operating farm in the current feed states it outright, and the board's General Orders treat quota with premises and quota without premises as different transactions — a farm with barns and no quota is land and buildings, not a producing operation, whatever the photos suggest. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Quota is measured differently in every sector on the same page.** Broiler quota is kilograms of live weight per cycle; turkey quota is pounds of turkey meat per specified period; hatching-egg quota is hen units (mainstream) or hatched chicks (specialty). A "quota included" line means three different things depending on which board's sector the farm is in. https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf and https://bcturkey.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/174_66.pdf and https://bcbhec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2022-Annual-Report.pdf
- **There are no official market numbers for this topic at all.** SnapStats publishes no farm or quota category, and no official source publishes poultry quota transaction prices — any "market stats" attached to a poultry-farm page are residential figures for the surrounding municipality, not the farm market.
- **The second-dwelling dream runs into the ALR, not the city.** Retiring parents commonly picture a manufactured home beside the farmhouse; the ALR Use Regulation allows that only as a family-occupancy additional residence (owner or specified relatives, 9 m width cap for a non-pre-existing manufactured home), and ALC policy requires the home to be demolished, removed or converted to non-residential use once it is no longer occupied as permitted. That condition survives resale and belongs in the purchase conversation, not after it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf
- **Board approval is a closing condition with a clock on appeals.** The BC Farm Industry Review Board supervises the regulated marketing system and hears appeals of board decisions, with appeals to be filed within 30 calendar days of the board's final decision — a refused or conditioned quota transfer has a short, hard appeal window. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/organizational-structure/ministries-organizations/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/npma-appeals
- **The October 31 deadline is the farm buyer's analogue of a strata document deadline.** Farm classification does not simply carry over: a buyer continuing to farm must file BC Assessment's General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase, late applications are not accepted, and the operation must keep meeting its size-band income threshold. Miss it and the property is taxed without farm class for the year. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **The sibling slugs are one inventory.** poultry-farms and poultry-farms-for-sale-bc showed the same active listings under the same page title at research time in the precedent file — a buyer browsing both pages is looking at one feed, not two markets. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms.html
