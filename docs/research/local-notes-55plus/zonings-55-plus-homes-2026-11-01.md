# Zoning (Site-Wide Topic) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Framing:** this is not an area file. The "zonings" page is the site's zoning directory ("Zoning Guide: Langley, Surrey, Abbotsford & Delta BC") — a card directory of municipal zoning bylaw summaries for Abbotsford, the Township of Langley, Surrey and Delta, with a Mission rural-zone tail, an address-check tool, and an "ALR basics" section. These notes cover how **zoning interacts with 55+ housing across the site's whole market area**: the province-wide strata age law that sits on top of every zone, the manufactured-home park zones, the seniors-relevant zones in each bylaw the directory carries, and the suite/ALR rules that decide whether a downsizer can add a home for family. There are no complexes to count on a topic page — Section 2 says so plainly and explains where the 55+ product actually comes from. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html

Farm, rural and acreage zones get full cards in every municipality; urban residential zones get intent-line cards — the directory is built farm-first. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- The page carries a free property address-check tool (zoning, ALR status, lot size and other public map checks) covering Surrey, Langley City and Township, and Abbotsford addresses — notably **not** Delta or Mission addresses, even though both appear in the directory. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- The page states it was "Checked against each official bylaw on October 2, 2026" and carries a "general information only… not legal advice" disclaimer. Zoning is per-parcel: which zone applies to a specific property, its ALR status and what can be built on it are checks with the municipality and the ALC, for any address, in every city on this page. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- Where 55+ housing concentrates, in zoning terms: there is no "55+ zone" anywhere in these bylaws. Age restriction in BC is a **strata bylaw** layer (Section 3), not a zoning layer — a 55+ building can sit in an ordinary apartment or townhouse zone, and a manufactured-home park zone says nothing about age either. What zoning does decide is the *form* seniors housing can take in each city: apartment/townhouse zones for strata product, manufactured-home zones for park product, and suite/SSMUH rules for staying in a house with family or a caregiver. Sources: Sections 2–3 below.

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**A zoning directory has no buildings of its own, and no complex is verified or counted in this file.** The four subsection types below are answered at the topic level — what the zoning layer does and does not establish — because naming individual 55+ complexes belongs in the area files, not on a bylaw directory page.

### Verified 55+ strata (ownership)

- None verified **by zoning**, and none can be: no zone in the Abbotsford, Township of Langley, Surrey, Delta or Mission bylaws summarized on this page designates a "55+" use. A complex is verified 55+ only when a public source states its strata age bylaw (or its MLS/building record states "Restricted Age: 55+") — that verification lives in the area local-notes files. A zone card showing an apartment or townhouse zone is never, by itself, evidence that a building in it is 55+. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

### Verified 55+ rental (not ownership)

- Same position: rental tenure and any age targeting are established by the operator and its program rules (e.g., BC Housing directories), not by the zone. The directory's residential sets include the apartment zones this product sits in, but the zone text states uses and densities, not tenant ages. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html

### Retirement residences and care (rental/care — NOT strata ownership)

- Care uses appear in these bylaws as institutional / community-care type uses in specific zones and comprehensive-development (CD) zones, which is why care campuses so often sit on site-specific CD zoning — but the operating licence layer is provincial (BC's Assisted Living Registry and Fraser Health), not municipal zoning. Zoning can tell you a care use is permitted on a parcel; it cannot tell you a residence is registered, funded or has space. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html and https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence

### Subsidized seniors rental

- Non-market seniors housing is delivered by BC Housing and non-profit societies under program agreements, frequently on institutional or CD-zoned land secured through rezoning — again, the subsidy and the age/income targeting come from the program, not the zone. The directory's role for this stock is the per-parcel check: current zone, whether a seniors project would need a rezoning, and ALR status. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html

### Checked and NOT 55+ (do not put these on a 55+ page)

- **Any building whose only "55+" evidence is its zone.** An apartment, townhouse or manufactured-home zone is not an age restriction — do not label product 55+ from a zone card. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **"45+" / "40+" / "19+" marketing labels.** None of these is a valid strata age bylaw anywhere in BC since Nov 24, 2022 (Section 3) — and none is a zoning category in any bylaw on this page either. Never count a "45+" complex as 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Manufactured-home park zones are not seniors zones.** The Township's MH-1, Surrey's RM-M and Abbotsford's manufactured-home zones (below) are form zones open to all ages unless a park separately has a lawful age rule — the zone itself sets no age. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **Old Surrey zone codes in listing text.** "RF-12" and the other RF/RH/RQ codes no longer exist in that form — Surrey's R1–R7 zones replaced them on July 8, 2024 (RF-12/RF-12C → R4; RF-SD → R6). A listing quoting an RF code is describing a repealed bylaw label, not a current zone and not an age restriction. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html and https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/12000

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now — province-wide, every city on this page.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. No municipal bylaw can restore a lower age threshold — zoning does not govern strata age bylaws at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, in every municipality in this directory. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw, and municipalities additionally regulate short-term rentals through their own bylaws and licensing — two separate layers, both worth checking. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **The bylaws behind the directory (name AND number):** Abbotsford — the City of Abbotsford zoning bylaw as the page presents it (the A1–A6 agricultural family, RR/CR/SR/SRH rural cards, and the 67-zone residential set); Township of Langley — **Zoning Bylaw No. 2500**; Surrey — **Zoning By-law, 1993, No. 12000**; Delta — the Delta zoning bylaw set behind the A1–A3 and 62-zone residential cards (per-zone detail on the page; individual Delta/Mission/Abbotsford bylaw numbers beyond the Township and Surrey numbers above: UNCONFIRMED in these notes — take them from the page's own bylaw links, not from memory). https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html and https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/12000
- **Manufactured-home park zoning — the zone layer a park buyer meets first:** Abbotsford carries RSMH/RSH and RH1 (manufactured home park) zones; the Township of Langley carries the MH-1 Residential Mobile Home Park Zone (minimum new lot 1.7 ha per its directory card); Surrey carries the RM-M Manufactured Home Residential Zone. The tenancy layer on top of the zone — pad rent, park rules, and what happens on redevelopment — sits under the provincial Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, not in any of these zoning bylaws. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **SSMUH changed every city's house zones, three different ways.** The same provincial small-scale multi-unit housing legislation shows up in the directory as dedicated SSMUH 1–4 zones in Abbotsford, as Surrey's full R1–R7 replacement set (adopted July 8, 2024, former RF/RH/RQ codes shown on the cards), and inside the Township's SR/R zone text (e.g., SR-1 and SR-3 cards describing small-scale multi-unit housing developments). For a 55+ buyer this is the rule set that decides whether a house lot can add units for family or a caregiver without rezoning. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **Secondary suites — three regimes on one page, never to be mixed:** in Surrey a suite is capped at 40% of the principal dwelling unit's habitable floor area (a "90 m² cap" is NOT current — the 90 m² figure on this page is the ALR additional-residence limit, a different rule); on ALR land a suite is permitted only inside the principal residence; and Abbotsford's A1 card states its own suite figure of up to 100 m² under that city's bylaw as the page summarizes it. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **ALR housing frame (applies on top of zoning in every city here):** one principal residence without an ALC application at a maximum total floor area of 500 m² (no more than 1,000 m² of fill), a secondary suite only within that principal residence, and at most one additional residence of up to 90 m² on properties of 40 ha or less or up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha. Local governments can add further restrictions. Strata plans in the ALR cause subdivision (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s.6), and most subdivision of ALR land needs ALC approval, including parcels under 2 acres. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **Card-level caution:** the directory's per-zone summaries were checked by the page against each bylaw on October 2, 2026 per its header, and bylaws amend constantly (the Township's SR-3B card alone records an addition by Bylaw No. 6144 on July 27, 2026). Card detail beyond the consistency checks recorded in the research file is UNVERIFIED in these notes — confirm current zone text with the municipality before writing an offer that depends on it. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is there a "55+ zone" I should search in?** No. No municipality in this directory zones for age. Age restriction is a strata bylaw under the Strata Property Act (only 55+ is valid), layered on top of whatever ordinary residential zone the building sits in — so a "55+ search" is a bylaw-document search, not a zoning search. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. This is provincial law; no city in the directory can change it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building for a parent or as an investment?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out my unit in a 55+ building?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. The tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals (and the municipality may licence them separately). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: A listing says the complex is "45+" — is that real?** No. Since Nov 24, 2022, only 55+ age bylaws are valid in BC strata; 45+ (and 40+, 19+) bylaws are invalid wherever they still appear in old documents or listing text. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: We're downsizing to a manufactured home — what does the zoning tell us?** Which park form is lawful where: parks sit in dedicated manufactured-home zones (Abbotsford RSMH/RSH and RH1, Township MH-1, Surrey RM-M in this directory). The zoning tells you the park can exist there; your actual rights as a pad tenant come from the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and the park's own rules — and a park's zone says nothing about whether it is age-restricted. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **Q: Can I add a suite or a small second home so family can help us age in place?** Often yes, and zoning is exactly where the answer lives — but it differs by regime: Surrey caps a suite at 40% of the principal unit's habitable area; ALR land allows a suite only inside the principal residence plus one additional residence of 90 m² (≤40 ha) or 186 m² (>40 ha); SSMUH rules may allow up to four units on a house lot depending on the city's implementation (Abbotsford's SSMUH 1–4 zones, Surrey's R1–R7 set, the Township's SR/R text). Check the specific parcel's zone card, then the bylaw. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: My Surrey listing says RF-12 — what zone is the lot actually in now?** R4. Surrey's R1–R7 zones replaced the RF, RH and RQ zones on July 8, 2024 (RF-12 and RF-12C became R4; RF-SD became R6), and the directory's Surrey cards show the former code on each card so old listing text can be translated. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html and https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/12000
- **Q: Can a care home or seniors residence be built on a residential street?** Only if the zone (or a site-specific CD zone created by rezoning) permits the institutional/community-care use — which is why so many care campuses sit on CD zoning. Whether the finished residence is a registered assisted-living residence is a separate provincial question answered by the Assisted Living Registry, not by the zoning map. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html and https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence
- **Q: Can I just rely on the zone card instead of checking with the city?** No. The page itself says summaries may not reflect recent amendments, it was last checked against the bylaws on October 2, 2026, and one mid-page segment (the Delta tail / Mission opening) could not even be re-read for the research file after the fetch service returned a 429 — that segment's detail is UNVERIFIED. Confirm the zone, permitted uses and ALR status for the specific parcel with the municipality and the ALC before buying. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Surrey's 2024 zone replacement is the single biggest "old listing text is wrong" trap in the market area.** R1–R7 replaced RF/RH/RQ on July 8, 2024 under provincial SSMUH legislation — any pre-2024 document, old MLS sheet or remembered zone code for a Surrey lot needs translating through the directory's former-code cards before it is relied on. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **The Township is still adding zones.** The directory's SR-3B card records that zone as added by Bylaw No. 6144 on July 27, 2026 — a live example of why the page carries a check date (October 2, 2026) and why a card is a summary, not the bylaw. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **The directory itself flags two bylaw naming inconsistencies** worth keeping in the rebuild: the Township's RU-6 zone is named "Rural Processing Zone" in s.104.1 while its Section 200 heading reads "Agricultural Processing Zone," and RU-11 carries the same kind of split ("Rural Estate Winery Zone" / "Rural Winery Zone"). If a search or a legal description uses the other name, it is the same zone. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **Farm class is the zoning-adjacent question acreage buyers forget.** BC Assessment's farm-class thresholds are $2,500 gross annual farm income for an operation of 0.8–4 ha, $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha for a larger operation, and $10,000 for an operation under 0.8 ha, with applications due by October 31 — the directory's buyer checklist points farm buyers at exactly this check. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification and https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
- **The page's address tool and its directory don't cover the same ground.** The free address check covers Surrey, Langley (City and Township) and Abbotsford only — a Delta or Mission address can be read in the directory but not run through the tool, so those parcels go straight to the municipality. https://jaredgibbons.ca/zonings.html
