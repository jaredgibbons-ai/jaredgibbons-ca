# Mission Foreclosures and Court Sales — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Topic scope:** this is a city-wide topic page for the City of Mission (unhyphenated slug: missionforeclosuresandcourtsales), not a neighbourhood page. In BC a "court-ordered sale" is a sale conducted under a court order inside a foreclosure proceeding; it is not a separate process, and "bank foreclosure" is a misnomer here — sales run through the court, not the bank, per this slug's research file. Section 2 is Mission's verified 55+ stock (the homes a 55+ buyer is actually watching for if one enters a court process); Section 4 answers court-sale questions only from official sources or marks them UNCONFIRMED. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. A court-ordered sale changes who owns the unit; it does not remove the age bylaw for whoever will live there. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. Cherry Lane's listing wording "55+ Community with Family Exemptions" points at this same framework — confirm the complex's registered bylaws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm and https://www.zolo.ca/mission-real-estate/32165-7-avenue/5
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The foreclosure process is provincial, and it sits on top of the strata rules:** a proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7 (1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Redemption and confirmation:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period that the court may fix — Rule 21-7 (5) (b); the Rules state no set length for that period. In default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted — Rule 21-7 (6). Even though the time for redemption has not expired, the person having conduct of a sale may apply for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7 (9). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct-of-sale mechanics (Rule 13-5 and practice direction PD-66): UNVERIFIED in this series** — neither text was retrieved from an official source on 2026-11-01 for the underlying research, so no upset-price, reserve-price or competing-bid mechanic is stated in this file. Get the seller's counsel's bid instructions for the specific file in writing.
- **Zoning: Zoning Bylaw 5949-2020** is Mission's zoning bylaw (verified on mission.ca Nov 1, 2026 — City amending bylaws cite "District of Mission Zoning Bylaw 5949-2020" as the bylaw being amended). https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-zoning-bylaw-housekeeping-amendments
- **Current plan: Official Community Plan Bylaw 6405-2026** replaces and repeals OCP Bylaw 6350-2025, keeping the same vision, land use framework, mapping and policy direction with a Regional Context Statement added (verified on mission.ca Nov 1, 2026 — the 5948-2020 number in older references is superseded; the current OCP chain is 6350-2025, replaced by 6405-2026). https://www.mission.ca/council-government/news/notice-public-hearing-april-7-2026 and https://www.mission.ca/media/file/public-hearing-notice-april-7-2026
- **Manufactured-home parks run on tenancy law, not strata law.** Pad tenancies at Mission Mobile Home Park and Oasis Country Estates fall under BC's Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act: the resident owns the home and rents the pad, pad rent may be raised only once in 12 months on at least three full months' written notice, and the raise is capped at the provincial limit (2.3% for 2026) plus a proportional share of changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees. http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Life lease is its own legal animal.** At Cedar Valley Manor the operator states the resident does not own the unit or the building and instead purchases an interest (the right to occupy), with the original entrance fee returned at termination less a set percentage for operation costs — no strata Form B, no title transfer. https://www.missionseniors.ca/our-communities/cedar-valley-manor/
- **Property taxes:** Mission's 2026 property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a flat 10% penalty added to any current balances outstanding on July 3, 2026 (Mission has no staged 5% + 5% scheme); additional taxes are levied for diking on top of the municipal, school, regional district and BC Assessment levies. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes and https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-rates

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5) (b). Any source quoting a standard number of months for a Mission file is not quoting the Rules. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What does court confirmation mean for my offer on a Mission home?** The person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7 (9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires, so until the order is made the sale is not final. Do not book movers on acceptance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** UNVERIFIED as a stated process rule in this file — the bid-rights wording in Rule 13-5 and the PD-66 bid timing were not retrieved from an official source on 2026-11-01. Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for, and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing before spending on inspections.
- **Q: Where can I check the court file for a Mission foreclosure?** Court Services Online (https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do) offers eSearch to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: Which court registry serves Mission?** UNVERIFIED as a stated mapping. Mission has no Supreme Court registry on the BC Courts locations list retrieved for the underlying research; that list shows a Supreme Court registry in neighbouring Abbotsford (32375 Veterans Way) and in Chilliwack (46085 Yale Road). Which registry a specific Mission file runs through is a fact of that file — read it in Court Services Online. https://www.bccourts.ca/supreme_court/locations.htm?location=kamloops and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually BUY in Mission if one comes up in a court process?** Only strata, life-lease or manufactured-home product can be bought at all: the verified buyable 55+ stock is Cherry Lane (32165 7th Ave, 20 townhouses, NWS2971) and Luther Place (7525 Martin Place, NWS2729) as strata; Cedar Valley Manor (8358 Cedar St) as a life-lease occupancy interest; and manufactured homes on rented pads at Mission Mobile Home Park (34519 Lougheed Hwy). The co-op (St. Andrew's Place), the rental buildings and the care residences cannot be bought. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: If I buy a Cherry Lane or Luther Place unit at a court-ordered sale, does the 55+ bylaw still apply to me?** Yes — the age bylaw runs with the strata corporation, not with the seller. The court's vesting order on confirmation vests title in the purchaser, but who may LIVE in the unit is still governed by the 55+ bylaw and the Regulation 7.01 exemptions (qualifying resident, legacy resident, live-in caregiver, younger spouse/partner, and the defined child cases). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: What taxes do I pay on a court-ordered purchase — PTT and GST?** UNVERIFIED in this file for a court-ordered purchase specifically: the underlying research could not load the Province's PTT page or an official GST source on 2026-11-01, so no PTT rate and no GST treatment is stated here. Have your lawyer cost both taxes on the specific property before you set your ceiling.
- **Q: Are Mission foreclosures discounted by a standard percentage?** No published source supports a standard foreclosure discount — UNVERIFIED. Foreclosure-specific market numbers (counts of Mission properties in foreclosure, court-ordered sale counts, discount statistics) are not published by SnapStats or any official source found, and Mission's general attached median rests on 10 solds, so value a specific unit from its own complex's sales, not from a discount rule or the community median (as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report).

- **One non-profit runs most of Mission's seniors housing ladder.** The Mission Association for Seniors Housing (MASH) operates The Cedars (7380 Hurd St), Welton Towers (33214 2nd Ave) and, through its life lease society, Cedar Valley Manor (8358 Cedar St), and states all of its housing is for people 55 and older. https://www.missionseniors.ca/our-communities/cedar-valley-manor/ and https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/About~the~FVRD/Documents/Homelessness/2020%20Mission%20Housing%20Inventory.pdf
- **Mission's tax penalty is a single flat step, not the staged pattern in some neighbouring cities.** Taxes were due July 2, 2026 and a flat 10% penalty landed on outstanding current balances on July 3, 2026 — a one-day miss costs the full 10%, and diking levies ride on top of the municipal rate. - **The rural 55+ option comes with dyke geography.** Mission Mobile Home Park sits in Hatzic, on the Hatzic Prairie side of the city behind the Dewdney Dyke system, on Lougheed Highway minutes from downtown by car — flood status is parcel-specific, so confirm the parcel's flood position and the pad tenancy terms together. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Bylaws/Planning~and~Land~Use/Area%20F%20-%20OCP%20Bylaw%20999%20-%20Hatzic%20Valley.pdf and https://www.cotala.com/tours/85367/FullPublic.pdf?i=979581669
- **The seller in a foreclosure sale is not an owner preparing the home for market in the ordinary way.** The sale runs inside a court proceeding under Rule 21-7, and buyers should expect the conditions in the court-approved form for that file, not a standard residential contract — the Rule 13-5 directions wording behind this point is UNVERIFIED in this series, so read the actual directions for the file. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
