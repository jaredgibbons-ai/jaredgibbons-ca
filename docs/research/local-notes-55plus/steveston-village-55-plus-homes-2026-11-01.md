# Steveston Village — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Steveston Village is the historic village core within the Steveston planning area of the City of Richmond, governed by the Steveston Area Plan (Schedule 2.4 of the City Official Community Plan). The City designates the core as the Steveston Village Heritage Conservation Area in the Steveston Area Plan. "Steveston Village" is also the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® (GVR) board sub-area name for the core. https://www.richmond.ca/business-development/planning/heritage/village-conservation.htm?PageMode=HTML
- Rough extent of the core: along Moncton Street and Chatham Street between No. 1 Road to the east and 7th Avenue and the waterfront to the west, with the South Arm of the Fraser River and Dyke Road to the south and Steveston Highway to the north. Properties inside the heritage conservation area are subject to Heritage Alteration Permit requirements under the Steveston Area Plan. https://citycouncil.richmond.ca/__shared/assets/CNCL_062209_Bylaw_840323090.pdf and https://www.richmond.ca/business-development/planning/heritage/village-conservation.htm?PageMode=HTML
- Village anchors: the Gulf of Georgia Cannery at 12138 4th Avenue, a national historic site; Garry Point Park, a 39-acre waterfront park at the mouth of the Fraser River close to the village; Imperial Landing Waterfront Park on the historic BC Packers cannery site; Steveston Community Park, 4011 Moncton Street, a 30-acre park with the Steveston Community Centre, pool and library in the park complex; Richmond Public Library's Steveston Branch (Easthope Hub) at 4320 Moncton Street. Shops, markets and services centre on Moncton Street, and the harbour has a public fish sales float. https://parks.canada.ca/culture/designation/lieu-site/gulf-of-georgia and https://www.richmond.ca/parks-recreation/parks-trails-cycling/trails-map/steveston.htm
- Transit: buses serve Steveston Exchange on Chatham Street, including TransLink route 401 via No. 1 Road to Richmond–Brighouse Station for Canada Line connections. Main roads: Moncton Street through the core, No. 1 Road north to Richmond centre, Steveston Highway east to Highway 99, Railway Avenue at the east side of the village, and No. 2 Road north from the village. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt401.pdf
- Schools: public schools in the Steveston area beside the village core include Westwind Elementary School, 11371 Kingfisher Drive, and R.A. McMath Secondary School, 4251 Garry Street. School District 38 catchments are address-based with no middle school tier, and the SD38 locator returned no result for the sample village address 4011 Moncton Street because it falls outside the locator's listed ranges for Moncton Street — village catchment assignments stay UNVERIFIED. https://sd38.bc.ca/schools/locations-catchments
- The village sits on the South Arm of the Fraser River behind the City dike system, and the City's Steveston Island Dike project addresses flood protection for the Steveston waterfront and harbour. Nearest hospital: Richmond Hospital, 7000 Westminster Highway. https://www.letstalkrichmond.ca/StevestonIslandDike
- Where 55+ housing concentrates: **the Chatham Street / No. 1 Road corner of the core.** The Maple Residences (4071 Chatham Street) and Bill Rigby Senior Apartments (4100 Chatham Street) stand on the same Chatham Street block at No. 1 Road, backing onto Steveston Community Park, and Austin Harris Residence (5411 Moncton Street) is on Moncton Street further west in the core. All three are rental, assisted or subsidized — the village core has no 55+ ownership stock. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 below.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Heritage rule — the defining rule of this sub-area:** Steveston Village is designated as a heritage conservation area in the Steveston Area Plan, protected heritage buildings and other resource types such as docks and landscape features are identified in the Official Community Plan, and properties inside the conservation area are subject to Heritage Alteration Permit requirements. A buyer of a village house or a mixed-use strata unit is buying into alteration controls other Richmond neighbourhoods do not carry. https://www.richmond.ca/business-development/planning/heritage/village-conservation.htm?PageMode=HTML
- **Plan:** the Steveston Area Plan (Schedule 2.4 of the Official Community Plan, Bylaw 7100), within the City of Richmond 2041 Official Community Plan framework, Bylaw 9000. https://richmond.ca/__shared/assets/steveston566.pdf
- **City zoning: Richmond Zoning Bylaw 8500.** Houses on village side streets sit in the Single Detached zones RS1 and RS2, with secondary suite rules in Section 5.4 (a suite must be at least 33.0 m² and no more than the lesser of 40% of the dwelling's floor area or 110 m²); village commercial and mixed-use zones named by the City include Steveston Commercial (CS3), Commercial Mixed Use (ZMU32) – Steveston Village, and Steveston Office Commercial (ZC49) – Chatham Street (Steveston). https://Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/RS1-RS2-RS324187.pdf
- **Suites and added units:** the City states it allows at least one secondary suite and/or accessory dwelling unit per lot, with Bill 44 small-scale multi-unit minimums of 3 units on lots of 280 m² or less and 4 units on larger lots — subject, inside the conservation area, to the heritage permit rule above. https://RICHMOND.CA/business-development/planning/housing-legislation.htm
- **Flood:** the village is behind the City dike system on the Fraser River floodplain (perimeter dikes with drainage pump stations, designed for a 1:500 event, dike raising from 3.5 m to 4.7 m under way); the Flood Plain Designation and Protection Bylaw 8204 sets dike setbacks and flood construction levels, and the Steveston Island Dike project — a proposed dike around Steveston Island from London Farm to Garry Point Park with a navigation gate for Steveston Harbour — is the City's long-term project for this waterfront. https://www.Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Bylaw_8204_0410201225280.pdf and https://www.letstalkrichmond.ca/StevestonIslandDike
- **Property taxes (city-wide Richmond rule):** property taxes are due once a year on the first business day of July — Thursday, July 2, 2026 — with a 5% penalty on any unpaid balance after that date and a further 5% penalty on balances still outstanding after September 1, 2026. https://richmond.ca/city-hall/finance/rates/duedates-taxes.htm
- **Subsidized seniors housing runs through BC Housing's Housing Registry** for this area (Zone 3: Richmond and West Delta directory, May 2025); assisted living at Austin Harris Residence runs through Vancouver Coastal Health assessment instead — two different doors for two different products on two village streets. https://learningondemand.bchousing.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Zone-3-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf and https://successbc.ca/assisted-living/services/austin-harris-residence/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Steveston Village?** None with an age restriction — no 55+ strata was verified in the core. The buyable stock is heritage-area detached houses and general-market mixed-use strata on Moncton/Chatham; the seniors-specific buildings in the core (The Maple Residences, Austin Harris Residence, Bill Rigby) are rental, assisted and subsidized. Richmond's verified 55+ ownership stock is outside Steveston (Quilchena Green in Terra Nova; Covenant Court on No. 2 Road). Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Is The Maple Residences a condo I can buy?** No. The Maple Residences at 4071 Chatham Street are 144 rental units owned and managed by the non-profit Steveston 284 Seniors' Housing Society, opened in 2012 at No. 1 Road and Chatham; all suites are rented as fully serviced suites with the services included in the monthly rental fee. https://pagesarchive.blackpress.ca/BC/Processed/RIR/120801/RIRN120801_A15.pdf and https://www.steveston.bc.ca/documents/201110_VillagerNewsletter_OCTOBER_FINAL._000.pdf
- **Q: How does Austin Harris Residence work — can I apply directly?** Not directly for a market suite: it is an affordable assisted-living residence (50 one-bedroom suites, S.U.C.C.E.S.S. Multi-Level Care Society) developed under the Independent Living BC Program, where residents pay 70% of after-tax income, and registration and eligibility assessment run through Vancouver Coastal Health's Home and Community Care Services Team. It is registered with BC's Assisted Living Registry, class SN. https://successbc.ca/assisted-living/services/austin-harris-residence/ and https://www.vch.ca/en/location/austin-harris-residence
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. This is the strata rule; the village's rental and assisted buildings set their own admission rules, which are UNCONFIRMED for age in the sources checked. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy a unit in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out my unit in a 55+ building?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals — a live point in the village, where short-term rental pressure from waterfront visitors is the version of the rule a buyer will actually meet. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: What does the heritage conservation area actually stop me doing?** Exterior alterations to properties inside the Steveston Village Heritage Conservation Area require a Heritage Alteration Permit under the Steveston Area Plan, and protected heritage buildings and resource types such as docks and landscape features are identified in the Official Community Plan. It does not stop a purchase — it changes renovation plans, timelines and resale positioning. https://www.richmond.ca/business-development/planning/heritage/village-conservation.htm?PageMode=HTML
- **Q: Which schools would grandchildren use?** UNVERIFIED for village addresses: the SD38 locator returned no result for the sample address 4011 Moncton Street because it falls outside the locator's listed ranges for Moncton Street, and SD38 has no middle school tier. Westwind Elementary (11371 Kingfisher Drive) and R.A. McMath Secondary (4251 Garry Street) are the public schools in the Steveston area — confirm schooling for the exact address with SD38 directly. https://sd38.bc.ca/schools/locations-catchments
- **Q: What transit serves the village?** Buses serve Steveston Exchange on Chatham Street, including TransLink route 401 to Richmond–Brighouse Station via No. 1 Road, for Canada Line connections. Other route numbers serving Steveston Exchange (402, 406, 407) are UNCONFIRMED on an official timetable in this pass. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt401.pdf
- **Q: Is the village in a floodplain?** Yes — the village is behind the City dike system on the Fraser River floodplain, Bylaw 8204 sets dike setbacks and flood construction levels, and the City's Steveston Island Dike project addresses long-term flood protection for the Steveston waterfront and harbour. https://www.letstalkrichmond.ca/StevestonIslandDike

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The village's biggest seniors building exists because a veterans' branch gave up its land.** The Maple Residences project was developed by Steveston 284 Seniors' Housing Society — formed by the Army, Navy and Air Force Veterans of Canada (Unit 284) with Steveston (Unit 284) Holdings Ltd. — on the ANAF site at No. 1 Road and Chatham Street, as two buildings in an "H" pattern with 144 seniors' units plus a commercial building containing the ANAF lounge and clubhouse. That is why the core's seniors housing is non-profit rental beside a veterans' clubhouse, not a strata building. https://www.steveston.bc.ca/documents/201110_VillagerNewsletter_OCTOBER_FINAL._000.pdf
- **Two seniors buildings share one Chatham Street block, with two different admission doors.** The Maple Residences (market-style serviced rental, apply to the society) and Bill Rigby Senior Apartments (subsidized, apply through BC Housing's Housing Registry) stand at 4071 and 4100 Chatham Street — a buyer touring one will see the other, and they are not the same product, price system or application route. https://learningondemand.bchousing.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Zone-3-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf
- **The assisted-living building on Moncton Street is priced as a share of income, not a rent.** Austin Harris Residence residents pay 70% of after-tax income for the suite plus hospitality services under the Independent Living BC Program — a fact no listing or directory headline states, and the reason it cannot be compared monthly-rate-to-monthly-rate with The Maple Residences two streets away. https://successbc.ca/assisted-living/services/austin-harris-residence/
- **The school locator simply has no answer for Moncton Street.** The SD38 locator returned no result for 4011 Moncton Street because the address falls outside its listed ranges for the street — village catchment marketing in a listing is therefore a claim to verify with the district directly, not a fact to repeat. https://sd38.bc.ca/schools/locations-catchments
- **Heritage protection here covers more than houses.** The Steveston Area Plan's conservation area identifies protected heritage buildings and other resource types such as docks and landscape features in the Official Community Plan — a waterfront village lot can carry protected features that are not a building at all. https://www.richmond.ca/business-development/planning/heritage/village-conservation.htm?PageMode=HTML
