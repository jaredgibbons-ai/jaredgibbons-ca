# Surrey Homes Over $1.8M — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Scope note:** this file covers the City of Surrey as a whole, for the price-band slug. The $1.8M band comes from the page slug and its research file only; no price is stated for any complex in this file. **The honest core of this file: Surrey's verified 55+ strata stock sits at the opposite end of the price spectrum from $1.8M+ detached homes.** The research file's own SnapStats reading makes the gap concrete at community level — Surrey's detached median sale price was $1,215,000 in September 2026 and South Surrey / White Rock's detached median was $1,628,000, both below the page's $1.8M floor, and SnapStats publishes no price-band cut, so no count or share of homes over $1.8M is stated anywhere here (as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report, via the slug's research file). A 55+ buyer and a $1.8M-band buyer are almost never shopping the same feed — the connection between the two pages is the downsizer who sells in one and buys in the other, which is what Section 4 addresses.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Property transfer tax — the general tiers a downsizer budgets on BOTH ends of the move:** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000. PTT is based on the fair market value on the day the property is registered at the Land Title Office, and it is not the same as annual property taxes. There is no PTT on selling — PTT is a buyer's tax, paid on the purchase the downsizer makes into the 55+ strata. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Further tax over $3,000,000:** if a property has residential property worth over $3,000,000, a further 2% tax applies to the residential property value greater than $3,000,000; on a mixed-class property the further 2% applies only to the residential portion. Relevant to a purchase in this slug's band, not to a typical 55+ strata purchase. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Neither BC purchase exemption reaches this band — and neither helps a repeat buyer downsizing into 55+:** the first time home buyers' program full exemption caps at a fair market value of $835,000 (partial to under $860,000) and requires the buyer to have never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world; the newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 with elimination at $1,150,000. A downsizer selling a $1.8M+ home has owned before by definition, so the first-time program is unavailable on the conditions as published — budget full PTT on the 55+ purchase at the general tiers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Home owner grant phase-out, directly relevant at this band:** the Province states the grant threshold for 2026 is $2,075,000 — a full regular grant at or below that assessed (or partitioned) value, reduced by $5 for each $1,000 of assessed value over $2,075,000, and $0 for the regular grant if the value is over $2,189,000. A seller leaving a home in this band may have been receiving a reduced grant or none; the 55+ strata they buy into is assessed on its own value for the grant they claim there. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **Manufactured-home parks run on a different statute.** Pad tenancies in BC manufactured-home parks are governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA) — the law that applies where a tenant rents a pad site but owns the manufactured home; where both the pad and the dwelling are rented, the Residential Tenancy Act applies instead. Age rules in a park are park rules/policies, not strata bylaws — get them in writing from the park, with the tenancy agreement. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
- **Surrey's manufactured-home park redevelopment policy:** the City's policy brochure states that if you own your manufactured home and pay pad rent, the MHPTA gives you rights; the property owner must give 12 months' notice of a final moving date using a Notice to End Tenancy, which can only be given after the owner receives all their permits, and the City's Manufactured Home Park Policy (stated in the brochure to be guidelines Council uses, not a law) requires an affordable housing program for residents when a park is redeveloped. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
- **Membership complexes are neither strata nor freehold purchase in the usual sense.** Sullivan Mews' own listings state it is a membership, not a strata, and that mortgages are not possible — financing, resale and transfer rules come from the membership documents, not the Strata Property Act. That matters to a downsizer planning to fund the purchase from a house sale with no mortgage bridge: read the membership transfer rules before the house sale completes. https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r3058951-15020-66a-avenue-49-surrey-bc-v3s-2a5/brochure
- **Service-plan style charges:** no verified 55+ strata complex in Surrey in Section 2 carries a stated mandatory service plan of the kind found in some other cities' 55+ buildings in this series — UNCONFIRMED for Surrey's complexes; any monthly charge beyond strata fees would be stated in the individual complex's strata documents and listing, and must be confirmed there before it is published. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **City zoning: Surrey Zoning Bylaw, 1993, No. 12000** governs the whole city, including its small-scale multi-unit housing zones RA to R6: up to 3 units on lots under 280 sq m, up to 4 units on lots over 280 sq m, and up to 6 units on eligible lots within 400 m of a frequent bus stop. This affects the detached streets a downsizer is selling from, not the strata / membership / park lots they are buying into. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **Property taxes (city-wide):** City of Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I am selling a Surrey home over $1.8M and downsizing. Is there any 55+ product at anywhere near that price point in Surrey?** No verified 55+ ownership product in Surrey is marketed as upper-end or luxury product in any source checked — the verified stock is 1989–2000 strata townhouses and condos (Manor Ridge 1989, Peninsula Village circa 1991–1993, Sullivan Mews 1994, St. Martin's Lane 1995, The Crescent 2000), a membership bungalow complex, and two manufactured-home parks. No price is stated for any complex in this file; the point is the product type, not a figure: the $1.8M band is detached houses, and the 55+ supply is strata, membership and pad-rent forms. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Do I pay property transfer tax when I SELL the $1.8M+ home?** No — PTT is paid by the buyer when a property is registered at the Land Title Office, based on fair market value on registration day. Your tax event is on the purchase side: the 55+ home you buy is taxed at the general tiers (1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000), with the further 2% only on residential value over $3,000,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Can I use the first-time buyer exemption on the 55+ purchase? I have not bought in years.** The program's conditions as published require that you have never owned a registered interest in a property that was your principal residence anywhere in the world, and never have received the exemption before — a seller downsizing out of a home they owned does not meet that condition, however long ago the purchase was. The full exemption also caps at a fair market value of $835,000 in any case. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. If we sell the house and buy into a 55+ strata, can we both live there?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). The complexes' own listings soften it further in their own words: Lakewood Heights states only one occupant needs to meet the age requirement, Manor Ridge states 1 occupant must be 55+, and Peninsula Village states one person 55+. Confirm the complex's bylaw in its strata documents. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://sothebysrealty.ca/en/property/british-columbia/region-fraser-valley/surrey-real-estate/3076580/6211-w-boundary-drive-no13/ and https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/6140-192-street/8
- **Q: What if a family member under 55 needs to live with us in the 55+ unit?** The Regulation 7.01 exemptions cover defined cases only: a live-in caregiver; a child under 19 in the specified resident's care; and an adult child the specified resident cared for before that child turned 19. Anyone outside those cases cannot reside in the unit unless they meet the age requirement or hold a legacy exemption — read the bylaw before assuming a multigenerational move works. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Is there a second monthly charge on top of strata fees in Surrey's 55+ buildings — a service plan?** None was verified for any Surrey complex in Section 2 — UNCONFIRMED. Unlike service-plan buildings documented elsewhere in this series, no Surrey listing or directory entry checked states a mandatory meal/housekeeping service plan; if a specific unit's listing states one, treat it as listing-stated for that unit and confirm it in the strata documents and any service agreement. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Could I buy the 55+ unit first, rent it out while my house sells, then move in?** In a strata, long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches: the tenant must still satisfy the 55+ bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. In the parks and at Sullivan Mews, rental and occupancy rules are the park's / membership's own documents, not strata law. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Does the home owner grant change when I move?** The grant is claimed on the principal residence you occupy, and at this slug's band the phase-out is the live issue: for 2026 the threshold is $2,075,000, the regular grant reduces by $5 per $1,000 over that value, and it is $0 over $2,189,000. Leaving a home assessed in that range and claiming on a lower-assessed 55+ strata is a different grant calculation — confirm the assessed value of the specific unit, not the old house. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **Q: Are pets allowed when we downsize?** Complex by complex: Manor Ridge listings allow 1 pet with restrictions; Peninsula Village listings state pets ok; Sullivan Mews listings allow 1 small pet; Westwood Estates listings allow large dogs with park approval, no vicious breeds. A large dog from the detached house is a real filter — check it complex by complex before shortlisting. https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/6140-192-street/8 and https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3114342-60-8254-134-street-surrey-british-columbia-v3w6m2-218800/brochure
- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink my resale buyer pool when I eventually sell the strata unit?** Mechanically, yes: the next buyer must also be able to live there under the bylaw — be 55+, be an exempt spouse/partner, caregiver or legacy case, or buy without living in the unit. That is the trade-off built into every BC 55+ strata since the 2022 law change confined age bylaws to 55+, and it applies on top of the already thin upper tail this slug's band represents on the detached side. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The two pages this file connects almost never overlap in inventory.** SnapStats publishes no price-band cut, and even the South Surrey / White Rock detached median ($1,628,000, September 2026) sits below the $1.8M floor — so the band is by construction the upper tail of the detached market, while every verified 55+ complex in Section 2 is a strata, membership or park form from 1989–2000. A buyer searching only the over-$1.8M feed will never see a 55+ listing, and a buyer searching only 55+ pages will never see the band (as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report, via the slug's research file).
- **One sub-area name does double duty and misleads both searches.** Panorama Ridge appears as a label on the over-$1.8M feed's cards and is also the MLS filing for Lakewood Heights and Lakewood Place, the Boundary Park 55+ townhouses — while the third Boundary Park complex, Lakeview Estates, is filed under West Newton. The sub-area name tells you nothing about which product, or which price world, a listing belongs to. https://jaredgibbons.ca/surrey-homes-over-1-8m.html and https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/6090-w-boundary-drive/104
- **Surrey's 55+ supply comes in three different legal forms, and a downsizer's sale proceeds meet three different purchase mechanics.** Strata (Form B, strata fees, the Strata Property Act), membership (Sullivan Mews — no strata lot, and its own listings state mortgages are not possible), and pad-rent manufactured homes (Westwood Court, Westwood Estates — MHPTA, a 12-month redevelopment-notice regime under the City's park policy). The form decides the documents and the exit before the floor plan matters. https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r3058951-15020-66a-avenue-49-surrey-bc-v3s-2a5/brochure and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
- **Two Surrey parks share almost the same name, in two different communities.** Westwood **Court** is the Fleetwood park at 8670 156 Street; Westwood **Estates** is the Newton park on 134 Street, whose own listings disagree on 8224 vs 8254 as the civic address. A listing alert set on one name does not cover the other park. https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/8670-156-street/9 and https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3121759-103-8224-134-street-surrey-british-columbia-v3w4v1-249800/brochure
- **No new age-restricted strata supply is being added anywhere in the city in any source checked.** The complexes in Section 2 are 1989–2000 stock — so a downsizer leaving a newer $1.8M+ detached home is moving into an older building stock, and the depreciation report, not the listing photos, is where the real comparison happens. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 above.
- **A care-sounding name in Newton is a recovery residence, not seniors housing.** Cedars at Panorama Ridge (6811 135A St) is class SU (supportive recovery) in the provincial Assisted Living Registry, not a seniors (SN) residence — the clearest Surrey example of why a name or "seniors" marketing language is never a substitute for the registry class or the filed bylaw. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ASurrey&address=&name=&page=2&order=alr_residence_type&sort=desc
