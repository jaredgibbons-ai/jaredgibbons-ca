## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The law that governs this page itself — CASL consent:** the CRTC states that when sending a commercial electronic message (e.g. email), you must (1) obtain prior consent from the recipient (either express or implied); (2) provide identification and contact information; and (3) include a working unsubscribe mechanism. A REALTOR newsletter carrying latest listings and market updates is a commercial electronic message on the CRTC's own examples, because a CEM includes offers to purchase, sell, barter or lease land and messages promoting a person as someone who does those things. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti/reg.htm and http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Express consent does not expire, and the sender must prove it:** the CRTC FAQ states express consent can be obtained in writing or orally, the recipient may withdraw it at any time, and the onus is on the person sending the message to prove they obtained consent. Whether the current signup flow creates and keeps such a consent record is UNVERIFIED — neither the form nor the provider could be seen in the underlying research. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Unsubscribe mechanics, in the CRTC's detail:** the mechanism must be "readily performed" — simple, quick and easy; the unsubscribe link must remain valid for at least 60 days after the message is sent; and an unsubscribe request must be processed without delay, and no later than 10 business days after receiving it. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Privacy framework:** the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada states PIPEDA sets the ground rules for how private-sector organisations collect, use and disclose personal information in commercial activities, under 10 fair information principles including Consent and Limiting use, disclosure, and retention; BC has its own private-sector privacy law deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA, so a BC subscriber list sits primarily under BC's regime for in-province handling, with PIPEDA behind it for cross-border handling. Whether the list's actual handling follows those principles is UNVERIFIED — it cannot be seen from the page. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/?wbdisable=false

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What will I actually get if I subscribe?** Per the page itself: a monthly newsletter "filled with latest real estate news, tips, and facts," under a headline promising "the Latest Listings, Market Updates, and Real Estate Tips Straight to Your Inbox." Whether issues are in fact sent monthly is UNVERIFIED — no issue was available to the underlying research. https://jaredgibbons.ca/subscribe-to-our-newsletter.html
- **Q: Can I unsubscribe, and how fast must it happen?** Yes — the page promises "You can unsubscribe any time," and the CRTC states the mechanics behind that promise: every commercial electronic message must include an unsubscribe mechanism that is readily performed, its link must remain valid for at least 60 days after sending, and a request must be processed without delay and no later than 10 business days after receipt. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Q: Will my email address be shared or sold?** The page states plainly, "we will not share your email address with others." That promise aligns with the privacy principles the Privacy Commissioner publishes — Consent, and Limiting use, disclosure, and retention among PIPEDA's 10 fair information principles — but the underlying research could not verify the list's actual handling behind the page, so the statement stands as the page's own commitment. https://jaredgibbons.ca/subscribe-to-our-newsletter.html and https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/?wbdisable=false
- **Q: Why does a real estate newsletter need my consent at all?** Because Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation requires it: the CRTC states that when sending a commercial electronic message you must obtain prior consent (express or implied), provide identification and contact information, and include a working unsubscribe mechanism — and a newsletter offering listings and promoting a REALTOR's services is a commercial electronic message on the CRTC's own examples. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti/reg.htm
- **Q: Does my signup consent ever expire?** Under CASL, no: the CRTC FAQ states express consent does not expire, though the recipient may withdraw it at any time by unsubscribing. The sender keeps the onus of proving the consent was given. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Q: If I'm watching for a 55+ home, what stock actually exists in this market?** The verified ownership stock in this series is concentrated, not spread evenly: six strata complexes down South Surrey's 152 Street corridor and in Elgin Chantrell, one in Port Coquitlam (Mayfair Terrace), four in Chilliwack, one strata complex in Langley (Creekside Villa), plus manufactured homes in four verified 55+ parks in Chilliwack and Langley — while Grandview Heights and Eastern Hillsides each have zero verified 55+ ownership complexes. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 above.
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ complex the newsletter might feature?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption), as they do for live-in caregivers, legacy residents, children under 19 in the resident's care, and adult children the resident cared for before they turned 19. In a manufactured-home park, the park's own rules govern instead — read them by park. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ building?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. The tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
