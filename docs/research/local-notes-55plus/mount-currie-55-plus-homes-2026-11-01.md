# Mount Currie — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**TENURE FLAG — read first, it decides everything on this page:** Mount Currie is the main community of the Líl̓wat Nation, on federal reserve lands. There is no fee-simple open market: land is reserve land administered by the Nation, and housing is allocated and financed through the Nation's own processes for its citizens. It is not a municipality, there is no strata housing, and the BC Strata Property Act's age-bylaw regime does not create a 55+ product here — the provincial 55+ law block in Section 3 is included for comparison exactly as the Province states it, and it does not apply to reserve-land housing the way it applies to off-reserve strata. Whether the Nation has enacted a formal land code under the First Nations Land Management Act is UNVERIFIED. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/ and https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Mount Currie sits in the Pemberton Valley approximately 160 km north of Vancouver and approximately 35 km north of Whistler via Highway 99 (the Sea-to-Sky Highway), and is also approachable via the Duffy Lake Road (Highway 99 east); the Village of Pemberton is the neighbouring municipality a few kilometres west (exact road distance on an official source: UNVERIFIED). https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- The Nation describes 10 reserves totalling about 2,930 ha, within a Traditional Territory of approximately 780,000 hectares extending north to Gates Lake, east to the Upper Stein Valley and west to the coastal inlets; the surrounding unincorporated Pemberton Valley (off reserve) is SLRD Electoral Area C. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- The Nation's Community Housing Plan names four neighbourhoods within the reserve lands: Mount Currie Village (IR 1 and IR 10), the centre with the administrative and civic facilities; Xet̓ólacw Village (IR 6), also called the Upper Community, the largest residential neighbourhood; Lillooet Lake Road (IR 3 and IR 8); and Kwetsa7 (IR 2), the last two less developed and rural residential in character. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf
- Community anchors named by the Nation: Úll̓us, the administrative building a block off Main Street (gym, banquet hall, Neighbourhood House, Infrastructure Department), and the commercial core, which includes a gas station/convenience store and a 12-unit motel, being redeveloped with plans for a permanent artisan's market. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/ and https://lilwat.ca/departments/infrastructure/
- Ts̓zil Learning Centre, 125 Lillooet Lake Road, is the Nation's learning centre (adult education, Ucwalmícwts language, history and culture, carpentry and other vocational training in partnership with Capilano University), relocated to a new 22,000 sq ft facility in fall 2018. https://www.capilanou.ca/programs--courses/program-profiles/lilwat-nation-language--culture-certificate/
- The Nation states the community is home to approximately 1,495 of its more than 2,275 citizens; the 2021 Census counted 1,240 people in private households in the Mount Currie census subdivision (Statistics Canada treats "Mount Currie" as an Indian reserve census subdivision, code 5931843, within the SLRD), with a median age of 33.2 years — a young community, which frames its housing priorities (Section 5). https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/ and https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/dp-pd/ipp-ppa/details/page.cfm?Lang=E&SearchText=&DGUID=2021A00055931843&GENDER=1&AGE=1&RESIDENCE=1&HP=0&HH=0
- Schools: Xet̓ólacw Community School is the Nation's own school in the Xet̓ólacw community (grade range and enrolment on an official source: UNVERIFIED); under the Local Education Agreement with School District 48, Líl̓wat students also attend Signal Hill Elementary and Pemberton Secondary School in Pemberton, and SD48's Bus 5 route runs "Owl Ridge, Reid Rd & Mt. Currie" to those schools, stopping at IR 10 Road and Spirit Circle on Pemberton Portage Road. https://lilwat.ca/departments/tszil-language-and-culture/advanced-education/ and https://22.files.edl.io/3fff/09/03/25/214527-07fe5ac2-2ab8-4eb4-9bfd-69ad562e6006.pdf
- Transit: BC Transit's Pemberton Valley Transit is cost-shared between BC Transit, the Village of Pemberton, the SLRD and Líl̓wat Nation; local service connects Xit'olacw and Mt Currie to the Village of Pemberton, and a commuter service runs between Pemberton and Whistler. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/889/862/pem_rg_sept2020.pdf
- Where 55+ housing concentrates: **nowhere as a market product — no source found places any 55+ complex, seniors building, retirement residence, care facility or age-restricted housing of any kind in Mount Currie.** Elders' housing exists as a stated priority inside the Nation's own housing system (Section 2 and Section 5), not as a named, purchasable complex. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**Mount Currie has ZERO verified 55+ ownership stock — and, more fundamentally, zero open-market housing stock of any age type. Housing here is Nation-administered housing on reserve land for Líl̓wat citizens: band housing, and member home ownership through the Nation's process with a Band Council Resolution and a Ministerial Loan Guarantee. No complex in this section is something a member of the public can buy, and no named Elders' or seniors' complex was found in any source checked — stating that plainly is the accurate content for a 55+ page on this area.**

### Verified 55+ strata (ownership)

- None — and the category does not exist here: tenure on the reserve lands is neither freehold nor strata. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf

### Verified 55+ rental (not ownership)

- None found as a named, age-restricted rental building. The Nation's housing system includes band (rental) housing allocated through its Housing Department, and its Community Housing Plan records Elders as a stated housing priority among members (after families with young children and multi-generational families) — but no source names a specific Elders' building, its address, its unit count or an age threshold. Any named "seniors building" for Mount Currie is UNCONFIRMED. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf

Where Líl̓wat Elders receive care services is through the Nation's Health & Healing and community programs (the Nation's 2024 annual report lists Home Care among its services); facility names, bed counts and eligibility are UNCONFIRMED here. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Lilwat-Nation-AR24_04_web.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **The tenure rule that comes first here: reserve land is not fee-simple, and there is no strata.** The Nation's housing runs through its Lands Department and Band Council processes: a land encumbrance check confirming a serviced lot is available and registered to the person, a Band Council Resolution (the Band Council acts as guarantor on the loan), possible Indigenous Services Canada housing subsidies, a Ministerial Loan Guarantee, and then bank financing — the Nation's published "Five Steps to Home Ownership on Reserve." Because the land is not a strata, BC strata age bylaws do not attach to it; whether the Nation has enacted a land code under the First Nations Land Management Act is UNVERIFIED, and any lot-specific question goes to the Nation's Lands Department. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **For comparison — only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now (off-reserve law).** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. This regime governs strata corporations under provincial law; Mount Currie has no strata corporations. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **For comparison — the bylaw restricts residency, not ownership (off-reserve law).** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **For comparison — built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01, off-reserve law):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **For comparison — rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide (off-reserve law).** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw; stratas may still ban or limit short-term rentals by bylaw. Neither rule creates or removes any right on reserve land, where rental housing is Nation-administered. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **No municipal zoning bylaw applies to the reserve lands:** there is no District/SLRD zoning for Mount Currie itself — land allocation runs through the Nation's Lands Department and Band Council processes. (The surrounding off-reserve Pemberton Valley is SLRD Electoral Area C, a separate jurisdiction with its own bylaws; do not apply them to reserve parcels.) https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Building costs and standards the Nation itself publishes (useful, dated guidance — not a bylaw):** new construction of a modest home at about $250–$300 per square foot including water, sewer and hydro connections, and modular/pre-manufactured homes built to CSA Z-240 at about $160–$180 per square foot installed and hooked up, with Mount Currie modulars required to meet zone 6 construction standards and a roof snow load rating of 136 lb per square foot. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Taxes:** standard municipal and regional district property taxes, and provincial property transfer tax, do not apply to reserve land the way they apply to fee-simple land off reserve; the Nation's own taxation laws or service fees for a specific lot are UNVERIFIED. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- **ALR:** the community sits in the Pemberton Valley near the Lillooet and Birkenhead rivers; reserve lands are not in the provincial Agricultural Land Reserve (a provincial designation over provincial/private land) — stated as UNVERIFIED pending an official citation in the area research, and any parcel question goes to the Nation's Lands Department. A community-specific floodplain map or flood construction rule for Mount Currie on an official source is likewise UNVERIFIED. (Area research file, 2026-11-01.)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can anyone buy a 55+ home — or any home — in Mount Currie?** No. Mount Currie is the main community of the Líl̓wat Nation on reserve land; housing is allocated and financed through the Nation's own process for its citizens, starting with the Nation's Housing Manager and Lands Department — there is no fee-simple open market and no 55+ product. Inquiries go to the Líl̓wat Nation (info@lilwat.ca, 604-894-6115, per the Nation's published contact information). https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf and https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- **Q: Does BC's 55+ strata law apply on reserve land?** The strata age-bylaw regime (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 — only 55+ bylaws valid since Nov 24, 2022, with Regulation 7.01 exemptions) governs provincial strata corporations; Mount Currie's housing is not strata — it is Nation-administered housing on federal reserve land, allocated through the Nation's Lands Department and Band Council processes, so no strata age bylaw creates a right to live there. The Nation's own land-code status is UNVERIFIED; lot questions go to the Nation. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Q: How does home ownership work for Líl̓wat citizens?** Through the Nation's five steps: meet the Housing Manager; get a land encumbrance check from the Lands Department confirming a serviced lot registered to you; obtain a Band Council Resolution (the Council acts as guarantor); check Indigenous Services Canada housing subsidies and the Ministerial Loan Guarantee; and arrange bank financing. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Q: Is there an Elders' or seniors' building in the community?** None is named in any source checked. The Nation's Community Housing Plan (2018–2033) records members ranking Elders among the community's housing priorities — after families with young children and multi-generational families — and the Nation runs Home Care among its Health & Healing services, but no specific Elders' complex, address, unit count or age threshold is published in the sources checked: UNCONFIRMED. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf and https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Lilwat-Nation-AR24_04_web.pdf
- **Q: Why is financing different on reserve?** Because the Band Council acts as guarantor and a Ministerial Loan Guarantee backs the loan, standard mortgage and foreclosure mechanics do not apply the way they do off reserve — the lender's security runs through the Nation's process, not against fee-simple title. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Q: What would a new home cost to build there, on the Nation's own guidance?** The Nation's material puts modest new construction at about $250–$300 per square foot including water, sewer and hydro connections, and CSA Z-240 modular/pre-manufactured homes at about $160–$180 per square foot installed and hooked up (zone 6 standards, 136 lb/sq ft roof snow load for Mount Currie). Treat these as the Nation's published guidance figures, not current quotes. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Q: If I want to buy near Mount Currie, where do I actually look?** The Village of Pemberton, a few kilometres west: fee-simple homes in a municipality with standard taxes and MLS sales — a completely different tenure from Mount Currie, which is the distinction to explain plainly to any buyer comparing the two. No Pemberton complex is verified as 55+ in this research. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- **Q: Is there market data — sales, prices — for Mount Currie?** No meaningful market data exists to quote: Mount Currie appears in neither SnapStats September 2026 report (FVR or GVR), and Nation-administered housing on reserve land does not trade on MLS the way fee-simple housing does, so MLS activity is not a measure of this community's housing. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Q: Can Elders get around without a car?** Better than in most rural areas in this set: BC Transit's Pemberton Valley Transit local service connects Xit'olacw and Mt Currie to the Village of Pemberton (and a commuter service runs Pemberton–Whistler), cost-shared by BC Transit, the Village, the SLRD and Líl̓wat Nation. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/889/862/pem_rg_sept2020.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The community renamed itself in living memory (Nation's 2014 annual report).** The name Mount Currie Band was officially changed to Líl̓wat Nation on September 12, 2014 — older documents, titles and listings using "Mount Currie Indian Band" are referring to the same Nation. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Lilwat-Nation-AR14_WEB.pdf
- **Housing demand here is the opposite of a retirement market (2021 Census / Housing Plan).** The census subdivision's median age is 33.2 years, and members surveyed for the Community Housing Plan ranked housing for families with young children as the top priority, followed by multi-generational families, Elders, and singles and couples — with rent-to-own and help building their own homes as the priority tenures. A 55+ page that frames Mount Currie as a retirement destination would invert the community's own stated priorities. https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/dp-pd/ipp-ppa/details/page.cfm?Lang=E&SearchText=&DGUID=2021A00055931843&GENDER=1&AGE=1&RESIDENCE=1&HP=0&HH=0 and https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf
- **The Nation is one of the community's largest employers.** Through its governance, corporations and businesses, the Nation provides employment for more than 400 community members — the economic base of the community is the Nation itself, not a commuter or retiree market. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- **The mountain the town is named for is the territory's centrepiece:** Mount Currie, 2,591 m (8,501 ft), at the centre of Líl̓wat Traditional Territory. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- **New housing is being built at a stated goal of 12 units per year** in both the Mount Currie and Xet̓ólacw communities under the Community Housing Plan (2018–2033) — growth here is a Nation capital program allocated to citizens, not developer product offered for sale, which is why "new development" marketing language never applies. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
