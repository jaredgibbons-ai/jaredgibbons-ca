# University VW (UBC / University Endowment Lands) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

**IDENTITY FLAG — READ FIRST: "University VW" is NOT in the City of Vancouver.** The MLS sub-area covers the University Endowment Lands (UEL) and the University of British Columbia's Point Grey campus lands. The UEL is an unincorporated community administered directly by the Province of BC, with no elected municipal council, governed under the University Endowment Land Act; UBC's campus lands are governed by the university. Postal addresses read "Vancouver, BC," which is the source of most confusion. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/local-governments/improvement-districts-governance-bodies/university-endowment-lands and https://www.universityendowmentlands.gov.bc.ca/library/Reports/Realtor%20Information%202026-03-12.pdf

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Two jurisdictions share this one MLS sub-area: (1) the UEL, between the City of Vancouver and UBC on the Point Grey Peninsula — the only part of BC where the provincial government provides local services directly to an urban area, with no municipal council — and (2) UBC's own residential neighbourhoods on campus lands. Day-to-day UEL services are delivered by the UEL Administration, guided by the UEL Official Community Plan and the Land Use, Building and Community Administration Bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/local-governments/improvement-districts-governance-bodies/university-endowment-lands
- The UEL Administration's own realtor information sheet stresses the same point: the UEL is not part of the City of Vancouver or UBC and is governed by different bylaws. The UEL comprises four areas (A, B, C and D) and has a population of nearly 4,000; Areas A, B and C are mostly single-family residential (about 1,300 people), and Area D is the commercial centre — Village Marketplace and leləm̓ — with shops, services and the UEL's multi-family residential units (about 2,700 people). https://www.universityendowmentlands.gov.bc.ca/library/Reports/Realtor%20Information%202026-03-12.pdf
- UBC's residential neighbourhoods, served by the University Neighbourhoods Association (UNA), are Chancellor Place, East Campus, Hampton Place, Hawthorn Place and Wesbrook Place (Wesbrook Village), home to roughly 15,000 residents in total; the UNA also lists a newer Central District. https://www.myuna.ca/about-us/ and https://www.myuna.ca/25/
- Wesbrook Village / Wesbrook Place is the village centre south of West 16th Avenue, on Wesbrook Mall and Birney Avenue / Webber Lane — and it is where this area's only seniors housing of any kind sits (Section 2). The village is anchored by a Save-On-Foods supermarket at 5945 Berton Avenue, with cafés, restaurants and services around it. https://dawn7.phas.ubc.ca/ubc-campus-resources/
- Chancellor Boulevard runs through the UEL's older freehold streets and Chancellor Place; Hampton Place (UBC's first campus neighbourhood, 1990s) sits around Hampton Place / Wesbrook Mall south of the main campus core; Hawthorn Place sits between East Mall and West Mall, south of Thunderbird Boulevard. https://ubcproperties.com/history/ and https://www.villagegatehomes.com/community/
- Pacific Spirit Regional Park wraps around the campus and the UEL: roughly 860–874 hectares of forest with over 55 km of trails (34 km multi-use), operated by Metro Vancouver, including the Wreck Beach foreshore. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/pacific-spirit-regional-park
- Public schools are provided by the Vancouver School Board (SD39), not a separate local district: University Hill Elementary (5395 Chancellor Boulevard), University Hill Secondary (3228 Ross Drive, in Wesbrook's orbit) and Norma Rose Point School (5488 Ortona Road, serving Wesbrook Place). https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/039 and https://www.vsb.bc.ca/university-hill-elementary/about-us and https://www.vsb.bc.ca/norma-rose-point-school/about-us
- Transit is bus-based: the UBC Exchange is one of TransLink's busiest bus hubs, and the R4 41st Ave RapidBus runs from UBC Exchange to Joyce–Collingwood Station via Oakridge–41st Avenue. There is no SkyTrain station in the UEL or on campus. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- Where 55+ housing actually concentrates: it does not concentrate — there is exactly one seniors site in the whole sub-area, on Wesbrook Mall in Wesbrook Village (Section 2). No source found for any 55+ or seniors housing in Hampton Place, Chancellor Place, Hawthorn Place, Acadia, East Campus or on the UEL side (Areas A–D) — see the checked entries in Section 2.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may pass a bylaw requiring one or more persons residing in a strata lot to have reached a specified age **not less than 55 years**; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 25+, 40+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol42/consol42/98043_07
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people younger than 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Act s. 123.2 and Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers for a resident dependent through illness, disability or frailty; and — effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or marriage-like partner, a child under 19 in the specified resident's care, and an adult child the specified resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation or section may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit **short-term** rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Tenure rule that dominates this area — UBC homes are leasehold, not freehold.** UBC Properties Trust's own history explains why: the provincial land grant limited campus land to institutional use and "[s]elling land outright for market housing was not an option," so UBC developed homes on a leasehold model, starting with Hampton Place as its first campus neighbourhood. UBC Campus and Community Planning confirms the structure: residential and commercial properties on the endowment lands are never sold outright, but leased as **prepaid 99-year leases**, with net revenue funding UBC's endowment. Remaining term therefore runs from each project's original lease date, and lenders price and lend against that remaining term — order and read the head lease on any purchase. https://ubcproperties.com/history/ and https://elementslab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Wesbrook-Study_reducedsize_secure2.pdf
- **The freehold exception is on the UEL side, not the campus side.** The UEL's older residential streets (Areas A–C) are predominantly freehold lots originally granted by the Crown — the opposite tenure from the UBC neighbourhoods across Wesbrook Mall. Confirm tenure on title for every property; the postal address will not tell you which side of that line a home is on. (Tenure split as documented in the area research file: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/local-governments/improvement-districts-governance-bodies/university-endowment-lands and https://elementslab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Wesbrook-Study_reducedsize_secure2.pdf)
- **Land use on the UEL side is provincial, not City of Vancouver.** The UEL is governed under the University Endowment Land Act by the Minister (Ministry of Housing and Municipal Affairs), with the UEL Official Community Plan and the Land Use, Building and Community Administration Bylaw as its land-use instruments — City of Vancouver zoning, permits and bylaws do not apply. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/local-governments/improvement-districts-governance-bodies/university-endowment-lands and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol15/consol15/00_96469_01
- **Small-scale multi-unit housing in the UEL runs through its own districts.** The UEL realtor sheet states the UEL implements provincial SSMUH through the R-4/R-6 District — some properties allowed up to 4 dwelling units on one lot, others up to 6, with greater density near frequent transit; secondary suites and accessory dwelling units are permitted but must be supported by a building permit and shown on the development-permit plans. https://www.universityendowmentlands.gov.bc.ca/library/Reports/Realtor%20Information%202026-03-12.pdf
- **Land use on the UBC side is the university's, plus the UNA.** UBC Properties Trust developed the neighbourhoods under UBC's Official Community Plan (now the Land Use Plan), and the UNA delivers municipal-like services to the residential neighbourhoods under its Neighbours Agreement with UBC — community centres, parks, roads, parking and recreation — instead of a city doing it. https://ubcproperties.com/history/ and https://www.myuna.ca/about-us/
- **Property tax — UEL side: provincial rural-area tax, not city tax.** The UEL realtor sheet states that, as an unincorporated community administered directly by the Province, UEL property taxes are levied by the **Surveyor of Taxes through the Rural Property Tax Branch**, bills are issued annually in June, and — a trap for Vancouver buyers — **City of Vancouver water billing is NOT included on the property tax bill**; water is billed separately. https://www.universityendowmentlands.gov.bc.ca/library/Reports/Realtor%20Information%202026-03-12.pdf
- **UEL tax due date and penalties:** because the UEL is taxed as a rural area, annual taxes are payable to the Surveyor of Taxes and due **July 2** of the year first levied; a penalty of 5% of the unpaid taxes is added to any portion unpaid on July 2, and a further 5% to any portion still unpaid on October 31 (Taxation (Rural Area) Act, s. 11). Rural tax notices are mailed by May 31, and payment must be **received** by the due date to count as on time. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/important-dates/overdue
- **Property tax + levy — UBC neighbourhood side: the Neighbours Levy tops the rural tax up to a Vancouver-equivalent total.** The UNA states the Neighbours Levy, a tax collected from residents, "ensures that the sum of taxes and the levy combined is equivalent to what would be paid by a property owner in the City of Vancouver for a similarly assessed property." A UBC-neighbourhood tax notice that looks low on the provincial line alone is not the whole carrying cost — confirm the levy in the strata budget / disclosure for the specific unit. https://www.myuna.ca/budget2026/
- **A 55+ page must not import City of Vancouver rules.** Vancouver's zoning, secondary-suite, short-term-rental and permitting rules do not govern either the UEL (provincial bylaws) or UBC's neighbourhoods (UBC Land Use Plan and UNA neighbourhood regulations) — check the instrument for the side of the line the specific property sits on. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/local-governments/improvement-districts-governance-bodies/university-endowment-lands and https://www.myuna.ca/about-us/

- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy a unit there as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a Tapestry condominium?** Two layers say yes, with one catch. Provincially, every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022; and the MLS information for this complex records rentals as OK. The catch: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. Confirm both in the current bylaws before buying as a landlord. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r2919542-3382-wesbrook-mall-505-vancouver-bc-v6s-0a7/brochure
- **Q: Besides strata fees, is there a second charge at Tapestry?** Expect one. The listing information for a condominium home describes a basic service package on top of ordinary ownership costs — a dining credit for the restaurant or lounge, weekly housekeeping and a 24-hour emergency communication system — because the condominiums share a serviced retirement community with the rental residence. The current amount and whether it is mandatory for every condominium owner: UNCONFIRMED from operator/strata sources in this pass — confirm in the Form B, the strata budget and the service agreement. https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r2919542-3382-wesbrook-mall-505-vancouver-bc-v6s-0a7/brochure
- **Q: Who do I pay property tax to — Vancouver?** No one in this sub-area pays the City of Vancouver. On the UEL side, taxes are levied by the provincial Surveyor of Taxes (Rural Property Tax Branch), bills issue in June, are due July 2 with 5% + 5% rural penalties, and water is billed separately — it is not on the tax notice. In UBC's neighbourhoods, residents also pay a UNA Neighbours Levy set so that taxes plus levy equal what a similarly assessed City of Vancouver property would pay. https://www.universityendowmentlands.gov.bc.ca/library/Reports/Realtor%20Information%202026-03-12.pdf and https://www.myuna.ca/budget2026/
- **Q: Where is the hospital, and is it a full emergency department?** The hospital is on campus: UBC Hospital, 2211 Wesbrook Mall (Vancouver Coastal Health), with an Urgent Care Centre. Note the limit a senior buyer should know before relying on it: UBC's own Campus of Care page states the UBC Hospital Emergency Department is open **8 a.m. to 8 p.m. every day** — it is not a 24-hour emergency department — and the separate UBC Urgent and Primary Care Centre at 6165 Agronomy Road takes walk-ins for urgent, non-life-threatening concerns. https://www.vch.ca/en/location/ubc-hospital and https://www.ubc.ca/campus-of-care/ and https://www.vch.ca/en/location/ubc-urgent-and-primary-care-centre-upcc
