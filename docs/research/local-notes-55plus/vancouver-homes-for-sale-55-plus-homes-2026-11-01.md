## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. Terraces on 7th's 55-plus restriction, as stated in its listing and building data, is the form of bylaw that survives. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw — a live issue in downtown and Yaletown towers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **How the City creates new 55+ rental:** Vancouver's Seniors Housing Rezoning Policy (approved July 23, 2024; last amended May 19, 2026) treats "Social Housing (where occupancy is restricted to older adults 55+)" as social housing secured by a housing agreement restricting occupancy to residents aged 55 or older, owned and operated by a non-profit or government, and secures seniors supportive or independent living housing as rental — no stratification, no separate sales — for 60 years or the life of the building. Terraces on 7th predates this framework; it is not a template the City's current policy reproduces. https://guidelines.vancouver.ca/policy-rezoning-seniors-housing.pdf
- **Tax calendar:** City of Vancouver collects property tax twice a year — advance taxes were due February 3, 2026 and main taxes July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty on unpaid balances after the main-tax deadline. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/deadlines-and-penalties.aspx
- **Empty Homes Tax:** Vancouver owners of residential property must submit an annual property status declaration on or before the 2nd business day in February under the City's Vacancy Tax By-law No. 11674, and a vacancy tax unpaid as of the 10th business day of April bears a 5% penalty. The current Empty Homes Tax rate: UNVERIFIED in this pass — not stated on the City pages read here. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/11674c.pdf and https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/empty-homes-tax.aspx
- **Tenure check that decides value in False Creek:** False Creek South homes sit on City-held land under ground leases — the lease terms and end dates for a specific building must be read off the building's own lease documents, and leasehold does not comp against freehold product. Broadway Lodge's own City-land lease expires in 2040, per the 2022 Council motion. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/false-creek-south-leases-on-city-land.aspx and https://council.vancouver.ca/20220608ag/documents/cfsc10.pdf
- **Heritage areas:** Chinatown, Gastown, Shaughnessy and Yaletown carry City heritage regulations and design guidelines — confirm what applies to a specific property before promising exterior changes. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/vancouvers-heritage-areas.aspx
- **Subsidized seniors housing runs through BC Housing's Housing Registry** (Zone 6: Vancouver directory, May 2025), and the City points seniors to the Seniors Services Society's Seniors Housing Directory of BC for the full list. https://www.bchousing.org/publications/Zone-6-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf and https://vancouver.ca/people-programs/find-supportive-housing-for-seniors.aspx

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Vancouver?** On the verified record for the nineteen consolidated sub-areas: one building — Terraces on 7th, 1570 West 7th Avenue in Fairview, a 52-unit strata (built 2002/2003, see the source note in Section 2) whose listing states a 55 plus age restriction and whose building data records age restrictions in the strata bylaws, operating as a boutique retirement community for independent living. Everything else verified in the city is subsidized rental, retirement rental or care. Confirm the current bylaw wording and any mandatory services agreement in the strata documents before offering. https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3117996-1001-1570-w-7th-avenue-vancouver-british-columbia-v6j5m1-940000/brochure and http://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/terraces-on-7-1570-west-ave
- **Q: Is Terraces on 7th a rental retirement home or a condo I own?** Both halves are documented: suites are listed for sale as strata units (MLS R3117996 is a sale listing), and the operator markets the same address as a retirement community for independent living with private suites. The ownership is strata; what the retirement-community operation adds — services, meals, any mandatory charge on top of strata fees — is UNCONFIRMED from public sources and is the first thing to verify in the disclosure and strata documents. https://terracesonseventh.com/ and https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3117996-1001-1570-w-7th-avenue-vancouver-british-columbia-v6j5m1-940000/brochure
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live at Terraces on 7th?** Under provincial law, yes — the Strata Property Regulation requires an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children under 19 in the resident's care and adult children cared for before 19 are also exempt in the defined cases. Confirm the building's own bylaw tracks the regulation. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy a Terraces suite as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. Under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a 55+ parent can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ building?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. The tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink my resale buyer pool?** Mechanically, yes: the next buyer must also be able to live there under the bylaw — be 55+, be an exempt spouse/partner, caregiver or legacy case, or buy without living in the unit. In Vancouver that trade-off comes with exactly one verified building to buy it in, which cuts both ways — scarcity inside a thin market. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: How do I get into the subsidized buildings — Glynn Manor, Collingwood Towers, Grandview Towers and the rest?** They are non-profit seniors rentals in BC Housing's Zone 6 directory (May 2025) — apply through BC Housing's Housing Registry or directly with the operator, depending on the directory section, and expect a waitlist. The City points seniors to the Seniors Services Society's Seniors Housing Directory of BC for the full list. https://www.bchousing.org/publications/Zone-6-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf and https://vancouver.ca/people-programs/find-supportive-housing-for-seniors.aspx
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Vancouver General Hospital, 899 West 12th Avenue, on the West Broadway corridor in Fairview — a few blocks from Terraces on 7th and the West 7th Avenue seniors pocket, and the hospital most central-Vancouver seniors actually use. https://www.vch.ca/en/location/vancouver-general-hospital
- **Q: Will new 55+ condos I can buy be built under the City's seniors policy?** No — the City's Seniors Housing Rezoning Policy secures new 55+ social housing as rental only, with no stratification and no separate sales, for 60 years or the life of the building. New City-policy seniors supply adds rental stock, not ownership stock. https://guidelines.vancouver.ca/policy-rezoning-seniors-housing.pdf
- **Q: What extra Vancouver costs catch buyers out?** Two: the two-step tax calendar (advance taxes in February, main taxes in July, 5% penalty after the main-tax deadline) and the annual Empty Homes Tax property status declaration every residential owner must file by the 2nd business day in February — a missed declaration deems the property vacant under By-law No. 11674. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/deadlines-and-penalties.aspx and https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/11674c.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Vancouver's 55+ ownership market, on this record, is a single 52-unit building.** Nineteen sub-areas were checked name by name; eighteen verify zero age-restricted strata. A buyer who wants a Vancouver address plus a 55+ bylaw is shopping Terraces on 7th's resale listings, or dropping the bylaw requirement. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **False Creek South is one of the oldest-skewing pockets in the city, and Council has the numbers:** the June 8, 2022 Council motion records that in False Creek South nearly one in three residents are above the age of 65 — over 2,150 seniors (65 years and over), 35.9% of the area's population, up from 30.4% in the 2016 Census and more than double the 17% for the city as a whole — with very few opportunities to move into appropriate "rightsized" housing. That demographic pressure sits blocks from the city's one verified 55+ ownership building. https://council.vancouver.ca/20220608ag/documents/cfsc10.pdf
- **Terraces on 7th's build year is stated two ways in its own public record:** the sale listing says "Built in 2002," the building-data source says built in 2003, and a 2003 provincial release records independent living units announced for "The Terraces on 7th Avenue, located at 1570 West 7th" — a one-year discrepancy to settle from the strata plan and depreciation report, not from marketing. https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3117996-1001-1570-w-7th-avenue-vancouver-british-columbia-v6j5m1-940000/brochure and http://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/terraces-on-7-1570-west-ave and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/archive/2001-2005/2003MCAWS0129-001127-Attachment1.pdf
