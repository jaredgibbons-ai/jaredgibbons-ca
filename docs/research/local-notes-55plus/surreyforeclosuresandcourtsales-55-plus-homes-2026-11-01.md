## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. A court-ordered sale changes who owns the unit; it does not remove the age bylaw for whoever will live there. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The foreclosure process is provincial, and it sits on top of the strata rules:** a proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property must be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7 (1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Redemption and confirmation:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period that the court may fix — Rule 21-7 (5) (b); the Rules state no set length for that period. In default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted — Rule 21-7 (6). Even though the time for redemption has not expired, the person having conduct of a sale may apply for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7 (9). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5) (b). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What does court confirmation mean for my offer on a Surrey home?** The person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7 (9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires, so until the order is made the sale is not final. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** UNVERIFIED as a stated process rule in this file — the bid-rights wording in Rule 13-5 and the PD-66 bid timing were not retrieved from an official source on 2026-11-01. Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for — in Surrey's deep condo market, assume other buyers are watching the same listing — and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing.
- **Q: Where can I check the court file for a Surrey foreclosure?** Court Services Online (https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do) offers eSearch to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do

Surrey has a courthouse serving the Provincial Court (Surrey Courthouse, 14340 57 Ave), but the BC Courts Supreme Court locations list retrieved for the underlying research lists no Supreme Court registry in Surrey; the nearest registries on that list include New Westminster, plus Abbotsford and Chilliwack. Which registry a specific Surrey file runs through is a fact of that file — read it in Court Services Online. https://www.bccourts.ca/supreme_court/locations.htm?location=kamloops and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: Which Surrey community should I search first for a 55+ home that could come up in a court process?** South Surrey, for depth: Peninsula Village, Semiahmoo Estates, Bishop's Green, St. Martin's Lane, the 13965 16 Ave building and The Crescent are all there. Newton is next (Chelsea Gardens, Charleston Green, Glenwood Village, the Boundary Park trio and Sullivan Mews, plus the Westwood Estates and Newton parks), then Cloverdale (Manor Ridge) and Fleetwood (Westwood Court park). No 55+ strata complex was verified in City Centre, Whalley or Guildford in this series. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: If I buy a unit in a 55+ complex at a court-ordered sale, does the 55+ bylaw still apply to me?** Yes — the age bylaw runs with the strata corporation, not with the seller. The court's vesting order on confirmation vests title in the purchaser, but who may LIVE in the unit is still governed by the 55+ bylaw and the Regulation 7.01 exemptions. In a manufactured-home park or at Sullivan Mews, the equivalent question is answered by the park rules / membership documents, not the Strata Property Act. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r3058951-15020-66a-avenue-49-surrey-bc-v3s-2a5/brochure

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Surrey's 55+ supply comes in three different legal forms, and a court process treats each form differently.** Strata (Peninsula Village, Manor Ridge, the Lakewood complexes — a strata lot with title a vesting order can transfer), membership (Sullivan Mews — no strata lot, and its own listings state mortgages are not possible), and pad-rent manufactured homes (Westwood Court, Westwood Estates, Newton Mobile Home Park — MHPTA, where the home and the pad tenancy are separate things). The form decides what a foreclosure sale can actually sell, before the floor plan matters. https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r3058951-15020-66a-avenue-49-surrey-bc-v3s-2a5/brochure and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
- **One short Newton drive holds three 55+ complexes filed under two different sub-areas.** Lakewood Place (6061) and Lakewood Heights (6211) on W Boundary Drive are filed under Panorama Ridge, while Lakeview Estates (6090), between them, is filed under West Newton — a buyer searching one sub-area's page misses a third of the Boundary Park cluster. https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/6090-w-boundary-drive/104
- **Two complexes on the same Newton street carry almost the same name and opposite age rules.** Glenwood Village (7156 121 St, NWS2768) is 55+; Glenwood Village Heights (6875 121 St, NWS3423) advertises no age restrictions. In a foreclosure feed, where listings are thin and fast, the strata plan number is the reliable separator. https://www.estateblock.com/surrey/204-7156-121-st-42603 and https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/6875-121-street/132
- **Two Surrey parks share almost the same name, in two different communities.** Westwood **Court** is the Fleetwood park at 8670 156 Street; Westwood **Estates** is the Newton park on 134 Street, whose own listings disagree on 8224 vs 8254 as the civic address. A listing alert set on one name does not cover the other park. https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/8670-156-street/9 and https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3121759-103-8224-134-street-surrey-british-columbia-v3w4v1-249800/brochure
- **The seller in a foreclosure sale is not an owner preparing the home for market in the ordinary way.** The sale runs inside a court proceeding under Rule 21-7, and buyers should expect the conditions in the court-approved form for that file, not a standard residential contract — the Rule 13-5 directions wording behind this point is UNVERIFIED in this series, so read the actual directions for the file. Occupancy (vacant vs tenanted) is likewise a per-file question: a tenanted unit and a vacant unit in the same Whalley tower can both appear in foreclosure proceedings with completely different possession pictures. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Surrey's OCP changed under every older page in June 2026.** The Surrey 2050 OCP (Bylaw No. 21900) received final adoption on June 15, 2026 and replaces PlanSurrey 2013 (Bylaw No. 18020) — a seller or listing quoting an 18020-era designation for a lot near these complexes is quoting a superseded plan. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/r133-plansurrey-2013-official-community-plan-surrey-2050-official-community-plan-alignment-related-amendments
- **No new age-restricted strata supply is being added anywhere in Surrey in any source checked.** The complexes in Section 2 are 1988–2000 stock (Manor Ridge 1989, Charleston Green and Glenwood Village 1988, Peninsula Village circa 1991–1993, Sullivan Mews 1994, St. Martin's Lane 1995, The Crescent 2000) — so Surrey's 55+ ownership supply is a fixed, aging pool that turns over listing by listing, which is why a court-ordered unit in one of these complexes draws attention out of proportion to the city's size.
