# Boyd Park (Richmond) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

Naming note, stated openly: "Boyd Park" is not a City of Richmond neighbourhood or planning-area name. The label comes from Hugh Boyd Community Park at No. 1 Road and Francis Road; the City plans this ground as Seafair / West Richmond, and board and listing sources use "Boyd Park" as the sub-area name for the streets around the park — including the Apple Greene condo cluster on No. 1 Road, which listings file under Boyd Park. This file covers that park-anchored pocket inside Seafair. https://www.richmond.ca/parks-recreation/parks/parksearch/park.aspx?ID=8 and https://www.letstalkrichmond.ca/OCP2050-phase2

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, whatever an older building database still records for Apple Greene. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The City's own seniors threshold is 55.** Richmond's 2019 Housing Needs and Co-Location staff report discusses seniors' housing need with seniors defined as individuals aged 55 years and older, and records that 49 percent of all Richmond applicants on the BC Housing social housing waitlist in 2017 were categorized as seniors. https://citycouncil.richmond.ca/__shared/assets/_2_HousingNeeds_CoLocationOpp_PLN_071619_54160.pdf
- **City zoning: Richmond Zoning Bylaw 8500, Single Detached zones RS1 and RS2 (Section 8.1)** are the pocket's documented house zones, with minimum lot areas by subzone including RS1/B 360.0 m², RS1/E 550.0 m², RS1/F 828.0 m² and RS1/K 315.0 m² and a 24.0 m minimum depth in most subzones; Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing zones RSM/S, RSM/M, RSM/L and RSM/XL also apply in the bylaw as amended. https://Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/RS1-RS2-RS324187.pdf
- **Suites — the actual ageing-in-place mechanism here:** a secondary suite is a permitted secondary use in RS1/RS2 — Section 5.4 states a suite minimum of 33.0 m² and a maximum of the lesser of 40% of the dwelling floor area or 110 m² — and the City states it allows at least one secondary suite and/or accessory dwelling unit per lot, with Bill 44 small-scale multi-unit minimums of 3 units on lots of 280 m² or less and 4 units on larger lots. A suite is how a caregiver or family member lives on a Boyd Park lot. https://RICHMOND.CA/business-development/planning/housing-legislation.htm
- **Plan: the City of Richmond 2041 Official Community Plan, Bylaw 9000,** covers the area; no Boyd Park plan exists in its Schedule 2 structure — the surrounding ground is planned as Seafair / West Richmond. https://www.richmond.ca/city-hall/strategies/ocp/about.htm
- **Flood — Richmond is a dike-protected island city:** Richmond is made up of islands within the floodplain of the Fraser River at an average of 1 metre above sea level; the City's flood protection system includes 49 km of dikes and 39 drainage pump stations, is designed to handle a 1:500 flood event, and dikes are being raised from 3.5 metres to 4.7 metres for sea level rise. The Flood Plain Designation and Protection Bylaw 8204 sets flood construction levels and dike setbacks for new construction. https://richmond.ca/services/water-sewer-flood/dikes.htm and https://www.Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Bylaw_8204_0410201225280.pdf
- **Property taxes and utilities:** City of Richmond property taxes are due once a year on the first business day of July — Thursday, July 2, 2026 — with a 5% penalty on any unpaid balance after that date and a further 5% penalty on balances still outstanding after September 1, 2026. Richmond's 2026 typical single-family metered utility total is $2,251 per year, including a $342 flood protection line. https://richmond.ca/city-hall/finance/rates/duedates-taxes.htm and https://RICHMOND.CA/city-hall/finance/utility-fees/utilityrates.htm
- **Subsidized seniors housing runs through BC Housing's Housing Registry** (Zone 3: Richmond and West Delta directory, May 2025). https://learningondemand.bchousing.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Zone-3-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Boyd Park?** None that any source verifies. There is no age-restricted strata building in the pocket; the one big complex board sources file here — Apple Greene on No. 1 Road — carries no age restriction in any source checked, and the rest of the pocket is detached houses. A 55+ buyer here is buying a regular house or condo with no age bylaw. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Apple Greene looks like a retirement complex — pools, clubhouse, guest suite. Is it 55+?** No source says so. Building sources record its restrictions as pets and rentals only, and current listings describe renovated family/investor-suitable units across the street from Seafair Shopping Centre and minutes from West Richmond Community Centre. The amenities are 1970s resort-style strata amenities, not an age bylaw — confirm on the Form B for any specific purchase, but do not shop it as a 55+ building. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/apple-greene-8860-no-1-road and https://www.robbiejohal.com/listing/r3116006-8740-no-1-road-111-richmond-bc-v7c-4l5/brochure
- **Q: How do seniors actually age in place in this pocket?** In their own houses, using the suite rules: RS1/RS2 lots allow a secondary suite as a secondary use (33.0 m² minimum; maximum the lesser of 40% of the dwelling or 110 m²), and the City allows at least one secondary suite and/or accessory dwelling unit per lot on covered lots — space for a caregiver or family member on the same lot. https://RICHMOND.CA/business-development/planning/housing-legislation.htm
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. If we buy into a 55+ strata elsewhere in Richmond, can we both live there?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption); children under 19 in the resident's care and adult children cared for before 19 are also exempt in the defined cases. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building for a parent?** Ownership is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. Under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a 55+ parent can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: Is Boyd Park in a floodplain?** All of Richmond is designated flood plain under Bylaw 8204, behind a 49 km dike system designed for a 1:500 event with dike raising from 3.5 m to 4.7 m under way — and this pocket's west side is the dike and West Dyke Trail itself. Habitable floors in new construction must meet the lot's flood construction level, which is a real cost item on any rebuild. https://richmond.ca/services/water-sewer-flood/dikes.htm
- **Q: Which elementary school serves a house near the park?** UNCONFIRMED — the two program files for this ground disagree (locator not run in the area research file; the Seafair file states Diefenbaker Elementary for 9200 No. 1 Road). SD38 catchments are address-based, so run the exact address in the district locator before relying on any catchment. https://sd38.bc.ca/schools/locations-catchments
- **Q: What transit and recreation can I use day to day?** Route 401 on No. 1 Road connects the pocket to Richmond–Brighouse Station and Steveston; Hugh Boyd Community Park (pitch and putt, turf fields, tennis, playground) and West Richmond Community Centre (fitness centre, gymnasium, squash, badminton) are inside the pocket, and the West Dyke Trail runs its west side. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt401.pdf and https://www.richmond.ca/parks-recreation/parks/parksearch/park.aspx?ID=8
- **Q: If I need care later, can I stay in Boyd Park?** No care home, assisted-living residence or retirement residence with a Boyd Park address was verified. Richmond's registered assisted-living residences are in Steveston, Hamilton and the City Centre, and the west-side subsidized seniors buildings are on Blundell Road, No. 2 Road and Railway Avenue, south and east of the pocket. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ANorth%20Vancouver&f%5B1%5D=city%3ARichmond&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&name=&address=&order=alr_legal_operator_name&sort=desc

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **"Boyd Park" is a board and listing label, not a City neighbourhood.** The City plans and serves this ground as Seafair / West Richmond; the name a buyer searches comes from Hugh Boyd — Richmond's first Warden, whose house hosted the Township's first council meetings — carried by the park, the community centre site and Hugh Boyd Secondary School. Two files in this research program (the Seafair notes and this one) cover overlapping ground under the two names. https://www.letstalkrichmond.ca/OCP2050-phase2 and http://richmondarchives.ca/tag/city-government/
- **Apple Greene's levy history is the real due-diligence item, not an age rule.** A current listing for 8740 No. 1 Road states the building has completed major improvements and that a $25K special levy is fully paid, and an earlier listing described a full exterior redo — roof, rain screen, siding, balconies. In a 1973–1975 complex of this size, the capital-project record matters more to a downsizer than any restriction. Listing-stated — confirm against the strata documents. https://www.robbiejohal.com/listing/r3116006-8740-no-1-road-111-richmond-bc-v7c-4l5/brochure and https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/listing/r2510212-213-8720-no-1-road
- **The elementary catchment for the park's own address is not settled in the record.** The area research file leaves the SD38 locator result for 9200 No. 1 Road UNVERIFIED while the companion Seafair file names Diefenbaker Elementary — a live conflict a buyer should resolve in the locator, not from either file. https://sd38.bc.ca/schools/locations-catchments
- **Dixon Elementary is mid-upgrade.** The Province's February 2026 announcement lists seismic upgrades at Alfred B. Dixon Elementary (9331 Diamond Road, at the pocket's north-west edge) among Richmond projects opening in 2026. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026INF0008-000184
- **Seafair Centre's official paper trail is thin.** The centre across No. 1 Road from Apple Greene is the pocket's commercial anchor in daily use, but its street address, grocery anchor and tenant list rest only on the centre's property-manager page, which is not an official source — UNVERIFIED on official sources. Sources: the area research file (sources read 2026-11-01).
- **The dike is part of the utility bill:** Richmond's 2026 typical single-family metered utility total of $2,251 per year includes a $342 flood protection line — Boyd Park homes pay annually for the dike at their west edge. https://RICHMOND.CA/city-hall/finance/utility-fees/utilityrates.htm
- **Richmond's two money dates:** 2026 property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty after that date and a further 5% on any outstanding balance after September 1, 2026. https://richmond.ca/city-hall/finance/rates/duedates-taxes.htm
