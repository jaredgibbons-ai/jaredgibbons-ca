# FAQ for Home Buyers in BC — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Scope note:** this is a province-wide topic file, not a neighbourhood file. It takes the general BC buyer FAQ and gives it the 55+ angle: which of the FAQ's tax and program answers change when the buyer is over 55 (most do not), which ones turn on first-time status rather than age, and the 55+-specific questions — a spouse under 55, renting the unit out, and the smaller resale pool — that a general FAQ does not ask. **Two federal figures that earlier passes in this program could not verify are verified in THIS pass from live CRA pages: the Home Buyers' Plan limit ($60,000) and the FHSA lifetime limit ($40,000).** See Section 3 and Section 5.

An answer from one layer does not carry into another. Sources: the rule entries in Section 3 below.
- Where 55+ fits in a general buyer FAQ: only where a rule actually mentions age or residency — the strata 55+ bylaw regime in Section 3. The tax programs below turn on first-time status, principal-residence use, value and size, not on the buyer being 55+. No source read for this file states an age-55 condition in the PTT, GST rebate, FHSA or HBP rules as published on the pages cited. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/what-home-buyers-plan.html

### The purchase types the FAQ covers — as product types

- **Resale strata (including a resale 55+ strata lot).** The standard ownership product in this series: a strata lot in a corporation whose bylaws may restrict residents to 55+ under Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2. For taxes, a resale is the PTT case — no GST rebate in this file is written for a resale purchase. Named, verified examples are in the city files. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Newly built homes and presales.** The GST case: GST applies to a newly built or substantially renovated home, which is what both federal rebates in Section 3 are written around, and the BC newly built home exemption (in the calculator file of this series) is the PTT case a repeat buyer can still reach. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Manufactured homes on a rented pad (own the home, rent the site).** A purchase type a 55+ buyer meets constantly in this series, and the FAQ's registered-title answers do not map onto it cleanly: a park sale is an assignment of a pad tenancy under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **BC property transfer tax — general tiers:** PTT is based on fair market value on the day of registration, at 1% up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **BC first-time home buyers' program — thresholds (settled in this program):** a qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price where the property is used only as a principal residence, has a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), is 0.5 hectares or smaller and contains only residential improvements; a partial exemption may apply where fair market value is more than $835,000 but less than $860,000. The buyer-side conditions (citizenship/permanent residency, BC residence test, never having owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world, never having received the exemption before) are as published on the Province's page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **First-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate — VERIFIED from live CRA pages in this program:** Bill C-4, which introduced the rebate, received Royal Assent on March 13, 2026. Together with the GST/HST new housing rebate, it provides 100% of the GST (or the federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more — a maximum rebate of $50,000. The window covers purchase agreements from March 20, 2025 through 2031, as stated in the verified facts for this program. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **GST/HST new housing rebate (the pre-existing rebate):** generally 36% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) paid, to a maximum of $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and not available at $450,000 or more. This is the rebate a non-first-time buyer of a new home is left with. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Home Buyers' Plan — VERIFIED in this pass from the live CRA page (previously UNVERIFIED in this program because the page returned 403):** the CRA states the HBP allows a withdrawal from RRSPs to buy or build a qualifying home for yourself or for a specified disabled person, and that "Currently, the HBP withdrawal limit is $60,000." The CRA also states the temporary repayment relief for a first HBP withdrawal between January 1, 2026 and December 31, 2028: the start of the 15-year repayment period is deferred to the fifth year following the year of the first withdrawal (a 2026 first withdrawal means a first repayment year of 2031). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/what-home-buyers-plan.html
- **FHSA lifetime limit — VERIFIED in this pass from a live CRA page (previously UNVERIFIED as a lifetime figure in this program):** the CRA's FHSA pages work from a lifetime FHSA limit of $40,000 — stated expressly as "The lifetime FHSA limit = $40,000" in the CRA's FHSA definitions and used throughout its participation-room calculations, alongside the $8,000 annual amount. The CRA's FHSA main page describes the account as a registered plan for a first-time home buyer to save to buy or build a qualifying first home tax-free up to certain limits, with $8,000 of participation room in the first year the account is opened and generally deductible contributions. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account/investments-your-fhsa.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **FHSA + HBP can stack for the same home:** the CRA states a person can withdraw amounts from an RRSP under the HBP and make a qualifying withdrawal from an FHSA for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. With this pass's verified figures, that stack is up to $40,000 of FHSA room plus up to $60,000 under the HBP, per qualifying person, subject to each program's own conditions. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/what-home-buyers-plan.html
- **Down payment and mortgage insurance (CMHC):** mortgage loan insurance is required when a down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more (where insurance is not available); CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **CMHC premium-range FLAG — checked for this file as instructed:** the live FAQ page itself does **not** repeat the error — it states only that mortgage insurance (e.g., through CMHC) "adds a premium" and gives no percentage range, so there is nothing to flag on this page. The wrong range (2.80%–4.00%, against CMHC's published 0.6%–4.5%) is carried on the live mortgage-calculator and first-time-buyer pages in this program's research, NOT on this FAQ page. Do not "correct" this page by importing a range; if a range is added in the rebuild, it is 0.6%–4.5%. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Strata age law — the 55+ block every buyer FAQ needs:** under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw below 55 has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022. The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it. Exemptions in Strata Property Regulation 7.01 include legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide:** as of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Subjects and deposits — page statements, UNVERIFIED as norms in this file:** the live page's practice statements — a deposit "typically around 5% of the purchase price" due on or within 24 hours of subject removal; a typical subject period of 7 days; a home inspection fee of $400–$700; closing costs of 1.5%–4% of price as a rule of thumb; pre-approval rate holds of 90–120 days; completion "commonly 2–3 months" — have no official source and are negotiable or market-dependent in fact. They are recorded here as page content only, not as rules, and no figure from that list is adopted in Section 4. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html
- **Rescission period — UNVERIFIED in this file:** the live page states a 3-business-day Home Buyer Rescission Period with a fee of 0.25% of the purchase price if the buyer rescinds. No BCFSA rescission-period page was opened for this file, so that statement is recorded as the page's statement only — confirm the current rescission rules with a REALTOR® or lawyer before writing a subject-free offer. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html
- **Other live-page figures left UNVERIFIED in this file:** the first-time home buyer's tax credit figure on the page ("up to $1,500 in federal tax relief") and the page's additional foreign-buyer PTT statement ("an additional 20%") — neither was re-read on a CRA or Province page opened for this file, so neither figure is adopted here. The page's HBP ($60,000) and FHSA ($40,000) figures, by contrast, ARE now verified — see the two entries above. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What is a subject, and what happens at subject removal?** A subject is a condition that must be met before the deal becomes firm — the live page lists financing, inspection, property disclosure statement, title search and strata document review as the common subjects, worked through during a negotiable subject-removal period; after removal the deal is firm and the deposit is at risk, on the page's own framing. The page's "typically 7 days" period is practitioner copy, UNVERIFIED as a norm in this file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html
- **Q: How much property transfer tax will I pay, and what does a first-time buyer save?** At the general rates: 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. A qualifying first-time buyer is exempt on the first $500,000 of the price at a fair market value of $835,000 or less, with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Do I pay GST on a home purchase in BC?** On a new build or presale, yes — 5% at completion; resale homes are generally exempt, as the live page states it and as the rebate frame confirms (only new or substantially renovated housing carries the rebates). A qualifying first-time buyer can recover up to $50,000 through the first-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate (100% of the GST to $1 million, gradually reduced to $1.5 million, none at $1.5 million or more, Bill C-4 Royal Assent March 13, 2026); the pre-existing new housing rebate alone returns 36% of the GST to a maximum of $6,300, phasing out between $350,000 and $450,000. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html
- **Q: What is the minimum down payment, and when is mortgage insurance required?** 5% on homes of $500,000 or less; 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999; and 20% at $1,500,000 or more. Below 20% down, mortgage loan insurance is required, and CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount — not 2.80%–4.00%. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: Can I combine the Home Buyers' Plan and the FHSA, and how much can I take out?** Yes for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal — and in this pass both amounts are verified from live CRA pages: up to $60,000 under the HBP (repayable over 15 years, with the repayment start deferred to the fifth year for a first withdrawal in 2026–2028), and an FHSA lifetime limit of $40,000. Earlier passes left both figures UNVERIFIED because the CRA HBP page returned 403; that block is now cleared. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/what-home-buyers-plan.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account/investments-your-fhsa.html
- **Q: I am over 55 and have owned before. Am I a "first-time buyer" for any of this?** Age is not the test in any program in Section 3 — first-time status is. For the BC PTT program the published condition is never having owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world; a repeat buyer does not meet it however long ago the ownership was. The federal programs carry their own first-time definitions on their own CRA pages — check each program on its own page rather than carrying one answer across all of them. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata we buy?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19; live-in caregivers are also exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a 55+ unit I buy — to anyone?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC strata: every rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, because the bylaw governs who may reside, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink my resale buyer pool?** Mechanically, yes: the next buyer must also be able to live there under the bylaw — be 55+, be an exempt spouse/partner, caregiver or legacy case, or buy without living in the unit (ownership itself is not age-restricted). That trade-off is built into every BC 55+ strata since the 2022 law change confined age bylaws to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: If I write a subject-free offer, can I still back out?** Do not rely on the live page's rescission summary alone: the page states a 3-business-day rescission period with a 0.25% fee, but that statement is UNVERIFIED in this file — no BCFSA page was opened for it — so confirm the current rescission rules, and whether they apply to your purchase type, with your REALTOR® or lawyer before writing without subjects. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **VERIFICATION UPDATE — the two figures this program kept withholding are now on the record.** Earlier files in this series deliberately stated no HBP limit and no FHSA lifetime limit, because the CRA's HBP page returned HTTP 403 and the FHSA main page verified only the $8,000 first-year room. In this pass both pages read cleanly on canada.ca: the HBP page states "Currently, the HBP withdrawal limit is $60,000," and the CRA's FHSA pages state the lifetime FHSA limit as $40,000. Any rebuild text that still calls those figures unverified is now stale. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/what-home-buyers-plan.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account/investments-your-fhsa.html
- **The HBP repayment clock changed for 2026–2028 withdrawals, and a 55+ buyer using RRSP money needs to know it.** For a first HBP withdrawal between January 1, 2026 and December 31, 2028, the start of the 15-year repayment period is deferred to the fifth year after the year of the first withdrawal — a 2026 withdrawal's first repayment year is 2031, on the CRA's own example. That interacts with retirement-age RRSP decisions in a way a general FAQ does not spell out; the deferral itself is the verified fact here, and its planning use is a question for the buyer's own adviser. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/what-home-buyers-plan.html

A rebuild that "harmonizes" the FAQ by copying a range from the calculator page would import the error into a currently clean page. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-buyers-in-bc.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **The GST rebates are two rebates wearing one headline.** The $50,000 figure is the combined effect, for a first-time buyer, of the new FTHB GST/HST rebate together with the pre-existing new housing rebate; a buyer who is not first-time is back to the old rebate alone — 36% of the GST, maximum $6,300, gone at $450,000. On most BC new-home prices that difference is the whole story, and the live FAQ page does not state the old rebate's own numbers. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
