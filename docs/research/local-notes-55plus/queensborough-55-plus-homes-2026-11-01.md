# Queensborough (New Westminster) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Queensborough is the City of New Westminster's island neighbourhood on the eastern tip of Lulu Island, comprising District Lots 757 and 758 in the Zoning Bylaw wording, bordered by the Fraser River (main arm) to the south and east and the North Arm to the north, and sharing Lulu Island with the City of Richmond. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf
- The City plans the area through the Queensborough Community Plan, a schedule to the Official Community Plan (Our City 2041, Bylaw No. 7925, 2017), together with the Queensborough Eastern Neighbourhood Node Master Plan. https://www.newwestcity.ca/ocp/past-event-materials-and-council-reports
- Connection to the rest of the city: Queensborough is joined to mainland New Westminster only by Highway 91A, the Queensborough Connector, over the Queensborough Bridge across the North Arm to Marine Way / Stewardson Way, with Highway 91 at the south end in Richmond — every road trip to Uptown, Downtown or Royal Columbian Hospital crosses that bridge. The second link is the City's Q to Q Ferry between the Port Royal dock in Queensborough and Quayside on the mainland (see Section 5). https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf and https://www.newwestcity.ca/qtoqferry
- Main island streets: Ewen Avenue (the Main Street commercial strip), Salter Street and Howes Street; the newer development area at the north end is Port Royal, where the Salter Street strata buildings cluster. https://www.newwestcity.ca/development-policies-and-process/development-permit-areas/sb_expander_articles/1184.php
- Schools: Queensborough is the one New Westminster neighbourhood with its own elementary and middle schools on the island — Queen Elizabeth Elementary and Queensborough Middle School, 833 Salter Street. For the sample address 921 Salter Street, the SD40 locator result recorded in the master research is Queen Elizabeth Elementary (K–4), Queensborough Middle (5–8) and New Westminster Secondary (9–12) — the secondary school is on the mainland, across the bridge. https://newwestschools.ca/our-schools/ and https://newwestschools.ca/our-schools/registration/
- Parks and recreation: Ryall Park (sports fields, courts, spray park, community garden, Boro All Wheel Park) sits beside the Queensborough Community Centre, the on-island City recreation facility, and Port Royal Park has a riverfront trail. https://www.newwestcity.ca/parks-and-recreation/book-facilities-fields-and-parks/articles/5540.php and https://www.newwestcity.ca/facilities
- Library: New Westminster Public Library's Queensborough Branch is at 920 Ewen Ave, on the island; the Main Branch is at 716 6th Avenue on the mainland. https://www.nwpl.ca/
- Health care: the nearest hospital is Royal Columbian Hospital, 330 East Columbia Street, on the mainland across the Queensborough Bridge; there is no hospital, care home or seniors residence on the island in the sources checked. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/New-Westminster/royal-columbian-hospital
- Transit: no SkyTrain station is in Queensborough; buses connect to 22nd Street Station and New Westminster Station, including TransLink route 104 (Annacis Island / 22nd Street Station, with Port Royal service on weekdays). https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/schedules-by-region/2024-schedules/september/burnaby-new-west-p155.pdf
- Where 55+ housing actually sits: nowhere on the island that any source verifies. Queensborough's listed stock is the largest of any New Westminster sub-area in its research batch (41 detached and 72 condos & townhomes listings in September 2026) and none of it is age-restricted — the island's housing story is family product, above all Port Royal. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report, via the area research file.)

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**Queensborough has ZERO verified 55+ ownership stock — no 55+ strata among the Port Royal buildings or the older island complexes, no 55+ manufactured-home park, and no seniors building of any tenure with a Queensborough address was verified.** The island's newest and largest complexes were checked by name (see Checked list); all are general-market, and several market themselves explicitly on family features such as a community child care centre.

### Verified 55+ strata (ownership)

- None. Every named Queensborough strata checked — The Peninsula, Glasshouse Lofts, Paddler's Landing and the other Salter Street buildings in Port Royal, plus Via on Hampton Street — shows general-market, family-oriented marketing in its sources and no age restriction anywhere.

### Verified 55+ rental (not ownership)

- None age-restricted. The island does have general rental apartments — The Dockyards at Port Royal, 202 Salter Street, is a rental apartment building marketed on waterfront loft-style living with no age restriction stated — but it is not 55+ rental. No purpose-built 55+ rental building with a Queensborough address appears in any source checked. https://www.apartmenthomeliving.com/apartment-finder/The-Dockyards-At-Port-Royal-New-Westminster-BC-20113437

### Retirement residences and care (rental/care — NOT strata ownership)

- None on the island. BC's Assisted Living Registry lists exactly two registered seniors assisted-living residences in New Westminster — Thornebridge Gardens, 649 8th Avenue, and Victoria Heights, 230 Ross Drive — both on the mainland, and the city's long-term care home, AgeCare Royal City, is at 77 Jamieson Court on the mainland (see the Fraserview NW file). A Queensborough senior who needs care crosses the bridge for every rung of it. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ANew%20Westminster&f%5B1%5D=city%3ANorth%20Vancouver&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&name=&address=&order=alr_legal_operator_name&sort=asc and https://www.agecare.ca/communities/royal-city/

### Subsidized seniors rental (non-profit, income-based — not ownership)

- None seniors-specific on the island in the sources checked. The City's non-market inventory's seniors-specific buildings (Dunwood Place, 901 Colborne St; Legion Manor, 505 6th Ave, and the like) are all on the mainland, and no Queensborough-address entry in the inventory or in BC Housing's Zone 5 directories is designated seniors-only. https://newwestcity.ca/database/rte/Housing%20web%20update%20-%20Non-Market%20details.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. No Queensborough strata checked has used this power — the absence of a 55+ bylaw is the verified fact here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **City zoning: Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001** governs the island, with two Queensborough-specific rules that matter here: s.190.36 provides that no Queensborough (District Lots 757 and 758) subdivision may produce a lot under one-half acre (0.20 ha) or with frontage under 66 feet (20.12 m) unless the land can connect to the City sanitary sewer system; and the bylaw's RT-3, RT-3A and RT-3B townhouse districts (ss.417–419) are the Queensborough townhouse districts, separate from the mainland RT-2 series. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/100_Introduction(2).pdf and https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Zoning_Bylaw_No_6680_2001_as_of_June_30_2022_(Complete).pdf
- **Plan: the Queensborough Community Plan** (a schedule to Our City 2041, OCP Bylaw No. 7925, 2017) governs the area; the (RLR) Residential – Low Rise designation in the OCP amendment materials (Bylaw No. 8574, 2026) provides compact-lot single detached form, low-rise buildings up to six storeys, and stacked and side-by-side townhouses. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Draft_Queensborough_Land_Use_Designations_and_Map___March_9_2026.pdf
- **Flood Hazard Development Permit Area — the island's defining rule set:** Queensborough is Floodplain Zone 1 in the City's floodplain study: a low-lying area with high groundwater, poor compressible soils and poor drainage, protected by a shared dyking and pumped drainage system with Richmond. The City's Flood Hazard DPA sets minimum construction levels of 3.53 metres above Geodetic Survey of Canada datum for Area A (generally downstream from Derwent Way on the south side and Wood Street on the north side) and 4.20 metres above GSC datum for Area B (upstream of Derwent Way and Wood Street). Every purchase here — strata or house — needs the property's flood construction level and an insurance quote before subjects come off. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf and https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Flood_Hazard_DPA.pdf
- **City taxes and utilities:** New Westminster 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on unpaid current taxes on July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on September 3, 2026; the City operates its own electrical utility and City water / sewer services, and Queensborough drainage is pumped as part of the shared dyking and pumped drainage system with Richmond. https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php and https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Queensborough?** None that any source verifies. The island had the largest listed stock of any New Westminster sub-area in September 2026 — 41 detached and 72 condos & townhomes — and every named complex checked, above all the Port Royal buildings on Salter Street, is general-market family housing. A senior who buys here is buying the island lifestyle (flat riverfront walks, on-island schools, community centre and library branch), not an age-restricted community. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report, via the area research file; building checks Nov 1, 2026.)
- **Q: Port Royal feels quiet and single-level in places — is any of it a retirement community?** No. Port Royal is a master-planned family community: its own marketing and current listings point to a community park, community gardens, a community child care centre, the riverwalk and the Q-to-Q ferry. No Port Royal building — The Peninsula, Glasshouse Lofts, Paddler's Landing, Timber House, The Dockyards — carries a 55+ bylaw or seniors designation in any source checked. https://vancouvernashdom.com/new-westminster-queensborough-apartments/SearchResults.form/?_pg=3 and https://vancouvernashdom.com/new-westminster-queensborough-apartments/SearchResults.form/?_pg=2
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy a unit in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: What does the floodplain designation actually mean for my purchase?** The whole neighbourhood is Floodplain Zone 1 behind a perimeter dyke built in the 1960s protecting approximately 350 hectares, and the City calls Queensborough its highest priority for flood protection improvements. New buildings must meet flood construction levels of 3.53 m (Area A) or 4.20 m (Area B) above GSC datum. For an existing home or strata unit: confirm its construction level, read the strata's insurance and depreciation documents (for strata), and price flood insurance before subjects come off — not after. http://www.newwestcity.ca/services/public-safety/flood-management and https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Flood_Hazard_DPA.pdf
- **Q: Can I rent out a Queensborough condo or townhome?** Long-term rental-restriction bylaws have been void province-wide since Nov 24, 2022; short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw, and the City's own rules apply on top. Confirm the specific building's current registered bylaws before counting on rental income. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: How do I get off the island without a car?** Two ways: TransLink buses to 22nd Street Station and New Westminster Station (route 104 serves Port Royal on weekdays), and the City's Q to Q Ferry from the Port Royal dock to Quayside, beside New Westminster Station. The ferry's funding is dependent on the City's annual budget approval process, and its operator's next term runs Aug 26, 2025 to December 31, 2030 — a car-free senior should treat the ferry as a valued service to verify, not a permanent utility. https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/schedules-by-region/2024-schedules/september/burnaby-new-west-p155.pdf and https://www.newwestcity.ca/qtoqferry
- **Q: Where is the hospital, and what happens in an emergency?** Royal Columbian Hospital, 330 East Columbia Street, is on the mainland — every trip crosses the Queensborough Bridge (Highway 91A). There is no hospital or care facility on the island, so bridge traffic is a real planning factor for a senior with health needs, not a commute footnote. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/New-Westminster/royal-columbian-hospital
- **Q: Are there heritage or character restrictions on the older island houses?** Queensborough properties are documented in the City's Queensborough Residential Heritage Inventory and Queensborough Industrial Heritage Inventory, reflecting the area's historic industrial base — a listing in an inventory is documentation, and any specific property's heritage status and what it restricts must be confirmed with the City for that address. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-research-and-resources/sb_expander_articles/585.php
- **Q: If I need help later, can I stay in Queensborough?** Not in a seniors building — there is none on the island at any tenure: no retirement residence, no assisted living, no long-term care and no subsidized seniors building. The ladder is entirely mainland: registered assisted living at Thornebridge Gardens (649 8th Ave) and Victoria Heights (230 Ross Dr), long-term care at AgeCare Royal City (77 Jamieson Court), and the hospital in Sapperton. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ANew%20Westminster&f%5B1%5D=city%3ANorth%20Vancouver&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&name=&address=&order=alr_legal_operator_name&sort=asc and https://www.agecare.ca/communities/royal-city/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The dyke is older than most of the housing, and the City is still fixing it (City news release, Feb 14, 2023):** the City received $800,000 from the provincial Community Emergency Preparedness Fund for the Queensborough Dike Shoreline Protection Rehabilitation project, restabilising eroding dike slopes at two locations on the northeast side of Lulu Island, behind 345 Gifford Street and behind 805 Boyd Street. A buyer on the northeast side of the island is buying behind a work site the City itself identified. https://www.newwestcity.ca/2023/02/14/city-of-new-westminster-receives-provincial-funding-for-queensborough-dike-rehabilitation.php
- **Two flood numbers, split by two streets (City Flood Hazard DPA, read Nov 1, 2026):** the minimum construction level is 3.53 m above GSC datum in Area A (downstream from Derwent Way / Wood Street) and 4.20 m in Area B (upstream of them) — two otherwise similar Queensborough homes on opposite sides of that line were built to standards differing by 67 centimetres. Which area a property sits in is a documents question, answered before subjects, not a listing feature. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Flood_Hazard_DPA.pdf
- **The ferry is a budget line, not a bridge (City Q to Q Ferry page, read Nov 1, 2026):** the City states funding for the Q to Q Ferry depends on annual budget approval, that 2024 operating costs were $875,000 with $742,000 covered from general taxation and $133,000 from ticket sales, and that dock and dredging maintenance runs about $250,000 a year from the capital budget. It is the island's second link to the mainland and its most fragile one — the Queensborough Transportation Plan material treats a fixed pedestrian bridge as a long-term exploration, not a project. https://www.newwestcity.ca/qtoqferry
- **The half-acre rule most buyers never hear about (Zoning Bylaw s.190.36):** a Queensborough lot under one-half acre (0.20 ha) or under 66 feet (20.12 m) of frontage cannot be created by subdivision unless the land can connect to the City sanitary sewer system. Most island lots are sewer-connected, but the connection status of a specific older lot governs what can be done with it — and it is UNVERIFIED for any individual lot until checked. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/100_Introduction(2).pdf
- **Detached sellers and strata sellers are living in different markets (September 2026):** 41 detached listings produced exactly 1 sale (2% sales ratio) while condos & townhomes moved at 13% (72 listings, 9 solds) — on an island with no 55+ stock at all, the segment a downsizer can actually transact in is the strata segment, and the segment that is stuck is the house segment they are usually selling out of (as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report, via the area research file).
- **The island's seniors share is the city's smallest (City Age-Friendly Community Strategy, April 2017):** the City's age-friendly strategy records Queensborough with a small portion of seniors — 9% of its seniors living alone, against Uptown's much higher concentrations — which is the demographic backdrop to the verified fact of this file: family housing was built here, seniors housing was not. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Age_Friendly_Community_Strategy___reduced_size.pdf
