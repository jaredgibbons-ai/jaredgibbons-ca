## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://choa.bc.ca/resources/strata-property-act/legislative-updates/
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. This matters in a foreclosure purchase exactly as in an ordinary one: winning the unit does not waive the residency rule, and a court vesting order vests title — it does not amend the strata's bylaws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. Rosewood Court's building record still carries "rentals not allowed" — SOURCE CONFLICT, flagged: for a long-term rental that line cannot be relied on, in a court-ordered purchase or any other. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/rosewood-court-22611-116-ave
- **How a BC foreclosure starts:** "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7 (1); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). (Read directly in this slug's own research pass.) https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **The order and the redemption period:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period that the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the respondent's equity of redemption — Rule 21-7 (5) (b); the court may also make a final order of foreclosure, and may determine or certify the amount due — Rule 21-7 (5) (a) and (c). The Rules state no fixed length for the redemption period. Interest on what is due is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem the property (Law and Equity Act, s. 19), so an owner who redeems before confirmation takes the property out of the sale. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Conduct and approval (stated from the program's Langley foreclosure file, which read these Rules on BC Laws — see the source-conflict flag in the framing note):** in default of payment a final order of foreclosure may be granted (Rule 21-7 (6)); a party may instead apply at any time for an order that the property be sold (Rule 21-7 (7)); if the court orders a sale, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" (Rule 13-5 (3)); the court's directions may fix the manner of sale, a reserve or minimum price, and bid and offer rights, and may direct sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" (Rule 13-5 (4)); and the person with conduct may apply, even before the redemption period expires, for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser (Rule 21-7 (9)) — the vesting order is what cleans title for the buyer. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

- **Where the proceeding is filed:** a foreclosure proceeding is commenced at the Supreme Court registry in the municipality where the land is located, if there is one there, and otherwise at a registry in the judicial district in which the land is located, unless the court otherwise orders (Law and Equity Act, s. 21 (2)). Which registry serves a given Maple Ridge parcel is a file-level check — the live page's New Westminster-or-Vancouver statement is its own, not a verified mapping. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Manufactured home parks run on different law.** Pad tenancies are governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and its Regulation (B.C. Reg. 481/2003), not the Strata Property Act; the Regulation expressly contemplates parks where every site is reserved for a tenant who has reached 55, and a park landlord may refuse an assignment where the proposed purchaser does not meet the park's age requirement in such a park. A park home in a foreclosure feed is therefore two files, not one: the court file for the home, and the park's assignment consent for the pad. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Co-op and life-lease age rules are contract/membership rules, not strata bylaws.** St. George's Village's 55-year membership qualification, Haney Pioneer Village's 60+ threshold and the Legion life leases' terms are set by those organizations' own documents; the provincial strata exemptions do not automatically apply to them. https://www.stgeorgesvillage.ca/_files/ugd/070889_39221c746a9b483980e20a3596cdb580.pdf and https://www.chf.bc.ca/find-a-co-op/
- **City zoning: Zoning Bylaw No. 7600-2019; OCP Bylaw No. 7060-2014.** Houses sit under RS-1 (s. 605 — minimum lot area 668.0 m², width 18.0 m, depth 27.0 m, maximum coverage 40%); the bylaw also includes the dedicated manufactured-home park zone, s. 626, Zone RMH. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Maple Ridge — including if one comes up court-ordered?** Seven verified 55+ ownership complexes: four Fraserview Village strata buildings (Rosewood Court, Emerald Manor, The Willows, Castlemount), one 55+ co-operative (St. George's Village, Cottonwood), and two 55+ manufactured-home parks (Alouette, East Central; Val Maria, Southwest). Separately labelled: Haney Pioneer Village Co-operative is 60+, not 55+, and the Legion Gardens / Legion Manor life leases are purchased leaseholds, not strata. Any of the strata or park product can in principle be the subject of a Rule 21-7 proceeding, which by its terms covers "mortgaged property" without a property-type limit. Sources: Section 2 above. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: If a unit in a 55+ building comes up as a court-ordered sale, is my accepted offer final?** No. Where the court has directed sale by contract conditional on the approval of the court, the person with conduct of sale must still apply under Rule 21-7 (9) for an order confirming the sale and vesting title in you — until that order, the sale is not final, and the court's directions may allow others to bid, make offers or meet bids at that stage. (Rule statements per the Langley foreclosure file's BC Laws reads — see the framing note.) https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: Does buying through the court get me around the 55+ bylaw?** No — the age bylaw governs residency in the strata regardless of how the lot was acquired, and a vesting order vests title; it does not amend the strata's bylaws. An under-55 buyer can own, but may not be able to reside, and any tenant must also satisfy the bylaw or an exemption. In a park, the park's assignment consent applies on top of the court process. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Q: Can the owner still save the home after I've offered?** The owner may redeem by paying what is due plus interest to the date of payment (Law and Equity Act, s. 19), within the redemption period the court fixed in its order under Rule 21-7 (5) (b) — the Rules set no standard length. The person with conduct of sale may apply to confirm a sale even before that period expires, which is why the file's stage matters before you spend money on inspections. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** Treat it as a real possibility to plan for: the court's sale directions under Rule 13-5 (4) may fix bid and offer rights, and the live page describes sealed, unconditional competing bids at the hearing — that description is the page's own statement, UNVERIFIED as a general rule for this slug (the PD-66 bid-timing text was not opened in its pass). Get the seller's counsel's bid instructions for the specific file in writing before you spend. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02 and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mapleridgeforeclosuresandcourtsales.html
- **Q: How do I check the court file on a Maple Ridge foreclosure?** Court Services Online eSearch shows the file number, registry, parties and filed documents for Supreme Court civil files, class "H - Foreclosure," and is open to anyone with Internet access. The petition, the amount certified due and the orders made are the facts of the deal. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: Are court-ordered sales cheaper — is there a standard foreclosure discount?** No published source supports one: SnapStats publishes no foreclosure category, no official source publishes Maple Ridge foreclosure counts or discount statistics, and the general market context is already negotiated (Maple Ridge detached sold at a 97% sale-to-list ratio and condos & townhomes at 94% in September 2026, SnapStats, cited in Section 1). The live page's own FAQ makes the same point in its words — discounts reflect condition, as-is risk or occupancy rather than automatic below-market pricing. Any agent quoting a typical Maple Ridge court-ordered discount is stating an UNVERIFIED figure. https://jaredgibbons.ca/mapleridgeforeclosuresandcourtsales.html
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a Maple Ridge 55+ strata bought through the court?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption), with further defined exemptions for children and live-in caregivers. The purchase route does not change the exemptions. Note the co-ops and Legion life leases set their own occupancy terms, which the strata exemptions do not govern. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Is a retirement residence ever a foreclosure purchase?** No — Maple Ridge Seniors Village, Chartwell Willow, Sunwood, Greystone Manor and Royal Crescent Gardens are rental/care buildings; they do not sell unit by unit, in a foreclosure or otherwise. The same applies to the subsidized buildings (Cottonwood Park, Fraserwood, Maple Ridge Towers, Maple Towers). Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: What condition should I expect, and is the home vacant?** Expect the conditions in the court-approved form for that file, not a standard residential contract — the seller in a foreclosure sale is not an owner preparing the home for market in the ordinary way. Whether a property is vacant or occupied is a matter of the individual court file, not a published statistic: one visible feed card for this page is expressly tenanted month-to-month, which illustrates the point — possession is a per-property question. The Rule 13-5 directions and Schedule 'A' wording behind the live page's "as is, where is" description are UNVERIFIED for this slug — see Section 3. https://jaredgibbons.ca/mapleridgeforeclosuresandcourtsales.html and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Ridge Meadows Hospital, 11666 Laity Street — a Fraser Health community hospital with 24/7 emergency care serving Maple Ridge and Pitt Meadows. The Ridge Meadows Urgent and Primary Care Centre is in Haney Place Mall, 11900 Haney Place, open 7 days a week, 9:00 a.m.–8:00 p.m. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Maple-Ridge---Pitt-Meadows/ridge-meadows-hospital
