# How to Buy a Mobile Home in BC — 55+ Manufactured-Home Parks: Local Notes (Province-Wide)

This file is PROVINCE-WIDE, not one area. It covers 55+ manufactured-home (mobile home) parks across British Columbia for the how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc guide page. The five sections are adapted to the province-wide topic. Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named parks themselves.

## 1. WHERE 55+ MANUFACTURED-HOME PARKS CONCENTRATE IN BC

- The legal frame is provincial, not municipal: the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on, in any BC municipality; zoning for parks is set by each municipality in its own bylaw (for example, Surrey's Zoning Bylaw No. 12000 has a Manufactured Home Residential Zone, RM-M, that permits manufactured home parks). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7915-0417-00.pdf
- **Lower Mainland / Fraser Valley is the densest verified cluster in this research.** Named 55+ parks verified by name in this pass sit in Surrey (Westwood Estates; Newton Mobile Home Park; and the bare-land strata Sea Ridge Bays in South Surrey), in the Township of Langley at Aldergrove (the Pioneer Park co-operative), and in Maple Ridge (Val Maria). Sources: the park entries in Section 2 below.
- **The Okanagan is the second verified cluster in this research,** represented by Pinecrest Mobile Home Park in Lake Country (District of Lake Country, between Kelowna and Vernon), whose listings describe a 55+ park in the heart of Lake Country. Source: Section 2 below.
- **The Fraser Valley east of the Lower Mainland core** is represented by Cultus Lake Village at Cultus Lake (near Chilliwack), whose listings describe a 55+ community in a family-owned and operated park. Source: Section 2 below.
- Not every park in these regions is 55+, and the age label does not travel with the region: two large Surrey parks on King George Boulevard (Crispen Bays and Crestway Bays) are listed by a park directory as all-ages, and one large South Surrey park (Breakaway Bays) produced conflicting age claims in this research — see "Checked and NOT verified as 55+" in Section 2. Verify the individual park, never the city. https://www.mhbo.com/browse/mobile_home_parks/bc/surrey
- **Vancouver Island and the BC Interior beyond the Okanagan:** listings for individual 55+ manufactured homes on the Island and in the Interior appear in search results, but no additional Island or Interior park was verified by park name, street address and age restriction from a public source in this pass — UNCONFIRMED as named-park concentrations. Do not publish a province-wide distribution claim beyond the clusters above.
- A province-wide official list of manufactured-home parks does not exist in the sources checked: the earlier topic research found no official municipal or provincial list of park names and addresses, which is why Section 2 is a verified representative set rather than an inventory. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes

## 3. THE BC RULES THAT GOVERN 55+ MANUFACTURED-HOME PARKS

- **The governing statute is the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA).** It applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on; a home on land the owner also owns is dealt with through the land title system instead, and if a person rents both the home and the site from an owner, the Residential Tenancy Act — not the MHPTA — applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **The 55+ rule in a park is a park designation, not a strata bylaw.** The Province states the general rule for a designated 55-plus park: at least one tenant on the site must be 55 years of age or older. Individual parks can state their occupancy rule more strictly in their own documents — one Aldergrove co-op listing states both occupants must be 55 — so the park's own tenancy agreement and rules are the operative document, not the strata age-bylaw regime (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2 applies to strata corporations, not to pad tenancies). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://murphyrealestategroup.ca/recip.html/1272399.search/SearchResults.form
- **Buying in a park is an assignment, not a new tenancy.** When a home in a park is sold, the buyer does not get a new tenancy — the existing pad tenancy agreement is assigned to the purchaser. Before the seller asks the park owner to consent, the seller must give the proposed purchaser a copy of any written part of the tenancy agreement and any written park rules that apply to the tenancy. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Park owner consent and the 10-day rule:** assignment needs the park owner's prior written consent (or consent deemed given, or an order of the director). The request is made in writing on the approved form (RTB Form 10), the park owner must respond so the home owner receives the response by the 10th day after the request is received, and if there is no response by the end of the 10th day, consent is deemed to have been given; the park owner must not charge a fee or receive a benefit for giving consent. On assignment, the purchaser becomes the tenant of the park owner and assumes the rights and obligations under the Act and the tenancy agreement from the assignment date, on the same terms as before. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Pad rent increases — the allowable formula:** a landlord may increase pad rent once every 12 months, with at least 3 full months' notice, and the increase is capped by formula — the inflation rate (the 12-month average change in the BC all-items Consumer Price Index) plus a proportional amount (the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park). The formula is set in the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Regulation. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park rules:** park rules are part of the tenancy agreement; a rule is enforceable only if it applies to all tenants fairly, is clear enough for a reasonable tenant to follow, is properly noticed, and does not change a material term of the tenancy agreement. Since the April 30, 2024 regulation change, park rules can only be changed once a year and with three months' notice — see Section 5. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/manufactured-home-tenancy and https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2024hous0072-000659
- **Park closure or redevelopment:** a landlord converting all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use, or to a residential use other than a manufactured home park, must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give a 12-month notice to end tenancy, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; a tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, paying rent to the move-out date. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Registration of the home itself — the Manufactured Home Registry:** a sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, the province's central register of manufactured home ownership, administered through BC Registries. On a sale, the original signed Bill of Sale and the Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership are filed through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary), and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Property tax and the Home Owner Grant:** a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, and the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. A manufactured home occupied as a principal residence by its registered owner can qualify for the Home Owner Grant, applied for each year. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **Municipal layer:** there is no province-wide park zoning — each municipality zones manufactured home parks in its own bylaw — and some cities add a park-redevelopment policy on top of the Act; Surrey's Manufactured Home Park Policy brochure, for example, walks residents through the 12-month notice, permits-first and $20,000 compensation steps and names help organizations including the BC Manufactured Home Owners association and the Tenant Resource & Advisory Centre. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: If I buy a mobile home in a park, do I own the land?** No — in a pad-rental park you own the home and rent the site under a pad tenancy agreement; the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act applies to a person who owns a manufactured home and rents the site it sits on. The exceptions in Section 2 prove the rule: in a bare-land strata park such as Sea Ridge Bays you own the lot as a strata lot, and in a co-operative such as Pioneer Park the land is held through the co-op. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://realtylink.org/en/print/property/263120958
- **Q: How is financing different for a home on a rented pad?** The home is registered in the Manufactured Home Registry rather than dealt with as land title real property, and the transfer is only effective once registered, filed through a lawyer or notary with the signed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership. Lender and mortgage-insurer rules for manufactured homes (chattel vs land-home treatment, age and foundation requirements) were not confirmed on an official source in this research — UNCONFIRMED; confirm financing for the specific home and tenure before removing subjects, and note the extreme case in the Surrey research: a membership (non-strata, non-park) complex whose listing states no mortgages are possible at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: Can the park owner refuse to let me buy a home in the park?** The sale needs the park owner's prior written consent to assign the tenancy — but the owner must respond within 10 days of the written request (RTB Form 10), silence counts as consent given, and no fee can be charged for consent. Listings phrase this as "subject to park approval". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf and https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/6571-king-george-boulevard/6
- **Q: How much can my pad rent go up each year?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, by no more than the inflation rate (BC consumer price index) plus a proportional amount for the park's changes in local government levies and regulated utilities, divided by the number of sites in the park. For scale, the RTB's own rent-increase form for manufactured home sites states the formula outcome as 3% plus the proportional amount for increases effective in 2025, and 2.3% plus the proportional amount for increases effective in 2026. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes and https://www.wageestate.com/api/documents/bc/RTB-11A.pdf
- **Q: Can I move my home to another park later?** The Act's framework assumes a home can be relocated — the closure-compensation rules even add compensation where a home cannot be moved — but the practical answer depends on the home's age, condition, CSA label and whether another park will accept it, none of which an official source guarantees: UNCONFIRMED for any specific home. What is fixed in law is the paperwork: any later sale or transfer is only effective once registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, and the CSA certification label must be attached to the home at the point of sale — a missing label is a red flag for moving, financing and resale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry and https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2018MAH0066-001111
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold or redeveloped?** A sale of the park alone does not end your tenancy — your assigned tenancy continues on the same terms. A conversion of all or a significant part of the park is different: the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, give 12 months' notice, and pay you $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; you may leave early on at least 10 days' written notice. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo62/loo62/161_2000
- **Q: Is a park's 55+ age restriction legally valid?** Yes, as a park designation under the provincial framework: the Province states that in a designated 55-plus park at least one tenant on the site must be 55 years of age or older. This is a different legal mechanism from a strata's 55+ bylaw — do not apply strata exemption rules to a pad tenancy; read the park's tenancy agreement and rules, which can state the occupancy rule more strictly. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/starting-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: What paperwork should the seller hand me before I write an offer?** The written tenancy agreement and the written park rules — the regulation requires the seller to give the proposed purchaser both before the park owner is even asked to consent to the assignment — plus the current pad rent and the date of the last increase, so the increase formula can be checked. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Do I pay property tax on a home I own on a rented pad?** Yes — a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value does not include the value of the land when the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land. Some parks include the land portion of property taxes inside the pad rent (Westwood Estates and Val Maria listings both state this) — check what the specific park's pad rent includes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://www.zolo.ca/maple-ridge-real-estate/21163-lougheed-highway/32
- **Q: A directory calls a park 55+, but a listing in it says no age restrictions. Which is right?** Treat the park as UNCONFIRMED until its own rules settle it — that exact conflict occurred in this research at Breakaway Bays in South Surrey (directory: 55+; MLS listing: "No age or rental restrictions"). Ask the park for its current tenancy agreement and park rules in writing before relying on either claim. https://www.mhbo.com/mobile-home-park/45813-breakaway-bays-1840-160th-street-surrey-bc-v4a-4x4 and https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r3075290-1840-160-street-75-surrey-bc-v4a-4x4/brochure

## 5. RECENT CHANGES, WITH DATES (official sources only)

- **June 6, 2018 — closure compensation replaced and increased.** The Province's information bulletin states the new compensation for tenants of manufactured home parks facing eviction took effect June 6, 2018: compensation on a park closure/conversion became a flat $20,000, increased from the previous requirement of 12 months of pad rental costs. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2018MAH0066-001111
- **June 6, 2018 — added compensation where a home cannot be relocated.** The same changes require park owners to compensate tenants for the assessed value of the home over $20,000 where the manufactured home cannot be relocated (previously there was no compensation in that case), and clarify that a tenant who is unable to relocate their home is not responsible for disposal costs. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2018MAH0066-001111
- **June 6, 2018 — bad-faith closure compensation increased.** A landlord who gives notice of a park closure but fails to proceed with it after eviction must pay tenants 12 months' pad rental or $5,000, whichever is greater — increased from the previous 6 months' pad rental in the Province's April 12, 2018 backgrounder summary of the amendments. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2018MAH0066-001111 and https://news.gov.bc.ca/files/BG_Improved_Security_for_Tenants.pdf
- **2019 — rent increases tied to inflation only.** The Province's September 4, 2019 release states rent increase limits are tied only to the inflation rate (the 12-month average percentage change in the BC Consumer Price Index), and that for manufactured home park tenancies the rate is that inflation figure plus a proportional amount for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees — the formula structure still in use. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2019MAH0130-001702
- **April 30, 2024 — park rule changes limited to once a year.** The Province announced April 30, 2024 that changes to the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Regulation mean park owners may change park rules only once per year, must give tenants three months' notice of a rule change (up from two weeks), and must give that notice on a form approved by the Residential Tenancy Branch. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2024hous0072-000659
- **2025 / 2026 — the formula's inflation component, year by year.** The RTB's Notice of Rent Increase form for manufactured home sites (RTB-11) states the maximum formula as 3% plus the proportional amount for rent increases with an effective date in 2025, and 2.3% plus the proportional amount for rent increases with an effective date in 2026 — the percentages change annually while the levies/utilities proportional amount is park-specific. https://www.wageestate.com/api/documents/bc/RTB-11A.pdf
- **October 20, 2025 — Surrey's new tenant-protection framework expressly leaves parks to the provincial Act.** Surrey adopted its Tenant Protection Bylaw No. 21788 and Rental Replacement Policy No. O-63 on October 20, 2025 for multi-unit rental redevelopment, and the bylaw does not apply to property governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act — a dated reminder that park residents' redevelopment protection remains the provincial 12-month notice and $20,000 framework plus any city park policy, not the new city bylaw. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-development-application-process/submitting-a-development-application/rental-housing-redevelopment and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_reg_21788.pdf
- Anything further — for example, any post-2024 change to the $20,000 compensation figure or to the assignment rules: no official source stating such a change was found in this pass — UNCONFIRMED. Do not publish a "new rules" claim for a park purchase without checking the BC Gov manufactured-homes pages and BC Laws for the current text.
