# Brentwood Park (Burnaby) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Brentwood Park is the residential sub-area in north-west Burnaby around Brentwood Park Elementary, south and east of the Brentwood Town Centre, between the town centre towers and the single-family streets toward Parkcrest. The City publishes no street-by-street written boundary for the MLS sub-area — exact boundary streets are UNVERIFIED. Sources: the area research file (sources read 2026-11-01) and https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/041
- The MLS name: "Brentwood Park" is the GVR board sub-area name as it appears in the SnapStats GVR September 2026 report under community "Burnaby." It is also the elementary school's name; the City's planning framework for the area is the Brentwood Town Centre, one of Burnaby's four town centres. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report) and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/about-burnaby/town-centres
- The market is two-part: detached houses on the residential streets around the school, and a very large tower and low-rise condo stock around the town centre and Holdom stations — September 2026 recorded 360 condos & townhomes listings against 21 detached listings, and 39 attached solds against 3 detached solds. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report)
- Schools are School District 41 (Burnaby): 41 elementary and 8 secondary schools city-wide, no middle-school tier — elementary is K–7 and secondary is 8–12. SD41 schools in or beside this sub-area on the Province's list are Brentwood Park Elementary (K–7), 1455 Delta Ave, Gilmore Community Elementary (K–7), 50 S Gilmore Ave, and Kitchener Elementary (K–7), 1351 Gilmore Ave, with Burnaby North Secondary (8–12), 751 Hammarskjold Dr, the nearest secondary. Catchments are address-based through the SD41 locator; the locator result for a Brentwood Park sample address is UNVERIFIED. https://burnabyschools.ca/about-sd41-overview/ and https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/041
- Transit: Brentwood Town Centre Station and Holdom Station, both Millennium Line, serve the area's edges; Brentwood Town Centre Station at Lougheed Highway and Willingdon Avenue is also an R2 RapidBus connection point. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/about-burnaby/town-centres
- Library: the branch serving north Burnaby is the McGill Branch, 4595 Albert Street, north-east of the sub-area. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/city-departments/burnaby-public-library
- Seniors recreation: the Confederation Seniors' Centre, 4585 Albert St, is a City of Burnaby 55+ RECREATION centre (memberships $22.60/year single-centre and $56.45/year multi-centre, effective September 1, 2026) north-west of the sub-area — an amenity for area seniors, not housing. http://www.burnaby.ca/confederation55+
- Health care: the city's hospital is Burnaby Hospital, 3935 Kincaid St (Fraser Health), in central Burnaby. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Burnaby/burnaby-hospital

Current listings class Rosser Avenue addresses in this stretch (for example the Solo District towers at 2008 Rosser Ave) as "Brentwood Park," which is the basis for placing Rideau in this sub-area; the exact MLS boundary is UNVERIFIED, as above. Rideau is a non-profit affordable seniors rental / assisted-living residence — there is no 55+ strata to buy anywhere in Brentwood Park. https://vancouvernashdom.com/burnaby-north-brentwood-park-apartments/SearchResults.form/?_pg=7 and https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/province-to-finance-affordable-seniors-housing-in-burnaby

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**No verified 55+ strata ownership complex was found in this sub-area.** Brentwood Park has hundreds of strata units, but every tower and low-rise checked is general-market product with no age restriction stated. The one verified seniors building, Rideau, is a non-profit rental and registered assisted-living residence. A buyer cannot purchase in it — do not let a 55+ page imply otherwise.

### Verified 55+ strata (ownership)

- None verified in this sub-area.

### Verified 55+ rental (not ownership)

- No standalone market 55+ rental apartment building was verified in this sub-area. The area's seniors rental is inside Rideau (below): BC Housing describes that building as a seniors' rental complex preserving 138 units of affordable rental housing for seniors — rental that sits inside a registered assisted-living residence, not a rental building a buyer or tenant shops for on the open market. https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/province-to-finance-affordable-seniors-housing-in-burnaby

### Retirement residences and care (rental/care — NOT strata ownership)

- **Rideau Retirement Residence — 1850 Rosser Ave, V5C 5E1.** A 19-storey non-profit seniors residence owned and operated by the Chalmers Foundation, registered in BC's Assisted Living Registry (class SN, seniors). BC Housing provided approximately $37.4 million to finance the foundation's purchase and renovation of the building, preserving 138 units of affordable rental housing for seniors; the foundation assumed ownership and operation, tenants were not to be displaced by the acquisition, and monthly rents were to remain the same. The Chalmers Foundation describes Rideau as providing affordable housing for seniors, with residents choosing to live independently or use its assisted-living services. Rental/care only — nothing to buy. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ABurnaby&f%5B1%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASupportive%20Recovery&name=&address=&order=alr_residence_type&sort=asc and https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/province-to-finance-affordable-seniors-housing-in-burnaby

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Assisted living here is registry housing, not strata:** Rideau is registered with BC's Assisted Living Registry (class SN) under the Chalmers Foundation; its assisted-living services are a health-registry product, and its rental preservation was financed through BC Housing's HousingHub program — neither route involves a purchase or a strata bylaw. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ABurnaby&f%5B1%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASupportive%20Recovery&name=&address=&order=alr_residence_type&sort=asc and https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/province-to-finance-affordable-seniors-housing-in-burnaby
- **Zoning bylaw — note the name change.** Research briefs for Burnaby sometimes still cite the "Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965". The City states the new **Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026** was adopted June 9, 2026, replaced the former Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965, and took effect July 1, 2026. Use the 2026 bylaw. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/bylaws/zoning-bylaw
- **R1 SSMUH zoning:** Burnaby consolidated its single- and two-family districts into the R1 Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing district in June 2024 — 3 units on lots up to 280 m², 4 units on larger lots, and 6 units on lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent-transit stop; SSMUH lots require a minimum lot width of 10 m and minimum lot area of 281 m², lots with two or more primary units may be stratified, and secondary suites count toward the unit total and cannot be stratified. On Brentwood Park's detached streets south of the town centre, that is the rule set behind new duplex, multiplex and suite forms — none of it age-restricted. The tower lands near the stations follow the town-centre framework, not R1. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **OCP: the Burnaby 2050 Official Community Plan** was adopted December 9, 2025, replaces the 1998 OCP, and takes precedence over older community-plan land use maps where they conflict; the area falls under the Brentwood Town Centre framework within it. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-12-11/city-burnaby-adopts-burnaby-2050-official-community-plan
- **Property taxes:** Burnaby's 2026 property taxes were due Friday, July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date; the City's 2026–2030 Financial Plan approved a 2.9% tax increase (about $61 on the average home). The detached single-family water utility charge is $652.33, with a 5% discount if paid by the due date. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Brentwood Park?** None that any source verifies as an age-restricted strata. The sub-area's attached stock is deep — 360 condos & townhomes listed in September 2026 — but it is general-market tower and low-rise product. A 55+ buyer here is buying an ordinary condo or house with no age bylaw; the one seniors building, Rideau, is rental/care. Sources: Section 2 above. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report)
- **Q: What is Rideau at 1850 Rosser Ave — can I buy a unit there?** No. Rideau is a 19-storey non-profit residence owned and operated by the Chalmers Foundation: 138 units of affordable rental housing for seniors, with independent living and assisted-living services, registered in BC's Assisted Living Registry. You apply to live there; there is no strata title to buy. https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/province-to-finance-affordable-seniors-housing-in-burnaby and https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ABurnaby&f%5B1%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASupportive%20Recovery&name=&address=&order=alr_residence_type&sort=asc
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere in Burnaby?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Is there a seniors centre near Brentwood Park?** The nearest City 55+ centre is the Confederation Seniors' Centre at 4585 Albert St, north-west of the sub-area — recreation and society clubs, with a 55+ single-centre membership at $22.60/year and a multi-centre membership at $56.45/year effective September 1, 2026. It is not housing. http://www.burnaby.ca/confederation55+
- **Q: What transit can I actually use day to day?** Two Millennium Line stations frame the area — Brentwood Town Centre Station (also an R2 RapidBus connection) and Holdom Station — plus the town centre's bus routes. The detached streets south of Lougheed Highway are a walk or a bus ride from either station; station proximity varies by street. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/about-burnaby/town-centres
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Burnaby Hospital, 3935 Kincaid St, in central Burnaby — south of this sub-area. There is no hospital in Brentwood Park itself. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Burnaby/burnaby-hospital
- **Q: If I need help later, can I stay in Brentwood Park?** At the assisted-living step, yes: Rideau offers independent living through to assisted-living services in the same Rosser Avenue building, through the non-profit operator and the Assisted Living Registry route. No long-term care home was verified inside this sub-area in the sources checked — the care step beyond assisted living means leaving Brentwood Park. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ABurnaby&f%5B1%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASupportive%20Recovery&name=&address=&order=alr_residence_type&sort=asc

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The seniors tower was bought to keep it affordable, not to redevelop it.** BC Housing's financing (approximately $37.4 million, through HousingHub) let the Chalmers Foundation purchase and renovate the 19-storey Rideau building with tenants not displaced and rents held the same; the announced renovation scope included the water-treatment system and the building envelope. A buyer searching "Rosser Avenue seniors" is looking at a preservation deal, not a sales project. https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/province-to-finance-affordable-seniors-housing-in-burnaby
- **Rosser Avenue carries both ends of the market.** The same street holds Rideau's non-profit seniors residence (1850 Rosser Ave) and the Solo District's market condo towers (2008 Rosser Ave), both classed as Brentwood Park in listings — street name alone tells you nothing about tenure or age restriction here. https://vancouvernashdom.com/burnaby-north-brentwood-park-apartments/SearchResults.form/?_pg=7
- **Depth cuts both ways for a downsizer selling a condo here.** With 360 attached listings and 39 attached solds in September 2026 (an 11% sales ratio), a Brentwood seller competes inside their own building and the two nearest towers first — the sub-area average is background, not pricing evidence. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report)
- **The detached streets and the towers live under different rule books.** Houses south of the town centre sit in the R1 SSMUH district (3–4 units on most lots, up to 6 near frequent transit, multi-unit lots stratifiable), while the station lands follow the Brentwood Town Centre framework under the Burnaby 2050 OCP — check which framework a specific lot falls under before assuming infill or assembly value. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite and https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-12-11/city-burnaby-adopts-burnaby-2050-official-community-plan
- **Burnaby replaced its entire zoning bylaw in 2026.** The Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026 (adopted June 9, 2026, in force July 1, 2026) repealed and replaced the Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965 — documents or summaries that still cite the 1965 bylaw are out of date. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **Burnaby taxes have two penalty steps.** 2026 property taxes were due July 3, 2026; a 5% penalty applies after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date, and the detached water charge ($652.33) carries a 5% early-payment discount. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties and https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees
