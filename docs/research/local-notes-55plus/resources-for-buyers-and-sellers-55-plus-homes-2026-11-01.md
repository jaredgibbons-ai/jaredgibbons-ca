## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**This page has no buildings of its own — Section 2 is the 55+ buyer's document checklist: what proves a building is 55+, in the order to check it. A building belongs on a 55+ page only when the first two items below say so.**

### The proof checklist (use for any candidate building)

- **1. The registered strata bylaws.** The bylaw that governs is the strata's registered bylaw set — under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, only a bylaw restricting residents to age 55 or older can be valid at all (since Nov 24, 2022). A listing, a building database or a directory entry is a lead, not proof. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **2. The Form B Information Certificate.** The Form B is the strata's own certificate for the specific lot — it is where the current bylaw position, strata fees, parking/storage allocations and special levies are confirmed for a purchase. This program's files repeatedly direct the buyer to confirm the age test, the stall and locker, and any mandatory service charge on the Form B and in the strata documents, not from listing copy. Source pattern: the sibling files in ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/ (for example the Quilchena Green conflict note in the Richmond city-wide file in this batch).
- **3. The strata document package.** Depreciation report, contingency reserve fund position, strata minutes, insurance summary and any service agreement (mandatory meal/housekeeping plans exist in this market — Mayfair Terrace in Port Coquitlam carries a listing-stated mandatory base service plan on top of strata fees, and Terraces on 7th in Vancouver operates as a retirement community inside a strata; in both cases what is mandatory, and at what charge, is settled only in the documents). https://www.zolo.ca/port-coquitlam-real-estate/2266-atkins-avenue/311 and https://terracesonseventh.com/
- **4. Title and tenure.** Freehold strata, strata leasehold, City-land ground lease (False Creek South), co-op shares and life-lease rights are different purchases. Tenure is read off title and the organisation's own documents — listing text (for example a Yaletown feed card's "prepaid Crown lease to 2084") is UNVERIFIED until then. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/false-creek-south-leases-on-city-land.aspx and https://jaredgibbons.ca/yaletown-condos-for-sale.html
- **5. For non-strata forms, the form's own law.** Manufactured-home parks: the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and its Regulation (B.C. Reg. 481/2003), with park age rules operating through park rules and assignment consent. Co-operatives and life leases: the organisation's own membership / lease documents — the provincial strata exemptions do not automatically apply. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003 and https://www.chf.bc.ca/find-a-co-op/

### Subsidized and care stock — the registries to check (not ownership)

- **BC Housing's Housing Registry** is the application door for subsidized seniors rental: Zone 3 (Richmond and West Delta), Zone 4 (Tri-Cities / Maple Ridge / Pitt Meadows) and Zone 6 (Vancouver) directories list the buildings by area. https://learningondemand.bchousing.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Zone-3-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf and https://www.bchousing.org/publications/Zone-6-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf
- **BC's Assisted Living Registry** is the check for a retirement residence that offers assisted living: it registers residences by city and class (for example the Port Coquitlam and Richmond residences named in this program's files). Publicly funded assisted living and long-term care are accessed through the health authority (Fraser Health / Vancouver Coastal Health) after assessment — not by purchase. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ARichmond&f%5B1%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&name=&address=&order=alr_title&sort=desc
- **The Seniors Services Society's Seniors Housing Directory of BC** is the cross-check list the City of Vancouver itself points seniors to. https://vancouver.ca/people-programs/find-supportive-housing-for-seniors.aspx and https://www.seniorsservicessociety.ca/housing-finder/page/67/

- **A directory placement is not proof.** Granville Gardens Co-op (Richmond) sits in a BC Housing seniors directory while its operator describes an all-ages co-op; 1105 Seymour (Vancouver) sits in a seniors directory while housing families. The operator's documents and the registered bylaws decide — see the Richmond and Vancouver city-wide files in this batch. https://www.granvillegardenshousingcoop.ca/ and https://www.greenbuildingaudiotours.com/buildings/1105_seymour_street
- **Amenities are not an age bylaw.** Pools, clubhouses, libraries and concierges appear in general-market buildings across this program (Apple Greene in Richmond; Yaletown Ltd in Vancouver) with no age restriction in any source. Sources: the Richmond and Yaletown files in this batch.
- **Stale restriction data is the norm, not the exception.** Building databases still display rental bans the Province voided in 2022 and age figures (45+) from a bylaw class that no longer exists. Date every restriction claim and re-verify it against items 1–2 of the checklist above. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Strata frame, in the Province's own terms:** strata owners own their strata lot and together own the common property as a strata corporation, and strata disputes can be resolved using the Civil Resolution Tribunal. Work touching common property is the corporation's to approve, not the individual owner's — the rule behind the hub's painting, staging and repair categories on a strata purchase. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing
- **Representation rules:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and BCFSA states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Property transfer tax — the cost every buyer using this hub is budgeting for:** the Province's general rates are 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000; in most cases property transfers are completed by a legal professional, and the tax is based on fair market value on the day the transfer is registered at the Land Title Office unless an exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Tax relief that matters at 55+:** the Province's Benefits Connector states the regular property tax deferment loan program is for people 55 and older (plus surviving spouses and persons with disabilities), and the additional home owner grant (total $845 in Metro Vancouver / Fraser Valley) is for seniors 65+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/home/benefits
- **Manufactured-home pads follow tenancy law, not strata law:** the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act covers most manufactured home tenancies in BC, pad rent can be increased only once every 12 months with at least three full months' written notice, capped at the provincial limit (2.3% for increases taking effect in 2026) plus a proportional levy/utility amount. http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Federal buyer programs:** FHSA and Home Buyers' Plan dollar limits stay UNVERIFIED in this file — no live CRA page was read for them in this pass, and no limit is stated here rather than estimated. Confirm current limits on the CRA pages before relying on either program.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Who are the professionals on the resources page?** None are named. The page lists thirteen categories — Mortgage Brokers; Home Inspectors; Lawyers & Notaries; Appraisers; Surveyors; Oil Tank Removal; Handyman Services; Professional Painters; Junk / Rubbish Removal; Pressure Washing; Insurance Providers; Professional Organizers; Home Staging Services — and states that Jared can connect you with trusted local professionals, with a recommendation available if you contact him. https://jaredgibbons.ca/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.html
- **Q: A listing says a building is 55+. How do I know that's real?** Run the Section 2 checklist: the strata's registered bylaws first, then the Form B for the specific lot. Since Nov 24, 2022 only a 55+ bylaw can be valid at all — a building advertised as 40+ or 45+ strata is advertising a bylaw that no longer has effect — and this program found buildings marketed as 55+ whose current sources state no age restriction at all (they are listed, with sources, in the sibling area files). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases, and live-in caregivers are exempt. In parks, co-ops and life leases the organisation's own documents govern — ask for them at the same time. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ building?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Can the agent showing me a 55+ unit also act for the seller?** No. BCFSA states a licensed professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: What does a buyer actually pay in property transfer tax?** The general rates are 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion over $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000, calculated on fair market value at registration unless an exemption applies; in most cases a legal professional completes the transfer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: How do I apply for subsidized seniors housing?** Through BC Housing's Housing Registry — the Zone directories (Zone 3 Richmond/West Delta, Zone 4 Tri-Cities/Maple Ridge/Pitt Meadows, Zone 6 Vancouver) list the buildings, and the Registry takes the application. Assisted living and long-term care run through the health authority after assessment, and registered residences can be checked in BC's Assisted Living Registry. https://learningondemand.bchousing.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Zone-3-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf and https://www.bchousing.org/publications/Zone-6-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf
- **Q: Can I defer my property taxes at 55+?** The Province's Benefits Connector states the regular property tax deferment loan program is for people 55 and older (plus surviving spouses and persons with disabilities), and the additional home owner grant (total $845 in Metro Vancouver / Fraser Valley) is for seniors 65+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/home/benefits
- **Q: How much do the services on this page cost, and how fast will I get a name?** UNVERIFIED — the page publishes no prices, fees, timelines or response commitments for any of its thirteen categories, and how the referral list is maintained cannot be confirmed from the page itself; the page's only stated mechanism is to contact Jared for a recommendation. Verify a recommended professional's own credentials and insurance directly — the trades answer to their own bodies, whose requirements are UNVERIFIED in this file. https://jaredgibbons.ca/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.html and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The resources page is a referral offer, not a directory.** There are no names to call — thirteen categories, zero named professionals — so the working step is to contact Jared for the specific category, and to ask who he is sending and why. https://jaredgibbons.ca/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.html
- **The cheapest 55+ verification is also the most skipped.** Every false or stale 55+ claim found in this program — invalid 45+ figures, voided rental bans, directory placements for all-ages buildings, amenity sets mistaken for bylaws — would have been caught by the same two documents: the registered bylaws and the Form B. Listing copy caught none of them. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Two different doors lead to "seniors housing."** Subsidized rental goes through BC Housing's Housing Registry; assisted living and long-term care go through the health authority (Fraser Health / Vancouver Coastal Health) after an assessment. Applying to the wrong door is the most common delay this program's files document. https://www.bchousing.org/publications/Zone-6-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf
- **The hub's one very specific category is oil tank removal** — the only property-condition specialty among thirteen general trades, reflecting how often buried tanks surface in South Surrey and Langley transactions. The rules, permit requirements and cost range for removing a buried residential oil tank in Surrey or Langley are UNVERIFIED in this file — no municipal or provincial source was opened for it, so nothing about the process is stated here beyond the category itself. https://jaredgibbons.ca/resources-for-buyers-and-sellers.html
- **Licensing checks split by profession.** BCFSA's consumer protections cover real estate licensees — commissions and fees information, duties to clients and non-clients, and a complaints route — but the hub's inspectors, appraisers, surveyors and contractors answer to their own bodies. Check each recommended professional's credentials directly; this file states no licensing rule for them. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
