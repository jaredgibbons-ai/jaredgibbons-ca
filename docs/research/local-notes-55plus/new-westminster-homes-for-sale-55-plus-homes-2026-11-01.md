# New Westminster (City-Wide) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Scope note: this is the CITY-WIDE file for the City of New Westminster, consolidating the sub-area files in this program (Downtown NW, Fraserview NW, Moody Park, Quay, Queens Park, Connaught Heights, Glenbrooke North, Queensborough, plus Brunette and North Arm). The headline result is plain: New Westminster has ZERO verified 55+ strata ownership complexes city-wide. Its seniors housing is real but it is rental, subsidized, retirement-residence and care housing — none of it is a strata home a buyer can purchase with a verified 55+ bylaw.**

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. In New Westminster this matters mainly as a verification rule: no city building checked in this program carries a verified 55+ bylaw at all. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **City zoning: Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001** is New Westminster's zoning bylaw (consolidated text as of June 30, 2022 read in this program). Single-detached homes sit in the RS districts (s. 310) — RS-1 minimum site area 557.40 m² (6,000 sq ft) with floor space ratio 0.5 — and the RS districts allow secondary suites, with laneway / carriage houses regulated under the same bylaw; townhouse form is regulated under Townhouse Residential Districts RT-A and RT-B where designated. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Zoning_Bylaw_No_6680_2001_as_of_June_30_2022_(Complete).pdf
- **Plan: Our City 2041, Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 7925, 2017** governs the city; Downtown is designated a Regional City Centre under its Downtown Community Plan (Schedule C of the OCP), and the OCP is under a 2025–2026 update process (OCP Amendment Bylaw No. 8574, 2026). https://www.newwestcity.ca/ocp/past-event-materials-and-council-reports
- **Infill / SSMUH position (June 2026):** Council adopted amendments to the OCP and Zoning Bylaw to permit up to six units of infill housing (secondary suites, laneway and carriage houses, triplex, quadplex and other multiplex forms) on single detached and duplex properties across most of the city, under zones RGO-A (up to four units) and RGO-B (up to six units), without a site-specific rezoning. Queensborough's SSMUH compliance was extended by the Province to May 4, 2029. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects and https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Attachment_C2___Small_Scale_Multi_Unit_Housing_Legislation_and_Implementation_Summary.pdf
- **Secondary suites:** a suite is a separate dwelling unit within a house, accessory to a single detached dwelling or duplex and contained within the same structure; suites cannot be strata-titled, sold or used for short-term rentals, and all properties with suites pay annual secondary suite water and sewer charges. Suite size cap and owner-occupancy rule: UNVERIFIED — neither is stated in the City guide read in this program. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Secondary_Suite_Guide_August_2025.pdf
- **The City's actual seniors-housing lever is adaptable housing, not age restriction:** since a Zoning Bylaw amendment adopted June 28, 2011 (Bylaw No. 7464, 2011), a minimum of 40% of all single-storey dwelling units in multiple-unit residential buildings city-wide must be built as adaptable dwelling units under the BC Building Code — the City states the policy is a proactive means of planning for its aging population. Adaptable is not age-restricted: any buyer can purchase these units. http://newwestcity.ca/housing/adaptable-housing
- **Property taxes:** City of New Westminster property taxes for 2026 were due Thursday, July 2, 2026; a 5% penalty applies to current taxes remaining (including unclaimed Home Owner Grants) on July 3, 2026, and a second 5% penalty applies to any unpaid balance on September 3, 2026. The City held no 2026 tax sale — all delinquent property taxes had been collected. https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php and https://www.newwestcity.ca/2026/09/24/2026-no-property-tax-sale.php
- **Tax relief at 55+:** the Province's regular property tax deferment loan program is for people 55 and older (plus surviving spouses and persons with disabilities), and the additional home owner grant (total $845 in Metro Vancouver / Fraser Valley) is for seniors 65+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/home/benefits

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in New Westminster?** None that any source verifies. Every sub-area file in this program — Downtown, Fraserview, Moody Park, Quay, Queens Park, Connaught Heights, Glenbrooke North, Queensborough, Brunette, North Arm — returns zero verified 55+ ownership complexes. A buyer who needs a legal 55+ purchase is, on the verified record, shopping in another municipality (Maple Ridge's Fraserview Village, Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam are the nearest verified pockets in this program). Sources: Section 2 above and ~/workspace/local-notes/port-coquitlam-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **Q: A listing says a New Westminster condo is 55+. How do I check it?** Against the strata's registered bylaws and the Form B, not the listing copy. The city research carried exactly one such claim — a Fraserview condo on Blackberry Drive, in listing text only — and it was never confirmed on a strata, developer or manager source, so it stays UNCONFIRMED. https://jaredgibbons.ca/new-westminster-homes-for-sale.html and ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-13/new-westminster-homes-for-sale.md
- **Q: What seniors housing does the city actually have?** Rental and care depth, not ownership: two registered assisted-living residences (Thornebridge Gardens, Victoria Heights), long-term care (AgeCare Royal City, Queen's Park Care Centre, Kiwanis Care Centre), and nine subsidized seniors buildings in the Zone 5 directory, filled through BC Housing / society application routes rather than purchase. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata?** Yes, where a genuine 55+ strata exists — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a qualifying resident, with further defined exemptions for children and live-in caregivers. In New Westminster the question is academic for purchase, because no verified 55+ strata exists in the city. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I want to downsize but stay in New Westminster. What actually works?** The house-based routes: an RS-district house with a secondary suite (for family or a caregiver) or a lot that can take a laneway/carriage house under Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001, and — since June 2026 — up to four to six infill units on most single detached and duplex properties under RGO-A / RGO-B. None of those routes is age-restricted; they are downsizing routes, not 55+ product. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Zoning_Bylaw_No_6680_2001_as_of_June_30_2022_(Complete).pdf and https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects
- **Q: Can a New Westminster secondary suite be sold separately or used as a short-term rental?** No — the City's secondary suite guide states suites cannot be strata-titled, sold or used for short-term rentals, and properties with suites pay annual suite water and sewer charges. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Secondary_Suite_Guide_August_2025.pdf
- **Q: Are the Quay towers a seniors option? They look quiet and level.** They are general-market strata. Checked building by building, none carries an age restriction, and a lease listing at 680 Quayside Drive records "Senior Community: No." What the Quay does offer a downsizer is level, elevator, waterfront product — without any age bylaw. https://www.zolo.ca/new-westminster-real-estate/680-quayside-drive/3302
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Royal Columbian Hospital, 330 E Columbia St in Sapperton — a major tertiary care facility immediately adjacent to Sapperton SkyTrain station, which is the practical transit fact for a senior living anywhere in the city. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/New-Westminster/royal-columbian-hospital#.XSdr3flKhGM
- **Q: What is Century House?** The City's seniors activity centre at 620 Eighth Street, membership-based for adults 50+ ($25.00 annual membership on the current City page), dating to 1958 and on the Canadian Register of Historic Places. It is an amenity to live near — it is not housing, and its 50+ rule is a membership rule. https://www.newwestcity.ca/parks-and-recreation/facilities/century-house/1000
- **Q: Can I defer my property taxes at 55+?** The Province's Benefits Connector states the regular property tax deferment loan program is for people 55 and older (plus surviving spouses and persons with disabilities). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/home/benefits

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The city has planned for aging without ever age-restricting a strata.** Its lever since 2011 is the adaptable-housing rule — 40% of single-storey units in multiple-unit buildings must be built adaptable — which is why New Westminster has plenty of level, elevator product a senior can buy and no 55+ bylaws attached to any of it. http://newwestcity.ca/housing/adaptable-housing
- **Two seniors buildings carry standing unit-count conflicts.** Dunwood Place: 193 units (City 2009 inventory / Zone 5 directory) vs 191 capacity (2026 Senior Living Magazine directory). Legion Manor: 123 units (City 2009 inventory) vs 129 homes (Zone 5 directory). Both conflicts are flagged in Section 2 rather than resolved — seniors-housing numbers in this city need a primary source before they are quoted. https://newwestcity.ca/database/rte/Housing%20web%20update%20-%20Non-Market%20details.pdf
- **The board and the City draw Downtown differently.** The City's OCP neighbourhoods map names one area "Downtown (including Quayside)," while the board data lists Downtown and Quay as two separate sub-areas — a listing's sub-area label and a City plan reference for the same tower can therefore disagree without either being wrong. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/OUR_CITY_2041_Official_Community_Plan_New_Westminsters_Residential_Neighbourhoods_Map.pdf
- **The city's two registered assisted-living residences are both upland.** BC's Assisted Living Registry lists exactly two seniors (class SN) residences in New Westminster — Thornebridge Gardens (649 8th Ave) and Victoria Heights (230 Ross Drive) — neither on the waterfront, in Downtown or in Queensborough, which is where search traffic for "New Westminster seniors" usually starts. https://connect.health.gov.bc.ca/assisted-living-residence?f%5B0%5D=city%3ANew%20Westminster&f%5B1%5D=city%3ANorth%20Vancouver&f%5B2%5D=residence_type%3ASeniors%20and%20Persons%20with%20Disabilities&name=&address=&order=alr_legal_operator_name&sort=asc
- **Queensborough runs on its own provincial clock.** Its SSMUH compliance was extended by the Province to May 4, 2029, separate from the mainland city's June 2026 infill framework — infill claims for an island lot and a mainland lot are checked against different timelines. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Attachment_C2___Small_Scale_Multi_Unit_Housing_Legislation_and_Implementation_Summary.pdf
- **The feed count and the board snapshot do not describe the same shelf.** The live city page carried 573 results at reading, while the SnapStats September 2026 rows count 164 detached and 444 attached listings (608 total) — different sets at different moments; neither should be quoted as the other. https://jaredgibbons.ca/new-westminster-homes-for-sale.html (as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats FVR/GVR September 2026 report)
