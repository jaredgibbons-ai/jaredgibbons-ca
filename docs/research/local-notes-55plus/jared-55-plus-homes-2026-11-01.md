## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** legacy residents; live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide** since Nov 24, 2022; stratas may still ban or limit short-term rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Each service-area city runs its own bylaws — a rule from one does not travel:** Surrey's OCP is Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted Jun 15, 2026); White Rock's zoning is Bylaw 2024 No. 2506; Langley City's zoning is Bylaw 2025 No. 3300; the Township of Langley's is Bylaw 1987 No. 2500; Delta's OCP is the consolidated Bylaw 8400 (2024), with no lettered schedules. Sources: the sibling city files in ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/
- **Representation rules (the regulator behind the person this page profiles):** real estate trading services in BC are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), which states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and work for only the buyer or the seller in a transaction. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Property transfer tax — the cost a reader arriving from this bio will budget for:** the Province's general rates are 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000; the tax is based on fair market value on the day the transfer is registered at the Land Title Office unless an exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Tax relief that matters at 55+:** the Province's Benefits Connector states the regular property tax deferment loan program is for people 55 and older (plus surviving spouses and persons with disabilities), and the additional home owner grant (total $845 in Metro Vancouver / Fraser Valley) is for seniors 65+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/home/benefits

- **Q: A building is advertised as 55+. How do I know that's real?** The registered strata bylaws first, then the Form B for the specific lot. Since Nov 24, 2022 only a 55+ bylaw can be valid at all, and this program found buildings marketed as 55+ whose current sources state no age restriction — they are listed, with sources, in the sibling area files. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption); children and live-in caregivers are exempt in defined cases. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy a unit in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out my unit in a 55+ building?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. The tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink my resale buyer pool?** Mechanically, yes: the next buyer must also be able to live there under the bylaw — be 55+, be an exempt spouse/partner, caregiver or legacy case, or buy without living in the unit. That is the trade-off built into every BC 55+ strata since the 2022 law change confined age bylaws to 55+. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Is Harrison Landing in Langley City a condo I can buy?** No. Harrison Landing (20899 Douglas Crescent) is a rental retirement residence — 67 suites marketed to independent seniors aged 55+ — and it is registered as an assisted-living residence. The condo-style suites are rented, not sold. https://harrisoncommunities.com/senior-housing-langley/
- **Q: What protections do I have when I hire a real estate professional in BC?** BCFSA states a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform them of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, disclose at the outset whether they can represent you and how to complain to BCFSA, and work for only the buyer or the seller in a transaction. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Can I defer my property taxes at 55+?** The Province's Benefits Connector states the regular property tax deferment loan program is for people 55 and older (plus surviving spouses and persons with disabilities). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/home/benefits
