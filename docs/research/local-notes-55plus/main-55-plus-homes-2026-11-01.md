# Main (Vancouver) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- "Main" is the Main Street corridor in Vancouver East: the retail and residential spine running south from False Creek / Terminal Avenue through Mount Pleasant and Riley Park toward the Sunset local area at East 49th Avenue. The City lists "Main" as a recognised smaller area, but it is not one of the City's 22 distinct local areas — the corridor crosses the Mount Pleasant, Riley Park and Sunset local areas, and Main Street is also the line that splits the board's Mount Pleasant VE (east) and Mount Pleasant VW (west) sub-areas. A block-by-block board mapping of the Main sub-area is UNVERIFIED. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/mount-pleasant.aspx and https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/areas-of-the-city.aspx
- The corridor's commercial heart is where Broadway, Kingsway and Main Street meet; Main Street is crossed by Terminal Avenue, East Broadway, Kingsway, East King Edward Avenue, East 33rd, East 41st and East 49th Avenues. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/mount-pleasant.aspx
- Schools (SD39; catchments are address-based — the corridor crosses several): Livingstone Elementary, 315 East 23rd Avenue, and Wolfe Elementary, 4251 Ontario Street, in Riley Park; Queen Alexandra Elementary, 1300 East Broadway, at the corridor's north-east edge; Vancouver Technical Secondary, 2600 East Broadway, is the large comprehensive serving this part of the east side. Whether a given corridor address feeds Vancouver Technical: UNVERIFIED. https://vsb.bc.ca/list-of-vsb-schools-by-city-neighborhoods and https://vsb.bc.ca/catchment-boundaries
- Civic anchors: the Mount Pleasant Community Centre and the Vancouver Public Library's Mount Pleasant Branch are co-located at 1 Kingsway, where Kingsway meets Main Street — a roughly 30,000 sq ft LEED Gold civic building opened in December 2009 that also includes rental housing. At the south end, Sunset Community Centre is on Main Street near East 49th Avenue. https://mountpleasantcc.ca/facilities-rentals/ and http://www.vpl.ca/location/mount-pleasant-branch and https://vancouver.ca/parks-recreation-culture/sunset-community-centre.aspx
- Parks: Guelph Park, 2390 Brunswick Street — the park known by its adopted "Dude Chilling Park" sign — one block east of Main in Mount Pleasant; Robson Park at 599 Kingsway at East 15th Avenue. https://covapp.vancouver.ca/ParkFinder/parkdetail.aspx?inparkid=136
- Transit: route 8 runs via Main Street itself; the corridor is crossed by the 9 and 99 B-Line at East Broadway, the R4 41st Ave RapidBus and the 41 at East 41st Avenue, and the 49 at East 49th Avenue. The future Mount Pleasant Station (Broadway Subway) sits at Main Street and Broadway and is scheduled to enter service in fall 2027 as part of the 5.7 km Broadway Subway. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/stop/50849/schedule and https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025TT0110-001048
- Health care on the corridor: Mount Saint Joseph Hospital sits just east of the corridor on Prince Edward Street; Vancouver General Hospital, 899 West 12th Avenue, is west of the corridor. https://www.providencehealthcare.org/en/visitor-guides/mount-saint-joseph-hospital-patient-visitor-guide/when-you-arrive
- Where the seniors housing actually is: not on the Main Street storefront blocks themselves. It clusters just east of Main in two spots — Cavell Gardens, a supportive seniors rental residence on Sophia Street in Mount Pleasant at the corridor's north end, and the Little Mountain blocks between East 33rd and East 37th Avenues, where a 55+ social housing building (155 East 37th Avenue) stands a short walk from the Little Mountain Place long-term care home (330 East 36th Avenue). No seniors-specific building was verified on the Main Street frontage south of East 37th down to East 49th. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 below.

## 2. BUILDINGS AND COMPLEXES

**No verified 55+ strata ownership complex was found in this sub-area.** The Main corridor's verified seniors housing is one supportive rental residence, one subsidized 55+ social housing building and one long-term care home — all rental or care, nothing to buy.

### Verified 55+ strata (ownership)

- None verified in this sub-area. The corridor's strata stock (heritage conversions, low-rise and mid-rise condos near Main and Kingsway) is general-market; no source checked states a 55+ age bylaw for any corridor strata building.

### Verified 55+ rental (not ownership)

- None verified as a market-rental 55+ apartment building on the corridor. The rental seniors housing that exists here is the supportive residence and the subsidized building below.

### Retirement residences and care (rental/care — NOT strata ownership)

- **Cavell Gardens — 2835 Sophia Street.** A supportive seniors rental residence in Mount Pleasant, one block east of Main Street. Its own site calls it "a full service retirement community" offering studio, one-bedroom and one-bedroom Deluxe (2 bath) suites, with respite stays available, and states it is "Celebrating 24 years of Service." A supportive-housing directory description sets out what the rent model includes: meals in a common dining room, weekly housekeeping and limited laundry, a range of social, recreational and fitness activities, and a 24-hour emergency response system. The operator notes Mount Saint Joseph Hospital is within a two-block radius. Rental with services — nothing to buy. Whether Cavell Gardens is registered as assisted living: UNCONFIRMED (it describes itself as supportive housing, and it did not appear as a registered assisted-living residence in the registry results read for this pass). https://cavellgardens.com/ and https://healthchoicesfirst.com/business/cavell-gardens
- **Little Mountain Place — 330 East 36th Avenue.** A Vancouver Coastal Health long-term care home on the Little Mountain social housing lands between Main and Ontario Streets — professional care and supervision for adults with complex care needs who cannot live safely and independently at home. Care housing, allocated through the long-term care system — nothing to buy or rent by application to a landlord. https://www.vch.ca/en/location/little-mountain-place

### Subsidized seniors rental (non-profit, income-based — not ownership)

- **The More Than a Roof seniors building — 155 East 37th Avenue.** The first new social housing building on the Little Mountain redevelopment site, and the corridor's one verified 55+ rental building: the operator, More Than a Roof Housing Society, lists 155 East 37th Avenue as "a dynamic 55+ community offering one and two bedroom apartments close to transit and shopping," with 53 one- and two-bedroom units. The Province's opening release (April 9, 2015) describes 47 one-bedroom and six two-bedroom affordable apartments for seniors in a five-storey building developed by BC Housing and Holborn Properties, managed by More Than a Roof, with an amenity room, community garden and a weekly low-cost food program; a City rezoning report confirms the building has been occupied since April 2015 and was offered to qualifying former Little Mountain Housing residents. Social housing rental — nothing to buy, and entry is through the social housing system, not the open market. https://morethanaroof.org/category/seniors/ and https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2015MNGD0024-000479 and https://council.vancouver.ca/20160629/documents/pspc3.pdf

- **The rental housing inside 1 Kingsway** (co-located with the Mount Pleasant Community Centre and library) is general City rental housing in a civic building — no seniors designation is stated for it. https://mountpleasantcc.ca/facilities-rentals/

Do not list it as a 55+ option. https://www.rew.ca/guide/articles/vancouver-group-aims-to-build-little-mountain-cohousing-complex-1.1786811

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Side-street zoning: R1-1 District Schedule of Zoning and Development By-law No. 3575** — minimum site area 306 m² for a single detached house or duplex and for a multiple dwelling of 3 to 4 units (minimum frontage 10.0 m), 464 m² for 5 units, 557 m² for 6 to 8 units; a single detached house may include a secondary suite and a laneway house, and a multiplex may have up to 6 units or up to 8 rental units. The district schedule for the Main Street commercial frontage itself: UNVERIFIED in the area research. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/zoning/zoning-by-law-district-schedule-R1-1.pdf
- **The Broadway Plan governs the corridor's north end** — approved June 22, 2022, a 30-year framework (Clark Drive to Vine Street, 1st to 16th Avenues) providing for buildings up to 20–30 storeys near the future Mount Pleasant Station, with renter protections and tenant relocation requirements that matter to anyone underwriting the corridor's older rental stock; Council approved updates in December 2024. Citywide direction now comes from the Vancouver Official Development Plan, which replaced the Vancouver Plan on March 31, 2026. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/broadway-plan-approved-by-council.aspx
- **How the City handles seniors housing:** the Zoning and Development By-law's congregate-housing definition covers facilities with residential units for persons aged 55 or over with shared dining and support services, and the City states Council encourages supportive, assisted living and affordable rental housing for seniors while City staff do not manage the facilities or their rental processes — applicants go through the operator, BC Housing, or the Seniors Services Society's Seniors Housing Directory of BC. https://council.vancouver.ca/previous_years/990727/p4.htm and https://vancouver.ca/people-programs/find-supportive-housing-for-seniors.aspx
- **Tax calendar:** City of Vancouver 2026 advance taxes were due February 3, 2026 and main taxes July 3, 2026 (5% penalty on the unpaid balance after each due date). The Empty Homes Tax — 3% of assessed taxable value for homes vacant for the 2025 reference year, 2025 payment due April 16, 2026 — is a live holding cost for investors holding the corridor's older rental or vacant stock. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/deadlines-and-penalties.aspx and https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/empty-homes-tax.aspx

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy on the Main corridor?** None that any source verifies as age-restricted. There is no 55+ strata building on the corridor in the sources checked; the seniors addresses here (Cavell Gardens, the More Than a Roof building at Little Mountain, Little Mountain Place) are supportive rental, subsidized 55+ social housing and long-term care. A 55+ buyer on Main buys a regular condo, townhome or house with no age bylaw. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Can I buy into Cavell Gardens?** No. It is a rental supportive-housing residence for seniors at 2835 Sophia Street — studio to one-bedroom-deluxe suites with meals, housekeeping and a 24-hour emergency response included in the supportive model, plus respite stays. You rent; there is no strata lot. https://cavellgardens.com/ and https://healthchoicesfirst.com/business/cavell-gardens
- **Q: How do I get into the 55+ building at Little Mountain?** It is social housing, not market rental: 53 affordable apartments for seniors at 155 East 37th Avenue, developed by BC Housing and Holborn and managed by the More Than a Roof Housing Society, which lists it as a 55+ community. The building was first offered to qualifying former Little Mountain Housing residents when it opened in April 2015; current availability runs through the social housing system and the operator, not through listings. https://morethanaroof.org/category/seniors/ and https://council.vancouver.ca/20160629/documents/pspc3.pdf
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere in Vancouver?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — a BC age bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: When does the Main Street SkyTrain station open?** Mount Pleasant Station, at Main Street and Broadway, is scheduled to enter service in fall 2027 as one of six underground stations on the 5.7 km Broadway Subway — a dated event in the Province's release, not an open-ended promise. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025TT0110-001048
- **Q: Where is the hospital?** Mount Saint Joseph Hospital is just east of the corridor at its Prince Edward Street entrance; Vancouver General Hospital, 899 West 12th Avenue, is west of the corridor. Cavell Gardens' operator notes Mount Saint Joseph is within a two-block radius of that residence. https://www.providencehealthcare.org/en/visitor-guides/mount-saint-joseph-hospital-patient-visitor-guide/when-you-arrive and https://cavellgardens.com/
- **Q: Can a side-street house lot hold a suite or extra homes for family?** Yes, where the R1-1 schedule is met — a house may include a secondary suite and a laneway house, and a multiplex may have up to 6 units or up to 8 rental units, with minimum site areas from 306 m² to 557 m² by form. That, not a 55+ building, is the corridor's practical multi-generational route. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/zoning/zoning-by-law-district-schedule-R1-1.pdf
- **Q: Is flood risk a factor on the corridor?** Only at the north end: the corridor's False Creek / Terminal Avenue lowland sits in the City's designated floodplain (the False Creek coastal floodplain), with Building By-law setback and flood construction level requirements for parcels in it; the residential grid south of 2nd Avenue sits on the Mount Pleasant slope. A specific parcel's status: UNVERIFIED. https://opendata.vancouver.ca/explore/dataset/designated-floodplain/information/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The corridor's 55+ building is a replacement-housing artifact.** Little Mountain was built in 1954 by the federal government as the first large-scale modern social housing project in Vancouver (224 units). When the site was redeveloped, the very first replacement building completed — ahead of the market towers — was the 53-unit seniors building at 155 East 37th Avenue, occupied April 2015, with former Little Mountain residents first in line. The full rezoning contemplates approximately 1,573 dwelling units including 282 social housing units. https://council.vancouver.ca/20160629/documents/pspc3.pdf
- **The seniors cluster hides one block off Main.** Both Little Mountain seniors addresses (155 East 37th Avenue and Little Mountain Place at 330 East 36th Avenue) sit on the Ontario-to-Main block grid, not on Main Street frontage — a buyer driving the corridor will pass within a block of the area's entire subsidized and care supply without seeing a sign on Main. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Cavell Gardens predates the branding around it.** The operator states it is celebrating 24 years of service and markets its location as the "Soma" district of Mount Pleasant — the residence is an established supportive-housing building on a quiet side street (Sophia), while the towers and the subway station reshaping the corridor are all newer than it is. https://cavellgardens.com/
- **The Broadway Plan's renter protections are a pricing input, not fine print.** The plan carries tenant relocation and renter protection requirements for the corridor's older rental buildings near the future station — factor them into any redevelopment underwriting at the north end before assuming plan density equals entitled density. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/broadway-plan-approved-by-council.aspx
- **"Mount Pleasant" is an 1888 name with an industrial root:** the area high above False Creek was named in 1888 after the Irish birthplace of the wife of H.V. Edmonds, and Brewery Creek's water supply made it an early industry and settlement site — the heritage stock on the side streets (and the Lee Building at Main and Broadway) dates from that era, not from the recent condo cycle. https://vancouver.ca/news-calendar/mount-pleasant.aspx and https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/mount-pleasant-historic-context-statement.pdf
- **The Empty Homes Tax bites hardest exactly here:** corridor investors holding older vacant or rental stock face the annual declaration and 3%-of-assessed-value tax on vacant homes on top of the February/July property tax dates — a holding cost that does not exist for the same purchase in Burnaby or the Fraser Valley. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/empty-homes-tax.aspx
