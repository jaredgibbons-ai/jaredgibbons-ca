## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1/123.2; any bylaw below 55 has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022 (Bill 44). A foreclosure does not remove a strata's age bylaw — a buyer of a 55+ unit through a court-ordered sale must still be able to reside under it or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** Under-55s can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Regulation 7.01):** legacy residents; live-in caregivers; younger spouse/partner; children under 19 in the resident's care; adult children cared for before 19 (expanded May 1, 2023). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide** since Nov 24, 2022; short-term rental bylaws are still allowed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Foreclosure process — the petition and redemption (Rule 21-7):** a foreclosure proceeding must be started by petition (Rule 21-7 (1)); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be a respondent (Rule 21-7 (2)); the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125 (Rule 21-7 (4.1)); the court may order payment within a redemption period it fixes — the Rules state no fixed length — and foreclosure of the equity of redemption in default (Rule 21-7 (5) (b)); a final order of foreclosure may follow default (Rule 21-7 (6)); a party may apply at any time for an order that the property be sold (Rule 21-7 (7)). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Court confirmation of a sale (Rule 21-7 (9)):** even before the redemption period expires, the person having conduct of a sale may apply for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — until that order, the sale is not final. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct of sale and court approval (Rule 13-5):** the BC Real Estate Association's practice article records that a lender may apply under Rule 13-5 for "conduct of sale," that Rule 13-5 (4) authorizes the court to direct the manner of sale including sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court," and that the order will almost always require the sale to be subject to court approval. The detailed Rule 13-5 mechanics and the court's Practice Direction PD-66 were not retrieved from an official text in the base research — treat bid mechanics for a specific file as file-specific and get them from the seller's counsel. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/proceeding-with-caution-court-ordered-sales-in-the-foreclosure-context-590/
- **"As is, where is":** the same BCREA article states properties sold under court order are sold "as-is, where-is," meaning no guarantees or warranties are made — the lender with conduct of sale is not the owner and cannot give an owner's disclosures. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/proceeding-with-caution-court-ordered-sales-in-the-foreclosure-context-590/
- **City rules: Delta Zoning Bylaw No. 7600; OCP Bylaw No. 8400 (2024), "Housing Our Future"** — Delta's current consolidated OCP. Foreclosure is a provincial court process; no Delta-specific foreclosure rule was found on an official municipal source, and any City statement about a specific property (taxes, utilities, permits) is a per-property check: UNVERIFIED here. http://www.delta.ca/building-development/housing-our-future
- **Secondary suites in Delta:** legalized in single-family dwellings in 2010; Bylaw No. 8242 (2023) amended Bylaw No. 7600 so a suite's floor area is less than 50% of the dwelling's gross floor area (excluding an attached garage) — relevant when valuing a court-ordered house with a suite. https://delta.civicweb.net/document/218413/

A buyer's lawyer must confirm both on the specific property before an offer. (General PTT tiers are stated in the sibling Ladner topic files from the Province's PTT page; they are deliberately not repeated here as foreclosure-specific advice.)

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Is my accepted offer final? What does court approval mean?** No — not on acceptance alone. The person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7 (9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser, and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires. Under Rule 13-5 the court directs the manner of sale, including sale by contract conditional on court approval, and the order will almost always require court approval of the sale. Do not book movers on acceptance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/proceeding-with-caution-court-ordered-sales-in-the-foreclosure-context-590/
- **Q: What does "as is, where is" actually mean for me?** The property is sold with no guarantees or warranties — the seller with conduct of sale is the lender, not the owner, and cannot disclose the property's condition the way an owner can. BCREA's practice article puts it directly: court-ordered properties are sold "as-is, where-is," meaning no guarantees or warranties are made. Budget for your own inspection and due diligence before subjects come off. https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/legally-speaking/proceeding-with-caution-court-ordered-sales-in-the-foreclosure-context-590/
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer at court?** UNVERIFIED as a stated process rule in this file — the bid-rights wording in Rule 13-5 and the PD-66 timing were not retrieved from an official source. Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for, and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing before spending on inspections. Source: the UNVERIFIED list in the area research file, ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/ladnerforeclosuresandcourtsales.md.
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — it is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5) (b). A final order of foreclosure may follow default under Rule 21-7 (6), and a sale can be confirmed even before the period expires under Rule 21-7 (9) — ask which stage the file is at before spending money. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Do I pay GST on a foreclosure purchase?** UNCONFIRMED from an official source in this research — no CRA or Excise Tax Act source on GST treatment of a foreclosure / court-ordered sale was read, so no GST answer is stated. Have your lawyer confirm GST (and PTT) on the specific property before you set your ceiling; do not assume a used home's usual treatment carries over without that check.
- **Q: Where can I check the court file for a Ladner foreclosure?** Court Services Online offers eSearch to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: If I buy a unit in a 55+ building (Delta Green, South Pointe, Laburnum Park Place) through a court-ordered sale, does the 55+ bylaw still apply to me?** Yes — the age bylaw is a strata bylaw, not a term of the owner's mortgage, and court confirmation vests title in the purchaser under Rule 21-7 (9) without removing strata bylaws. The resident must still meet the 55+ requirement or an exemption (younger spouse/partner, caregiver, legacy and care cases under Regulation 7.01). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a 55+ unit I buy this way?** Long-term rental bans are void province-wide since Nov 24, 2022; the tenant must still satisfy the 55+ bylaw or an exemption, and short-term rentals can still be restricted. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: Are Ladner foreclosures usually bargains?** No figure can be stated: no official source publishes Ladner foreclosure counts or discount-to-market statistics, and SnapStats publishes no foreclosure category and no South Delta section — any "typical discount" claim for Ladner is UNVERIFIED. ## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **"Bank foreclosure" is a misnomer in BC.** Sales run through the Supreme Court, not the bank: the lender petitions, the court fixes a redemption period, and a judge confirms the sale and vests title. The timeline is the court's, not a listing agent's. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **The court file is public and is the real due-diligence document.** Court Services Online shows the petition, the amount certified due and the orders made for class "H - Foreclosure" files — in a process where the seller gives no warranties, the file is where the facts of the deal live. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Ladner's 55+ stock is small enough that a single court file changes the picture.** Ladner has three verified 55+ ownership communities in total — a 24-unit condo building (Delta Green) and two 1989 bare land strata rancher communities (South Pointe, Laburnum Park Place) — so a court-ordered listing in any one of them can be the only 55+ ownership listing live in Ladner at that moment. No count of current foreclosure listings is published: UNVERIFIED. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **A vesting order is what cleans the title.** On confirmation, the court's order directs the disposition of the proceeds and vests title in the purchaser (Rule 21-7 (9)) — that order, not a standard conveyance promise, is the buyer's title protection in this process. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **No Ladner foreclosure statistics exist to lean on.** SnapStats has no foreclosure category and no South Delta community section, and Court Services Online publishes no area statistics — a buyer pricing a Ladner court-ordered sale is pricing off the specific file and the building's own history, not a published discount benchmark. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Delta's tax calendar still runs during a court file.** Delta's 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty after that date and a further 5% after September 2, 2026 — arrears and penalties on the specific property are a per-property City check, UNVERIFIED for any given foreclosure listing. https://www.delta.ca/services/property-taxes-utilities/property-taxes
