## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **City zoning: Zoning Bylaw No. 7600-2019** contains the land-use and development regulations for every property in Maple Ridge (verified on the City's planning pages Nov 1, 2026 — the number is 7600-2019, not 7600-2020); its RS-1 standard (s. 605) is a minimum lot area of 668.0 m², width 18.0 m, depth 27.0 m and maximum coverage 40%. https://www.mapleridge.ca/build-do-business/land-development/planning-application-forms-procedures and http://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/2020-07-28-zoning-bylaw-7600-2019-second-reading
- **City plan: Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 7060-2014** is the OCP bylaw cited on the City's own OCP chapter documents (verified Nov 1, 2026 — not 7601-2020). https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/01ocp-chapter-1201402201612431396
- **Added units:** eligible single-detached and duplex lots may have up to three, four or six units under the City's small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH) bylaws — a development-value question on any large urban lot in this band, settled lot by lot, not by the listing price. https://www.mapleridge.ca/build-do-business/planning-land-use/community-planning/housing-action/housing-target-order/new
- **Manufactured home parks run on different law.** Pad tenancies are governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and its Regulation (B.C. Reg. 481/2003), not the Strata Property Act; the Regulation contemplates parks where every site is reserved for a tenant who has reached 55 (or two or more tenants, at least one of whom has reached 55), and a park landlord may refuse an assignment where the proposed purchaser does not meet the age requirement in such a park. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Property transfer tax at this band:** the Province publishes general PTT at 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; if a property has residential property worth over $3,000,000, a further 2% tax applies to the residential property value greater than $3,000,000 (on farm-classed land, up to 0.5 hectares used for a dwelling is treated as residential). PTT is based on fair market value on the day the property is registered at the Land Title Office. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Neither BC purchase exemption reaches this band:** the first time home buyers' program full exemption caps at a fair market value of $835,000 — far below this page's floor — so a buyer here budgets full PTT. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Home owner grant phase-out, directly relevant at this band:** the Province states the grant threshold for 2026 is $2,075,000 — a full regular grant at or below that assessed (or partitioned) value, reduced by $5 for each $1,000 of assessed value over $2,075,000, and $0 for the regular grant if the value is over $2,189,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **FHSA and Home Buyers' Plan dollar limits: UNVERIFIED** — no live CRA page was read for this file, so no FHSA or HBP limit is stated here.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I am selling a Maple Ridge home over $1.8M and downsizing. Is there any 55+ product at anywhere near that price point in Maple Ridge?** No verified 55+ ownership product in Maple Ridge is an upper-end or estate product in any source checked — the verified stock is 1990s strata apartments (Fraserview Village), manufactured homes on rented pads, co-operative townhouses and Legion life leases. No price is stated for any complex in this file; the point is the product type: the $1.8M band is detached houses and acreages, and the 55+ supply is apartment, park, co-op and life-lease form. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Do I pay property transfer tax when I SELL the $1.8M+ home?** No — PTT is paid by the buyer when a property is registered at the Land Title Office, based on fair market value on registration day. Your tax event is on the purchase side: the 55+ home you buy is taxed at the general tiers (1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000), with the further 2% only on residential value over $3,000,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Is there extra property transfer tax on a home over $3 million?** Yes. The Province applies a further 2% tax to the residential property value greater than $3,000,000, on top of the general tiers; on a mixed-class property the further 2% applies only to the residential portion, and on farm-classed land up to 0.5 hectares used for a dwelling is treated as residential. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Can I use the first-time buyer exemption on the 55+ purchase?** The first time home buyers' program full exemption caps at a fair market value of $835,000 in any case, and a downsizer selling a home they owned does not meet the program's buyer conditions as published — budget full PTT on the 55+ purchase at the general tiers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. If we sell the house and buy into a 55+ strata, can we both live there?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Note the guarantee is a strata rule: the St. George's co-op's own form allows a dependent aged 19+ to live with a member, and whether a younger spouse qualifies as that "dependent" is UNCONFIRMED — ask the co-op directly. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www.stgeorgesvillage.ca/_files/ugd/070889_39221c746a9b483980e20a3596cdb580.pdf
- **Q: Could I buy the 55+ unit first, rent it out while my house sells, then move in?** In a strata, long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches: the tenant must still satisfy the 55+ bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. In the parks and co-ops, rental and occupancy rules are the park's / co-op's own documents, not strata law. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: Does the home owner grant change when I move?** The grant is claimed on the principal residence you occupy, and at this band the phase-out is the live issue on the sale side: for 2026 the threshold is $2,075,000, the regular grant reduces by $5 per $1,000 over that value, and it is $0 over $2,189,000. The 55+ unit you buy into is assessed on its own value for the grant you claim there. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
- **Q: Are pets allowed when we downsize?** Complex by complex: Rosewood Court records pets allowed with restrictions; The Willows' listing states 2 cats or 2 dogs (or 1 of each); the Alouette park's listing states no dogs, indoor cat only; Val Maria's listing states 2 small pets allowed. A large dog from the acreage is a real filter — check it complex by complex before shortlisting. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/rosewood-court-22611-116-ave and https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r3067037-12868-229th-street-18-maple-ridge-bc-v2x-6r1/brochure and https://www.canadashomesearch.com/listing/r3089789-18-21163-lougheed-highway-maple-ridge-british-columbia-v2x2r4-259000/brochure
- **Q: What parcel checks matter most on a Maple Ridge acreage over $1.8M?** Three, all parcel-specific: ALR status, through the Agricultural Land Commission's maps and online mapping tools; floodplain status on Fraser River frontage (flood construction level 6.7 m geodetic, covenant on title); and escarpment status on the south-western slopes (geotechnical report within 300 m of the crest, detailed report within 100 m). https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/ and https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/floodplain-construction-0 and http://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/fraser-river-escarpment-information-package
- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink my resale buyer pool when I eventually sell the strata unit?** Mechanically, yes: the next buyer must also be able to live there under the bylaw — be 55+, be an exempt spouse/partner, caregiver or legacy case, or buy without living in the unit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
