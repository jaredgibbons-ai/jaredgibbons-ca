# London Landing, Steveston (Richmond) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

**SLUG-NAME MISMATCH — read first:** this page's slug is "lower-landing," but no City of Richmond source read uses the name "Lower Landing" for any Richmond area. The official name for this Steveston ground is **London Landing** — it is in Richmond Zoning Bylaw 8500 as the site-specific "London Landing (Steveston)" zones, and on the ground at London's Wharf Park, 6062 Dyke Road. This file is written on the London Landing basis, following the area research file, and the mismatch is flagged here rather than silently resolved. (An unrelated "Lower Landing" exists as a Chilliwack / Paragon sub-area name, so a slug mix-up is possible.) https://www.Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/ZMU2033849.pdf

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- London Landing is the waterfront pocket at the east edge of Steveston, at the south foot of No. 2 Road where London Road and Dyke Road meet the South Arm of the Fraser River. It sits inside the Steveston planning area under the Steveston Area Plan (Schedule 2.4 of the Official Community Plan). No City source read publishes a boundary for a "London Landing" neighbourhood — UNVERIFIED as a bounded area; the sourced anchors are London's Wharf Park at 6062 Dyke Road, London Road (The Pier at London Landing, 6160 London Road), Princess Street (London Landing townhouses, 13200 Princess Street) and the No. 2 Road addresses 12771–13131 now in rezoning. https://www.richmond.ca/parks-recreation/parks/parksearch/park.aspx?ID=136 and https://citycouncil.richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Application_by_Jim_Pattison_Developments_for_Rezoning_at_13131__13111__1303179429.pdf
- The name is a settler-family landing name: Steveston heritage material records that in 1881 brothers Charles and William London bought 200 acres at the south end of No. 2 Road at what became known as London's Landing. https://stevestonheritage.ca/about/
- Nearby areas: Steveston Village to the west along the South Arm waterfront, Steveston South (the surrounding board sub-area — in board listing practice this ground falls inside Steveston South), Steveston North across Steveston Highway, and London/Steveston / South Arm to the east and north-east. https://www.richmond.ca/parks-recreation/parks-trails-cycling/trails-map/steveston.htm
- The waterfront anchor: London's Wharf Park, 6062 Dyke Road, is just under 4 acres with the No. 2 Road Pier, river views, trail connections to the South Arm Dyke and Steveston Greenways networks, a playground and a public restroom; the City's Steveston Greenways material describes the London's Landing / No. 2 Road Fishing Pier area as historic, with a fishing float, picnic tables and interpretation signage. https://www.richmond.ca/parks-recreation/parks/parksearch/park.aspx?ID=136 and https://www.richmond.ca/parks-recreation/parks-trails-cycling/trails-map/steveston.htm
- Recreation and library: Steveston Community Park, 4011 Moncton Street, holds the Steveston Community Centre, pool and library complex for the district, and Richmond Public Library's Steveston Branch (Easthope Hub) is at 4320 Moncton Street — both west in the village. https://www.richmond.ca/parks-recreation/parks-trails-cycling/trails-map/steveston.htm
- Schools: School District 38 (Richmond) catchments are address-based with no middle-school tier (elementary K–7, secondary 8–12); Lord Byng Elementary (3711 Georgia Street), Steves Elementary (10111 Fourth Avenue), Westwind Elementary (11371 Kingfisher Drive) and R.A. McMath Secondary (4251 Garry Street) serve the wider Steveston district, but the SD38 locator result for a London Landing address (sample: 6160 London Road) is UNVERIFIED — check the exact address. One Harbour Walk listing states Homma Elementary and McMath Secondary catchments for 13040 No. 2 Road — listing-stated, not locator-verified. https://sd38.bc.ca/schools/locations-catchments and https://www.boldcalgaryrealestate.ca/listing-crea/r3076565-18-13040-no-2-road-richmond-british-columbia-v7e0c4/brochure
- Health care: the nearest hospital is Richmond Hospital, 7000 Westminster Highway (Vancouver Coastal Health). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/health/about-bc-s-health-care-system/heath-care-partners/hospitallist.pdf
- Transit: buses serve Steveston Exchange on Chatham Street in the village, including TransLink route 401 (One Road) to Richmond–Brighouse Station via No. 1 Road for Canada Line connections; a route stopping inside London Landing itself is UNVERIFIED. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt401.pdf
- Where the seniors housing actually is: **not in this pocket — it is in Steveston Village, west along Chatham Street, Moncton Street and Garry Street** (The Maple Residences, Austin Harris Residence, Wisteria Place, Bill Rigby Senior Apartments — each verified by name in Section 2 and each explicitly outside the London Landing pocket). London Landing's own buildings — The Pier and Harbour Walk — are general-market strata with no age restriction in any source checked.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The City's own seniors threshold is 55.** Richmond's 2019 Housing Needs and Co-Location staff report discusses seniors' housing need with seniors defined as individuals aged 55 years and older. https://citycouncil.richmond.ca/__shared/assets/_2_HousingNeeds_CoLocationOpp_PLN_071619_54160.pdf
- **Zoning — the pocket's own zones are mixed-use, not seniors zones:** Richmond Zoning Bylaw 8500 Section 20.20 is the site-specific "Commercial Mixed Use (ZMU20) – London Landing (Steveston)" zone, created by Bylaw 8818 (September 24, 2012), whose stated purpose is to provide for commercial, residential and industrial uses in the Steveston area, with a maximum floor area ratio of 1.2 plus 0.1, maximum lot coverage of 76% and maximum height of 21.0 m; the ZMU24 zone (Bylaw 9094, 2014) was created for 13040 No. 2 Road. Neither zone carries any age or seniors-use requirement — seniors housing here would be a market or non-profit decision, not a zoning category. https://www.Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/ZMU2033849.pdf and https://citycouncil.richmond.ca/agendafiles/Public_Hearing_5-20-2014.pdf
- **Houses at the edges:** detached houses near the pocket sit in Richmond Zoning Bylaw 8500 Single Detached zones RS1 and RS2 (Section 8.1), with secondary suite rules in Section 5.4 (suite minimum 33.0 m²; maximum the lesser of 40% of the dwelling floor area or 110 m²) — the suite is the ageing-in-place mechanism for the pocket's house streets. https://Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/RS1-RS2-RS324187.pdf and https://RICHMOND.CA/business-development/planning/housing-legislation.htm
- **Heritage edge:** the Steveston Village Heritage Conservation Area sits west of this pocket, and properties inside it are subject to Heritage Alteration Permit requirements under the Steveston Area Plan; whether any London Landing parcel sits inside the conservation area boundary is UNVERIFIED. https://www.richmond.ca/business-development/planning/heritage/village-conservation.htm?PageMode=HTML
- **Flood — a pocket that fronts the river behind the dike:** the pocket fronts the South Arm of the Fraser River behind the City dike system; Richmond is made up of islands within the Fraser River floodplain at an average of 1 metre above sea level, protected by 49 km of dikes and 39 drainage pump stations, designed to handle a 1:500 flood event, with dikes being raised from 3.5 metres to 4.7 metres for sea level rise. New construction is subject to flood construction levels and dike setbacks under the Flood Plain Designation and Protection Bylaw 8204. https://richmond.ca/services/water-sewer-flood/dikes.htm and https://www.Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Bylaw_8204_0410201225280.pdf
- **Property taxes and utilities:** City of Richmond property taxes are due once a year on the first business day of July — Thursday, July 2, 2026 — with a 5% penalty on any unpaid balance after that date and a further 5% penalty on balances still outstanding after September 1, 2026. Richmond's 2026 typical single-family metered utility total is $2,251 per year, including a $342 flood protection line. https://richmond.ca/city-hall/finance/rates/duedates-taxes.htm and https://RICHMOND.CA/city-hall/finance/utility-fees/utilityrates.htm
- **Subsidized seniors housing runs through BC Housing's Housing Registry** (Zone 3: Richmond and West Delta directory, May 2025). https://learningondemand.bchousing.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Zone-3-Seniors-and-Adults-with-Disabilities.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in London Landing?** None that any source verifies. The pocket's strata buildings — The Pier (6160 London Road) and Harbour Walk (13040 No. 2 Road) — carry no age restriction in any source checked, and there is no age-restricted strata anywhere in Steveston in this pass. A 55+ buyer here is buying a regular waterfront condo or townhome with no age bylaw, in a pocket whose amenity (the wharf, the pier, the dyke trails) is the draw. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Is this area really called "Lower Landing"?** No City of Richmond source uses that name. The official name is London Landing — it is in the City's Zoning Bylaw as the "London Landing (Steveston)" zones and on the ground at London's Wharf Park — and it comes from Charles and William London, who bought 200 acres at the south end of No. 2 Road in 1881. Search and title work should use London Landing / Steveston South. https://www.Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/ZMU2033849.pdf and https://stevestonheritage.ca/about/
- **Q: Where do Steveston seniors actually live, if not in London Landing?** In the village's non-profit and rental stock, west of the pocket: The Maple Residences (4071 Chatham St, 144 independent-living rental units, non-profit society), Wisteria Place (4388 Garry St, 107 independent-living suites, opened 2021), Austin Harris Residence (5411 Moncton St, 50-unit registered assisted living), and BC Housing–listed subsidized buildings including Bill Rigby Senior Apartments (4100 Chatham St). All rental or care — none of it is ownership. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. If we buy into a 55+ strata elsewhere in Richmond, can we both live there?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption); children under 19 in the resident's care and adult children cared for before 19 are also exempt in the defined cases. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building for a parent?** Ownership is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. Under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a 55+ parent can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ strata?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. The Pier's own listings already state rentals allowed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation and https://www.vancouverhomesearch.com/listing/r3079967-6160-london-road-131-richmond-steveston-south-homes-for-sale-675000/brochure
- **Q: What is being built on No. 2 Road at London Landing?** As of the July 8, 2026 City staff report, Jim Pattison Developments' application RZ 25-009451 proposes rezoning 13131, 13111, 13031, 12931 and 12771 No. 2 Road from Light Industrial (IL) to a new "Residential / Limited Commercial (ZMU63) – London Landing (Steveston)" zone, with OCP Amendment Bylaw 10771 and Zoning Amendment Bylaw 10772, for a five- and six-storey residential and commercial mixed-use project — an application in process, not an approval, and not a seniors project on any source read. https://citycouncil.richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Application_by_Jim_Pattison_Developments_for_Rezoning_at_13131__13111__1303179429.pdf
- **Q: Is London Landing in a floodplain?** The pocket sits directly behind the South Arm dike; all of Richmond is designated flood plain under Bylaw 8204, behind a 49 km dike system designed for a 1:500 event with dike raising from 3.5 m to 4.7 m under way, and dike setbacks and the flood construction level govern any rebuild or addition on dyke-adjacent lots. https://richmond.ca/services/water-sewer-flood/dikes.htm and https://www.Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Bylaw_8204_0410201225280.pdf
- **Q: What are the strata risks specific to these buildings?** They are mixed-use strata — residential over commercial/industrial-permitted uses under the ZMU zones (The Pier's commercial space includes restaurants and retail) — so the depreciation report, contingency fund and commercial-component cost sharing deserve an early read; The Pier's listings also note city utilities included in the strata fee, a geothermal building-specific arrangement to confirm in the documents. https://williamsengineering.com/projects/the-pier-at-london-landing/ and https://www.sellvanhomes.ca/listing-rebgv_sold/r2938130-6168-london-road-311-richmond-bc-v7e-3s3/brochure
- **Q: If I need care later, can I stay in Steveston?** The Steveston ladder exists, but it is rental and care, west of the pocket: independent rental at The Maple Residences or Wisteria Place, then registered assisted living at Austin Harris Residence (5411 Moncton St), with access assessed through Vancouver Coastal Health. Nothing on that ladder is in London Landing itself, and none of it can be bought. https://successbc.ca/assisted-living/services/austin-harris-residence/ and https://www.steveston.bc.ca/documents/201111_VillagerNewsletter_NOVEMBER_FINAL._001.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The slug name is wrong, and the correction is documented, not cosmetic.** "Lower Landing" appears in no City source read; the zoning bylaw, the wharf park and the buildings all say London Landing, and the research program's own Steveston South file treats this ground as Steveston South's east edge. A page titled "Lower Landing" will not match how the City, the board or buyers name this ground. https://www.Richmond.ca/__shared/assets/ZMU2033849.pdf
- **The No. 2 Road strip is a live rezoning file right now (2026).** The July 8, 2026 staff report on RZ 25-009451 also proposes redesignating the No. 2 Road lands from "Industrial" to "Local Village – Tier 1" in the Steveston Waterfront Neighbourhood Land Use Map — views, traffic and construction on the pocket's main street are in play, and the five addresses (12771–13131 No. 2 Road) are named in the application. https://citycouncil.richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Application_by_Jim_Pattison_Developments_for_Rezoning_at_13131__13111__1303179429.pdf
- **The wharf park itself was under public review in 2026.** The City ran a 2026 "Have Your Say" process on recent changes and future improvements at London's Wharf Park (6062 Dyke Road) — the pocket's front-door amenity is an active City file, not a finished one. https://www.richmond.ca/parks-recreation/parks/parksearch/park.aspx?ID=136
- **Steveston's seniors stock is young, non-profit and land-lease based.** Wisteria Place (107 suites) opened in 2021 on land leased from the Steveston Buddhist Temple and was fully occupied within about a year of opening; The Maple Residences (144 units) opened in 2012 on the ANAF veterans' society site at No. 1 Road and Chatham Street. The pattern a buyer should see: Steveston adds seniors housing through societies on institutional land, not through market strata — which is why there is no 55+ building to buy here. https://optimaliving.ca/2021/10/wisteria-place-opens-its-doors-to-new-residents-and-the-richmond-community/ and https://pagesarchive.blackpress.ca/BC/Processed/RIR/120801/RIRN120801_A15.pdf
- **The pocket's buildings predate their own neighbourhood brand.** The Pier's rezoning was approved September 24, 2012 under Bylaw 8818 — the same bylaw that created the ZMU20 "London Landing (Steveston)" zone — and Harbour Walk's ZMU24 followed in 2014; the "London Landing" name in City law is barely a decade old, built on an 1881 landing name. https://citycouncil.richmond.ca/__shared/assets/Oris_PublicArt_CNCL_07131541953.pdf and https://stevestonheritage.ca/about/
- **The dike is part of the utility bill:** Richmond's 2026 typical single-family metered utility total of $2,251 per year includes a $342 flood protection line — in London Landing that charge buys the South Arm dike at the end of London Road. https://RICHMOND.CA/city-hall/finance/utility-fees/utilityrates.htm
- **Richmond's two money dates:** 2026 property taxes were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty after that date and a further 5% on any outstanding balance after September 1, 2026. https://richmond.ca/city-hall/finance/rates/duedates-taxes.htm
