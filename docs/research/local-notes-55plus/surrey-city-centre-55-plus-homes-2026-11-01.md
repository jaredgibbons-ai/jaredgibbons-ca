# Surrey City Centre — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**NAMING NOTE:** "Surrey City Centre" is the City of Surrey's official name for the downtown core of Whalley — the City lists it as "City Centre (Whalley)," one of its six communities — but the Fraser Valley board's sub-area for this geography is **"Whalley,"** not "Surrey City Centre." Board reporting carries a "Whalley" sub-area and no "City Centre" sub-area, so listings and stats for the downtown are filed under Whalley. Title and counts in this file follow that geography (downtown core inside Whalley). https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- The core is the downtown around King George Boulevard, City Parkway and 104 Avenue, inside the larger Whalley community in northwest Surrey; Guildford is east, Newton south, Bridgeview and the Fraser River north, and Scott Road / North Delta west. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- Planning frame: the City Centre Plan (approved 2017) designates a high-density mixed-use downtown, and an update underway adds a defined Central Business District, an Entertainment District, and more transit-oriented development around stations; in October 2025 Council endorsed creating an Entertainment District in City Centre. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- This is Surrey's tower market: the Whalley sub-area's September 2026 SnapStats row showed 635 attached listings against 86 detached — the deepest attached inventory of any Surrey sub-area — with high-rise towers near the stations, mid-rise and townhouse product at the core's edges, and older single-detached stock in surrounding Whalley blocks. (Area research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/surrey-city-centre.md, citing stats/subarea-stats-sep2026.csv, read 2026-11-01.) https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- Transit: Whalley/City Centre is the only Surrey community served by SkyTrain — Surrey's four Expo Line stations (Scott Road, Gateway, Surrey Central and King George) are all in this area — and the R1 King George Blvd RapidBus runs through the core from Newton Exchange to Guildford via Surrey Central and King George stations. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey and https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- Institutions in the core: SFU Surrey and KPU's Civic Plaza campus, Surrey City Hall, the City Centre Library, Holland Park (the downtown's festival green space) and the Chuck Bailey Recreation Centre (being expanded); Central City mall anchors retail at Surrey Central and King George Hub adds grocery and services at King George Station. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- Schools in the area: A.H.P. Matthew Elementary (K–7, 13367 97 Ave) and École Kwantlen Park Secondary (8–12, 10441 132 St, French Immersion and Inter-A), School District 36; catchments are address-specific — for the test address 13367 97 Ave the SD36 locator gives A. H. P. Matthew Elementary and Queen Elizabeth Secondary. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in/surrey and https://www.surreyschools.ca/kwantlenpark
- Flood/ALR: City Centre is upland, away from Surrey's floodplain lowlands (the City's floodplain areas are the Nicomekl/Serpentine coastal floodplain toward Cloverdale and Newton, plus the Little Campbell River area in the south), and the core is outside the ALR — designated for high-density mixed-use downtown development. Confirm parcel-level status on the City's COSMOS mapping. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- Where 55+ housing concentrates: there is no 55+ strata cluster in the downtown core. The seniors presence in City Centre is rental/care — a retirement residence on 102nd Avenue and a registered assisted-living residence on 100 Ave (Section 2). The one verified 55+ strata name in wider Whalley, Camberley Green, sits in east Whalley outside the downtown core and is in Surrey's re-consolidation queue — it is flagged, not counted (see Section 2).

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. Note the stale-data trap this creates downtown: older building sources still print "rentals not allowed" for some Whalley complexes (the Camberley Green building page is one example) — a pre-2022 restriction line does not override the provincial rule, and the current Form B and bylaws govern. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/camberley-green-10172-141-street
- **45+ labels are not valid age bylaws.** A complex marketed as "45+" holds no enforceable strata age bylaw in BC — 45+ bylaws have been invalid since Nov 24, 2022 (see the first bullet). If a Whalley/City Centre source calls a complex "45+," flag it; do not count it as verified 55+.
- **City plan: Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 21900** ("Surrey Official Community Plan Bylaw, 2026, No. 21900") is Surrey's current OCP — the former OCP Bylaw No. 18020 is not current and must not be cited as current. The downtown's own layer is the City Centre Plan (2017, update underway). https://pub-e61c5ee2a8704225bfa8de0c280e8a2c.r2.dev/bylaws/082baa6293643c966caa6d2ce04a8a540be9ee7dc26a69bf824f78267b55e6ce.pdf and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- **Density charges changed mid-2026.** City Centre projects seeking bonus density paid Community Amenity Contributions under Surrey's two-tier density-bonus framework, but the City states CACs were superseded by its Amenity Cost Charge and bonus density provisions as of June 15, 2026, with the CAC page kept as a historic reference for eligible in-stream applications. Exact per-file CAC/ACC/DCC amounts are application-specific — UNVERIFIED as general figures. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/community-amenity-contributions
- **Assisted living access runs through the health authority, not a listing.** Gateway's 60 publicly subsidized units are a Fraser Health service; Chartwell Imperial Place is a private-pay retirement residence. Neither is applied for or bought through MLS. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-at-Location/7/3/assisted-living-at-gateway-independent-living-for-seniors
- **City taxes (City of Surrey):** property taxes are due July 2 (notices sent end of May; if the due date falls on a weekend or statutory holiday, payment the next business day is without penalty), with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Surrey City Centre?** None that any source verifies. There is no age-restricted strata in the downtown core — a 55+ buyer here is buying a regular tower or townhouse unit with no age bylaw, and getting the SkyTrain, Holland Park and station-area services instead of an age restriction. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: I saw a listing say "1 occupant must be 55+" downtown — is The Breezeway a 55+ building?** UNCONFIRMED. One current listing for The Breezeway, 13910 101 Avenue (MLS R3159929), carries that line, and no building or strata source checked corroborates an age bylaw for the complex. Do not buy on that line alone — the test is the strata's registered bylaws and the Form B for the unit. https://vancouvernashdom.com/north-surrey-whalley-apartments/SearchResults.form/?_pg=18
- **Q: What about Camberley Green? It is advertised as a gated 55+ community.** The listings do verify that character — gated 55+, 40 rancher townhomes at 10172 141 Street — but that is east Whalley, outside the downtown core, and the complex sits on Surrey's re-consolidation queue, so it is not counted in the Surrey roster or in this file. A buyer who wants it is shopping Whalley, not City Centre. https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/10172-141-street/107
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building as an investment or for a parent?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a City Centre tower unit?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022, even where older building pages still print a restriction. A strata can still restrict short-term rentals, and in a true 55+ building the tenant would still have to satisfy the age bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: If I need support later, can I stay in City Centre?** At the rental/care step, yes: Chartwell Imperial Place (13853 102nd Ave) offers independent living through assisted living on one site, and Gateway Assisted Living (13787 100 Ave) is a registered residence with 60 publicly subsidized units through Fraser Health. Both are rentals — staying in the core past independent strata living means renting, not owning. https://chartwell.com/bc/surrey/imperial-place and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-at-Location/7/3/assisted-living-at-gateway-independent-living-for-seniors
- **Q: Are pets allowed at the retirement residence?** It differs by building: Chartwell Imperial Place describes pet-friendly suite layouts, while Fraser Health states Gateway residents are not permitted to have pets (supervised visiting pets are welcome). Confirm the current policy with the residence before relying on either. https://www.chartwell.com/en/bc/surrey/imperial-place/suite-plans and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-at-Location/7/3/assisted-living-at-gateway-independent-living-for-seniors
- **Q: Is City Centre in a floodplain?** No — the core is upland, away from Surrey's Nicomekl/Serpentine coastal floodplain and the Little Campbell River area, and outside the ALR. Flood status is still a parcel check on the City's COSMOS mapping, but there is no area flood regime on the downtown. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- **Q: What transit can I actually use day to day?** The best in Surrey: Expo Line SkyTrain at Surrey Central and King George stations in the core (plus Gateway and Scott Road in wider Whalley), and the R1 King George Blvd RapidBus through the core. This is the only Surrey community on the SkyTrain network. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey and https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Your search has to be filed under "Whalley."** Board reporting and MLS sub-area data for downtown Surrey sit under Whalley — a literal "City Centre" sub-area search returns nothing, because no such board sub-area exists, even though the City officially calls the downtown "City Centre (Whalley)." https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- **The inventory depth cuts both ways.** Whalley's 635 attached listings in September 2026 were the deepest of any Surrey sub-area — downtown sellers compete with new-build launches in a way sellers in Surrey's 55+ pockets never face, and downtown buyers have more comparable sales to price against than almost anywhere else in the city. (Area research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/missing/surrey-city-centre.md, citing stats/subarea-stats-sep2026.csv, read 2026-11-01.)
- **The two seniors buildings in the core are on parallel streets, two blocks apart, with opposite pet rules and opposite funding.** Chartwell Imperial Place (private-pay, pet-friendly suites, 13853 102nd Ave) and Gateway (60 publicly subsidized units, no resident pets, 13787 100 Ave) read as one "seniors cluster" on a map and are different products in every way that matters. https://chartwell.com/bc/surrey/imperial-place and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-at-Location/7/3/assisted-living-at-gateway-independent-living-for-seniors
- **Stale restriction lines are everywhere in Whalley building data.** Because rental-restriction bylaws died province-wide on Nov 24, 2022 without any bylaw amendment being required, older building pages still showing "rentals not allowed" (Camberley Green's page among them) are describing a void bylaw — the Form B, not the aggregator page, is the document to trust. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Camberley Green's own facts do not agree with each other.** Listings and building sources give its build year as 1989, 1990 and 1991, and spell the name both "Camberley Green" and "Camberly Green" (the strata plan is NWS2957) — one more reason it stays in the re-consolidation queue rather than the roster until a primary source settles it. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/camberley-green-10172-141-street and https://www.zolo.ca/surrey-real-estate/10172-141-street/107
- **The plan is changing under the towers.** The City Centre Plan update adds a Central Business District and an Entertainment District (Council endorsed the Entertainment District in October 2025), and large multi-phase applications such as Onni's Regency Gardens redevelopment (10520 132 St / 105–105A Ave area) are in process — construction disruption and amenity upside both follow the plan, street by street. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan and https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7924-0084-00
- **The CAC era ended mid-2026.** Density charges on new City Centre projects switched from Community Amenity Contributions to the Amenity Cost Charge regime on June 15, 2026 — relevant to presale pricing comparisons between in-stream older projects and new launches, and a date older Surrey guides get wrong. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/community-amenity-contributions
