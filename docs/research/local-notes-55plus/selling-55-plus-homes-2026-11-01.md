# Selling — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

This file is a UTILITY page (slug `selling`), not a neighbourhood. It is framed around selling a 55+ home — a resale unit in an age-restricted strata, a park home, a co-op share or a life lease — in South Surrey and Langley, the selling page's stated scope, with the region-wide verified stock picture drawn from the sibling files. Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT THIS HOME TYPE

- **The buyer pool is set by provincial law.** Only 55+ age bylaws are valid in BC strata (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2, Regulation 7.01, since Nov 24, 2022); the bylaw restricts who may reside, not who may own, and required exemptions cover legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and — expanded May 1, 2023 — younger spouses/partners, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children cared for before 19. A seller's marketing must describe that pool accurately. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide** since Nov 24, 2022 — a seller cannot market a 55+ unit as "no rentals allowed" for long-term rentals, though short-term rental bylaws remain allowed and any tenant must still meet the age bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **The regulator's framework (BCFSA):** a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, explain the duties owed to clients and non-clients, warn unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, work for only the buyer or the seller in a transaction, and make the representation, duties and BCFSA complaint-route disclosures at the outset of dealings; dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Cooling-off / rescission (BCFSA):** if a buyer rescinds an accepted offer under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining balance of the deposit to the buyer, with no separate release form signed by either party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **Deposits generally (BCFSA):** a buyer's deposit is usually held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder; where subject clauses are not removed, the deposit is not automatically returned and both parties must sign a release, except where the buyer exercises the right of rescission. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **BC home flipping tax (Province):** the tax applies to profit from selling a BC property (including presale contracts) owned for less than 730 days, under the Residential Property (Short-Term Holding) Profit Tax Act effective January 1, 2025; the rate is 20% of net taxable income for a property disposed of within 365 days and gradually decreases over the next 365 days, with no tax at 730 days; a return must be filed within 90 days of the sale where the seller is subject to the tax or an exemption applies only after filing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/bc-home-flipping-tax
- **Selling a park home is an assignment (MHPTA s.28):** the site tenancy transfers to the buyer with the park owner's prior written consent (or an order of the director, or authorization in the tenancy agreement); the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting; and the pad rent and the date of the next increase carry over unchanged to the buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **The selling page's cost list is page content, flagged in the research file:** it lists legal or notary fees for the title transfer, legal fees to discharge the mortgage, GST on legal fees, any lender prepayment penalty, and the seller's share of the current year's property taxes — attributing the list to BCFSA — and contains no commission line at all; whether that list matches BCFSA's own selling-costs page in full is UNVERIFIED. ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/selling.md

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink the buyer pool for my home?** Mechanically, yes: the next resident must meet the 55+ requirement or a Regulation 7.01 exemption (younger spouse/partner, defined children, live-in caregiver, legacy resident) — though an under-55 buyer may still purchase without residing. That narrower pool is the trade-off built into every BC 55+ strata since Nov 24, 2022. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: What are the steps to sell, according to the selling page?** Eight steps: a free first chat; pricing from recent nearby sales and current listings; getting ready with cleaning, small repairs and decluttering; professional photos, video, floor plans and drone photos, then MLS® listing; showings with feedback after each one; offers walked through on price, deposit, conditions and dates; conditions and the cooling-off period; and closing through a lawyer or notary, with keys on possession day. https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html
- **Q: What does it cost to sell?** The page lists legal or notary fees for the title transfer, legal fees to discharge the mortgage, GST on legal fees, any lender prepayment penalty, and the seller's share of the current year's property taxes if unpaid — and states no commission figure, which is given in the listing conversation under the BCFSA framework. https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Can a buyer back out after I accept their offer?** For most homes, yes, inside the Home Buyer Rescission Period: the buyer may rescind within three business days after acceptance, and the brokerage then pays you the 0.25% rescission fee directly from the deposit and returns the balance to the buyer, with no release form required. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **Q: I bought recently. Do I pay tax if I sell now?** Possibly the BC home flipping tax: it applies to profit on a BC property owned less than 730 days, at 20% of net taxable income within 365 days, gradually decreasing to no tax at 730 days, with a return due within 90 days of the sale where the tax or a filing-based exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/bc-home-flipping-tax
- **Q: How is selling a park home different from selling my strata unit?** There is no land or strata lot to convey: the home's ownership transfer is registered in the Manufactured Home Registry, and the site tenancy is assigned to the buyer with the park owner's prior written consent, at no consent fee, with the pad rent unchanged. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf and ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/how-to-buy-a-mobile-home-in-bc-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **Q: Can I market my 55+ unit as "no rentals" to attract owner-occupiers?** No for long-term rentals: every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. A strata can still restrict short-term rentals, and any tenant must still satisfy the age bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **Q: Should I renovate before listing?** The selling page's own guidance is the small jobs: cleaning, small repairs and clearing clutter, with staging help included — it states no renovation is needed. Whether its "staging help, professional video, floor plans and drone photos with every listing" promise applies to every listing in practice is a page claim, UNVERIFIED from the page itself. https://jaredgibbons.ca/selling.html and ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/selling.md
- **Q: How long will my 55+ home take to sell?** UNCONFIRMED as a figure: the page's own answer is that timing depends on the home, the price and the market at the time, and no days-on-market figure for 55+ stock is stated in this research. ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/selling.md
- **Q: What disclosures apply when I hire a realtor to sell?** BCFSA states the professional must give you commission and fee information, explain the duties owed to clients and non-clients, warn unrepresented parties of their risks, act for only one side of the transaction, and make the representation and complaint-route disclosures at the outset of dealings. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **A seller in a one-building city prices differently from a seller in Abbotsford.** Port Coquitlam has exactly one verified 55+ strata building, while an Abbotsford seller competes inside a 26-complex market — scarcity of the type itself is part of the listing story in the thin markets and irrelevant in the deep ones. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **The service-plan building hides its real monthly cost in plain sight.** At Mayfair Terrace, listing information states a mandatory base service plan on top of strata fees — a seller there who quotes only the strata fee will lose the buyer's trust at the documents stage; the service agreement is part of the price conversation from day one. Source: ~/workspace/local-notes/port-coquitlam-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **Similar names poison comparable searches on both sides of Boundary Drive.** Lakewood Heights, Lakewood Place and Lakeview Estates are three separate Surrey stratas on one drive; Glenwood Village (55+) and Glenwood Village Heights (no age restriction) are neighbours in Newton. A 55+ seller's "comparables" pulled by name alone will quietly include the wrong buildings. Source: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/surrey-homes-for-sale-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **The cooling-off fee needs no one's signature.** If a buyer rescinds in the three-business-day window, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% fee directly from the deposit and returns the balance — no release form is signed by either party, unlike a failed subject removal, where the deposit is not automatically returned. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **The flipping tax counts from purchase, and the clock is long.** 730 days is two full years; a seller who bought a 55+ unit for a parent and sells inside that window can face 20% of net taxable income inside the first 365 days, and may owe a return within 90 days even where an exemption applies only after filing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/income-taxes/bc-home-flipping-tax
- **The selling page promises more media than the marketing page names.** The selling page states staging help, professional video, floor plans and drone photos come with every listing; the site's marketing-plan page names none of video, drone or floor plans. A seller should confirm the actual deliverables for their listing in the listing appointment. ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-16/selling.md
- **A park home's buyer inherits the pad rent exactly.** On assignment the pad rent and the next increase date carry over unchanged and the park cannot charge a consent fee — so a park seller cannot offer a "new-tenant" rent deal, and a below-market pad rent is a genuine, documentable selling feature. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
