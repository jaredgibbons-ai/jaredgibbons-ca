# Queens Park (New Westminster) — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

**NAME POINT — read first:** "Queens Park" (no apostrophe) is the MLS®/Paragon sub-area name and the page slug; "Queen's Park" (with apostrophe) is the City's spelling for both the park itself and the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area that covers the neighbourhood. The neighbourhood and the park are not the same thing: the park sits in the eastern portion of the neighbourhood, and the residential streets around it are the sub-area.

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Queens Park is a central mainland neighbourhood in the City of New Westminster, between Royal Avenue and Sixth Avenue, and between Sixth Street and McBride Boulevard, with Queen's Park itself in the eastern portion of the neighbourhood. Nearby areas: Glenbrooke North to the north, Downtown and the Quay to the south, Uptown to the west along Sixth Street / Sixth Avenue, and Sapperton beyond McBride Boulevard to the east. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Schedule_B_to_Official_Community_Plan___SOS.pdf
- Main streets: Royal Avenue, Sixth Avenue, Sixth Street and McBride Boulevard bound and cross the area; McBride Boulevard links north to East Columbia Street and Sapperton and south toward the Pattullo Bridge approach. Sixth Street at the west edge is a commercial street serving the neighbourhood (per the Heritage Conservation Area Statement of Significance); a named shopping centre inside Queens Park confirmed on an official source: UNCONFIRMED. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Schedule_B_to_Official_Community_Plan___SOS.pdf
- Heritage is the defining fact of the area: the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area (HCA) is currently the only HCA in New Westminster, and it places a layer of heritage protection over ALL properties in the area regardless of construction age, classed Protected or Non-protected (detail in Section 3). https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- Schools: School District 40 (New Westminster) runs a three-tier system (elementary K–5, middle 6–8, secondary 9–12) with address-based catchments. Herbert Spencer Elementary, 605 Second Street, is in the area and offers Early French Immersion as a Program of Choice — not any address's catchment school. For the sample address 800 Queens Avenue, the SD40 locator result recorded in the master research (locator run 2026-10-21) is École Qayqayt Elementary (K–5), Fraser River Middle (6–8) and New Westminster Secondary (9–12). https://newwestschools.ca/our-schools/registration/ and https://newwestschools.ca/our-schools/
- Parks and recreation: Queen's Park itself offers a rose garden, spray park, picnic shelters, tennis and pickleball courts, sports fields and stadium, a skate park, an off-leash dog park, Queen's Park Arena and the Queen's Park Sportsplex. The city's main aquatic centre, the təməsew̓txʷ Aquatic and Community Centre, 65 East Sixth Avenue, is in Uptown. The nearest library branch is the Main Branch, New Westminster Public Library, 716 6th Avenue, at the Uptown edge. https://www.newwestcity.ca/parks-and-recreation/book-facilities-fields-and-parks/articles/5540.php and https://www.newwestcity.ca/facilities and https://www.nwpl.ca/
- Health: Queens Park Care Centre, 315 McBride Blvd (Fraser Health), is at the east edge of the neighbourhood (Section 2); the nearest hospital is Royal Columbian Hospital, 330 East Columbia Street (Fraser Health), in Sapperton. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/New-Westminster/royal-columbian-hospital
- Transit: no SkyTrain station is inside Queens Park; the nearest stations are Columbia Station and New Westminster Station (Expo Line). TransLink route 105 (Uptown / New Westminster Station) and route 102 (Victoria Hill / New Westminster Station) serve the adjoining corridors. https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/schedules-by-region/2024-schedules/september/burnaby-new-west-p155.pdf
- Market shape (for context): the sub-area is predominantly single-detached houses, and the September 2026 listing stock matches that shape — 7 detached listings against 1 condo & townhome listing, with zero sales in the month in either segment (as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report, via the area research file).
- Where the seniors housing actually is: there is no 55+ strata, seniors rental, retirement residence or subsidized seniors building verified in Queens Park. The one seniors facility at the neighbourhood's edge is a long-term care centre on McBride Boulevard (Section 2). New Westminster's subsidized seniors towers and its retirement residence cluster in other sub-areas — Uptown / Moody Park, Brow of the Hill, the West End, Victoria Hill and Sapperton.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. (In Queens Park this is mostly background law: the stock is freehold detached, where no strata age bylaw exists at all.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Heritage Conservation Area — the rule set that actually governs a Queens Park purchase:** the Queen's Park HCA is currently the only HCA in New Westminster; it places heritage protection over ALL properties in the area regardless of construction age, classed Protected or Non-protected. Building Permit applications for work such as new buildings, demolition, or changes affecting the front, sides or visible roofline of Protected properties, and subdivision applications, require a Heritage Alteration Permit (HAP) reviewed for design-guideline compliance, and Protected properties also need an HAP for exterior changes that do not require a Building Permit. Standard in-kind maintenance does not require an HAP. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Protected-property maintenance and incentives:** Protected properties are also under the City's Heritage Property Maintenance Standards Bylaw (minimum maintenance standards so heritage properties are not lost through neglect); Protected-property incentives include additional density, special BC Building Code provisions, density transfer, relaxed laneway/carriage house guidelines and no heritage permit fees. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Zoning: Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001** (consolidated as of June 8, 2026 on the City's current consolidation) — houses sit under the Single Detached Residential Districts (RS series, s.310), RS-1 minimum site 557.40 sq. m. (6,000 sq. ft.), floor space ratio 0.5, overlaid by the HCA guidelines and Heritage Alteration Permit rules. Subdivision inside the HCA also requires a Heritage Alteration Permit — the zone minimum alone does not settle what a specific lot can do. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Zoning_Bylaw_No_6680_2001_as_of_June_30_2022_(Complete).pdf and https://www.newwestcity.ca/zoning-bylaw/sb_expander_articles/1618.php and https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Secondary suites:** the City's Secondary Suites guide (updated August 2025) states only one secondary suite is permitted per house, though a property may have both a secondary suite and a laneway or carriage house, subject to Zoning Bylaw s.190.29, the Building Code and the Tree Protection Bylaw — and inside the HCA, the heritage permit layer applies on top for Protected properties. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Secondary_Suite_Guide_August_2025.pdf
- **Adaptable housing rule:** since a Zoning Bylaw amendment adopted June 28, 2011 (Bylaw No. 7464, 2011), a minimum of 40% of all single-storey dwelling units in multiple-unit residential buildings city-wide must be built as adaptable dwelling units under the BC Building Code — the City states the policy is a proactive means of planning for its aging population. Adaptable is not age-restricted. http://newwestcity.ca/housing/adaptable-housing
- **Plans:** Our City 2041, the Official Community Plan (Bylaw No. 7925, 2017), including the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area (Schedule B, adopted by OCP Amendment Bylaw No. 7926, 2017). A floodplain zone for this area: UNCONFIRMED in the sources read — the governing overlay here is heritage, not floodplain. https://www.newwestcity.ca/ocp/past-event-materials-and-council-reports
- **Property taxes and utilities:** City of New Westminster 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on current taxes remaining unpaid on July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on any unpaid balance on September 3, 2026. New Westminster operates its own electrical utility alongside City water / sewer services. https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What 55+ homes can I actually buy in Queens Park?** None that any source verifies — zero age-restricted stock of any kind was found in this sub-area. What a buyer can buy here is a heritage detached house (or, rarely, a general-market strata unit) with no age bylaw; the "55+" content of a Queens Park purchase is the HCA's Protected / Non-protected rules and the care centre at the area's edge, not an age-restricted community. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Is the Queens Park neighbourhood the same as Queen's Park the park?** No. "Queens Park" (no apostrophe) is the MLS® sub-area / neighbourhood name; "Queen's Park" is the City's spelling for the park in the neighbourhood's eastern portion and for the Heritage Conservation Area covering the residential streets around it. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Q: What does the Heritage Conservation Area mean for an older homeowner?** Every property is classed Protected or Non-protected regardless of age. New buildings, demolition, subdivision and specified exterior changes need a Heritage Alteration Permit reviewed against the design guidelines; Protected properties need an HAP even for some exterior changes that need no Building Permit, and are under the Heritage Property Maintenance Standards Bylaw. Standard in-kind maintenance needs no HAP. For an aging owner, that means exterior projects carry a permit step an owner outside the HCA does not face — price that into any renovation plan before subjects. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Q: Is there an upside to Protected status, or only constraint?** Both, on the City's own list: Protected-property incentives include additional density, density transfer, relaxed laneway/carriage house guidelines, special BC Building Code provisions and no heritage permit fees — a Protected listing can carry development value a Non-protected one does not. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Could we both live in a 55+ strata elsewhere in BC?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. (Relevant to Queens Park buyers only if they move to a true 55+ building elsewhere — no Queens Park home carries an age bylaw.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I add a suite or a carriage house for a caregiver or family member here?** The City allows one secondary suite per house, and a property may have both a suite and a laneway or carriage house (Zoning Bylaw s.190.29 and Building / Tree Protection Bylaw compliance required) — and Protected properties in the HCA have relaxed laneway/carriage house guidelines as one of the heritage incentives, with the Heritage Alteration Permit process applying on top. The parcel-level Protected vs Non-protected status: UNCONFIRMED for any specific property — confirm with the City per property. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Secondary_Suite_Guide_August_2025.pdf and https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Q: Where is the hospital, and what care is at the door?** Royal Columbian Hospital, 330 East Columbia Street in Sapperton (Fraser Health). At the neighbourhood's east edge, Queen's Park Care Centre, 315 McBride Boulevard, provides long-term care (148 publicly subsidized beds), general rehab and adult day programs — the adult day program is the in-area support an independent Queens Park senior can actually use while still living at home. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/New-Westminster/royal-columbian-hospital and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/New-Westminster/queens-park-care-centre
- **Q: What transit can I actually use day to day?** No station is in the sub-area; the nearest are Columbia and New Westminster Stations (Expo Line), with TransLink routes 105 and 102 serving the adjoining corridors. Daily life on the interior heritage streets is car-or-bus, not station-based. https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/schedules-by-region/2024-schedules/september/burnaby-new-west-p155.pdf
- **Q: How should a Queens Park home be priced when the sub-area barely trades?** Not from the sub-area's own month: September 2026 recorded 0 sales from 7 detached listings and 0 from 1 strata listing, so no sales ratio applies. Price from the New Westminster community evidence instead (detached median $1,365,000, 94% sale-to-list, 35 days on market, as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report) and say plainly that the sub-area sample is too small to carry its own number.
- **Q: If I need help later, can I stay in Queens Park?** At the adult-day-program and long-term care steps, at the area's east edge — Queen's Park Care Centre (315 McBride Blvd) offers both, with long-term care admission through the Fraser Health system. At the assisted-living and subsidized-rental steps, no: Thornebridge Gardens (649 8th Ave) is in Moody Park / Uptown, and the subsidized towers are in Brow of the Hill, Uptown and the West End. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/New-Westminster/queens-park-care-centre

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Lead every Queens Park file with the heritage check:** confirm whether the property is classed Protected or Non-protected in the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area before pricing renovations, additions, demolition or subdivision — Protected properties need a Heritage Alteration Permit even for some exterior changes that need no Building Permit, and deferred exterior maintenance on a Protected home is both a Heritage Property Maintenance Standards Bylaw compliance issue and a negotiation point. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **The HCA covers new houses too:** the protection layer applies to ALL properties in the area regardless of construction age — a recently built Queens Park house is still classed Protected or Non-protected and still sits under the design guidelines. "It's a new build, so heritage doesn't apply" is the area's most expensive assumption. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- **Two name traps in one small area:** the board writes the neighbourhood "Queens Park" while the City writes the park and the HCA "Queen's Park"; and the subsidized "Hillside Place" that sounds local is at 1025 Queens **Avenue** in Uptown, while the co-op that carries the neighbourhood's name ("Queen's Park Housing Co-op") is at 386 Ginger Drive in Glenbrooke North's geography. A Queens Park page built from names alone will import three other areas' buildings. https://newwestcity.ca/database/rte/Housing%20web%20update%20-%20Non-Market%20details.pdf
- **Glenbrooke North next door is the control case:** it holds some of the city's oldest dwellings alongside Queens Park and is documented in the same Historic Neighbourhood Context Statement work — but it is NOT in the HCA, so the same-age house one sub-area north carries no Heritage Alteration Permit layer. Which side of the park edge a house sits on changes its renovation law, not just its marketing. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Queens_Pk_NHood_Ctxt_Stmt_14_April_2009%281%29.pdf
- **Zero sales is a data point, not a verdict:** this sub-area recorded no sales at all in September 2026 (0 of 7 detached, 0 of 1 strata) — one month with zero sales says nothing about values in a small, hold-for-decades heritage neighbourhood, and any "Queens Park market" figure quoted from that month should be refused. Community figures: as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats GVR September 2026 report.
- **New Westminster runs its own electrical utility** — unlike the BC Hydro service in neighbouring cities, a Queens Park home's electrical costs run through the City's own utility, and New West taxes have two penalty dates (July 3 and September 3, 2026). https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php
