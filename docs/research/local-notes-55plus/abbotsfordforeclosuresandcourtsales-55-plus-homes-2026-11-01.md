## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- **A foreclosure starts by petition.** "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Supreme Court Civil Rules, Rule 21-7 (1); all persons whose interest is sought to be extinguished must be respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **The redemption period is whatever the court fixes.** The court may order a respondent to pay what is due within a redemption period the court may fix, failing which the respondent is foreclosed of the equity of redemption — Rule 21-7 (5) (b). The Rules state no set length. In default of payment, a final order of foreclosure may be granted — Rule 21-7 (6). A party of record may apply at any time for an order that the property be sold — Rule 21-7 (7). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Court confirmation is what makes a sale final.** Even before the redemption period expires, the person having conduct of a sale may apply for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7 (9). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Conduct-of-sale mechanics are file-specific and partly UNVERIFIED in this program.** The detailed mechanics in Rule 13-5 (conduct of sale, reserve/upset price, bid rights, Form 60) and the foreclosure practice direction PD-66 were not retrieved from an official source on 2026-11-01, so no conduct, upset-price or competing-bid mechanic is stated as a general rule here — get the seller's counsel's bid instructions for the specific file in writing. Source: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/abbotsfordforeclosuresandcourtsales.md (UNVERIFIED items 3–4).
- **Court records are public through Court Services Online.** It is BC's electronic court registry; eSearch is available to anyone with Internet access, Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry location, party names and filed documents, and its file classes include "H - Foreclosure". https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Property transfer tax still applies to a court-ordered purchase, on the Province's general rules.** PTT is payable when a person purchases or gains an interest in property registered at the Land Title Office, unless an exemption applies; it is based on fair market value on the day of registration, at 1% up to and including $200,000, 2% on the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% above $2,000,000. The Province's PTT transaction types expressly include "Foreclosure" and "Court order". https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Manufactured-home pads follow tenancy law, not strata law.** The Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act covers most manufactured home tenancies in BC — the buyer owns the home and rents the site. Pad rent can be increased only once every 12 months, with at least three full months' written notice, capped at the provincial limit (2.3% for increases taking effect in 2026) plus a proportional amount for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees. http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Abbotsford property taxes:** due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on September 16, 2026 — the City's flat-10%-in-two-steps schedule a buyer inherits at completion if taxes are unpaid. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is my accepted offer on a court-ordered 55+ home final?** No. The person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7 (9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires. Until that order is made, the sale is not final. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer at the approval hearing?** UNCONFIRMED as a stated process rule in this program — the bid-rights wording in Rule 13-5 and the PD-66 sealed-bid timing were not retrieved from an official source on 2026-11-01. Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for, and get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing before spending on inspections. Source: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/abbotsfordforeclosuresandcourtsales.md (UNVERIFIED item 4).
- **Q: Is a court-ordered sale sold "as is"?** The research file records the structural point only: the seller in a foreclosure sale is not an owner preparing the home for market in the ordinary way — the sale runs inside a court proceeding under Rule 21-7, and buyers should expect the conditions in the court-approved form for that file, not a standard residential contract. The Rule 13-5 directions wording behind an "as is" clause is UNVERIFIED in this program, so no general "as is" rule is stated here — read the actual court-approved contract for the file. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period, and can it end my deal?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5) (b). A sale can be confirmed before it expires under Rule 21-7 (9), and in default of payment a final order of foreclosure may follow under Rule 21-7 (6) — ask which stage the file is at before spending money. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Where do I check the court file for an Abbotsford foreclosure?** Court Services Online offers eSearch to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure". Abbotsford also has its own Supreme Court registry at 32375 Veterans Way for in-person registry inquiries. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do and https://www.bccourts.ca/supreme_court/locations.htm?location=kamloops
- **Q: Do I pay property transfer tax on a court-ordered purchase?** On the Province's general rules, yes, unless an exemption applies: PTT is payable on a purchase registered at the Land Title Office, based on fair market value on the day of registration, and the Province's PTT transaction types expressly include "Foreclosure" and "Court order". Have your lawyer cost it on the specific property before you set your ceiling. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Do I pay GST on a court-ordered 55+ purchase?** UNCONFIRMED in this program — no official GST treatment for a court-ordered sale was loaded on 2026-11-01, and the research file states no GST treatment. Do not assume a court sale is GST-free or GST-taxable; have your lawyer confirm the GST position on the specific property (new vs. used, builder vs. individual seller) before offering. Source: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/abbotsfordforeclosuresandcourtsales.md (Section 4, buyer costs; UNVERIFIED item 7).
- **Q: Does buying through the court change the 55+ age bylaw?** No source states any such effect, and the process rules point the other way: the vesting order under Rule 21-7 (9) vests title in the purchaser, but the strata's registered 55+ bylaw (Strata Property Act ss. 123.1/123.2) still governs who may live in the unit. A buyer who cannot reside under the bylaw or an exemption should not assume a court purchase cures that — UNCONFIRMED that any exception exists; none is stated in the sources. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a court-bought 55+ unit?** Yes, if the purchase completes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: Can I rent out a court-bought unit in a 55+ building?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Abbotsford is the one city in this batch with its own Supreme Court registry.** Abbotsford Registry, 32375 Veterans Way — a buyer can check a file's registry location on Court Services Online and attend to registry matters in the same city as the property, instead of travelling to another registry town. https://www.bccourts.ca/supreme_court/locations.htm?location=kamloops and https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **There is no published foreclosure discount to quote.** SnapStats publishes no foreclosure category and no official source publishes Abbotsford foreclosure counts or discount-to-market statistics — both are UNVERIFIED, and any "foreclosures sell for X% less" figure has no source in this program. General-market context only (September 2026, SnapStats FVR report): Abbotsford detached median $949,000 at 39 days on market; condos & townhomes median $497,500 at 32 days. Source: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-7/abbotsfordforeclosuresandcourtsales.md (Section 2).
- **The 55+ supply a foreclosure buyer is fishing in is old, low-rise and concentrated.** Of the 26 verified strata complexes, most were built between 1979 and 1996 in the Clearbrook, Mill Lake and George Ferguson Way corridors — a court-ordered unit in Abbotsford is almost always a resale unit in one of those named complexes, not new stock. Sources: the building entries in Section 2 above.
- **One name will waste your search time if you trust it.** "Lakeway Gardens" / Lakeview Gardens at 2410 Emerson St is described as 55+ in a listing, but its name and sub-area conflict between sources and it is NOT verified in this program — a buyer who finds it marketed as a foreclosure should verify the registered strata name, plan and bylaws before relying on the 55+ label. Source: ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/abbotsford-west-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **A life lease cannot be foreclosed and resold like a strata lot.** Garden Park Tower and Cascade Court sell a lifetime right to occupy, redeemed by the operator on departure under the lease terms — there is no strata title for a lender to foreclose on in the Rule 21-7 sense described here, and no Form B or depreciation report. The Clearbrook Golden Age Society's own warning is that life lease models vary widely and professional advice should be taken before signing. https://gardenparktower.ca/life-lease
- **Title is cleaned by the court, not by the seller.** On confirmation, the court's order directs the disposition of the proceeds and vests title in the purchaser — the vesting order is what cleans the title for the buyer, which is the one structural advantage of the court route over an ordinary distressed sale. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
