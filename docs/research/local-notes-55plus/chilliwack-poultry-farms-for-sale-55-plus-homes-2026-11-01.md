# Chilliwack Poultry Farms — 55+ Homes: Local Notes

Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Plain finding up front: no age-restricted product of any kind exists on Chilliwack poultry farms, and none can exist in the usual form. A poultry farm is a working, supply-managed farm operation on freehold farm land, mostly in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR). Farms are fee-simple, not strata — there is no strata corporation, so there is no age bylaw to set, and ALR residences cannot be stratified. No 55+ strata, manufactured-home park, co-operative, rental or care complex was verified on Chilliwack farm land or in the farm districts. Chilliwack's verified 55+ stock is urban — 5 strata complexes plus Cottonwood Village park — listed in Section 2 for contrast. What a 55+ poultry buyer actually faces here is ALR housing law, farm classification, and the quota and board-approval system. Precedent for this treatment: the Chilliwack Farms file in this series.**

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **The strata age-bylaw rules, for the record — with no local strata to apply them to.** Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now (Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2; age bylaws below 55 invalid since Nov 24, 2022, under Bill 44); the bylaw restricts residency, not ownership; Strata Property Regulation 7.01 exempts legacy residents, live-in caregivers, and — expanded May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children cared for before 19; and residential rental-restriction bylaws have been void since Nov 24, 2022, while short-term restrictions remain allowed. A farm buyer gets none of this machinery, because a farm purchase is fee-simple land, not a strata lot. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **ALR housing caps are the rules that actually bind a 55+ farm buyer.** Land in the ALR may generally have no more than one residence per parcel unless an additional residence qualifies (Agricultural Land Commission Act, s. 20.1); a principal residence built without an ALC application must have a total floor area of 500 m² or less, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill placed for its construction, and a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence; local governments may further regulate or restrict residences in the ALR. An additional residence beyond what the ALR Use Regulation permits requires a Non-Adhering Residential Use (NARU) application, and the Commission must not grant permission unless the residence is necessary for a farm use. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_policy_l-26_-_non-adhering_residential_use_applications.pdf
- **The second-dwelling rule is the ageing-in-place rule on a farm.** The ALC permits one additional residence without an ALC application where its conditions are met — detached, with a total floor area of 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller and 186 m² or less on parcels larger than 40 ha — and Chilliwack's bylaws must also permit it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Additional manufactured home on a farm: family occupancy only.** Under the ALR Use Regulation's additional-residence provisions (s. 34(3) as summarised in ALC Policy L-25), an additional residence that is a manufactured home and not a pre-existing structure is permitted only if it is 9 m or less in width and is used only by the owner or specified relatives of the owner (parent, grandparent or great-grandparent, sibling, or child, within the regulation's meaning). ALC Policy L-25 adds that if it is no longer occupied as permitted it must be demolished, removed, or converted to a non-residential use. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf
- **Chilliwack adds a Farm Home Plate permit area on top of the ALR rules.** The Chilliwack 2050 OCP, Bylaw No. 5500, includes in its Schedule D the permit areas D-1 Drinking Water Supply, D-2 Hillside Environment, D-3 Riparian Area, D-4 Farm Home Plate and D-5 Hillside Development — the Farm Home Plate DPA is the City's own siting control over where the farm residence sits on the parcel. https://chilliwack.com/ocp
- **City zoning: Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000** — Section 6 sets the agricultural zones: AL (Agricultural Lowland), AU (Agricultural Upland), AS (Agricultural Small Lot), AR (Agricultural Residential), AC (Agricultural Commercial) and AFP (Agricultural Food Processing). The same bylaw defines POULTRY as any domesticated fowl including chickens, turkeys, ducks, geese and swans, and lists General Agriculture as a permitted principal use in its AL, AU and AS zones. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- **Barns are protected farm structures.** Under the Agricultural Land Reserve Use Regulation, B.C. Reg. 30/2019, s. 5, a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation is a designated farm use that a local government enactment may not prohibit (it may be regulated), provided it is required for the farm use on the property and in line with its level — the wording under which a poultry barn is treated as a farm building in the ALR. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019
- **Quota, not the house, is the regulated asset.** For chicken, quota is the amount of chicken, in kilograms of live weight, the BC Chicken Marketing Board allots to a grower; the board's General Orders name mainstream and specialty classes, deal separately with transfer of quota with registered premises and acquisition/disposition of quota without registered premises, and the board approves quota transactions, checking that growers are in good standing. No board source states any age criterion for holding quota or being licensed as a grower — age plays no documented role in the quota system. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf
- **Farm classification is a continuing obligation, not a one-time label.** BC Assessment's minimum gross farm income requirements at farm gate prices are $2,500 if the farm operation is between 0.8 ha and 4 ha, $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if the operation is greater than 4 ha, and $10,000 if the operation is less than 0.8 ha; applications must be submitted by the legislated deadline of October 31 for the next assessment and tax year, and a buyer who plans to continue farming a farm-classified property must submit a General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Property taxes — two regimes, depending on which side of the city line the parcel sits.** City of Chilliwack parcels: notices mailed at the end of May, due the first working day after July 1, a 5% penalty after July 2, 2026 and a further 5% after August 4, 2026. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2522 A parcel in FVRD Electoral Area D (Rosedale) pays rural taxes to the Province under the Taxation (Rural Area) Act, s.11: due July 2, with a 5% penalty on any portion unpaid on July 2 and a further 5% on any portion unpaid on October 31. City dates do NOT apply there. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/rural-area and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01
- **Property transfer tax:** the Province's general rates are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion above $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% above $2,000,000, with a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. How PTT and GST are apportioned between land, buildings, residence and quota on a specific poultry purchase: UNVERIFIED — no farm-transfer tax source was opened for this topic. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Biosecurity is a sale condition, not a footnote.** Highly pathogenic avian influenza is a reportable disease in Canada; the CFIA establishes primary control zones where it has been detected, permits are required to move birds, products and by-products through a control zone, and on a quarantined farm nothing bird-related enters or leaves without CFIA permission — which can affect showings, bird placement and completion timing on any poultry purchase, whatever the buyer's age. https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is there a 55+ poultry farm or an age-restricted farm community in Chilliwack?** No — none was verified on any source. Poultry listings carry no age restriction, and the age-bylaw mechanism in BC belongs to strata corporations, which fee-simple farm parcels do not have; ALR residences cannot be stratified in any case. Chilliwack's verified 55+ complexes and its one 55+ park are urban. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: I'm 65 and want to retire onto a small poultry operation. What am I actually buying?** Land (usually ALR), the barn package, the farm residence, and quota only where the seller includes it — the live page states quota is sometimes included in an asking price and sometimes priced separately (site copy; read the specific contract). You would also be stepping into a licensed business: growers are licensed by the BC Chicken Marketing Board annually, and quota transfers need board approval. https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwack-poultry-farms-for-sale.html and https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf
- **Q: What limits the house I can build or keep on the parcel?** On ALR land, a principal residence built without an ALC application is capped at 500 m² total floor area, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill for its construction, and a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence. In the city, the OCP's Farm Home Plate development permit area (Schedule D-4) also controls where on the parcel the residence sits. Confirm the parcel's ALR status on the ALC maps before designing anything. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://chilliwack.com/ocp and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Q: Can my adult child or a caregiver live in a second dwelling on the farm?** The ALC permits one additional residence without an application where its size rules are met — 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller, 186 m² or less on larger parcels, detached — and City bylaws must also permit it. If the additional residence is a manufactured home, it is narrower still: 9 m or less in width, occupied only by the owner or specified relatives. There is no strata-style caregiver exemption here because there is no strata; the land rules are the whole answer. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf
- **Q: Does my age help or hurt a quota purchase?** No board source states any age criterion for quota allotment, transfer approval or grower licensing; the board's stated checks are good standing and appropriate allocation of production for the growing cycle. Age is simply not a documented factor — anything beyond that is UNCONFIRMED. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf and https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Q: If the farm has no quota, can I just buy quota separately?** The General Orders treat acquisition or disposition of quota without registered premises as its own case, separate from transfer with premises, and transfers are facilitated privately rather than through a board-run central platform. No official source publishes quota prices in any sector, so do not price quota from listings: UNVERIFIED as a figure. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Q: If I stop farming, do I keep farm-class taxes?** Not automatically: BC Assessment requires a producing farm to keep meeting minimum gross farm income thresholds that scale with the operation's size ($2,500 at 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha), and a buyer continuing to farm must file the General Application for Farm Classification by October 31 after purchase. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Q: Can a municipality stop me adding a poultry barn in the ALR?** No — under B.C. Reg. 30/2019 a structure for an intensive livestock operation is a designated farm use a local enactment may not prohibit, though it may regulate it, and the structure must be required for the farm use and in line with its level. Chilliwack's own bylaw defines POULTRY and lists General Agriculture as a permitted principal use in its AL, AU and AS zones. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019 and https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- **Q: What happens to my purchase during an avian influenza outbreak?** HPAI is reportable to the CFIA; in a primary control zone, permits are required to move birds, eggs and by-products, and on a quarantined farm nothing bird-related moves without CFIA permission — placements, showings and completion dates can all be delayed. Confirm the property's control-zone status before fixing a completion date. https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected
- **Q: Where is the hospital for a farm-district address?** Chilliwack General Hospital, 45600 Menholm Rd — a Fraser Health community hospital with emergency and acute care. The Chilliwack Urgent and Primary Care Centre is at #104 - 7955 Evans Road. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Chilliwack/chilliwack-general-hospital and https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Chilliwack/Chilliwack-Urgent-and-Primary-Care-Centre

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Agriculture is the city's land base, not a fringe use.** The City states agriculture accounts for 67% of Chilliwack's total land base and 29% of economic activity — which is why the "seniors community on farmland" product does not exist here: the belt is working farm ground under an Agricultural Area Plan. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- **"Quota included or not" is the single most important line on these pages.** The board's General Orders treat quota with premises and quota without premises as different transactions — a farm with barns and no quota is land and buildings, not a producing operation, whatever the photos suggest. The Chilliwack page's own copy says quota is sometimes included and sometimes priced separately; the contract, verified with the applicable board, is the only safe source. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf and https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwack-poultry-farms-for-sale.html
- **Quota is measured differently in every sector on the same page.** Broiler quota is kilograms of live weight per cycle; turkey quota is pounds of turkey meat per specified period; hatching-egg quota is hen units (mainstream) or hatched chicks (specialty). A "quota included" line means three different things depending on which board's sector the farm is in. https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf and https://bcturkey.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/174_66.pdf and https://bcbhec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2022-Annual-Report.pdf
- **There are no official market numbers for this topic at all.** SnapStats publishes no farm or quota category and does not cover Chilliwack, CADREB publishes board-wide statistics only, and no official source publishes Chilliwack poultry-farm counts, quota prices or per-acre poultry values — each is UNVERIFIED, and the live page's claim that Chilliwack is often less expensive than Abbotsford on a land-per-acre basis is the page's own copy, not adopted as a figure. https://www.cadreb.com/market/ and https://jaredgibbons.ca/chilliwack-poultry-farms-for-sale.html
- **The second-dwelling dream runs into the ALR, not the city.** Retiring parents commonly picture a manufactured home beside the farmhouse; the ALR Use Regulation allows that only as a family-occupancy additional residence (owner or specified relatives, 9 m width cap for a non-pre-existing manufactured home), and ALC policy requires the home to be demolished, removed or converted to non-residential use once it is no longer occupied as permitted. That condition survives resale and belongs in the purchase conversation, not after it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/policies/alc_-_policy_l-25_-_manufactured_homes_in_the_alr.pdf
- **Board approval is a closing condition with a clock on appeals.** The BC Farm Industry Review Board supervises the regulated marketing system and hears appeals of board decisions, with appeals to be filed within 30 calendar days of the board's final decision — a refused or conditioned quota transfer has a short, hard appeal window. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/organizational-structure/ministries-organizations/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/npma-appeals
- **A "Chilliwack poultry" search crosses a government boundary.** Rosedale parcels in the results sit in FVRD Electoral Area D, not the City of Chilliwack — different planning authority, different (rural, provincial) tax schedule, different penalty dates (further 5% on October 31, not August 4). Confirm which local government, tax schedule and bylaws apply to the specific parcel. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas.html and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96448_01
