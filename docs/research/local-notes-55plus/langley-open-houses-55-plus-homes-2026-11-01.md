Researched Nov 1, 2026. Every fact carries its source. Anything no source states is marked UNCONFIRMED. No sold prices, no listing addresses beyond the named complexes themselves.

**Scope note: this is a topic file, not an area file. ## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://choa.bc.ca/resources/strata-property-act/legislative-updates/
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. Several Langley buildings in Section 2 still carry "rentals not allowed" in their building records (Parkway Estates, Parkway II, Willow Park Estates); for a long-term rental that line cannot be relied on. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **The open-house exception to disclosure of representation.** Real Estate Services Rules s. 54(3) (formerly Rule 5-10(3)) provides that, unless a licensee solicits or receives information from a party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate, a disclosure of representation is not required when the licensee is only (a) hosting an advertised open house, or (b) providing factual responses to general questions from the party. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo107/loo107/209_2021 and http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **What that means at the door:** chatting factually with the host at a Langley open house does not by itself make you their client — but once you share your motivation, budget or needs with a licensee who represents the seller, the disclosure rules engage, and BCFSA states a licensee must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party to the transaction. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Dual agency:** BCFSA states that in BC a licensed real estate professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, and that dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances — so the host at an open house cannot simply "also represent" a buyer who walks in. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Identification or sign-in for visitors:** no BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found states an identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors — UNVERIFIED as a rule. A host may still ask, as a matter of the seller's instructions; that practice is not in an official source. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **How open-house dates and times are set and entered for publication, and how ShowingTime and TouchBase schedule or announce open houses in Langley:** no official or board public source states it — UNVERIFIED. ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/langley-open-houses.md
- **Manufactured-home parks run on park rules, not strata law:** the age rules at Cedar Creek Estates, Fernridge Park and Pineridge are park rules attached to a leasehold pad, and Pioneer Park's are co-op rules attached to share ownership — the Strata Property Act exemptions above do not answer an occupancy question in any of them. Get the park's or co-op's current rules in writing before or at a viewing. ~/workspace/local-notes/55-plus/langley-foreclosures-court-ordered-sales-55-plus-homes-2026-11-01.md
- **Two zoning bylaws, by municipality:** the City of Langley's zoning bylaw is Zoning Bylaw 2025, No. 3300, which applies to the entire area within the City's boundaries and repealed Bylaw No. 2100; the Township of Langley is governed by its own Zoning Bylaw 1987, No. 2500, which "applies to the entirety of the Township." Which bylaw governs a viewed home depends on which Langley it sits in — the feed does not make that distinction for the visitor. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/zoning-bylaw and https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/zoning-bylaw.aspx
- **Strata documents are the verification step an open house cannot do.** The age bylaw, pet rule, occupancy limits (for example Willow Park Estates' listing-stated 2-occupant maximum) and any service charges live in the strata's registered bylaws and the Form B — a host's verbal summary at a viewing is not a source. Confirm the package with your own agent before writing an offer. Sources: Section 2 above.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Which Langley open houses are actually 55+?** Only a viewing at one of the 15 verified ownership communities in Section 2 — 11 strata complexes (Huntsfield Green, Parkway Estates, Parkway II and Glenmont Park in Langley City; Willow Park Estates in Willoughby; Churchill Park, Spencer Green, Chartwell Green and Chelsea Green in Walnut Grove; Creekside Villa in Aldergrove; Waterfront at Bedford Landing in Fort Langley) and 4 manufactured-home/co-op communities (Cedar Creek Estates, Fernridge Park and Pineridge in Brookswood; Pioneer Park in Aldergrove). Everything else on the feed is general market, whatever the page it appears on. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: The card shows two different times. Which open-house time do I trust?** Neither can be confirmed from the page itself: on every labelled card read for the research file, the top time block is exactly one hour later than the "Open House on..." line under it, and the listing's own text agrees with the block on some cards and with neither on another. Confirm the actual time with the listing brokerage before setting out — arriving an hour late to a two-hour viewing is the practical risk for a 55+ buyer travelling across Langley. https://jaredgibbons.ca/langley-open-houses.html
- **Q: Does every listing on this page hold an open house?** No, on the evidence of the first page: five of twelve cards carried an open-house time block and seven carried no open-house label or time at all in the page text. Treat an unlabelled card as a listing to confirm privately, not as an open house. https://jaredgibbons.ca/langley-open-houses.html
- **Q: Do I need my own agent to attend, and can the host act for me?** No official source requires you to bring an agent. The host is the seller's licensee: BCFSA states a professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, dual agency has been prohibited in BC since 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances, and unrepresented consumers must be told the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: What can I safely say to the host?** Factual questions about the home are the safe ground: under Rules s. 54(3), disclosure of representation is not required while a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or giving factual responses to general questions — unless the licensee solicits or receives information about your motivation, financial qualifications or needs. Save your budget, motivation and needs for your own agent. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo107/loo107/209_2021
- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a home we view at a 55+ open house?** Yes, in a strata — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. In a manufactured-home park or the Pioneer Park co-op, the park's or co-op's own rules govern instead — the strata exemptions do not apply there. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy a 55+ home I see at an open house, for a parent or as an investment?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident, and any tenant must also satisfy the bylaw or an exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q: What should I check about access before I go?** Form differs sharply inside the 55+ stock itself: Huntsfield Green and Creekside Villa are rancher (ground-oriented) townhouse communities, Churchill Park's townhouses are two-storey duplex-style, and Parkway Estates' building source records an elevator and wheelchair access among its amenities. Whether a specific unit is step-free, and whether a specific open house offers step-free entry, is not stated in any source for a specific viewing — UNCONFIRMED per unit; ask the listing brokerage before travelling. https://www.tmbrealestate.com/walnut-grove-townhomes/churchill-park and https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/parkway-estates-5360-205-street
- **Q: What strata documents should I ask for after a viewing I like?** The Form B and the registered bylaws are what verify the age rule, the pet rule and any occupancy limit the host described — several Langley buildings still show "rentals not allowed" or pet lines in public building data that provincial law or the registered bylaw may not match (long-term rental-restriction bylaws have been void since Nov 24, 2022). Do not rely on the listing line or the host's summary. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals and Section 2 above.
- **Q: Are pets allowed in the homes I'd be viewing?** Complex by complex, and the records conflict in places: Parkway Estates and Parkway II building sources record pets not allowed; Willow Park Estates records pets allowed with restrictions; Chartwell Green listings allow 2 pets with a dog size limit; Fernridge Park listings state no pets. The registered bylaw or park rule for the specific complex governs — never the listing that happens to be showing. Sources: Section 2 above.

- **"55+ townhouse" does not mean single-level in Langley.** Churchill Park's 147 townhouses are two-storey duplex-style; the rancher product is Huntsfield Green and Creekside Villa. A buyer downsizing specifically to avoid stairs has to sort the 55+ stock by form, not just by the age label. https://www.tmbrealestate.com/walnut-grove-townhomes/churchill-park and https://www.zolo.ca/langley-real-estate/27435-29a-avenue/45
- **Three Langley seniors names are traps at viewing stage.** "The Maple" (subsidized rental, 5460 203rd St) is not "Maple Manor" (general-market rental, 20117/20119 56th Ave); "Sunridge Gardens" is a rental residence, not condos; and "Morningside Estates" (5365 205 St) could not be verified as 55+, while its verified neighbour Glenmont Park (5375 205 St) could — a visitor can easily attend the wrong building on the same street. https://www.bchousing.org/news/releases/opening-more-seniors-housing-in-langley and https://www.zolo.ca/langley-real-estate/5375-205-street/110
- **The "rentals not allowed" lines in Langley building records are legally dead for long-term rentals.** Parkway Estates, Parkway II and Willow Park Estates all carry that line in public building data, and all such bylaws have been void since Nov 24, 2022 — if a host repeats the line at a viewing, the surviving restrictions are the age bylaw itself and any short-term rental rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **No one publishes whether a Langley open house worked.** SnapStats publishes no open-house category and no official source publishes Langley open-house counts, traffic or conversion statistics — so any claim about how busy a given 55+ open house will be, or how fast its building sells at open houses, is UNVERIFIED. ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-9/langley-open-houses.md
