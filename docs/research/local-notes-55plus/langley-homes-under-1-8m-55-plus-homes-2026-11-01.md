## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT 55+ HOMES

- **Only 55+ age bylaws are legal in BC strata now.** Under the Strata Property Act, ss. 123.1 and 123.2, a strata may restrict residents to age 55 or older; any age bylaw set below 55 (19+, 40+, 45+) has been invalid since Nov 24, 2022, when Bill 44 (Building and Strata Statutes Amendment Act, 2022) took effect. This is the test for Highgate Terrace above: its record says "age restrictions" without a number — if the registered bylaw sets any age other than 55, it cannot be valid in that form today. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **The bylaw restricts who may LIVE in the unit, not who may own it.** The Province states people under 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to reside in it; the exemptions attach to a "specified resident" who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Built-in exemptions (Strata Property Regulation 7.01):** residents who were lawfully living in the unit when a 55+ bylaw passed (legacy residents); live-in caregivers; and — expanded effective May 1, 2023 — a younger spouse or partner, children under 19 in the specified resident's care, and adult children the specified resident cared for before they turned 19. Read listing wordings like Willow Park Estates' "at least one owner must be 55" against these exemptions: a listing summary is not the bylaw, and the bylaw cannot remove the regulation's exemptions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2020-2024/2023HOUS0038-000601.htm
- **Rental-restriction bylaws are void province-wide.** As of Nov 24, 2022, no strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw — all existing ones are invalid, and no bylaw amendment is needed for that to apply. Stratas may still ban or limit SHORT-TERM rentals by bylaw. Several Langley buildings above still carry "rentals not allowed" in their building records (Parkway Estates, Parkway II, Willow Park Estates, and the Brookswood parks' listing records) — for a long-term strata rental that line cannot be relied on; in a park, renting runs through park rules instead. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Manufactured-home parks and the co-op run on their own rules, not strata law:** the age rules at Cedar Creek Estates, Fernridge Park and Pineridge are park rules attached to a leasehold pad, and Pioneer Park's are co-op rules attached to share ownership — the Strata Property Act exemptions in this section do not answer an occupancy question in any of them. Pad tenancies are governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and its Regulation (B.C. Reg. 481/2003), whose assignment rules expressly recognise a park where every site is reserved for a tenant who has reached 55 (per Human Rights Code s. 10(2)(b)(i)), and allow a park landlord to refuse an assignment where the proposed purchaser does not meet the park's age requirement. Get the park's or co-op's current rules in writing. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Two municipalities, two zoning bylaws:** Langley City Zoning Bylaw 2025 No. 3300 applies to the entire area within the City of Langley and repealed the City's Zoning Bylaw 1996 No. 2100; the Township's complexes sit under Township of Langley Zoning Bylaw No. 2500, whose Residential Compact Lot Zone R-CL (s. 403) sets Willoughby's compact-lot pattern (s. 403.7: lots from 220 m² minimum to 464.5 m² maximum, minimum frontage 11 m front-loaded and 8.2 m rear-loaded). Neither bylaw creates a 55+ status — in every complex above, the age rule comes from a strata bylaw, park rule or co-op rule, not from zoning. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/schedule-zoning-bylaw-2025-no-3300pdf and https://www.tol.ca/en/services/resources/bylaw-services/bylaws/Zoning-Bylaws/Section-400-Residential-(R,-MH,-FH-and-CRE)-Zones.pdf
- **Property transfer tax and the first-time buyers' program, on the Province's published figures:** general PTT is 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000 — purchases in this band are taxed in the 1% and 2% tiers only. Under the first time home buyers' program, the full exemption requires a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024) and exempts the tax on the first $500,000 of the purchase price, with a partial exemption only above $835,000 and below $860,000, subject to the program's other conditions — so the program bears only on the lowest-priced end of this band, which is where Langley's 55+ park homes and older condos sit. FHSA and Home Buyers' Plan dollar limits: UNVERIFIED in this file — not read off a live CRA page in this pass. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Property taxes — two calendars:** Township of Langley property taxes and utility fees were due Thursday, July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on current-year taxes unpaid at midnight July 2, 2026 and a further 5% at midnight September 3, 2026, and utility fees billed separately; the City of Langley also sets a July 2 due date, with payments received after it incurring a penalty (the City's penalty rate is not stated on the page cited — UNCONFIRMED). https://www.tol.ca/en/services/property-taxes.aspx and https://www.langleycity.ca/PayPropertyTaxes

- **Q: My spouse is under 55. Can we both live in a Langley 55+ strata?** Yes — provincial rules require an exemption for a younger spouse or partner of a resident who meets the age requirement (or holds a legacy exemption). Children can also be exempt in defined cases: a child under 19 in the resident's care, and an adult child the resident cared for before that child turned 19. Note the manufactured-home parks and the Pioneer Park co-op set their own occupancy rules, which these strata exemptions do not govern — one Pioneer Park listing states both occupants must be 55. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://murphyrealestategroup.ca/recip.html/1272399.search/SearchResults.form
- **Q: I'm under 55. Can I buy in a 55+ building for a parent, or as an investment?** Ownership itself is not age-restricted — the bylaw governs who may reside. The Province states under-55s can own strata lots in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in them; a parent who is 55+ can be the resident. In a park, the purchase is an assignment of the pad tenancy and the park can refuse an assignment where no purchaser meets the age requirement. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/crbc/crbc/481_2003
- **Q: Can I rent out a unit in a 55+ building?** Long-term rental bans no longer exist in BC strata — every strata rental-restriction bylaw became invalid on Nov 24, 2022, whatever an older building summary still shows. Two catches remain: the tenant still has to satisfy the 55+ age bylaw or an exemption, and a strata can still restrict short-term rentals. Park homes are different: renting and assignment run through the park rules and the MHPTA. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q: How is Pioneer Park different from the Brookswood parks?** It is a co-operative, not a pad-rental park: listings state the buyer must be accepted by the co-op and must purchase co-op shares ($9,200 in one listing, refunded upon sale), and pays a monthly co-op fee ($510 in the same listing) rather than pad rent to a landlord. At Cedar Creek, Fernridge and Pineridge the tenure recorded is leasehold (non-strata) — you own the home and hold the pad under the park's rules. Confirm current figures and rules with the co-op or park before writing. https://murphyrealestategroup.ca/recip.html/1272399.search/SearchResults.form?_pg=3 and https://www.estateblock.com/langley/5-3031-200-st-1968212
- **Q: Are pets allowed in Langley's 55+ complexes?** Complex by complex, and the records conflict in places: Willow Park Estates listings record cats and dogs OK to a limit of two in one source and a one-pet weight limit in another — SOURCE CONFLICT between listings, flagged; Parkway Estates and Parkway II building sources record pets not allowed; Creekside Villa records pets allowed with restrictions (2 maximum, shoulder-height limit in listings); Cedar Creek Estates records pets allowed with restrictions; Fernridge Park listings state no pets; Pioneer Park listings state 2 pets allowed with no size restrictions. The registered bylaw or park rule for the specific complex governs — never the listing that happens to be showing. Sources: Section 2 above.
- **Q: Is Sunridge Gardens a condo I can buy under this band?** No — Sunridge Gardens, 22301 Fraser Hwy, is a Bria Communities rental retirement residence (independent living suites plus a licensed care centre). Rental residences do not sell unit by unit. The same applies to Magnolia Gardens, Chartwell Langley Gardens and Langley Seniors Village. https://centurygroup.ca/seniors-living/
- **Q: Where is the hospital, and what transit is coming?** Langley Memorial Hospital, 22051 Fraser Highway, is the Fraser Health community hospital for Langley, with the Langley Urgent and Primary Care Centre at 202 - 20434 64 Avenue. On transit, TransLink states the Surrey-Langley SkyTrain extension will run 16 km along Fraser Highway from King George Station to 203 Street in Langley City, with an anticipated in-service date of 2029 — the corridor that runs past the Langley City 55+ cluster. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Langley/langley-memorial-hospital and https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/rapid-transit-projects/surrey-langley-skytrain
- **Q: Does the 55+ restriction shrink my resale buyer pool?** Mechanically, yes: the next buyer must also be able to live there under the bylaw — be 55+, be an exempt spouse/partner, caregiver or legacy case, or buy without living in the unit. In the parks, the pool is narrowed the same way through assignment consent; at Pioneer Park it is further narrowed by the co-op acceptance and share purchase steps. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **Langley's 55+ map is two clusters and a fringe, not a spread.** Downtown Langley City holds four strata complexes within a few blocks of 205/206 Street and 53 Avenue; Walnut Grove holds four more along the 88 Avenue / 208 Street corridor; Brookswood holds three of the four manufactured-home communities. A buyer searching "Langley 55+ under $1.8M" without a community name is really choosing between those clusters. Sources: Section 2 above.
