- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s.42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute the notice within 15 days, may in defined circumstances (home cannot be moved, no site within a reasonable distance, no tax owing on the home) apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes

ALR residences cannot be stratified without the ALC's permission — depositing a strata plan is a subdivision, and a person must not subdivide agricultural land unless permitted by the Commission or under the ALR General Regulation (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). This is a permission rule, not an absolute ban, and ALC Information Bulletin 05 contains no stratification statement and is not cited for it. A specific parcel's reserve status is checked on the ALC maps by PID or civic address; no ALR vs non-ALR split is published for this topic and none is stated. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: If I buy a home in an Abbotsford park, do I own land or acreage?** No — on a rented pad you own the manufactured home and hold a manufactured home site tenancy under the MHPTA, paying monthly site rent to the park. Land ownership exists only in the own-land forms: fee-simple lots, or a bare-land strata lot where that form exists. A pad is never acreage. (§2.)
- **Q: Is there an official median price for Abbotsford manufactured homes?** No. SnapStats publishes no manufactured-home category and no acreage category, so no median, sales ratio or inventory figure for Abbotsford manufactured homes exists in the sources used, and none is stated in this file. The §1 figures (detached median $949,000; Condos & Townhomes median $497,500 in September 2026) are all-residential community context only. (§1.)
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up each year?** Once every 12 months, on at least 3 full months' notice, and no more than the yearly limit set by the Residential Tenancy Branch: for manufactured home site tenancies the 2026 limit is 2.3% plus a proportional amount for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees, and the 2027 limit is 2.2% plus the proportional amount. No pad-rent dollar figure is stated as fact in this file — the page's $650–$950 range is UNVERIFIED. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Are Paradise Park, Georgian Park and Maplewood Estates land I can buy?** No — they are this program's Abbotsford 55+ manufactured-home parks (tenure records in the 55-plus sibling), and a park home there sits on a rented pad. Verified as parks is not verified as lot stock: you buy the home and take the site tenancy, not the land. (§2.)
- **Q: The page says most Central Abbotsford and Clearbrook parks are 55+ — is that right?** UNVERIFIED as stated — the program record confirms three 55+ parks by name (Paradise Park, Georgian Park, Maplewood Estates) but no Abbotsford-wide age-restriction split was confirmed on a park or official source, and the page's "majority" claim is not carried as fact. Confirm a specific park's age rule from the park's own documents. (§2.)
- **Q: Why does the CSA label matter when buying a manufactured home?** The CSA label is the home's certification decal — Z-240 series for mobile homes, A-277 series for modular homes, with a unique identifier usually near the front door or on the electrical panel. The Province states a manufactured home may not be sold unless the original legible CSA decal is attached; if it is lost, the home must be re-certified, with Technical Safety BC arranging an electrical inspection and an approval label that can be used in lieu of the CSA label to list the home for sale. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: Does PTT apply when I buy a manufactured home on a rented pad?** UNVERIFIED in this file: the live page states flatly that PTT does not apply / buyers are exempt on the chattel, but no Province PTT passage stating that treatment was opened in this program — flagged in this file only. A lawyer or notary should confirm PTT and registration handling for the exact structure before subjects come off. (§3.)
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold for redevelopment?** A park conversion needs all necessary government permits and approvals in place first, then 12 months' notice covering a full year and $20,000 compensation per MHPTA s.42, with defined additional-compensation routes where the home cannot be moved and no site is available within a reasonable distance. Confirm the park's ownership and any known redevelopment signals as part of due diligence. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Can the park refuse the buyer I sell my park home to?** Assignment of the site tenancy needs the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed obtained under the Regulation), a director's order, or authorization in the tenancy agreement itself (MHPTA s.28, via RTB-10); consent may be withheld only in prescribed circumstances, and the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting (s.28(3)).
