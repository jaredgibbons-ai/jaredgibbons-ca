## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The process — confirmed in this slug's own research (Rule 21-7 (1)–(5)(c), BC Laws, batch-10 verification):** Step 1 — the petition: "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia — Rule 21-7 (1). Parties: all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished and all persons against whom any relief is sought must be made respondents — Rule 21-7 (2); a petitioner may also join a claim arising out of the mortgage or collateral security and a person liable to pay the mortgage debt — Rule 21-7 (3). Later interests: if the petitioner registers a certificate of pending litigation, a person who subsequently registers or files an interest need not be served, is bound by an order made in the proceeding, and may file a response to petition — Rule 21-7 (4). Information notice: a petitioner who serves the petition must include the informational notice for foreclosure proceedings in Form 125 — Rule 21-7 (4.1). Powers of the court: the court may make a final order of foreclosure — Rule 21-7 (5)(a); order that a respondent must, within a redemption period the court may fix, pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs, with foreclosure of the equity of redemption in default — Rule 21-7 (5)(b); and determine summarily, or order an account be taken and certified, the amount due — Rule 21-7 (5)(c). The redemption period has no fixed length in the Rule text read: it is the period the court fixes in its order. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rule text read in this program — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5)(b). Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections: redemption can end the deal. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: What can the court order at the first stage of a foreclosure?** Under Rule 21-7 (5) the court may make a final order of foreclosure, order payment of what is due within a court-fixed redemption period with foreclosure in default, or determine or have certified the amount due. Do not assume every file is at an early, reversible stage. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer at the approval hearing?** Treat a competing bid at the approval stage as a real possibility to plan for: the settled treatment (§3) runs competing-offer handling through Rule 13-5 conduct-of-sale directions and PD-66 hearing practice, with Form 60 certifying the result — while this slug's research file itself marks that zone UNVERIFIED program-wide, and the live page's courthouse competing-offer account is the page's own statement. Either way, the operative instructions are the specific file's, in writing, from the seller's counsel. https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html
- **Q: Are foreclosures cheaper than regular listings?** No published source in this program supports a standard foreclosure discount, and SnapStats publishes no foreclosure category, so no foreclosure price or discount figure is stated in this file. The live page's own caution stands as editorial: "foreclosures aren't always cheap." https://jaredgibbons.ca/foreclosures.html
- **Q: Does buying an acreage at a court sale change its ALR or farm-class position?** No — the land's ALR status, zoning and classification survive the sale (§3). What changes is the buyer's own homework: ALC permission is still required to stratify or subdivide ALR ground, and a buyer continuing to farm must file the farm-class application by October 31 after purchase.
