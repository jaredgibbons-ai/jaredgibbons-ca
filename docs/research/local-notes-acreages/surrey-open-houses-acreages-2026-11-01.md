## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The open-house rule itself — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), verbatim, sourcing stated:** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, states that "the disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate." That is the text as set out in that report (http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download, read 2026-11-01) — quoted here at exactly that sourcing level, not dressed up as a fresh pull of the current Rule text. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-2/surrey-open-houses.md, §4)
- **Representation at an open house:** a licensed real estate professional in BC must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party to the transaction. In practice: chatting factually with the host does not by itself make you their client — but once you start sharing your motivation, budget or needs with a licensee who represents the seller, the disclosure rules engage. For an acreage open house this matters more, not less: parcel value turns on motivation-sensitive facts (development intent, farm plans, holding horizon) that a visitor should save for their own agent. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Identification or sign-in for visitors:** an identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors is NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED as a rule; a host may still ask, as a matter of the seller's instructions, but that practice is not in an official source. (Research file above, §4.)
- **Surrey frame — where this topic's research and the siblings ground it:** Surrey's current Official Community Plan is the Surrey 2050 OCP, Bylaw No. 21900, adopted June 15, 2026 — the old OCP Bylaw No. 18020 is REPLACED and is never cited as current. Surrey Zoning Bylaw, 1993, No. 12000 governs what a lot seen at an open house can actually hold, including the SSMUH tiers (up to 3 units on lots under 280 sq m, up to 4 units on larger lots, up to 6 units on eligible lots within 400 m of a frequent bus stop) and the secondary suite cap of 40% of the principal dwelling's habitable floor area; Surrey's SSMUH framework has been in force since July 8, 2024. Streamside setbacks sit in Part 7A of Zoning Bylaw 12000 and vary by stream class — no single riparian figure is ever stated. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/r133-plansurrey-2013-official-community-plan-surrey-2050-official-community-plan-alignment-related-amendments and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing (~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/surrey-open-houses-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md, §3)

- **ALR — the Surrey acreage check an open house cannot answer:** Surrey's fringe acreage parcels can sit in the Agricultural Land Reserve, and no open-house card states ALR status. Parcel-level ALR status is a check through the Agricultural Land Commission, and if a parcel is in the ALR: subdivision requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)); residence caps are one principal residence to 500 m², a secondary suite ONLY inside the principal residence, and an additional residence capped at 90 m² (parcels of 40 ha or less) or 186 m² (over 40 ha). https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, for the working parcel seen at a viewing:** $2,500 of farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the farm operation for farm purposes in excess of 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha — Classification of Land as a Farm Regulation, B.C. Reg. 411/95, s.5, applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR wherever met; the flat figure is documented as the HORSE-PROPERTIES page's own research position and is never attributed to this topic's research. https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) on land bought after a visit:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $900,000 fully residential acreage purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $700,000 ($14,000) = **$16,000**. Anchor checks: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K cap→$14,000 ($6,000 FTHB); $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** (tier math: $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000) — a townhomes sibling elsewhere in the program carries $78,000 for $3.5M; that figure is an OPEN reconciliation item: follow $93,000, flag the conflict wherever met. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — ONLY at the CRA GI-003 level:** CRA Info Sheet GI-003 states most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (personal-use land is the example), while an individual's sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts. Farmland caution: the CRA treats farmland sales in a separate sheet as usually taxable — never assume a farm parcel is GST-exempt on the vacant-land rule alone; parcel-level treatment is UNCONFIRMED, and anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
