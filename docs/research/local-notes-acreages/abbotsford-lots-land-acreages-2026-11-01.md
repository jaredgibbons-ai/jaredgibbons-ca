- **ALR RULES IN FULL — ALR ground is one of this feed's three documented land types, so the full frame governs wherever a parcel is in the reserve:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified without the ALC's permission — depositing a strata plan is a subdivision, and a person must not subdivide agricultural land unless permitted by the Commission or under the ALR General Regulation (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). This is a permission rule, not an absolute ban, and ALC Information Bulletin 05 contains no stratification statement and is not cited for it. Residence caps as the ALC states them: land in the ALR may generally have no more than one residence per parcel unless the parcel qualifies for an additional residence, and local governments may further regulate or restrict residences through zoning; a principal residence must have a total floor area of 500 m² or less, and no more than 1,000 m² of fill (including structural aggregate for foundations and driveways) can be placed for its construction; a secondary suite is permitted ONLY when located within the principal residence, entirely contained within it; one additional residence, detached from the principal residence, is permitted up to 90 m² on properties of 40 ha or less and up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha, and building more than the permitted number, or larger than the size limits, requires ALC approval. The A1 zone permits one Accessory Farm Dwelling Unit per lot in accordance with the Agricultural Land Reserve Use Regulation, B.C. Reg. 30/2019, or as approved by the ALC, on a lot of at least 0.4 ha, to the same 90 m² / 186 m² maximums (program-carried City bylaw record). City zoning and the ALC frame both apply; neither displaces the other. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

- **Q: Can I build a house on vacant ALR land in Abbotsford?** Under the ALC frame, generally one principal residence of 500 m² or less total floor area, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill placed for its construction; a secondary suite ONLY inside that principal residence; and one additional detached residence up to 90 m² (properties of 40 ha or less) or 186 m² (over 40 ha). City zoning must also permit the residence, and anything larger or more numerous needs ALC approval. The live page's FAQ answer omits the fill limit and the additional-residence tiers — the ALC figures govern. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Can I stratify or subdivide an ALR parcel into strata lots?** Not without the ALC's permission: depositing a strata plan is a subdivision, and ALCA s.21(1) bars subdividing agricultural land unless the Commission permits it or the ALR General Regulation allows it (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s.6). Do not write "can never be stratified," and do not cite Bulletin 05 for stratification. (§3.)
