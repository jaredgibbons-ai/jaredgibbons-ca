## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The FTHB GST/HST rebate — verified figures in full:** amendments to Part IX of the Excise Tax Act in Bill C-4 introduced the rebate; Bill C-4 received royal assent on **March 13, 2026** (CRA governs). Together with the GST/HST new housing rebate, it provides **100% of the GST** (or the federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated single unit residential complex or residential condominium unit valued **up to $1 million**; between $1 million and $1.5 million the rebate is **gradually reduced**; and **no FTHB rebate is available at $1.5 million or more**. Maximum in dollars: up to **$50,000** — consistent with 100% of the 5% GST on a $1 million home in BC. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
- **Who counts as a first-time buyer (verified):** at least 18 years old, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and must not have lived in a home — in or outside Canada — that they or their spouse or common-law partner owned or jointly owned as a primary place of residence at any time in the calendar year of the purchase or in the preceding four calendar years. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Property and timing conditions (verified):** the buyer must meet the existing new housing rebate's eligibility criteria (or would meet them if that rebate's ceiling were $1.5 million instead of $450,000); must have entered a purchase and sale agreement with a builder **on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031** (for an owner-built home, construction must begin on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031); construction must begin before 2031 and be substantially completed before 2036; the buyer must be the first to occupy the home as a place of residence after substantial completion; and the buyer must not have previously applied for an FTHB rebate. The rebate also generally covers a home bought on land leased from the builder and a share in a co-operative housing corporation entitling the buyer to occupy a new unit. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Claiming (verified):** the application time limit is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction; a builder can credit the rebate against the amount payable when ownership transfers; where the builder does not credit it, the buyer applies directly to the CRA through a CRA account or by mailing Form GST190 (houses purchased from a builder); an owner-builder who started construction on or after March 20, 2025 applies directly using Form GST191. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html

- **The existing GST/HST new housing rebate (thresholds verified in the research pass):** generally **36% of the GST** (or federal part of the HST) paid on a newly constructed or substantially renovated single unit residential complex or condominium unit, to a **maximum rebate of $6,300**; for homes with a purchase price or fair market value between **$350,000 and $450,000** the rebate is gradually reduced, and no rebate is available at $450,000 or more. Housing-only — it does not rebate GST on bare land. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Does the first-time buyer GST rebate apply to bare land or an acreage purchase?** No. The rebate applies to new HOUSING — a newly constructed or substantially renovated single unit residential complex or condominium unit — not to bare land itself. Buying the lot gets no FTHB rebate on the land; building or buying the new home is what the rebate attaches to. (§2.) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: How much GST can a first-time buyer in BC get back on a new home?** Up to $50,000: together with the new housing rebate, the FTHB rebate returns 100% of the GST on a new or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more. (§3.)
- **Q: Is GST even payable when I buy vacant land?** At the CRA GI-003 level carried here: most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (personal-use land is the example), but a sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts — and developer/business sales are generally taxable. Farmland is treated in a separate CRA info sheet, usually taxable subject to limited exemptions. Anything finer is UNCONFIRMED — get a ruling-level answer from your accountant before completion. (§3.)
- **Q: Who counts as a first-time home buyer for this rebate?** An individual who is at least 18, is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and has not lived in a home that they or their spouse or common-law partner owned or jointly owned as a primary place of residence in the calendar year of purchase or in the preceding four calendar years. (§3.)
- **Q: I am building on land I already own — can I claim?** The owner-built route is inside the rebate's frame: construction must begin on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, be substantially completed before 2036, and you must be the first to occupy the home; an owner-builder applies directly to the CRA using Form GST191, usually within two years of finishing construction. The rebate is on the new housing, not the land. (§§2–3.)
- **Q: What happened to the old GST new housing rebate?** It still exists on its own terms: generally 36% of the GST paid, to a maximum of $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and unavailable at $450,000 or more — the FTHB rebate is the separate top-up that takes a qualifying first-time buyer's combined recovery to 100%. Do not double-count the old rebate: the $50,000 figure exists only because of the FTHB top-up. (§3.)
- **Q: How and when do I claim the FTHB rebate?** Usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction: a builder can credit the rebate at closing and submits the jointly completed Form GST190, or you apply directly to the CRA through a CRA account or by mailing Form GST190 — and Form GST191 for an owner-built home. Settle the crediting arrangement before completion. (§3.)
- **Q: Can I combine the GST rebate with BC's property transfer tax exemption?** Yes — they are separate federal and provincial programs: the BC first-time program exempts PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price where the fair market value is $835,000 or less, with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000. PTT, unlike the GST rebate, applies to a land purchase too. (§3.)
- **Q: My presale was signed before March 20, 2025 — do I qualify?** No — eligibility turns on a builder agreement entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, with construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036; a presale signed before March 20, 2025 is outside the rebate no matter the price. Check the signing date before the price. (§3.)
- **Q: Is the $1.25 million / 50% example on the page the CRA's formula?** Not as verified here: the CRA publication opened in the research pass says only that the rebate is "gradually reduced" between $1 million and $1.5 million, so the page's straight-line mechanism and its $1.25M row are UNVERIFIED as a mechanism — the verified anchors are 100% to $1M (max $50,000) and nil at $1.5M. (§3.)
