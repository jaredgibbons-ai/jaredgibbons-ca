## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The regulator:** real estate trading services in BC are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); the Province's Buying and selling a home page directs prospective purchasers to BCFSA for purchasing information. A licensee must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **BCFSA Rule 5-10(3) — carried verbatim, sourcing stated:** as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes (the source the research re-verified for this file), Rule 5-10(3) states that a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. In practice: factual chat with the host does not by itself make you their client — but once you share motivation, budget or needs with a licensee representing the seller, the disclosure rules engage. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Identification / sign-in:** no BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source states an identification or sign-in requirement for open-house visitors: UNVERIFIED as a rule — a host may still ask, on the seller's instructions; that practice is not in an official source.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Do I need my own agent to attend a Tsawwassen open house?** No official source requires it. Rule 5-10(3) excuses disclosure of representation while a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or answering general factual questions — but the moment you share motivation, financial qualifications or needs, that changes, and BCFSA requires unrepresented consumers to be told the risks of dealing with a licensee who represents the other party. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: How do I know the open-house time is current?** Each listing carries an OPEN HOUSE label with a date and times — but on this feed the card time and the time inside the listing's own text differ by one hour (card 12:00–2:00 PM vs text 11:00 AM–1:00 PM). Which is authoritative is UNVERIFIED: confirm with the listing brokerage before you go. https://jaredgibbons.ca/tsawwassen-open-houses.html
- **Q: Do I have to sign in or show ID?** No BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source states a sign-in or ID requirement for open-house visitors: UNVERIFIED as a rule. A host may still ask, on the seller's instructions. (§3.)
- **Q: Is the host the seller's agent?** The host is a licensee connected to the listing side; a licensee must work for either buyer or seller and must inform consumers of the duties owed to clients and non-clients — assume anything non-factual you tell the host can reach the seller. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Which Tsawwassen areas appear on the feed?** On the cards read for the research: Tsawwassen Central, Tsawwassen North, English Bluff and Cliff Drive, across condos, townhomes and detached houses. The feed is Tsawwassen-wide and changes as listings come and go — it is not an acreage roster. (§2.)
- **Q: Does an open house at a large lot mean it's an acreage I can subdivide or stratify?** No — the feed establishes nothing about the parcel. Subdivision runs through the City against zone minimums (Delta zoning number CONTESTED, §3), and if the parcel is in the ALR, subdivision needs ALC approval and stratification is barred outright. Check title, zoning and ALC maps, not the feed card.
- **Q: How should I plan a run across Tsawwassen?** Group by the clusters the feed actually spans — Tsawwassen North (Highway 17 side), Central and Cliff Drive (56 Street / 12 Avenue side), English Bluff (the west bluff) — using 56 Street and 12 Avenue as the spine, and use South Delta Recreation Centre (1720 56 Street) or Centennial Beach between stops. (§1.)
- **Q: What should I ask the host?** Factual questions — the Rule 5-10(3) zone. Save motivation, budget and needs for your own agent: once shared with the seller's licensee, the disclosure rules engage and the information sits with the other side's representative. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Q: If I offer after an open house on a lot or farm-edge property, what taxes bite?** PTT on the tiers in §3 (worked anchors given), and GST only at the GI-003 general level — farmland is usually taxable under CRA's separate sheet, so the contract must state the position. Both are UNCONFIRMED for a specific parcel until its facts are applied. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Where do I do consumer homework before offer day?** Start at the regulator — the Province directs prospective purchasers to BCFSA for purchasing information; read its consumer pages before offer day, not after. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home
