- **ALR RULES IN FULL — the governing frame for ALR parcels, City and FVRD side alike:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps as the ALC states them: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m², with no more than 1,000 m² of fill placed for its construction; a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence (detached, where conditions are met and City/FVRD bylaws also permit it) capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. Local governments may further regulate or restrict residences in the ALR. One hectare = 2.471 acres. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure:** $2,500 of farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha; applications by October 31 for the following taxation year. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR. Stated explicitly for this slug: the flat form is NOT documented in Mission farms' own research (the research states the full three tiers) — it is owned by the HORSE-PROPERTIES page, a different slug, and is never attributed to this file. Farm class is the holding-cost difference between a working Mission parcel and an idle one — a new owner who plans to continue farming must apply by October 31, and a buyer whose plan cannot meet the threshold for the operation's size puts the classification, and its tax treatment, at risk. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification

- **Subdivision and servicing (City side):** the City's servicing bylaw is the Development and Subdivision Control Bylaw (DSCB) No. 5650-2017, stated from a City source (title confirmed on mission.ca — a City-hosted copy is titled "City of Mission Development and Subdivision Control Bylaw 5650-2017").
