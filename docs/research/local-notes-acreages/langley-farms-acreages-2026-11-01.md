## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **Zoning: Township of Langley Zoning Bylaw No. 2500** applies across the Township; Township material names Rural Zones RU-1 and RU-2 and Suburban Residential Zones SR-1, SR-2 and SR-3. The minimum lot size for temporary farm labourer accommodation in Bylaw No. 2500 (s. 201.3) was not confirmed from a Township-hosted bylaw text — UNCONFIRMED. - **ALR RULES IN FULL — the governing frame for Langley farm parcels:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval, stated as the ALC states it. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps as the ALC states them: land in the ALR may generally have no more than one residence per parcel unless an additional residence qualifies; a principal residence built without an ALC application must have a total floor area of 500 m² or less, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill placed for its construction, and a secondary suite is permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; local governments may further regulate or restrict residences in the ALR. One additional residence is permitted without an ALC application where its conditions are met — detached, with a total floor area of 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller and 186 m² or less on parcels larger than 40 ha — and Township bylaws must also permit it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure:** $2,500 of farm income if the total area of the farm operation is between 0.8 ha and 4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if the operation is greater than 4 ha; $10,000 if the operation is less than 0.8 ha — with applications by October 31. **Precision rule carried exactly: a flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR, and it is asserted ONLY where a file's own research documents it — the HORSE-PROPERTIES page owns that error. THIS file's research documents the correct three-tier form (above) and does NOT document the flat figure, so the error is NOT attributed to the langley-farms page or its research here.** Farm class is the holding-cost difference between a working Langley parcel and an idle one — confirm the parcel's current class and the income record before pricing the tax position. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **SSMUH — Township Bylaw 6020, NO 6-plex tier:** the Township's SSMUH frame is Bylaw 6020, and it carries NO 6-plex tier — a page claiming a Township 6-plex tier (the Brookswood DEVELOPMENT page's claim) carries that page's own defect, asserted only there. The Township states lots larger than 4,050 m² are eligible for one unit plus one secondary suite or accessory dwelling unit depending on zoning. Township suite cap: a secondary suite is capped at 120 m² (Township figure). On ALR land the ALC suite rule (inside the principal residence only) and the ALC caps govern over the municipal frame. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/small-scale-multi-unit-housing.aspx

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much of Langley is in the ALR?** The Township of Langley states 235 square kilometres of the Township is within the Agricultural Land Reserve; check a specific parcel on the ALC maps — the district name does not settle the parcel. https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/about-the-township.aspx and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- **Q: How big can a house be on ALR land?** A principal residence built without an ALC application must have a total floor area of 500 m² or less, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill placed for its construction; a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Can a Langley farm have a second dwelling?** On ALR land, one additional residence is permitted without an ALC application where the conditions are met — 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or smaller, 186 m² or less on larger parcels — and Township bylaws must also permit it. Off the ALR, the Township frame applies: lots larger than 4,050 m² are eligible for one unit plus one secondary suite or accessory dwelling unit depending on zoning, the suite cap is 120 m², and the coach house position at s.102 carries the live conflict flagged in Section 3. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.tol.ca/en/the-township/small-scale-multi-unit-housing.aspx
- **Q: Can I stratify the farm later — split it into strata lots for family?** No, if it is ALR: residences in the ALR cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)), and subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval in the first place. Do not price a Langley farm as if a strata exit can be added. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: What income does a farm need to keep farm classification?** All three tiers, not a flat figure: $2,500 for an operation of 0.8 ha to 4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha for a larger operation; $10,000 for an operation under 0.8 ha — applications by October 31. A page or listing stating only "$2,500+" understates the test for small and large parcels alike. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Q: Do I need a licence to use well water on a farm?** Domestic groundwater use is exempt from licensing; groundwater used for non-domestic purposes such as irrigation requires a water licence under the Water Sustainability Act, and well records are searched in the GWELLS registry — older wells may not appear in it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-rights/water-licences-approvals/new-requirements-for-groundwater-users
- **Q: What septic paperwork should a farm seller have?** The Authorized Person's record of sewerage system filing (before construction, alteration or repair) and letter of certification (after construction) — Fraser Health's rules make those the compliance documents for any onsite system. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/health-topics-a-to-z/onsite-sewerage-systems/regulatory-requirements-for-onsite-sewerage-systems
- **Q: Do I pay GST on a Langley farm?** Do not assume either way. The CRA's vacant-land sheet makes most individual personal-use sales exempt, but it treats farmland in a separate sheet as usually taxable with limited exemptions — a working farm parcel is the fact pattern where buyers get surprised. The answer is UNCONFIRMED for any specific parcel until vendor status and farm use are applied; the contract must state who bears GST. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: Does the farm come with quota or a marketing-board position?** UNCONFIRMED in this file: no marketing-board source was opened in the research, so no quota, board or sector position is stated for any Langley farm here — a generic listing feed is never presented as a confirmed operation in a named sector. Confirm any quota claim against the board's own records with your lawyer before pricing it. (Research file above, §5.)
- **Q: How do I check the flood and dyke picture for a valley farm?** The Township's documented dyke system is on the Fraser River at the Salmon River mouth, and parcel floodplain status is checked in the Township's GeoSource — do not rely on a listing's wording. Parcel-level status: UNCONFIRMED until GeoSource is read for that parcel. https://www.tol.ca/en/building-development/development-applications.aspx
