## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The settled court process, step by step:** (1) Petition — "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia (Rule 21-7 (1)); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents (Rule 21-7 (2)); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 (Rule 21-7 (4.1)). Rule 21-7 sets out no demand step before the petition; in the standard mortgage terms prescribed under the Land Title Act, if a default occurs, the lender may "demand payment of all the mortgage money," apply to the court for an order that the land be sold on terms approved by the court, or apply to the court to foreclose the borrower's interest. Narrower research stance noted in-file: this slug's research file verified Rule 21-7 live on 2026-11-01 but did NOT re-verify the prescribed mortgage-terms demand wording in its pass — the settled program position above is carried with that gap recorded. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo61/loo61/53_90_03
- **Order, redemption, final order:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period that the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the respondent's equity of redemption — Rule 21-7 (5) (b); the court may determine or certify the amount due and pronounce judgment for it — Rule 21-7 (5) (c) and (e). The Land Title Act refers to the court's order in a foreclosure as an "order nisi," with a later final order of foreclosure — Land Title Act, s. 271 (1). The redemption period has NO fixed length in the Rules: it is the period the court fixes in its order; interest is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem — Law and Equity Act, s. 19. In default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7 (6). A party may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7 (7). The Law and Equity Act's 6-month redemption direction (s. 16) covers a vendor's proceeding under an agreement for sale, NOT the Rule 21-7 mortgage process — it is not applied to mortgage foreclosures in this file. Narrower research stance noted in-file: this slug's research file did not re-verify the ss.19 / s.271 wording or the s.16 point in its pass (its UNVERIFIED items 5 and 10) — the settled program position governs. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol33/consol33/96250_18 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Conduct of sale and court approval:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell the property "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5 (3); the court's directions for a sale may appoint the person who is to have conduct of the sale, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of the value of the property — Rule 13-5 (4). The manner of sale the court fixes may be sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5 (4) (b); the person having conduct of a sale may apply to the court, even though the time for redemption has not expired, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7 (9); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in Form 60 by the person having conduct of the sale — Rule 13-5 (6). ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5) (b), and interest runs to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act, s. 19). Do not import the Law and Equity Act s. 16 six-month direction: it covers a vendor's proceeding under an agreement for sale, not the Rule 21-7 mortgage process. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Q: What does court confirmation mean for my offer on a Mission home?** The person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7 (9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — and that application can be made even before the redemption period expires, so until the order is made the sale is not final. Do not book movers on acceptance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** The court's sale directions may define the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids (Rule 13-5 (4) (d)), and practice direction PD-66 governs foreclosure proceedings — so a competing bid at the approval stage is a real possibility in this process. The precise timing and manner for competing bids under PD-66 are UNVERIFIED in this slug's research (the PD-66 text was not retrieved), so get the bid instructions for the specific file from the seller's counsel in writing and know your ceiling in advance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
