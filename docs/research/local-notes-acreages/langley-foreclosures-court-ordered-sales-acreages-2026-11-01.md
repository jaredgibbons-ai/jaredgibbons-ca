## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The settled court process, step by step:** (1) Petition — "A proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property or for redemption must be started by petition" in the Supreme Court of British Columbia (Rule 21-7 (1)); all persons whose interest in or claim to the mortgaged property is sought to be extinguished must be made respondents (Rule 21-7 (2)); a petitioner who serves the petition must include the court's informational notice for foreclosure proceedings, Form 125 (Rule 21-7 (4.1)). Rule 21-7 sets out no demand step before the petition; in the standard mortgage terms prescribed under the Land Title Act, if a default occurs, the lender may "demand payment of all the mortgage money," apply to the court for an order that the land be sold on terms approved by the court, or apply to the court to foreclose the borrower's interest. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo61/loo61/53_90_03
- **Order, redemption, final order:** the court may order that a respondent pay what is due under the mortgage and for costs within a redemption period that the court may fix, and that in default of payment the respondent is foreclosed of the respondent's equity of redemption — Rule 21-7 (5) (b); the court may determine or certify the amount due and pronounce judgment for it — Rule 21-7 (5) (c) and (e). The Land Title Act refers to the court's order in a foreclosure as an "order nisi," with a later final order of foreclosure — Land Title Act, s. 271 (1). The redemption period has NO fixed length in the Rules: it is the period the court fixes in its order; interest is calculated and payable to the date full payment is made to redeem — Law and Equity Act, s. 19. In default of payment within the redemption period, a final order of foreclosure may be granted on the petitioner's application — Rule 21-7 (6). A party may apply at any time for an order that the mortgaged property be sold — Rule 21-7 (7). Narrower research stance noted in-file: the Law and Equity Act's 6-month redemption direction (s. 16) covers a vendor's proceeding under an agreement for sale, NOT the Rule 21-7 mortgage process — it is not applied to mortgage foreclosures in this file. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol33/consol33/96250_18 and https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Conduct of sale and court approval:** if the court orders the property sold, a person has conduct of the sale and may sell the property "in the manner the person considers appropriate or as the court directs" — Rule 13-5 (3); the court's directions for a sale may appoint the person who is to have conduct of the sale, fix the manner of sale, fix a reserve or minimum price, define bid and offer rights, settle the particulars or conditions of sale, and obtain evidence of the value of the property — Rule 13-5 (4). The manner of sale the court fixes may be sale "by contract conditional on the approval of the court" — Rule 13-5 (4) (b); the person having conduct of a sale may apply to the court, even though the time for redemption has not expired, for an order confirming the sale, directing the disposition of the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — Rule 21-7 (9); the result of a sale by order of the court is certified in Form 60 by the person having conduct of the sale — Rule 13-5 (6). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Bids and PD-66:** the court's directions for a sale may define "the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids" — Rule 13-5 (4) (d); the Supreme Court's civil practice direction PD-66, "Foreclosure Proceedings," governs practice in these proceedings and replaced the earlier sealed-bid practice direction PD-62, per the Court of Appeal. Narrower research stance noted in-file: the timing and manner for submitting competing bids under PD-66 are UNVERIFIED in the research — the text of PD-66 was not retrieved from the court's website — so no bid-timing rule is stated here beyond the Rule 13-5 (4) (d) position above; likewise, that a court date is set for the approval of a sale in any given file is not stated on an official source in the research. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/ca/25/01/2025BCCA0108.htm

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How does a BC foreclosure start?** By petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia under Rule 21-7 (1); everyone whose interest in the property is sought to be extinguished must be made a respondent, and the petitioner must serve the court's foreclosure information notice, Form 125, with the petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: How long is the redemption period?** There is no fixed length in the Rules — the redemption period is the period the court fixes in its order under Rule 21-7 (5) (b), and interest runs to the date full payment is made to redeem (Law and Equity Act, s. 19). Do not import the Law and Equity Act s. 16 six-month direction: the research notes it covers a vendor's proceeding under an agreement for sale, not the Rule 21-7 mortgage process. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
- **Q: What does "subject to court approval" mean for my offer?** Where the court has directed sale by contract conditional on the approval of the court (Rule 13-5 (4) (b)), the person with conduct of sale applies under Rule 21-7 (9) for an order confirming the sale, directing the proceeds and vesting title in the purchaser — until that order, the sale is not final. Do not book movers on acceptance. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/168_2009_03
- **Q: Can someone outbid my accepted offer?** The court's sale directions may define the rights of a person to bid, make offers or meet bids (Rule 13-5 (4) (d)), and practice direction PD-66 governs foreclosure proceedings — so a competing bid at the approval stage is a real possibility in this process. The precise timing and manner for competing bids under PD-66 are UNVERIFIED in the research (the PD-66 text was not retrieved), so know your ceiling in advance rather than relying on a bid procedure summary. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: Where can I check the court file for a Langley foreclosure?** Court Services Online (https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/index.do) offers eSearch to anyone with Internet access; Supreme Court civil file details include the file number, registry, party names and filed documents, and foreclosure files are class "H - Foreclosure." The registry shown on the file itself is the answer for that file — which registry serves Langley as a general mapping is UNVERIFIED in the research and is not filled in here. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q: Is a "bank foreclosure" in Langley sold by the bank?** No — that search term is a misnomer in BC. Sales run through the Supreme Court foreclosure process: a person has conduct of the sale under court directions (Rule 13-5 (3)), and the court confirms the sale and vests title (Rule 21-7 (9)). https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo106/loo106/168_2009_02
- **Q: If the property is an acreage or farm, does the court process change its ALR or farm-class position?** No. The vesting order cleans title; it does not change ALR status, the ALC housing caps (500 m² principal; suite inside it only; additional residence 90 m² / 186 m² by the 40 ha line), or the BC Assessment farm-class income tiers. Price the parcel with those rules in force — and note the seller-side farm records a buyer would normally diligence may not exist in a conduct-of-sale file: UNCONFIRMED parcel by parcel. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Do I get a Form B and the usual strata documents on a court-ordered strata purchase?** UNCONFIRMED in this file's research — the document route inside a court sale is not assumed from the ordinary resale process. Budget for the possibility that strata diligence is thinner than a normal purchase and read the court file and the conditions of sale with your lawyer before offering. (Section 3.)
- **Q: What taxes do I pay on a court-ordered purchase — is PTT or GST different?** UNCONFIRMED as a court-sale position in this research, and kept distinct from the CRA GI-003 vacant-land position: neither the general PTT schedule nor GI-003 is presented here as the treatment of a court sale. Confirm both with your lawyer on the specific file before pricing the offer. (Section 3.)
- **Q: Can the owner still save the property after I have found it listed?** Yes, potentially: the owner may redeem by paying what is due plus interest to the date of payment (Law and Equity Act, s. 19) within the redemption period the court fixed, and a final order of foreclosure can be granted only in default of that payment (Rule 21-7 (6)) — while the person with conduct of sale may apply to confirm a sale even before the redemption period expires (Rule 21-7 (9)). Ask which stage the file is at before spending on inspections. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol2/consol2/96253_01
