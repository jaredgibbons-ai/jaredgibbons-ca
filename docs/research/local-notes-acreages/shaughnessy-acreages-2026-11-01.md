## 2. THE ACREAGE ITSELF

**No acreage market in Shaughnessy — stated plainly at the top: Shaughnessy is built-out estate ground, not acreage ground. Its large lots are urban fee-simple estate lots inside a heritage-conservation framework — among the largest standard residential lots in the city by subdivision minimum, but they are house lots, not acreage parcels, and no acreage product trades here. There is NO ALR ground documented in this slug's research, NO named acreage subdivision, NO bare-land strata acreage community, NO farm parcel market and NO presale acreage offering verified on any source (named presale projects: UNVERIFIED in the research pass; the sub-area is essentially fully built out as estate residential). Verified named acreage developments counted here: 0.**

- **The estate lot stock, as the City's own instruments state it.** The City's Subdivision By-law, Schedule A, sets First Shaughnessy's minimum parcel width at 100 ft (30.48 m) and minimum parcel area at 13,000 sq ft (1,207.7 m²) — which is why the district's estate lots are among the largest standard residential lots in the city. Build size is capped by the First Shaughnessy District Schedule: maximum floor space ratio 0.45 where a protected heritage property is conserved, otherwise 0.25 plus 139 m² to a maximum floor area of 911 m² — so even a very large lot cannot carry an unlimited-size new house. These are zoning and subdivision minimums/caps for urban estate lots, not a parcel roster. https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/subdiv/scheda.pdf and http://former.vancouver.ca/commsvcs/BYLAWS/zoning/fsd.pdf
- **Strata vs freehold:** almost entirely freehold detached — Edwardian and interwar mansions in First Shaughnessy, mid-century and modern estates further south, many on 100 ft+ frontages with mature landscaping; strata product is minimal (a small number of townhouse/condo projects at the edges, e.g., along Granville and Oak). There is accordingly no bare-land strata acreage here to document or count, in this file or any sibling. (Research file §5.)
- **Heritage retention is the lot's value question, not division.** First Shaughnessy's protected heritage properties (pre-1940) cannot simply be demolished; value often sits in retention-plus-infill/coach-house schemes permitted by the HCA — the framework gives owners of protected homes additional development options including coach house, multiple conversion dwelling and infill buildings as incentives to conserve the heritage house. A protected property can only be removed from the protected list through a Council process following a heritage character and value assessment. https://guidelines.vancouver.ca/bulletins/bulletin-first-shaughnessy-heritage-conservation-area-procedure-for-review-of-protected-heritage.pdf and http://council.vancouver.ca/20150721/documents/phea-QandA.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can I buy an acreage in Shaughnessy?** No — there is no acreage market here (Section 2). What exists is fee-simple estate houses on large urban lots, with minimal strata product at the edges counted in the condos and townhomes home files. Size alone does not make an estate lot an acreage.
- **Q: What is First Shaughnessy, and how is it different from Shaughnessy generally?** First Shaughnessy is the northern section, designated a Heritage Conservation Area on September 29, 2015 (By-law No. 11349), bounded by West 16th/Marpole/Wolfe/Richelieu to the north, Arbutus Street to the west, West King Edward Avenue to the south and the west lane of Oak Street to the east. The wider sub-area continues south of King Edward without HCA status. https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/vancouvers-heritage-areas.aspx
- **Q: Can a protected Shaughnessy heritage house be demolished?** Not as of right. A listed property's status is reviewed through a heritage character and value assessment, and removal from the protected list is a legislative act requiring Council approval at a public hearing. Establish protected-heritage status before anything else — it changes both value and marketing. https://guidelines.vancouver.ca/bulletins/bulletin-first-shaughnessy-heritage-conservation-area-procedure-for-review-of-protected-heritage.pdf
- **Q: What development options do protected-home owners get?** The HCA zoning provides incentives for conserving pre-1940 homes, including coach house, multiple conversion dwelling and infill building options — price a protected home with those incentives in view. http://council.vancouver.ca/20150721/documents/phea-QandA.pdf
- **Q: Can I split a Shaughnessy estate lot?** Only if the resulting parcels meet First Shaughnessy's subdivision minimums — 100 ft (30.48 m) minimum parcel width and 13,000 sq ft (1,207.7 m²) minimum parcel area — through the City's process under Subdivision By-law No. 5208 (fees by No. 14538, VERIFIED), and, in the HCA, only alongside the district schedule's floor-space caps (0.45 FSR with a conserved protected heritage property; otherwise 0.25 plus 139 m² to a 911 m² maximum). https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/subdiv/scheda.pdf and http://former.vancouver.ca/commsvcs/BYLAWS/zoning/fsd.pdf
