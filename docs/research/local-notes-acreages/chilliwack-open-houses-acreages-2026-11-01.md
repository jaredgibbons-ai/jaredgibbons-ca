## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The open-house rule itself — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3), verbatim, sourcing stated:** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes, provides that a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate. That is the text as set out in that report (http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download, read 2026-11-01) — quoted here at exactly that sourcing level, not dressed up as a fresh pull of the current Rule text. (Research file: ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/batch-6/chilliwack-open-houses.md, §4)
- **Representation at an open house:** a licensed real estate professional in BC must work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party to the transaction. BCFSA also states professionals cannot represent two or more clients in the same transaction whose interests are in conflict, except in the rarest of circumstances — dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018, so the host cannot simply "also represent" a buyer who walks in. In practice: chatting factually with the host does not by itself make you their client — but once you start sharing your motivation, budget or needs with a licensee who represents the seller, the disclosure rules engage. For an acreage open house this matters more, not less: parcel value turns on motivation-sensitive facts (development intent, farm plans, holding horizon) that a visitor should save for their own agent. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Identification or sign-in for visitors:** an identification or sign-in requirement for open house visitors is NOT stated in any BC Gov or BCFSA consumer source found — UNVERIFIED as a rule; a host may still ask, as a matter of the seller's instructions, but that practice is not in an official source. (Research file above, §4.)
- **Chilliwack plan and zoning frame — where this topic's research grounds it:** the Chilliwack 2050 Official Community Plan, Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 5500, presents the long-term vision and growth strategy for the whole city, including growth strategies for residential (urban, rural and hillside), commercial, industrial and institutional uses and the protection of the Agricultural Land Reserve. Development permit areas that can matter to a hillside or farm-edge visit sit in OCP Schedule D, including D-1 Drinking Water Supply, D-2 Hillside Environment, D-3 Riparian Area, D-4 Farm Home Plate and D-5 Hillside Development. Zoning Bylaw No. 5000 sets the urban residential zones, including R1-A (Urban Residential) with a minimum lot area of 500 m² on community water and sewer and a minimum lot width of 15 m on the same services, and R1-C (Urban Infill) with a minimum lot area of 300 m² on an interior lot and 360 m² on a corner lot. https://chilliwack.com/ocp and https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf

- **ALR — the Chilliwack acreage check an open house cannot answer:** the City states land in Chilliwack is in the Agricultural Land Reserve and the Agricultural Area Plan (OCP Schedule B1) covers that ALR land; no open-house card states ALR status. Parcel-level ALR status is a check through the Agricultural Land Commission, and if a parcel is in the ALR: subdivision requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)); residence caps are one principal residence to 500 m², a secondary suite ONLY inside the principal residence, and an additional residence capped at 90 m² (parcels of 40 ha or less) or 186 m² (over 40 ha). https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/2387/SB1_Agricultural_Area_Plan_1.pdf and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

- **Property transfer tax (PTT) on land bought after a visit:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example, inputs stated: a $900,000 fully residential acreage purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $700,000 ($14,000) = **$16,000**. - **GST on vacant land — ONLY at the CRA GI-003 level:** CRA Info Sheet GI-003 states most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (personal-use land is the example), while an individual's sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts. Farmland caution: the CRA treats farmland sales in a separate sheet as usually taxable — never assume a farm parcel is GST-exempt on the vacant-land rule alone; parcel-level treatment is UNCONFIRMED, and anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
