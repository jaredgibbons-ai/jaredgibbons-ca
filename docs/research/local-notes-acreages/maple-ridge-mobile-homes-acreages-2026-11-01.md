## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The governing statute for the dominant form is tenancy law, not land law:** manufactured home site tenancies are covered by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA); landlords and tenants must have a written tenancy agreement for the site. Maple Ridge zoning for parks is Zoning Bylaw No. 7600-2019, Zone RMH (Manufactured Home Park Residential) at s. 626; the RMH zone's own rules are UNVERIFIED in this research (consolidated bylaw text could not be opened, fetch 429). http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www.mapleridge.ca/sites/default/files/2024-01/Zoning%20bylaw%20report.pdf
- **Assignment on a sale in a park — the consent that replaces a land transfer:** assignment of a manufactured home site tenancy agreement permanently transfers the pad tenancy to the buyer when the home is sold and remains in the park; under s. 28 of the MHPTA a tenant may assign only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an arbitrator's order, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it; before asking, the seller must give the proposed buyer written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Pad rent increases — Act percentages only, never a dollar figure:** landlords in manufactured home parks can only increase rent with 3 full months' notice, can increase rent every 12 months, and the increase cannot exceed the yearly rent increase limit — **2026 = 2.3% + proportional; 2027 = 2.2% + proportional** — where the proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park; a landlord may also apply to the Residential Tenancy Branch for an additional increase for expenses or capital expenditures. No pad-rent dollar figure for any Maple Ridge park is published as fact in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Registration and moving — the registry rules:** the buyer and seller file the original signed and witnessed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership forms through a qualified supplier (a lawyer or notary); the Province directs buyers to make sure the CSA certification label is attached to the home at the point of sale. A permit to move a manufactured home is issued by the Manufactured Home Registry, a tax certificate for transport purposes is required (with exceptions including a move to a different pad within the same park), and the transport permit expires 30 days after issue. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Taxes on a home in a park:** the Province states a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value will not include the value of the land if the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. Maple Ridge property taxes for 2026 were due July 2, 2026; a 5% penalty applies to any outstanding balance on July 3, 2026 and an additional 5% on September 2, 2026 — the city cycle that applies to owned-land parcels in this topic. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home and https://mapleridge.ca/2329/Due-Dates-and-Penalties-Late-Payments
- **PTT — with the pad-tenure caution stated plainly:** the standard PTT tiers are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is a Maple Ridge park home an acreage purchase?** No. In a manufactured home park the buyer owns the manufactured home and rents the site it sits on, under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement governed by the MHPTA. No lot, no strata interest and no fee-simple land transfers — it is not lot stock, not land ownership and not acreage, whatever the park's total size. (§2; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf)
- **Q: Do I own the land under a park home?** Generally no: the Province describes park tenancies as owning the home and renting the site, and the home's assessed value for tax purposes does not include the land value in a park. The owned-land exception in this topic is a manufactured / modular home on its own fee-simple lot — a different transaction, documented as a form only in §2. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **Q: Can the park raise my pad rent when I buy?** No — on assignment of the site tenancy the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, and the scheduled date for a subsequent rent increase does not change. Increases otherwise require 3 full months' notice, at most once every 12 months, within the yearly limit (2026 = 2.3%; 2027 = 2.2%) plus the proportional levy / utility amount. No dollar pad rent for any park is stated as fact in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: What consent do I need to buy a home that stays in its park?** The landlord's prior written consent to assign the site tenancy under MHPTA s. 28 (or consent deemed given under the regulations, an arbitrator's order, or authorization in the tenancy agreement itself). Before asking, the seller must give you written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement — build the consent timeline into subjects. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Q: What is the difference between a mobile home and a modular home in BC?** Both are manufactured homes built off-site. The Province's registry distinguishes them by CSA standard: mobile homes are built under the Z-240 standards and modular homes under the A-277 standards, and the CSA label on the home states which applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: What if the home has lost its CSA label?** A manufactured home may not be sold unless it has the original and legible CSA decal attached. If the label is removed or lost, the home must be re-certified: Technical Safety BC arranges a licensed electrical contractor's inspection, and the electrical approval label prepared can be used in place of a CSA label to list the home for sale. Check the label before anything else. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Q: How does the purchase actually close?** Through the registry, not just the contract: the purchase is only effective once registered, through a lawyer or notary filing the Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership with the Manufactured Home Registry. A move needs a Registry transport permit (30-day expiry) and a tax certificate, with exceptions including a move to a different pad within the same park. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry

If one were offered, the BC strata block in §3 would govern the lot and the Form B would be the diligence document; a rental park has neither. (§2.)

No park's pad-rent dollar figure is confirmed in this file, so budget from the written tenancy agreement and park rules for the specific home, obtained before subject removal — never from a page range. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The transfer that matters in a park is invisible in the listing.** A park-home sale stands or falls on two documents a listing never shows: the MHPTA s. 28 assignment consent from the park landlord, and the Manufactured Home Registry registration that alone makes the sale effective. A buyer who subjects only on financing and inspection has missed the two steps that can actually stop the deal. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Assignment freezes the pad rent — a protection listings rarely explain.** On assignment the new tenant's pad rent stays the same and the next scheduled increase date does not reset. A park office or listing suggesting a transfer triggers a new "market" pad rent is contradicting the Province's assignment sheet. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **The CSA label is a value gate with no workaround at listing time.** Without the original legible decal (Z-240 mobile / A-277 modular) the home cannot be sold at all until Technical Safety BC re-certification — so two physically identical homes in the same park can differ by a whole re-certification process, and the label check costs nothing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
