ALR residences cannot be stratified without the ALC's permission — depositing a strata plan is a subdivision, and a person must not subdivide agricultural land unless permitted by the Commission or under the ALR General Regulation (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). This is a permission rule, not an absolute ban, and ALC Information Bulletin 05 contains no stratification statement and is not cited for it. Residence caps as the ALC states them: land in the ALR may generally have no more than one residence per parcel unless the parcel qualifies for an additional residence, and local governments may further regulate or restrict residences through zoning; a principal residence must have a total floor area of 500 m² or less, and no more than 1,000 m² of fill (including structural aggregate for foundations and driveways) can be placed for its construction; a secondary suite is permitted ONLY when located within the principal residence, entirely contained within it; one additional residence, detached from the principal residence, is permitted up to 90 m² on properties of 40 ha or less and up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha, and building more than the permitted number, or larger than the size limits, requires ALC approval. City zoning and the ALC frame both apply; neither displaces the other. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
