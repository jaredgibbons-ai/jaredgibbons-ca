## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The FTHB GST/HST rebate — verified figures in full (CRA):** Bill C-4, which introduced the rebate, received Royal Assent on **March 13, 2026** per CRA News No. 122 (CRA governs; this slug's research states March 13 only — no March 12 figure is documented in this slug's research, so none is carried or overwritten here). Together with the GST/HST new housing rebate it provides **100% of the GST** (or federal part of the HST) on a newly constructed or substantially renovated home valued **up to $1 million**, is **gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million**, and is **not available at $1.5 million or more** — a maximum of up to **$50,000**. Eligibility turns on a builder agreement entered into on or after **March 20, 2025 and before 2031** (owner-built construction beginning on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031), construction begun before 2031 and substantially completed before 2036, and the buyer being at least 18, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and a first-time buyer under the four-calendar-year look-back; claiming is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction, by builder credit at closing or directly to the CRA (Form GST190; Form GST191 for owner-built homes). Any straight-line formula for the reduction is UNVERIFIED — CRA says only "gradually reduced." The rebate is new HOUSING only, not bare land (§2). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **The existing GST New Housing Rebate:** generally **36% of the GST paid**, to a maximum rebate of **$6,300**, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between **$350,000 and $450,000**, and not available at $450,000 or more. Housing-only. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **CMHC premium range — the verified frame, and this slug's owned conflict:** mortgage loan insurance is required when a down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price; the minimum down payment is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more (where insurance is not available); **CMHC's published premium range is 0.6% to 4.5% of the total mortgage amount**, and the premium can be paid in full or added to the mortgage. The live page's range (2.80%–4.00%, plus a 0.20% surcharge over 25 years) conflicts with that published range and is UNVERIFIED (§5); the page's insured-mortgage price-cap date (under $1.5 million since December 15, 2024), its 30-year amortization availability, and its "CMHC Home Start" program name were likewise not re-opened on CMHC or Finance Canada pages: UNVERIFIED as stated. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://jaredgibbons.ca/first-time-home-buyer-program.html
- **BC property transfer tax and the first-time buyers' program (Province, verified):** a qualifying buyer (Canadian citizen or permanent resident; lived in BC at least a year immediately before registration or filed at least 2 income tax returns as a BC resident in the last 6 taxation years; never owned a registered interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world; never received the exemption or refund before) is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price where the property is used only as a principal residence, has a fair market value of **$835,000 or less** (effective April 1, 2024), is **0.5 hectares or smaller** and contains only residential improvements; a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000; the buyer must move in within 92 days of registration and occupy the home as a principal residence to the first anniversary of registration to keep the full exemption. Note for acreage buyers: the 0.5-hectare ceiling means most acreage parcels sit OUTSIDE this PTT exemption on size alone — stated from the verified rule, per parcel. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Newly built home exemption (Province, verified):** effective April 1, 2024, the fair market value threshold for a full exemption is $1,100,000, with a partial exemption phasing out over the $50,000 range above it and complete elimination at $1,150,000 for qualifying purchasers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — worked anchors with inputs + tier math:** PTT is 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Anchors (fully residential, no exemption, unless stated): $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 fully-qualifying first-time buyer); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M→$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M (math: $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000 = $93,000) — follow $93,000; a $78,000 figure for $3.5M appears ONLY in the south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m TOWNHOMES sibling and is flagged wherever met, never followed. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Does the first-time buyer GST rebate apply to bare land or an acreage purchase?** No — stated plainly. The rebate applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself. Buying the lot gets no FTHB rebate on the land; building or buying the new home is what the rebate attaches to. (§2.) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: How much GST can a first-time buyer get back on a new home in BC?** Up to $50,000: together with the new housing rebate, the FTHB rebate returns 100% of the GST on a new or substantially renovated home valued up to $1 million, is gradually reduced between $1 million and $1.5 million, and is not available at $1.5 million or more, for builder agreements entered into on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. (§3.) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: How much property transfer tax does a first-time buyer in BC actually save?** A qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price where the property's fair market value is $835,000 or less, it is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the buyer's principal residence; above $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. Most acreage parcels exceed the 0.5-hectare ceiling. (§3.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: What happened to the old GST new housing rebate?** It still exists on its own terms: generally 36% of the GST paid, to a maximum of $6,300, gradually reduced for homes priced or valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and unavailable at $450,000 or more. (§3.) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?** Yes — the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal; FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000. The HBP dollar figures on the live page remain UNVERIFIED (CRA HBP page returned HTTP 403 in the sibling passes). (§3.) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Q: Is a newly built home treated differently from a resale home?** Yes: the newly built home exemption has a full-exemption PTT threshold of $1,100,000 with a phase-out to $1,150,000, and a new home may also attract GST, which the federal FTHB rebate can offset by up to $50,000 for eligible buyers whose builder agreement dates from on or after March 20, 2025. (§3.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q: What down payment do I actually need?** Under 20% down requires mortgage loan insurance: the minimum is 5% for homes $500,000 or less, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000 up to $1,499,999, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more with no insurance available; CMHC's published premium range is 0.6%–4.5% of the total mortgage amount — not the page's 2.80%–4.00% range (§5). (§3.) https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: The page's worked example shows a $26,200 premium on a $700,000 townhouse — can I rely on it?** Only as page content: the example's PTT legs ($4,000 with the exemption versus $12,000 without) follow the verified rates and exemption, but the premium leg uses the page's own 2.80%–4.00% range, which conflicts with CMHC's published 0.6%–4.5% range and is UNVERIFIED — price insurance from the CMHC range and a lender quote, not from the example. https://jaredgibbons.ca/first-time-home-buyer-program.html
- **Q: How do I keep the PTT exemption after closing?** Move in within 92 days of registration and occupy the home as your principal residence to the first anniversary of registration, or part of the exemption is repayable. (§3.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Is GST even payable when I buy vacant land?** At the CRA GI-003 level carried here: most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (personal-use land is the example), but a sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts — and developer/business sales are generally taxable. Anything finer is UNCONFIRMED. (§3.) https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
