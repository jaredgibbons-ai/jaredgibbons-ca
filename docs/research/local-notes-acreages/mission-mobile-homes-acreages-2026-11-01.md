## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The governing tenancy law (the rule that replaces land rules on pad ground):** manufactured home park tenancies in BC are governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and its Regulation, administered through the Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB). In a manufactured home park, the tenant typically owns the manufactured home and rents the site (the pad) it sits on — the structure behind every pad-rent card on this page's feed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Pad rent increases — MHPTA percentages ONLY:** landlords in manufactured home parks can only increase rent with 3 full months' notice, can increase rent only once every 12 months, and the increase cannot exceed the yearly rent increase limit — for manufactured home site tenancies the limit is **2.3% for 2026 and 2.2% for 2027, plus a proportional amount** for changes in local government levies and regulated utility fees (the change in levies plus the change in regulated park utilities, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park); a landlord may apply to the RTB for an additional increase above the limit for increased operating or financing costs or for substantial and essential repairs or renovations. No dollar pad figure is attached to this rule anywhere in this file (§2). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Selling a home that stays in the park — assignment (MHPTA s.28):** a tenant may assign the site tenancy agreement to the buyer only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed to have been obtained under the Regulation), or with an order of the director authorizing the assignment, or where the tenancy agreement itself authorizes it; the landlord must not charge anything for considering, investigating or consenting to the assignment (s.28(3)); before requesting consent, the seller must provide the proposed buyer with written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; on assignment, the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date for a subsequent rent increase; a tenant who believes consent is being unreasonably refused can apply for RTB dispute resolution. Landlords are required to give tenants a written copy of the manufactured home park rules (if there are any) before signing the tenancy agreement. Note on sourcing: the RTB assignment information sheet was seen via search-result text in the research pass, not as a full read — carried with that sourcing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s.42, as stated on the Province's ending-a-tenancy page):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute the notice within 15 days, may in defined circumstances (home cannot be moved, no site within a reasonable distance, no tax owing on the home) apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What am I actually buying with a Mission manufactured home in a park?** In the usual park structure, you buy the manufactured home itself and rent the site (pad) it sits on under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act — which is why the pad rent, the park rules and the assignment process matter as much as the home's price. It is not land and not acreage. (§3.)
- **Q: How much can my pad rent go up?** Once every 12 months at most, on 3 full months' notice, and by no more than the yearly limit — 2.3% for 2026 and 2.2% for 2027 for manufactured home site tenancies — plus a proportional amount covering the park's change in local government levies and regulated utility fees divided across its sites; anything above that requires an RTB order. No dollar pad figure is stated as fact in this file — the feed's card figures are UNVERIFIED site copy (§2). (§3.)
- **Q: Can the park refuse my buyer when I sell?** The sale works by assigning your site tenancy agreement, which under s.28 of the MHPTA requires the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the Regulation), an order of the director, or authorization in the tenancy agreement itself; the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent; you must give the proposed buyer the park rules and written tenancy terms before requesting consent, the pad rent carries over unchanged on assignment, and an unreasonable refusal can be taken to RTB dispute resolution. (§3.)
- **Q: What happens if the park closes or converts?** A park conversion needs all permits in place first, then 12 months' notice and $20,000 compensation per MHPTA s.42, with defined additional-compensation routes (including, in defined circumstances, the home's assessed value minus the $20,000) and a further payment if the landlord does not proceed within a reasonable period. Confirm the park's ownership and any known redevelopment signals as part of due diligence. (§3.)
- **Q: What is the median price of a manufactured home in Mission?** SnapStats publishes no manufactured-home median — its Mission splits are Detached (median $814,000, September 2026) and Condos & Townhomes combined ($430,000), covering all dwellings; the live feed's first-page home prices are a listing snapshot, not a median.
