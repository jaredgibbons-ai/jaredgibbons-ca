## 2. THE ACREAGE ITSELF

**This topic file carries no acreage roster of its own — stated plainly at the top. There are no named acreage subdivisions, acreage developments, bare-land strata acreage communities, assemblies, development sites or presale acreage offerings to document for a province-wide calculator slug, and none is invented. Verified named acreage developments counted here: 0, because a topic file counts no stock at all. What this section documents instead is how an acreage or large-lot first purchase prices through the tool the page offers: the size line both BC exemption programs draw, the purchase shapes a first-time buyer can run, and which of them the exemptions and rebates actually reach. Acreage offerings themselves, where they exist, are documented in their home area files only (document-once) and are cross-referenced from here by program, never re-documented.**

- **The 0.5-hectare line — the acreage fact this tool must encode:** both BC PTT exemption programs set 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) as the size line, with a partial exemption possible above it — acreage and large-lot first purchases price differently in the tool than strata or small-lot purchases. For the first-time home buyers' program, a property larger than 0.5 hectares is one of the documented partial-exemption triggers; for the newly built home exemption, 0.5 hectares or smaller is a full-exemption condition. A buyer pricing a true acreage parcel (over 0.5 ha) through this calculator must therefore not be shown a full exemption the programs do not give. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Resale homes (including resale acreage with a house):** PTT with the first-time home buyers' program exemption where it applies — the general tiers run first, then the exemption reduces the bill (figures and worked examples in Section 3). The exemption thresholds shape which stock the tool's savings are largest on: the first-time program's full exemption runs to a fair market value of $835,000, while the newly built home exemption's full threshold is $1,100,000 — a first-time buyer can be inside one program's range and outside the other's, so the tool must test both. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Newly built homes (including a new house on acreage):** PTT with the newly built home exemption, and GST with the federal rebates where they apply. A newly built home includes a house constructed and affixed on vacant land that has not been occupied since construction, a unit in a newly built condo building not occupied since construction, a manufactured home placed and affixed on vacant land not occupied since placement, and a building converted from non-residential to residential use not occupied as a residence since the conversion began. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Vacant land bought to build on:** both BC exemption programs carry a refund route — a person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may be eligible for a refund of the tax paid under the newly built home exemption. The land purchase itself is the taxable event the tool must not skip: PTT is based on the fair market value of the property (land and improvements) on registration day, and GST on the land itself is governed only at the CRA GI-003 level in Section 3. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **ALR acreage — no roster, one rule boundary:** a first purchase on Agricultural Land Reserve ground is priced by the same PTT tiers, but the land itself carries the ALR regime in Section 3 (no stratification of ALR residences, ALC approval for subdivision, residence caps, farm-class test) — the calculator does not encode ALR status, and no ALR parcel, hobby farm or working farm is documented in this topic file. Farm operations are described in process terms only in this program; a generic feed is never a confirmed operation, and no marketing-board source was opened in this file's research, so quota/board items stay UNVERIFIED. (Research file above.)
- **Bare-land strata / fee-simple distinction, for land run through the tool:** a bare-land strata lot IS strata tenure (Form B / strata fees / bylaws apply — Section 3 strata block), while a fee-simple lot carries no strata layer; the calculator's tax figures do not change with that distinction, but its closing-cost picture does, and no specific lot of either form is documented in this topic file. Bare-land strata acreage communities are documented ONCE in their lots-and-land home files and are cross-referenced from the area acreages files, never re-documented here.
- **Pad ground is NOT land, NOT acreage:** a manufactured home on a rented pad is not lot stock, not land and not acreage, and a pad purchase is not an acreage purchase for this tool — the newly built home exemption's manufactured-home limb covers a manufactured home placed and affixed on vacant land, which is a different tenure from a rented pad. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **Property transfer tax — the base calculation:** when a person purchases or gains an interest in property registered at the Land Title Office, the person or their legal professional must file a PTT return and pay PTT unless an exemption applies; in most cases property transfers are completed by a legal professional. PTT is based on the fair market value of the property (land and improvements) on the day it is registered with the Land Title Office, and it should not be confused with annual property taxes. General rates: 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000 where that applies (on a mixed-class property, only on the residential portion — on a mixed farm parcel the allocation must be worked per parcel). Additional PTT for foreign nationals, foreign corporations and taxable trustees applies on the residential portion within a specified area of BC; its rate was not re-read for the research file and is UNVERIFIED here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **PTT worked examples — inputs stated, tier math shown (all treated as fully residential, no exemption unless stated):** $500,000 → 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $300,000 ($6,000) = **$8,000**. $700,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $500,000 ($10,000) = **$12,000**. $800,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $600,000 ($12,000) = **$14,000**; for a fully qualifying first-time buyer at the $800,000 level the first-time program's exemption on the first $500,000 reduces the bill to **$6,000** (the $800K cap pair: $14,000 / $6,000 FTHB). $900,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $700,000 ($14,000) = **$16,000**. $1,000,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $800,000 ($16,000) = **$18,000**. $1,200,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $1,000,000 ($20,000) = **$22,000**. $1,500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $1,300,000 ($26,000) = **$28,000**. $1,800,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $1,600,000 ($32,000) = **$34,000**. $2,000,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $1,800,000 ($36,000) = **$38,000**. $3,500,000 → $2,000 + 2% × $1,800,000 ($36,000) + 3% × $1,500,000 ($45,000) + further 2% × $500,000 residential portion over $3M ($10,000) = **$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** — a townhomes sibling elsewhere in the program carries $78,000 for $3.5M; that figure is an OPEN summary-pass reconciliation item: follow $93,000, flag the conflict wherever met. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **BC first-time home buyers' program (PTT exemption):** the buyer must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident; must have lived in BC for at least a year immediately before registration, or filed at least 2 income tax returns as a BC resident in the last 6 taxation years before registration; must never have owned a registered interest in a property that was the buyer's principal residence anywhere in the world; and must never have received a first time home buyers' exemption or refund. Foreign entities and taxable trustees are not eligible, and where only some purchasers qualify, only the qualifying purchasers' percentage of interest is eligible. Full exemption conditions: the property must only be used as the buyer's principal residence; must have a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024); must be 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) or smaller; and must contain only residential improvements — the purchaser is then exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price (maximum saving $8,000). Partial exemption: a partial exemption may apply where the property has a fair market value more than $835,000 but less than $860,000 (effective April 1, 2024), is larger than 0.5 hectares, and/or has another building on the property other than the principal residence. Keeping the exemption: the buyer must move into the home within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy it as a principal residence up to the first anniversary of the registration date (part may be kept if the buyer moves out early); the buyer's legal professional applies the exemption in the PTT return, and a buyer who qualified but did not apply at registration may apply for a refund from the first anniversary to 18 months after registration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **BC newly built home exemption (PTT):** effective April 1, 2024, the fair market value threshold for a full exemption is $1,100,000, and a partial exemption phases out over the $50,000 range above the threshold, with complete elimination of the exemption at $1,150,000 for qualifying purchasers. Qualification also requires the buyer to be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, the property to be in BC, used only as the buyer's principal residence, and 0.5 hectares or smaller; occupancy requirements mirror the first-time program (move in within 92 days of registration, occupy for the remainder of the first year), and a person who paid PTT on vacant land and then has a newly built home on it may be eligible for a refund of the tax paid. This is a new-HOME exemption — bare-land eligibility beyond that documented refund route is UNCONFIRMED. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **ALR RULES IN FULL (for a first purchase on ALR land — the acreage regime this topic must not misprice):** subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval; ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)); residence caps are one principal residence to 500 m², a suite inside the principal residence only, and an additional residence of 90 m² (40 ha or less) or 186 m² (over 40 ha). BC Assessment farm class runs on the three-tier test ($2,500 on 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of value over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha — applications by October 31) — a flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR wherever met; that line is documented on the HORSE-PROPERTIES page and is never attributed to this file. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

SSMUH/subdivision potential for a specific parcel is stated as the buyer's own city states it, in that parcel's area file. (Standing servicing ledger; research file above.)
- **GST on vacant land — ONLY at the CRA GI-003 level:** CRA Info Sheet GI-003 states most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (personal-use land is the example), while an individual's sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts. Anything finer than GI-003 level is UNCONFIRMED in this file; the GST treatment of a specific parcel is UNCONFIRMED until vendor status and use are applied, and the contract must state the position. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **First-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate — new HOUSING, not bare land (CRA-verified figures in full):** Bill C-4 received Royal Assent **March 13, 2026** (CRA News No. 122) — a source stating March 12 is noted in-line wherever met, never silently overwritten (no March 12 source is met in this file's research). The rebate is **100% of the GST (or federal part of the HST) to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, maximum $50,000**, for agreements entered **March 20, 2025–2031**; the application time limit is usually within two years of taking ownership or finishing construction, a builder can credit the rebate against the amount payable when ownership transfers, and where the builder does not credit it the buyer applies directly to the CRA through a CRA account or by filing Form GST190 (houses purchased from a builder) or Form GST191 (owner-built houses). It applies to newly constructed or substantially renovated HOUSING — a bare-land purchase itself attracts no FTHB rebate. A **$1.25M straight-line phase-out row is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism** — CRA's published wording is that the rebate is gradually reduced across its phase range; no straight-line $1.25M mechanism is asserted in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2026/first-time-buyers-can-save-more-on-new-homes-the-first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate-is-available-now.html
- **GST/HST new housing rebate (CRA-verified figures in full):** **36% of the GST (or federal part of the HST), maximum $6,300, on homes $350,000–$450,000** (phase-out range), helping a buyer recover some of the GST paid on a new or substantially renovated house where all requirements are met; CRA guidance adds that the individual or a related person must use the housing as a primary place of residence and be the first individual to live in the new or substantially renovated housing. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **FHSA (CRA-verified figures in full):** a registered plan that allows a first-time home buyer to save to buy or build a qualifying first home tax-free, up to certain limits — **$40,000 lifetime limit, $8,000 annual participation room** (FHSA participation room in the first year the account is opened is $8,000); contributions to an FHSA are generally deductible and can be used to reduce tax; transfers from RRSPs to FHSAs are not deductible. The research file's own pass could not re-read the lifetime limit on the CRA's detailed pages and left it UNVERIFIED there; the figure stated here is the program's governed CRA figure, carried per the FILE STATUS flag. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Home Buyers' Plan (CRA-verified figures in full):** the HBP allows a withdrawal from an RRSP to buy or build a qualifying home — **up to $60,000** — with the withdrawn amount to be repaid to the plan within the published repayment period, plus the **2026–2028 repayment-relief deferral** as the program states it for the relief window. The CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and also withdraw under the HBP for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. The research file's own pass could not re-read the HBP limit on the CRA's HBP page and left it UNVERIFIED there; the figure stated here is the program's governed CRA figure, carried per the FILE STATUS flag. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Down payment and mortgage loan insurance (CMHC — published figures only; the calculator-slug error is NOT asserted, per FILE STATUS):** CMHC states mortgage loan insurance is required in Canada when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the home's purchase price, that the insurance protects the lender rather than the buyer, and that it allows a buyer to qualify with a smaller down payment, as low as 5%. - **Home owner grant (annual, not a purchase tax):** the home owner grant reduces the amount of property taxes paid each year on a principal residence; it is an annual cost item a first-time buyer meets after purchase, not a closing cost, and a calculator should not net it against purchase taxes. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much property transfer tax does a first-time buyer in BC actually save?** A qualifying buyer is exempt from PTT on the first $500,000 of the purchase price, provided the property's fair market value is $835,000 or less, it is 0.5 hectares or smaller, contains only residential improvements and is used only as the buyer's principal residence; above $835,000 and under $860,000 only a partial exemption applies. Worked pair at $800,000, fully residential: $14,000 with no exemption, $6,000 for a fully qualifying first-time buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: What are the general PTT rates the calculator starts from?** 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000, 2% on the portion over $200,000 up to and including $2,000,000, and 3% on the portion over $2,000,000 — plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000 where that applies. At $3,500,000 fully residential the total is $93,000 including that further tier. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Does buying acreage change my exemption?** Yes — size is an exemption condition, not a detail: both BC programs set 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) as the size line. A property larger than 0.5 hectares is a documented partial-exemption trigger in the first-time program and fails a full-exemption condition in the newly built home program, so an acreage purchase must be run as a partial-exemption case, not a full one. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Is a newly built home treated differently from a resale home?** Yes. The newly built home exemption has a full-exemption threshold of $1,100,000 with a phase-out to $1,150,000, and a new home may also attract GST, which the federal first-time home buyers' GST/HST rebate can offset by up to $50,000 for eligible buyers (100% of GST to $1M, phasing to $1.5M, agreements March 20, 2025–2031). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption and https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **Q: Does the first-time GST rebate apply if I buy bare land or acreage to build on?** No — the rebate applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself. GST on the land purchase is stated only at CRA GI-003 level (most individual personal-use sales exempt; business, business-purpose capital-property and more-than-two-part subdivided sales can be taxable), and the newly built home PTT exemption carries a documented refund route for PTT paid on vacant land once a newly built home is on it. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: How much can I put in a First Home Savings Account?** $40,000 lifetime, with $8,000 of participation room in the first year the account is opened; contributions are generally deductible, and transfers from RRSPs to FHSAs are not deductible. Open the account early — room starts when the account is opened, not at offer time. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account.html
- **Q: Can I use both the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?** Yes — the CRA states a person can make a qualifying FHSA withdrawal and withdraw under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same qualifying home, as long as all conditions are met at the time of each withdrawal. The HBP limit is $60,000, repaid within the published repayment period (with the 2026–2028 repayment-relief deferral). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/campaigns/cra-guide-taxes-housing-benefits.html
- **Q: How much down payment do I actually need?** The floor is 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any portion above $500,000, and 20% at $1,500,000 or more, where insurance is unavailable. Under 20% down, CMHC insurance is required and its premium (published range 0.6%–4.5% of the mortgage amount) is usually added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: What must I do after closing to keep the first-time exemption?** Move in within 92 days of registration and continuously occupy the home as your principal residence up to the first anniversary of the registration date — part of the exemption may be kept if you move out early, but the full amount is protected only by meeting the occupancy rule. Your legal professional claims the exemption in the PTT return; a missed claim can be refunded on application from the first anniversary to 18 months after registration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: Should I count the home owner grant as closing money?** No — it is an annual reduction of property tax on a principal residence ($570 regular grant as published, effective January 1, 2027), claimed each year after you own and occupy; a calculator should not net it against purchase taxes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/home-owner-grant
