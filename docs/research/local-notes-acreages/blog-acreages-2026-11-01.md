## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

The rules below are the primary-source frame for the acreage questions the blog's sampled posts explain. They are carried from the official sources themselves — NOT from the posts' summaries — because the posts themselves direct readers to the government sources before buying, and state they are general information only, not legal or tax advice. Where a post's own statement and a primary source could diverge, the primary source governs and the post is a dated snapshot (its farmland rules are stated accurate as of October 18, 2026). https://jaredgibbons.ca/blog.html

- **ALR RULES IN FULL — the frame the blog's acreage strand explains:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m²; a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. BC Assessment farm-class thresholds, full three-tier form: $2,500 on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the farm operation for farm purposes in excess of 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha — B.C. Reg. 411/95, s.5, applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR wherever stated; it is not documented in this file's research and is not asserted here — the HORSE-PROPERTIES file owns that documented error in this program, and it is never attributed to the blog or to any other file. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf
- **Federal prohibition on purchase by non-Canadians — stated ONLY as this file's sources support, NOT conformed across files:** the farmland post itself covers "the federal prohibition" for Fraser Valley farmland as one of its subjects, citing Justice Laws and CMHC among its government sources, and notes the ban is scheduled to end January 1, 2027 under current law (per the research file's summary of the post). This file opens no primary prohibition text of its own, so the prohibition's application to any specific acreage purchase is UNCONFIRMED at the primary-text level here — the post is a dated explainer (rules accurate as of October 18, 2026), not the statute. The program carries three source positions on this prohibition across its files; this file conforms to none of them beyond what is written in this bullet. https://jaredgibbons.ca/blog.html (research file above, §§1, 4, 6)
- **Additional PTT / speculation and vacancy tax — as the post presents them, labelled post statements:** the farmland post covers BC's 20% additional property transfer tax and the Speculation and Vacancy Tax as part of its foreign-buyer explainer. Those rates and areas are carried here ONLY as the post's own subjects, not re-verified against the provincial pages in this file's pass — for a purchase, the provincial pages govern. Vancouver's Empty Homes Tax (3%) is VANCOUVER ONLY in any event and is never applied to Fraser Valley farm ground. https://jaredgibbons.ca/blog.html
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — the general frame behind the posts' tax content:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million (on farm-classed land used for an owner's/farmer's dwelling, up to 0.5 hectares is treated as residential property for the further-2% calculation, per the Province's PTT page as carried in the surrey-homes-over-1-8m research). Worked example, inputs stated: a $1,200,000 fully residential acreage purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $1,000,000 ($20,000) = **$22,000**. Anchor checks: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 FTHB); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→**$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M** (tier math: $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000) — a townhomes sibling elsewhere in the program carries $78,000 for $3.5M; that figure is an OPEN summary-pass reconciliation item: follow $93,000, flag the conflict wherever met. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — ONLY at CRA GI-003 level:** most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (personal-use land is the example), while an individual's sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts; farmland caution — the CRA treats farmland sales in a separate sheet as usually taxable, so a farm parcel is never assumed GST-exempt on the vacant-land rule alone. Anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED in this file, kept distinct from any court-sale treatment (none is documented for this topic). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **BC strata block — where a reader lands on strata ground from a post (redirect case):** Form B diligence; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2, May 1, 2023 exemptions). No strata purchase is documented in the sampled posts themselves. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **First-home programs — the land boundary:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent MARCH 13, 2026 per CRA) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself — 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; a source stating March 12 is noted in-line where met and never silently overwritten; a live page's $1.25M straight-line phase-out row is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism. The GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) is likewise housing-only. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. CMHC published premium range, for completeness where the siblings carry it: 0.6%–4.5%. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
