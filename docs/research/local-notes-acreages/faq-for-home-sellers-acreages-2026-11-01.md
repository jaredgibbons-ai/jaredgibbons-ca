## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **Selling without a licence — BCFSA:** a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves — for example, by selling or renting out their own property — while a person must not provide real estate services to or on behalf of another, for or in expectation of remuneration, unless licensed or exempt. That supports the page's "not legally required to use a REALTOR®" answer. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information
- **The regulator's consumer framework — BCFSA:** a licensed professional must give consumers information about commissions and fees, inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional representing another party, and only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction; the representation, duties and BCFSA complaint-route disclosures must happen at the outset of dealings. This page states no commission rate or amount. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Cooling-off / deposits — BCFSA (carried from the sibling selling research, as this slug's research records it):** where a buyer rescinds under the Home Buyer Rescission Period Regulation within three business days, the brokerage pays the seller the 0.25% rescission fee directly and returns the remaining deposit balance to the buyer, with no separate release form; otherwise a deposit is held in the brokerage's trust account with the brokerage as stakeholder, and is not automatically returned if subjects are not removed. The live FAQ page does not itself raise the rescission period. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- **PTT is the buyer's tax — Province:** PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value of the property on the day it is registered at the Land Title Office; the page's repeated statement that "property transfer tax is paid by the buyer, not the seller" matches that frame. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — worked anchors with inputs + tier math:** PTT is 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Anchors (fully residential, no exemption, unless stated): $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000 ($6,000 fully-qualifying first-time buyer); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M→$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M (math: $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000 = $93,000) — follow $93,000; a $78,000 figure for $3.5M appears ONLY in the south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m TOWNHOMES sibling and is flagged wherever met, never followed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **ALR RULES IN FULL (carried for the acreage reader — no ALR ground belongs to this topic):** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified WITHOUT THE ALC'S PERMISSION — depositing a strata plan is a subdivision, and a person must not subdivide agricultural land unless permitted by the Commission or under the ALR General Regulation (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). This is a permission rule, not an absolute ban, and ALC Information Bulletin 05 contains no stratification statement and is not cited for it. Residence caps: principal residence ≤500 m², secondary suite ONLY within the principal residence, one additional residence ≤90 m² (≤40 ha) / ≤186 m² (>40 ha). https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure:** $2,500 if the farm operation is 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if greater than 4 ha; and $10,000 if under 0.8 ha — applications by October 31, and a buyer continuing to farm applies by October 31 after purchase. No farm-class figure is documented in this slug's research. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **GST on vacant land — ONLY at CRA GI-003 level (for a seller of land):** most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (personal-use land is the example), while an individual's sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts; developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally taxable. Farmland caution: the CRA treats farmland sales in a SEPARATE info sheet, usually taxable subject to limited exemptions. Anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **First-home programs — the land boundary:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent MARCH 13, 2026 per CRA — CRA governs) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself — 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements Mar 20, 2025–2031. The GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) is likewise housing-only. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year — relevant to an acreage seller only as the buyer's frame on a new home, never as a land rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **BC strata block (for a seller of a bare-land strata lot):** Form B diligence; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since Jul 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since Nov 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since Nov 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2 + May 1, 2023 exemptions). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: I am selling — do I need a REALTOR® at all?** No — BCFSA's licensing guidance confirms a person does not require a licence to provide real estate services to themselves, such as selling their own property; a licence (or an exemption) is required to provide real estate services to or on behalf of another person for remuneration. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/licensing-exemptions-information
- **Q: Why isn't my home selling?** The page's answer: generally one or more of three reasons — it is overpriced relative to comparable recent sales and current competition, it is not marketed well enough, or it does not show well; the fix starts with a home evaluation against recent sales, active listings and expired or terminated listings. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: What are the costs of selling?** The page lists commission (split between the listing and buyer's agents), cleaning, staging and preparation, legal fees and disbursements, and a possible mortgage penalty — and states no commission figure, which BCFSA's framework places in the listing conversation. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html and https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **Q: Do I have to accept an offer?** The page's answer: no — you can accept it as-is, counter, or reject it; a counter voids the previous offer. The page's further "may be required to accept" claim is UNVERIFIED as a legal-effect claim (§3); read your own listing contract's stated terms and take that question to your agent or lawyer. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: Who pays the property transfer tax when I sell?** The buyer: PTT is payable by the purchaser on the fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office — when you buy your next home, you pay it as the buyer. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: How much PTT would a buyer pay on a $700,000 acreage purchase?** $12,000 before any exemption — 1% on the first $200,000 ($2,000) plus 2% on the next $500,000 ($10,000). (§3.) https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **Q: Should I price high and lower later?** The page's answer: no — the first two weeks on market are critical and a reduced listing reads as stale; price from sold evidence at launch. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: Will renovations increase my sale price dollar-for-dollar?** The page's position: renovations rarely return 100% of cost, so get a walkthrough opinion before spending. Its return ranges, attributed on the page to experience and the Appraisal Institute of Canada, are UNVERIFIED — no Institute source was opened in this pass. https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
- **Q: If I sell a vacant acreage lot, does GST apply?** Generally yes if you are a developer/business or the lot is newly subdivided; generally no for an individual's personal-use sale, per CRA GI-003 at the general level only. Finer points UNCONFIRMED. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: How long is a listing contract?** UNVERIFIED as the page states it: the page says Fraser Valley and Greater Vancouver listing contracts are typically 4–6 months with a claimed 60-day minimum under the named board's rules — no board rule page was opened in this pass, and the board name on the page is dated wording (§5). https://jaredgibbons.ca/faq-for-home-sellers.html
