## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The $1.5 million line is also the federal insured-mortgage line:** CMHC states mortgage loan insurance is required in Canada when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the purchase price; the insurance protects the lender, not the buyer, and lets a buyer qualify with a down payment as low as 5%. CMHC's down-payment rules as published: homes $500,000 or less require a minimum 5% down payment; homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999 require 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require a minimum down payment of 20% and mortgage loan insurance is not available. Every home on this page is, by the page's own price filter, on the insurable side of the line — a buyer here can purchase with less than 20% down, paying an insurance premium that CMHC puts at **0.6% to 4.5%** of the total mortgage amount, payable in full at purchase or added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) across this band — worked examples with inputs and tier math shown:** PTT is payable when a person purchases or gains an interest in property registered at the Land Title Office, unless an exemption applies; it is based on the fair market value of the property (land and improvements) on the day of registration, and it is not the same as annual property taxes. General rates: 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. A purchase under $1,500,000 only ever meets the 1% and 2% tiers — the 3% tier starts above $2,000,000, and the further 2% never applies in this band. Anchor checks, each at the standard rates with no exemption unless stated: **$500K→$8,000** (1% × $200,000 = $2,000 + 2% × $300,000 = $6,000); **$700K→$12,000** ($2,000 + 2% × $500,000 = $10,000); **$800K cap→$14,000** ($2,000 + 2% × $600,000 = $12,000; **$6,000 for a fully-qualifying first-time buyer after the $8,000 saving** on the first $500,000); **$900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.1M→$20,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000** ($2,000 + 2% × $1,300,000 = $26,000). Above the band, for completeness: $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; **$3.5M→$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M (tier math: $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000)** — a townhomes sibling in this program (south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m) carries $78,000 for $3.5M; this file follows $93,000, flags the conflict wherever met, and never silently carries $78,000. The "Over $3M — 5%" page row seen on band pages is a compression of 3% + 2% on the residential portion — flagged wherever met, never stated as a single rate. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **The two BC purchase exemptions sit inside this band:** the first-time home buyers' program exempts PTT on the first $500,000 where all conditions are met; a full exemption requires a fair market value of $835,000 or less (effective April 1, 2024), a property of 0.5 hectares or smaller containing only residential improvements and used only as the buyer's principal residence, and a partial exemption may apply between $835,000 and $860,000 — note the 0.5-hectare condition, which most true acreage parcels fail by size alone. The newly built home exemption: full exemption threshold $1,100,000 (effective April 1, 2024), phasing out over the $50,000 above it and completely eliminated at $1,150,000. A buyer above $1,150,000 in this band gets neither. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Additional PTT:** a foreign national, foreign corporation or taxable trustee must also pay additional PTT on the fair market value of the residential portion of a property within a specified area of BC; the rate is published on the Province's separate additional-PTT page and was not re-read for this file, so the rate is UNVERIFIED here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — ONLY at CRA GI-003 level:** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt. An individual's sale can also be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts; farmland caution — the CRA treats farmland sales in a separate sheet as usually taxable, so a farm parcel in this band is never assumed GST-exempt on the vacant-land rule alone. Anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **First-home programs — the land boundary:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent MARCH 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself — 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; a source stating March 12 is noted in-line where met and never silently overwritten; a live page's $1.25M straight-line phase-out row is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism (CRA says only "gradually reduced"). The GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) is likewise housing-only. Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal limit $60,000 per buyer (plus the 2026–2028 repayment-relief deferral carried in this program); First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **ALR RULES IN FULL — the frame for any band parcel a parcel check puts in the reserve (the Langley side of this band is where this bites):** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m²; a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. BC Assessment farm-class thresholds, full three-tier form: $2,500 on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the farm operation for farm purposes in excess of 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha — B.C. Reg. 411/95, s.5, applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR wherever stated; it is asserted ONLY in the horse-properties file in this program, is not documented in this file's research, and is not asserted here. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf

- **Q: How much PTT would I pay on a $1.4 million home in this band?** On the general tiers alone: 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) plus 2% × $1,200,000 ($24,000) = $26,000 — nothing in this band reaches the 3% tier (over $2,000,000) or the further 2% (residential value over $3,000,000); a BC exemption could reduce the bill further if you qualify. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

Most true acreage parcels exceed 0.5 hectares, so most acreage buyers in this band should budget the general tiers in Section 3. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/first-time-home-buyers
- **Q: How much down payment do I need just under the ceiling?** On CMHC's published rules at $1,499,999: 5% × $500,000 ($25,000) plus 10% × $999,999 ($99,999.90) = $124,999.90 minimum, with mortgage insurance required because the down payment is under 20% (premium 0.6%–4.5% of the mortgage amount). - **Q: The listing says the detached home has a suite — is it legal?** The feed shows suites are a recurring feature of this band's detached stock, but legality is municipal: Surrey caps a suite at 40% of the principal dwelling's habitable area, one per unit, never a separate strata lot; the Township of Langley caps a suite at 120 m²; Langley City caps at the lesser of 100 m² and 40%. - **Q: Will GST apply if the under-$1.5M property is vacant land or a farm parcel?** Only at the CRA GI-003 level can this file answer: developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally taxable; an individual's personal-use land is generally exempt; and the CRA treats farmland sales in a separate sheet as usually taxable, so a farm parcel is never assumed exempt on the vacant-land rule alone.
