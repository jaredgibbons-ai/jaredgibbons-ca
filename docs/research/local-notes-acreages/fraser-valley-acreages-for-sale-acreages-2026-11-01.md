## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **No single municipality's rule governs this page — stated first.** Municipal rules differ by parcel location: an acreage in the Township of Langley, the City of Abbotsford, the City of Surrey or the City of Chilliwack sits under a different zoning bylaw and OCP in each case, so no single municipality's lot-size, suite or farm-home-plate rule is stated for this regional page — confirm the rule in the municipality (or electoral area) where the parcel actually sits. The per-jurisdiction positions below are stated AS such and are never blended.
- **ALR RULES IN FULL — the one frame that DOES run valley-wide, wherever ALR ground exists:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified without the ALC's permission — depositing a strata plan is a subdivision, and a person must not subdivide agricultural land unless permitted by the Commission or under the ALR General Regulation (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). This is a permission rule, not an absolute ban, and ALC Information Bulletin 05 contains no stratification statement and is not cited for it. Residence caps as the ALC states them: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m²; a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha; local governments may regulate further but not be more permissive than the ALC's requirements. ALR short-term accommodation limits, relevant to acreage income plans: renting out the entire principal residence for short-term stays is not permitted outright in the ALR and requires ALC approval; an additional residence may be used for short-term accommodation only as agri-tourism accommodation on a property that is actively farmed and holds farm classification under the Assessment Act. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure:** a producing farm must meet minimum gross income at farm gate prices of $2,500 if the total area of the farm operation is between 0.8 ha (1.98 acres) and 4 ha (10 acres); $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if the operation is greater than 4 ha; and $10,000 if the operation is less than 0.8 ha — and new applications for farm classification must be submitted by the legislated deadline of October 31, for consideration for the next assessment and tax year. Farm classification continues only while the thresholds keep being met: BC Assessment states it will remove farm classification if a property no longer meets the requirements or the owner fails to provide supporting information when asked, and a buyer who plans to continue farming a farm-classified property must submit a General Application for Farm Classification by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR, asserted ONLY where a file's own research documents it — owned by the HORSE-PROPERTIES page; it is NOT documented in this topic file's own research and is never attributed to this file. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Servicing ledger — per jurisdiction, never borrowed across a boundary:** Township of Langley Subdivision and Development Servicing Bylaw 5382 (2019) CONFIRMED; Surrey Bylaw 8830 (1986) VERIFIED; Maple Ridge Bylaw 4800-1993 (City source); Mission Development and Subdivision Control Bylaw 5650-2017 (City source, title confirmed). - **Property transfer tax (PTT) on acreage — worked examples with inputs + tier math:** PTT is 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked example at $800,000 (a small non-ALR parcel with a house): $200,000 × 1% = $2,000, plus $600,000 × 2% = $12,000, total $14,000 — the $800K cap anchor ($6,000 after maximum First-Time Home Buyers' relief where it applies to a qualifying home purchase). Worked example at $1.5M (a mid-valley acreage with buildings): $2,000 + $1,300,000 × 2% = $26,000, total $28,000. Worked example at $3.5M fully residential (a large estate parcel): $2,000 + $1,800,000 × 2% = $36,000 (to $2M) = $38,000, plus $1,500,000 × 3% = $45,000 (to $3.5M), plus the further tier $500,000 × 2% = $10,000 (the residential portion over $3M), total **$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M — follow $93,000**; a $78,000 figure for $3.5M appears ONLY in the south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m TOWNHOMES sibling and is flagged wherever met, never followed (open summary-pass item). Full anchor set: $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K→$14,000; $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M→$93,000. Mixed-use and ALR parcels split residential vs non-residential portions on the return — the anchors above are the fully-residential math. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant and farm land — ONLY at CRA GI-003 level:** most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (personal-use land is the example), while an individual's sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts. Farmland is the caution: the CRA addresses sales of farmland by individuals in a SEPARATE info sheet and treats farmland sales as usually taxable subject to limited exemptions — no valley farm parcel is assumed GST-exempt on the "vacant land" rule alone. Worked example at GI-003 level only: an individual selling a personal-use acreage parcel they never used in a business and never subdivided — generally exempt, no GST in the price; the same parcel sold by a developer, or as a newly subdivided lot in the course of a business — generally taxable, GST applies on top of the price and the contract must say who bears it. Anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is there a published Fraser Valley acreage market statistic?** No. SnapStats — the permitted source for this research — publishes no acreage segment: no acreage count, share or median exists for the Fraser Valley or any community in it. Acreages sit inside their own communities' ordinary Detached rows, split only as Detached vs Condos & Townhomes. Never quote an "average acreage price" from this page. (§1.)
- **Q: How many homes can I have on an ALR acreage?** Generally one principal residence (maximum 500 m² total floor area), plus, where the property qualifies, one additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less or 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha, with a secondary suite ONLY inside the principal residence; more than that, or a larger residence, requires ALC approval, and local bylaws may restrict further. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Can I stratify or subdivide an ALR acreage into strata lots?** Not without the ALC's permission: depositing a strata plan is a subdivision, and ALCA s.21(1) bars subdividing agricultural land unless the Commission permits it or the ALR General Regulation allows it (ALC Information Bulletin 09, s.6). Do not write "can never be stratified," and do not cite Bulletin 05 for stratification. (§3.)
- **Q: What farm income does an acreage need to keep farm classification?** BC Assessment's minimum gross farm income (farm gate) thresholds are $2,500 for an operation of 0.8 ha to 4 ha, $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 ha for a larger operation, and $10,000 for an operation under 0.8 ha; new applications are due by the legislated October 31 deadline, and classification is removed if the requirements stop being met. (§3.)
- **Q: Which communities does this page's feed cover?** At reading, the visible cards were labelled with Langley sub-areas (County Line Glen Valley, Willoughby Heights, Campbell Valley, Salmon River, Otter District) and Abbotsford sub-areas (Abbotsford West, Poplar) — a regional feed, which is why no single community's statistics are presented as this page's figures and why every rule must be confirmed in the parcel's own municipality. https://jaredgibbons.ca/fraser-valley-acreages-for-sale.html
- **Q: What does an acreage purchase cost in transfer tax and GST?** PTT follows the tier math in §3 (e.g., $800K→$14,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $3.5M fully residential→$93,000 including the over-$3M tier). GST is a GI-003 question: personal-use land sold by an individual is generally exempt; business/developer sales and newly subdivided lots are generally taxable; farmland carries the separate CRA caution. Both answers are parcel-specific — the contract must state the GST position. (§3.)
- **Q: What diligence can the regional page not do for me?** The parcel-level work: ALR status on ALC mapping and title, well and septic records, water licences, floodplain and access. One feed card already shows why, describing an ALR parcel bordered by a park "partially outside the ALR" with buyer-to-verify language on other uses (listing text). (§§2–3.)
