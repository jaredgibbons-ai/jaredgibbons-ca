## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The Reciprocity route, in the page's own words:** the page's footer states the data relating to real estate on the website comes in part from the MLS® Reciprocity program of either the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® (GVR), the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board (FVREB) or the Chilliwack and District Real Estate Board (CADREB); real estate listings held by participating real estate firms are marked with the MLS® logo and detailed information about the listing includes the name of the listing agent; the representation is based in whole or part on data generated by the boards, which assume no responsibility for its accuracy; and the materials contained on the page may not be reproduced without the express written consent of either board. https://jaredgibbons.ca/our-listings.html
- **The feed is not limited to those three boards' data in practice:** the Penticton card carries an MLS® number in a different format and a listing brokerage of ROYAL LEPAGE NORTHSTAR (W.R.) — evidence the page aggregates Jared's listings across the board systems he sells through, however the site platform assembles them; the aggregation mechanics themselves are UNVERIFIED. https://jaredgibbons.ca/our-listings.html
- **Who owns the system and who may use it:** at the national level, the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA), a trade association, owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service® ("MLS®") and REALTOR®, and comprises over 100,000 real estate brokers and agents across 100 local real estate boards and associations across Canada. Agents and brokers must become members of a local real estate board and CREA in order to access the MLS® system — described by the Competition Bureau as a comprehensive database of current and historical listing information crucial to residential real estate brokerage services — and must adhere to both CREA's rules, such as the REALTOR® Code, and the rules of their respective local real estate boards. In order to use the MLS® system to facilitate real estate transactions, agents must be a member of CREA and agree to comply with CREA's rules. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Display of a seller's information has been a governed, litigated question — careful reading required:** the October 2010 consent agreement between the Commissioner of Competition and CREA — registered with the Competition Tribunal to remain in force for a 10-year period — required CREA not to adopt, maintain or enforce rules denying agents the ability to provide mere postings or discriminating against agents who offer them, and it also encompassed areas such as negotiations regarding the cooperating seller's commission and where a seller's information can be displayed. That agreement is cited as the documented federal milestone on MLS® display governance, not as a current BC display rule; its 10-year term as described in the Bureau's submission has run, and what followed it is UNVERIFIED in this file. The operative display rules for this page today are the boards' own MLS®/Reciprocity rules, which are not public documents. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **The BC regulator's rules that apply to every listing on this page:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed real estate professional in BC must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings: whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA; dual agency — representing two or more clients in the same transaction whose interests conflict — was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. Practical consequence for this page: a buyer who clicks "Schedule viewing / Email" on one of Jared's listings and is unrepresented is, in BCFSA's framework, a non-client dealing with the seller's professional until a client relationship is formed — the page's contact tools start that disclosure conversation, they do not replace it. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **ALR RULES IN FULL — the frame for any acreage listing on this page a parcel check puts in the reserve:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m²; a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. BC Assessment farm-class thresholds, full three-tier form: $2,500 on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the farm operation for farm purposes in excess of 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha — B.C. Reg. 411/95, s.5, applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" line is an ERROR wherever stated; it is asserted ONLY in the horse-properties file in this program, is not documented in this file's research, and is not asserted here. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf
- **BC strata block — where a listing on this page is strata (the industrial units, condos and the gated 55+ townhome on page one are the live examples):** Form B diligence; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2, May 1, 2023 exemptions). A fee-simple acreage listing carries none of it: no strata, no Form B, no strata fees — tenure proved from title, not the feed card. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- **PTT — worked examples with inputs and tier math shown (the closing cost on any purchase from this page):** PTT is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office and is not annual property tax. General rates: 1% to $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% above $2,000,000, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000. Anchor checks, standard rates, no exemption unless stated: **$500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K cap→$14,000 ($6,000 for a fully-qualifying first-time buyer after the $8,000 saving); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.1M→$20,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M→$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M (tier math: $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000)** — a townhomes sibling in this program (south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m) carries $78,000 for $3.5M; this file follows $93,000, flags the conflict wherever met, and never silently carries $78,000. The "Over $3M — 5%" page row seen on band pages is a compression of 3% + 2% on the residential portion — flagged wherever met. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — ONLY at CRA GI-003 level (the development-land listings on this page are where this bites):** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt. Anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED in this file. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **First-home programs — the land boundary:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent MARCH 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself — 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; a source stating March 12 is noted in-line where met and never silently overwritten; a live page's $1.25M straight-line phase-out row is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism (CRA says only "gradually reduced"). The GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) is likewise housing-only. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer (plus the 2026–2028 repayment-relief deferral carried in this program); First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. CMHC published mortgage-insurance premium range, for any insured purchase from this page: **0.6%–4.5%**. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
