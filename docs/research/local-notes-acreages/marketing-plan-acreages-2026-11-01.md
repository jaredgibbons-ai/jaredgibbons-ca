## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **Scope note for this section, stated first:** this topic file carries only the frames its own research documents — the BC regulator's framework that applies to a listing taken under this plan, and the standard BC tax/program anchors a seller conversation touches. No municipal zoning, OCP, ALR-subdivision or servicing frame is imported, because the research documents no ground for this slug; where a rule below would turn on a specific property (an acreage, a farm, a strata unit), that application is UNCONFIRMED from this page.
- **The BC regulator's framework:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed real estate professional in BC must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings: whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA; dual agency — representing two or more clients in the same transaction whose interests conflict — was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. Practical consequence for this page: commission and fee information is part of what BCFSA requires a licensee to give a consumer, but the page itself states no fee or commission figure — so any fee discussion for a seller reading this page happens off-page, and no fee is stated in this file. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
- **The listing-data notice the site carries:** the standard site footer notice states the data relating to real estate on the website comes in part from the MLS® Reciprocity program of either the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® (GVR), the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board (FVREB) or the Chilliwack and District Real Estate Board (CADREB); listings held by participating firms are marked with the MLS® logo and include the listing agent's name; the representation is based on board-generated data the boards do not warrant for accuracy; and the materials may not be reproduced without the boards' express written consent. At the national level, the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA), a trade association, owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service® ("MLS®") and REALTOR®, and agents and brokers must become members of a local real estate board and CREA in order to access the MLS® system and must adhere to CREA's rules and their boards' rules. https://jaredgibbons.ca/marketing-plan.html and https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **ALR RULES IN FULL — carried because a seller using this plan may bring farm-adjacent ground, stated as the general BC frame and NOT as a finding about this page:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps as the ALC states them: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m²; a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. Whether any of this applies to a specific seller's property is per-parcel: UNCONFIRMED from this page. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure:** $2,500 of farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha; applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR — stated expressly: the flat form is NOT documented in this slug's research; it is owned by the HORSE-PROPERTIES page, a different slug, and is never attributed to this file. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — general BC anchors:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked anchors (inputs + tier math): $500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K cap→$14,000 ($6,000 after maximum First-Time Home Buyers' relief where it applies); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M fully residential→$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M — follow $93,000; a $78,000 figure for $3.5M appears in the south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m TOWNHOMES sibling and is flagged wherever met, not followed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — ONLY at CRA GI-003 level:** most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (personal-use land is the example), while a sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts. Farmland is the caution: farmland sales must never be assumed GST-exempt on the "vacant land" rule alone. Anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED in this file — and nothing in this slug's research applies GI-003 to any specific property. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
