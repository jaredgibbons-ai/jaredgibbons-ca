## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **Pad rent increases — the complete regulated mechanism (Province / RTB), percentages ONLY:** a park landlord may increase rent only with 3 full months' written notice, only once every 12 months, and only up to the yearly rent increase limit; the 2026 limit is 2.3% plus the proportional amount and the 2027 limit is 2.2% plus the proportional amount; the landlord may not round up, may not carry forward an unused portion of a prior year's limit, and if proper notice was not given the increase takes effect on the earliest date that would comply. The proportional amount, exactly: the change in local government levies, plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park — only levies and regulated utility fees for the park's common property qualify. A landlord may apply to the RTB for an additional increase (Form 52) for financial loss from increased operating or financing costs, or for substantial and essential repairs or renovations that are reasonable, necessary and not expected to recur. Tenants do not have to pay an increase above the legal amount and may deduct an overpayment from future rent or apply for dispute resolution. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Assignment on a sale — what happens to the pad rent when a Langley park home is bought:** assignment of the site tenancy permanently transfers the pad tenancy to the buyer; under s.28 of the MHPTA a tenant may assign only with the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or where the tenancy agreement authorizes it; before asking, the seller must give the proposed buyer written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent; and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. Landlords and tenants in a manufactured home park must have a written tenancy agreement for the site. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s.42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute the notice within 15 days, may in defined circumstances (home cannot be moved, no site within a reasonable distance, no tax owing on the home) apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Registration and CSA labels:** manufactured homes include modular and mobile homes, typically built in a factory off-site and delivered and assembled on-site; the CSA label states the standard — modular homes under A-277, mobile homes under Z-240; in BC the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered with the Manufactured Home Registry, through a lawyer or notary filing the original signed and witnessed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership forms; a home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal, and a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification. A permit to move a manufactured home is issued by the Registry, a tax certificate for transport purposes is required (with exceptions including a move to a different pad within the same park), and the transport permit expires 30 days after issue. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Taxes on a home in a park:** the Province states a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value will not include the value of the land if the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner — the accurate version of the live page's "you pay taxes on the structure only" line. https://www2.gov.BC.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: How much is pad rent in Langley?** No official source publishes a Langley pad-rent figure, average or range, so no figure is stated in this file — and the live page's $650–$950 range is UNVERIFIED site copy, not an official statistic. A pad rent is set park by park in the written Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement; the figure that matters is the specific park's current rent, obtained from the park and the tenancy agreement before an offer. Never budget from an "average pad rent." https://jaredgibbons.ca/cost-of-mobile-home-pad-rental-fees-in-langley.html
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up each year?** Up to the yearly limit: 2026 = 2.3% plus the proportional amount; 2027 = 2.2% plus the proportional amount. The proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of sites in the park. Increases require 3 full months' notice and can occur only once every 12 months; anything above the limit requires an RTB order. Ask the park for its last proportional-amount calculation — it is park-specific. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Does the park raise my pad rent when I buy the home?** No. On assignment of the site tenancy under s.28 of the MHPTA, the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same and the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase does not change; the landlord's prior written consent is required (or deemed given under the regulations) and the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Q: What happens if the park is sold or converted to another use?** Under s.42 of the Act: the landlord must first have all necessary permits and approvals, then give 12 months' notice and pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the effective date; the tenant may leave early on 10 days' written notice, may dispute within 15 days, and may in defined circumstances claim additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000; if the landlord does not proceed within a reasonable period, a further payment of the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes
- **Q: Do I pay property tax on a manufactured home in a Langley park?** Yes, on the home: a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value does not include the value of the land where the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. https://www2.gov.BC.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **Q: Is a Langley park home acreage or land ownership?** Neither. You own the home and rent the site — no lot, no land title, no acreage. Own-land forms (fee-simple lots, bare-land strata) carry no pad rent and no MHPTA assignment step, but whether any specific Langley product sits in a bare-land strata is UNVERIFIED in this file's research. (§2.)
- **Q: Which municipality's rules govern my Langley park?** Pad tenancy is provincial (MHPTA) in both. Land-use rules split: Township parks sit under Township Zoning 2500 / SSMUH Bylaw 6020 / Servicing Bylaw 5382; City parks under City Zoning 2025 No. 3300 (adoption UNVERIFIED) / SSMUH Bylaw 3284. The park-zone section of either bylaw is UNVERIFIED in the research — confirm the park's municipality first, then its zone with that municipality. (§3.)
- **Q: What if an increase looks wrong?** Tenants do not have to pay above-limit increases, can deduct an overpayment from future rent with a written explanation, or can apply for dispute resolution. Close the registration, not just the contract: the purchase is only effective once registered through a lawyer or notary filing the Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership with the Manufactured Home Registry — and check the CSA label first, because without the original legible decal the home cannot be sold until Technical Safety BC re-certification. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
