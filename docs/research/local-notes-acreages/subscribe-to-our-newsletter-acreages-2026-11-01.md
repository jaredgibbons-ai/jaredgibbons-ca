## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **Why CASL is this page's acreage rule at all:** the rules that affect an acreage buyer on THIS page are not land rules — they are the rules governing the email channel through which acreage listings and market updates would reach that buyer. The land rules themselves (ALR, Township/City zoning, PTT, GST on vacant land) live in the program's land files and are cross-referenced at the end of this section, not re-documented here, because this slug's own research documents none of them.
- **Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) — the CRTC's framework:** CASL and its regulations target spam, computer programs installed without consent, and unsolicited redirections; the CRTC is the regulator that publishes the compliance framework. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti/reg.htm
- **The core rule for a message like this newsletter:** when sending a commercial electronic message (e.g. email or text), you must (1) obtain prior consent from the recipient (either express or implied); (2) provide identification and contact information; and (3) include a working unsubscribe mechanism. Violations may result in monetary penalties. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti/reg.htm
- **The trigger, in the CRTC's own terms:** CASL applies when (1) a commercial electronic message (CEM) is (2) sent to an electronic address — and a CEM includes offers to purchase, sell, barter or lease land or an interest in land, and messages promoting a person as someone who does those things. A REALTOR®'s newsletter carrying latest listings and market updates, sent to subscribers' email addresses, sits squarely in that definition on the CRTC's own examples — an acreage-listing roundup sent to this list would be a CEM on the same wording. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Express versus implied consent:** the CRTC FAQ states there are two types of consent under CASL — express and implied; express consent does not expire, however the recipient has the right to withdraw their consent at any time; express consent can be obtained in writing or orally, and in either case the onus is on the person sending the message to prove they obtained consent. What that means for this page: a voluntary signup through this page is the classic express-consent route — and the sender, not the subscriber, carries the burden of proving it. The consent record (who, when, how) has to exist on Jared's side; whether the current signup flow creates and keeps such a record is UNVERIFIED, because neither the form nor the provider could be seen. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Identification:** the second requirement is identification information in the message itself. The signup page carries Jared's name, brokerage, phone and email on its face (Section 1) — whether each newsletter issue carries the required identification is UNVERIFIED, because no issue was available to this research. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Unsubscribe mechanics, in the CRTC's detail:** an unsubscribe mechanism must be included in CEMs; a clear and prominent hyperlink in an email allowing the recipient to unsubscribe with a simple click is the CRTC's example, the mechanism must be "readily performed" — simple, quick and easy; the unsubscribe link must remain valid for at least 60 days after the CEM is sent; and an unsubscribe request must be processed without delay, and no later than 10 business days after receiving it. The page's own promise — "You can unsubscribe any time" — matches the direction of the law (express consent is withdrawable at any time), but CASL compliance is about the mechanism in every issue (working link, 60-day validity, 10-business-day processing), not the promise on the signup page. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm
- **Privacy — PIPEDA federally, and BC's own law provincially:** the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC) states the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) sets the ground rules for how private-sector organizations collect, use and disclose personal information in the course of for-profit, commercial activities across Canada; personal information includes any factual or subjective information, recorded or not, about an identifiable individual. Businesses subject to PIPEDA must follow its 10 fair information principles, set out in Schedule 1 of the Act: 1. Accountability; 2. Identifying purposes; 3. Consent; 4. Limiting collection; 5. Limiting use, disclosure, and retention; 6. Accuracy; 7. Safeguards; 8. Openness; 9. Individual access; 10. Challenging compliance. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/?wbdisable=false
- **The BC wrinkle, from the OPC itself:** British Columbia (with Alberta and Quebec) has its own private-sector privacy law that has been deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA, and organizations subject to a substantially similar provincial law are generally exempt from PIPEDA with respect to collection, use or disclosure that occurs within that province — while businesses handling personal information that crosses provincial or national borders remain subject to PIPEDA regardless. A BC REALTOR®'s subscriber list therefore sits primarily under BC's provincial regime for in-province handling, with PIPEDA behind it for cross-border handling; this file states the framework only and does not give legal advice on which statute governs a specific list. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/?wbdisable=false

Whether Jared's actual list handling (provider storage location, retention, access requests) follows those principles is UNVERIFIED — it cannot be seen from the page. https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/?wbdisable=false

- **Q: Can I unsubscribe, and how fast must it happen?** Yes — the page promises "You can unsubscribe any time," and the CRTC states the legal mechanics behind that promise: every commercial electronic message must include an unsubscribe mechanism that is readily performed, its link must remain valid for at least 60 days after sending, and a request must be processed without delay and no later than 10 business days after receipt. http://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm

- **Q: Why does a real estate newsletter need my consent at all?** Because Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation requires it: the CRTC states that when sending a commercial electronic message you must obtain prior consent (express or implied), provide identification and contact information, and include a working unsubscribe mechanism — and a newsletter offering listings and promoting a REALTOR®'s services is a commercial electronic message on the CRTC's own examples. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti/reg.htm
