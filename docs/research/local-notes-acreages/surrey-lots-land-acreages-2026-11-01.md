## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **OCP: Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted Jun 15, 2026) — current, in full.** It replaces the Surrey Official Community Plan Bylaw, 2013, No. 18020, which is never cited as current. Secondary plans (NCPs and town centre plans) sit under it, and parcel designations are read in COSMOS. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/r133-plansurrey-2013-official-community-plan-surrey-2050-official-community-plan-alignment-related-amendments
- **Zoning: Surrey Zoning By-law, 1993, No. 12000** divides the city into zones and regulates use, density, siting and subdivision; the SSMUH amendments (adopted Jul 8, 2024) consolidated 20 existing single-family and duplex zones into 9 zones (RA, R1, R2, R2-O, R3, R4, R5, R5-S, R6). https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_Zoning_12000.pdf
- **SSMUH / added units:** a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop, applying to eligible residential lots under 4,050 m² within the Urban Containment Boundary, with exemptions including certain hazard lands and properties not connected to community water and sewer. Added-unit permission is NOT permission to subdivide, does not create new lots, and does not waive floodplain, riparian or servicing requirements. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **Secondary suites:** only one suite per single-family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit, at most 40% of the principal unit's habitable floor area, never inside a coach house or garden suite, and never a separate strata lot. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- **Subdivision / servicing:** a new lot must meet its zone's minimum subdivision lot area in Bylaw No. 12000. **Surrey Subdivision and Development Bylaw, 1986, No. 8830 — VERIFIED** on the City's own pages; the City's process runs through its Engineering Land Development process to a Servicing Agreement defining off-site works and securities for final approval. No other city's servicing number is used for Surrey. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-development-application-process/servicing-agreement
- **Development Cost Charges:** the live page describes DCCs as per-lot or per-square-foot fees collected by the City of Surrey and Metro Vancouver at subdivision or building permit issuance to fund roads, water, sewer, drainage and parkland infrastructure. Current DCC rates from the City's DCC bylaw: UNVERIFIED in the research — direct opening of the City's DCC pages was stopped by a 429 response on 2026-11-01 and was not retried. https://jaredgibbons.ca/surrey-lots-land.html
- **Tree protection:** the live page states that protected trees on private lots require a City Tree Cutting Permit before removal, backed by an arborist report and a retention or replacement plan. The Tree Protection Bylaw's own thresholds: UNVERIFIED in the research — direct opening was stopped by the same 429 response. https://jaredgibbons.ca/surrey-lots-land.html
- **Floodplain and streamside:** Surrey's coastal floodplain runs along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers and the Serpentine–Nicomekl lowlands, so low-lying rural and edge parcels carry flood-construction and diking questions upland lots do not; parcel-level flood elevation: UNVERIFIED, confirmed with the City per parcel. Streamside setbacks are defined in Part 7A of Zoning Bylaw No. 12000, with different setbacks by stream class — NO single riparian figure is stated for Surrey land generally; Surrey plan areas record fish-bearing creeks whose riparian rules can affect parcels (for example, the South Newton plan area includes Archibald Creek headwaters, Sullivan Heights Creek and Hyland Creek tributaries). https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/newton-land-use-plans/south-newton
- **Short-term rentals (Surrey):** permitted, owner-only — only the property owner may host, the owner must live at the property as their primary residence, tenants cannot run one, business licence **$358 per year** on the City's program page. **Conflict disclosed:** the City's 2024 corporate report proposing the STR bylaw amendments put the licence at $350/yr plus fees — this file carries $358 with the $350 proposal disclosed. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/short-term-rentals
- **ALR RULES IN FULL — ALR ground exists on the Surrey edges in this topic, so the full frame governs:** subdivision of ALR land requires Agricultural Land Commission permission, apart from the limited cases in the Agricultural Land Reserve General Regulation where an approving officer may approve without the Commission. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). A principal residence built without an ALC application must be **500 m² or less** total floor area, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill for its construction; a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence; one additional detached residence is permitted without an application at **90 m² or less** (parcels of 40 ha or smaller) or **186 m² or less** (larger parcels), where Surrey bylaws also permit it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/application-and-notice-process/applications/required-documents/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure:** $2,500 of farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha; applications by October 31. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Where do buildable lots in Surrey come from?** New urban lots are created by subdivision against the parcel's zone minimum in Surrey Zoning By-law No. 12000, and by added-unit development on existing lots, since Surrey's SSMUH framework allows 3 units on lots under 280 m², 4 units on larger lots and up to 6 on eligible lots within 400 m of a frequent bus stop; on the agricultural edges, reserve land generally needs Agricultural Land Commission permission to subdivide. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **Q: Can I subdivide a farm parcel in Surrey?** Only within tight limits: reserve land generally needs Agricultural Land Commission permission to subdivide, apart from limited approving-officer cases in the Agricultural Land Reserve General Regulation, and any new lot must also meet its Bylaw No. 12000 zone minimum. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/application-and-notice-process/applications/required-documents/
- **Q: How big a house can go on a reserve parcel?** A principal residence built without an Agricultural Land Commission application must be 500 m² or less in total floor area, with no more than 1,000 m² of fill for its construction, and a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: Is the Glenwood Village assembly the same as the Glenwood Village townhouses?** No — expressly not. The assembly item in this topic is a QUEUE item, documented as such and counted nowhere; West Newton's built Glenwood Village (7156 121 St, NWS2768, 1988) is a built 55+ townhouse complex in its own home files. The two are never merged in this program. (§2.)
- **Q: What extra rules apply to a low-lying lot near the Nicomekl or Serpentine?** Surrey's coastal floodplain runs along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers and the Serpentine–Nicomekl lowlands, so a lowland parcel can carry flood-construction levels and diking-related requirements set by the City — confirm the parcel's position and levels with the City before writing. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands
- **Q: Are there market statistics for Surrey land or acreage?** No land or acreage figure is stated in this file: SnapStats publishes no vacant-land and no acreage category, so land sale counts, price per acre and land days on market are UNVERIFIED; the §1 figures are Surrey all-residential context only. (As of September 2026, Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report.)
- **Q: Does SSMUH permission let me split my lot?** No. Surrey allows 3 to 4 units on most SSMUH lots and up to 6 near a frequent bus stop, but that permission does not create new lots and does not waive floodplain, riparian or servicing requirements on the parcel. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
