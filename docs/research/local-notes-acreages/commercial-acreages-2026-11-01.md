## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **Per-jurisdiction rule, stated first and followed throughout this section:** this feed spans many municipalities across the Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver. - **The regulator's framework — the one frame this file's research DOES document region-wide:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed real estate professional in BC must give consumers information about commissions and fees, must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. Disclosure must happen at the outset of dealings — whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA; dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. At the national level, CREA owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service® ("MLS®") and REALTOR®, and agents and brokers must become members of a local real estate board and CREA to access the MLS® system and adhere to CREA's and their boards' rules. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Zoning / permitted use:** the page's editorial text itself names zoning as essential to commercial investing; whether a specific business use is permitted at a specific address is a municipal zoning question for that property, confirmed with that municipality — no zoning conclusion for any listing is stated in this file, and commercial zoning summaries for any municipality are UNVERIFIED in this file's research. https://jaredgibbons.ca/commercial.html
- **ALR RULES IN FULL — carried because Land Commercial and rural commercial parcels in this region can sit in the reserve, and wherever ALR ground exists this frame governs:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps as the ALC states them: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m² (with no more than 1,000 m² of fill for its construction); a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure (relevant only to a farm-classed commercial parcel):** $2,500 of farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha; applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR — stated explicitly for this slug: the flat form is owned ONLY by the HORSE-PROPERTIES page and is never attributed to this file. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification

- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — general provincial tiers stated, commercial application left at the research's silence:** the Province's general rates are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million.
