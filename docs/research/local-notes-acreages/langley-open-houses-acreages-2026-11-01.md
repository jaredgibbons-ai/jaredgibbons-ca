## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **The open-house rule, quoted verbatim with its sourcing stated — BCFSA Rule 5-10(3):** Rule 5-10(3) of the Real Estate Rules, **as set out in the Office of the Superintendent of Real Estate's November 2017 consultation report on the agency and disclosure rule changes** (the sourcing this slug's research re-verified at source), provides that a disclosure of representation is not required when a licensee is only hosting an advertised open house or providing factual responses to general questions, **unless the licensee solicits or receives information from the party about the party's motivation, financial qualifications or needs in respect of real estate**. In practice: chatting factually with the host does not by itself make you their client — but once you share motivation, budget or needs with a licensee who represents the seller, the disclosure rules engage. http://bcfsa.ca/media/1815/download
- **Representation and dual agency (BCFSA):** in BC a licensed real estate professional must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party, and must disclose at the outset whether they can represent the consumer as a client, their duties, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA. Real estate professionals cannot represent two or more clients in the same transaction whose interests conflict, except in the rarest of circumstances; dual agency was prohibited in BC in 2018 — so the host at an open house cannot simply "also represent" a buyer who walks in. Real estate trading services in BC are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA). https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home

- **ALR RULES IN FULL (the governing frame for any ALR parcel in either Langley municipality; none is documented through this feed):** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps: one principal residence per parcel, maximum 500 m²; a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. On ALR land the ALC suite rule controls instead of either municipal suite cap above — the regimes must not be mixed. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure:** $2,500 of farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha; applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR — stated explicitly for this slug: the flat form is NOT documented in this slug's research; it is owned by the HORSE-PROPERTIES page, a different slug, and is never attributed to this file. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) — worked anchors (inputs + tier math shown):** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. $500K → $8,000 ($2,000 + $6,000); $700K → $12,000; $800K cap → $14,000 ($6,000 after maximum First-Time Home Buyers' relief where it applies); $900K → $16,000; $1.0M → $18,000; $1.2M → $22,000; $1.5M → $28,000; $1.8M → $34,000; $2.0M → $38,000; $3.5M fully residential → $93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M — follow $93,000 wherever a $78,000 figure for $3.5M is met in a sibling elsewhere in the program (flagged, not followed). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — ONLY at CRA GI-003 level:** most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (personal-use land is the example), while a sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a subdivided parcel of more than two parts. Farmland is addressed by the CRA in a separate sheet and is usually taxable subject to limited exemptions. Anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **First-home programs — the land boundary:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent MARCH 13, 2026 per CRA; March 12 noted in-line in some sources — March 13 governs) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land — 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; a $1.25M straight-line row is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out — housing only. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **BC strata block (governs the condo / townhome share of any open-house run):** Form B diligence; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2, May 1, 2023 exemptions).
