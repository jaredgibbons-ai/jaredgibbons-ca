## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **Frames stated PER LABELLED EXAMPLE, never blended — there is no single municipal frame for this topic:** municipal zoning for a poultry operation is set by the municipality where the land sits. Labelled example (Chilliwack): Chilliwack's Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000 defines POULTRY as any domesticated fowl including chickens, turkeys, ducks, geese and swans, and lists General Agriculture as a permitted principal use in its AL, AU and AS zones. Chilliwack frame for reference: OCP 5500; SSMUH 5395; Zoning Bylaw 5000 §6 farm zones; CADREB board — CADREB publishes NO area stats and there is NO Chilliwack SnapStats. That example governs Chilliwack parcels only; an Abbotsford parcel is read under Abbotsford's bylaws (OCP = Abbotsforward 2050 CURRENT; Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014) and a Township of Langley parcel under the Township's (Zoning Bylaw 2500), each in its own area file — never blended into a topic-wide zoning claim. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- **ALR RULES IN FULL — the governing frame for Fraser Valley poultry ground:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps as the ALC states them: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m²; a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **The ALR barn rule:** under the Agricultural Land Reserve Use Regulation, B.C. Reg. 30/2019, s.5, the use of agricultural land to construct, maintain or operate a structure, other than a residential structure, that is necessary for a farm use is designated as a farm use and, under s.4, may not be prohibited by a local government enactment; s.5 states for greater certainty that this includes a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation — the wording under which a poultry barn is treated as a farm building in the ALR. The ALC's guidance says the same in plain terms: such a structure may not be prohibited by a local government bylaw, but it may be regulated, and it must be shown to be required for the farm use on the property and in line with the level of that farm use. Note the research file's precision: a rule stated in the words "poultry barn" in the ALR Use Regulation is UNVERIFIED — s.5 covers a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019 and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/763/2025/10/Necessary-Farm-Use-Structures-in-the-ALR-Guidelines.pdf
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure:** $2,500 of farm income on a farm operation of 0.8–4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the farm operation for farm purposes in excess of 4 ha on operations over 4 ha; $10,000 on operations under 0.8 ha — Classification of Land as a Farm Regulation, B.C. Reg. 411/95, s.5, with applications by October 31. A flat "$2,500+" statement is an ERROR — and precision matters here: the flat figure is documented as an error in the HORSE-PROPERTIES page's own research; it is NOT attributed to this topic's research file, which states no farm-class figure, and no flat line is asserted in this file. https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf
- **The chicken board:** the British Columbia Chicken Marketing Board (BCCMB) is a non-profit agricultural commodity board that monitors and regulates the production and marketing of chicken in BC; its jurisdiction covers all activities of broiler chicken production in BC from the time the day-old chick arrives on the farm, and its mandate includes overseeing policy, managing risk, and upholding national programs for supply management, food safety and animal welfare. In its own reporting its role covers setting provincial chicken prices, determining quota, allocating quota to growers, managing the quota system and collecting levies, for the period from the day-old chick arriving on farm until the chicken arrives at the processing plant. https://bcchicken.ca and https://bcchicken.ca/BCChickenAnnualReport2017.pdf
- **What broiler quota is, and who counts as a grower:** quota is the amount of chicken, expressed in kilograms of live weight, that the board allots to a grower for production and marketing; the board's General Orders name two classes — mainstream quota and specialty quota — with mainstream covering regular broilers, roasters, Cornish, or certified organic chicken. A "grower" is a person engaged in the production of chicken to whom the board has allotted quota or issued a permit that has not been cancelled; the Orders include parts on quota allotment, transfer of quota with registered premises, and acquisition or disposition of quota without registered premises. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Quota transfers:** the board's role is to approve quota transactions, which ensures growers are in good standing and production is allocated appropriately for each growing cycle, and quota transfers are facilitated privately rather than through a board-run central platform described in the board's consultation paper. Board-source wording for premises certification requirements on a chicken quota transfer: UNVERIFIED in the research file. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/QUAC-Discussion-Paper-FINAL.Oct_.2024.pdf
- **Hatching eggs:** the BC Broiler Hatching Egg Commission covers the step before the grower — its regulated product is a broiler hatching egg, a saleable chick or a broiler breeder; for mainstream production the system is hen-based (one quota unit equals one placeable broiler breeder hen) and for specialty production it is hatched chick-based (one quota unit equals one hatched chick). https://bcbhec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2022-Annual-Report.pdf
- **Turkey:** the British Columbia Turkey Marketing Board runs the turkey scheme; turkey quota in plain terms is the number of pounds of turkey meat a turkey producer is entitled to market in intraprovincial trade through normal marketing channels, or to have marketed on the producer's behalf by the board, during a specified period of time. Turkey quota transfer rules beyond the scheme's quota and licensing provisions, from the board's own site: UNVERIFIED in the research file. https://bcturkey.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/174_66.pdf
- **Egg (layer) sector: UNVERIFIED where no board source was opened.** Egg (layer) sector quota transfer rules from the BC Egg Marketing Board's own published material were NOT opened for the research file and stay UNVERIFIED site copy — no layer quota rule is asserted in this file. (Research file, UNVERIFIED list.)
- **Oversight and appeals:** the BC Farm Industry Review Board is an independent administrative tribunal that supervises the regulated marketing system in BC's agricultural sector and reviews a regulated marketing board's decision, order or determination on appeal, with appeals to be filed within 30 calendar days of the board's final decision. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/organizational-structure/ministries-organizations/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/npma-appeals
- **Biosecurity and avian influenza — a closing-condition rule, not background:** BC Gov poultry guidance defines biosecurity as management practices that reduce disease and pests entering a farm and limit their spread within and beyond the farm, using a Controlled Access Zone for the production area and a more restricted Restricted Access Zone where birds are housed. Highly pathogenic avian influenza is a reportable disease in Canada and cases must be reported to the CFIA; the CFIA establishes primary control zones where avian influenza has been detected and permits are required to move birds, their products and by-products through a control zone; on a farm under quarantine, no birds, bird products such as eggs, or bird by-products such as manure may enter or leave the property without CFIA permission — which can affect bird placement, showings and sale timing on a poultry farm. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/ai_enhanced_biosecurity_for_small_scale_poultry_producers.pdf and https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected
- **Subdivision and servicing:** for an ALR poultry parcel, subdivision requires ALC approval (above) in addition to the host municipality's Approving Officer process against its zone minimums and works-and-services requirements — read in the host municipality's own file, per labelled example, never blended. No topic-wide servicing number exists and none is asserted.
- **Property transfer tax (PTT) on farm land:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million (the residential-portion apportionment on a mixed farm purchase is parcel work — the anchors below are stated for a fully residential purchase). Worked example, inputs stated: a $2,000,000 fully residential purchase with no exemption = 1% × $200,000 ($2,000) + 2% × $1,800,000 ($36,000) = **$38,000**. Farmland is the caution: the CRA addresses sales of farmland by individuals in a SEPARATE info sheet and treats farmland sales as usually taxable subject to limited exemptions (including certain transfers to a related person for personal use). A working poultry farm is the business-use fact pattern — never assume a poultry parcel is GST-exempt on the vacant-land rule alone; the GST treatment of any specific operation (land, barns, quota and residence can each carry a different position) is UNCONFIRMED until the vendor's status, the parcel's farm use and the applicable CRA sheet are applied, and the contract must state the GST position. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What am I actually buying when I buy a "poultry farm"?** A package: fee-simple land (usually ALR), the barn package, the residence, and quota where the seller includes it — plus a step into an annually licensed grower role. Any one element can be excluded (the Abbotsford West feed listing states quota is not included). This file documents no roster — each operation is diligenced as its own package. https://jaredgibbons.ca/poultry-farms-for-sale-bc.html
- **Q: The feed card says "poultry farm" — is it a working operation?** Not on the feed's word alone. The research records the live pages' cards as residential/land listings whose text mentions a possible poultry use, a barn, or an operation with quota not included — a card is never a confirmed operation in this file. Confirm the operation, the quota and the board standing from the seller's records and the board, not the card. (Research file, §1.)
- **Q: Does the quota automatically come with the farm?** No. Quota transfers require board approval, and the General Orders deal separately with transfer of quota with registered premises and acquisition or disposition of quota without registered premises — listings may also state quota is not included. Establish exactly what quota is in the deal, in writing: class (mainstream vs specialty), kilograms live weight, and which transfer case applies. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Q: What is poultry quota, in plain terms?** For chicken, the amount of chicken, expressed in kilograms of live weight, that the BC Chicken Marketing Board allots to a grower for production and marketing (mainstream and specialty classes). Turkey quota is pounds of turkey meat per specified period; hatching egg quota is hen units (mainstream) or hatched chicks (specialty). https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- **Q: Can I price the quota from listings or a board price list?** No — quota trades are facilitated privately and no official source publishes quota prices; the board's quota exchange platform was stated to be in development, not confirmed operating. Do not price quota from listings. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Policy-Directions-Update-Sep-2026.pdf
- **Q: Who regulates poultry farming in BC, and can I appeal a board decision?** Each sector has its own board — BCCMB (chicken), BC Broiler Hatching Egg Commission (hatching eggs), BC Turkey Marketing Board (turkey) — supervised by the BC Farm Industry Review Board, an independent tribunal that also hears appeals of board decisions, to be filed within 30 calendar days of the board's final decision. Egg (layer) transfer rules from the BC Egg Marketing Board's own material: UNVERIFIED in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/organizational-structure/ministries-organizations/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/npma-appeals
- **Q: Can a municipality stop me building a poultry barn in the ALR?** Under the ALR Use Regulation, B.C. Reg. 30/2019, a structure for use in an intensive livestock operation is a designated farm use that a local government enactment may not prohibit — though it may be regulated, and the structure must be required for the farm use and in line with its level. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019
- **Q: Can I stratify the farm, or split the residence off?** No, on ALR land — residences in the ALR cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; SPA s.239; ALCA s.21(1)), subdivision within the ALR requires ALC approval, and the housing caps are one principal residence to 500 m², a suite inside that residence only, and an additional residence of 90 m² (40 ha or less) or 186 m² (over 40 ha). https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q: What farm income do I need for farm class?** All three tiers, not a flat figure: $2,500 on a 0.8–4 ha farm operation; $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value in excess of 4 ha over 4 ha; $10,000 under 0.8 ha — B.C. Reg. 411/95, s.5, applications by October 31. https://info.bcassessment.ca/about/Shared%20Documents/Classification_of_Land_as_a_Farm_Regulation.pdf
- **Q: What happens to a poultry farm sale during an avian influenza outbreak?** HPAI is a reportable disease; in a CFIA primary control zone, permits are required to move birds, eggs and by-products, and on a quarantined farm nothing bird-related enters or leaves without CFIA permission — which can delay placements, showings and completion. Confirm the property's status before scheduling placements or a completion date. https://www.inspection.gc.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/what-expect-your-animals-are-infected
- **Q: Do I pay GST on a poultry farm?** Do not assume the vacant-land exemption: the CRA treats farmland sales in a separate sheet as usually taxable with limited exemptions, and a working operation is business-use ground. The answer is UNCONFIRMED for any specific operation until vendor status and farm use are applied; the contract must state who bears GST on each element of the package. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
