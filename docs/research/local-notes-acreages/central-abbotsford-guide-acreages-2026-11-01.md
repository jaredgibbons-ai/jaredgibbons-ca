## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- "Central Abbotsford" is the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board sub-area name used in the SnapStats FVR September 2026 report; it is not confirmed as an official City of Abbotsford neighbourhood name — the City plans the core through the City Centre Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2913-2019) and the Historic Downtown Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2920-2019). https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/community-planning/neighbourhood-plans
- The City's own commercial study describes the City Centre as a roughly rectangular area bordered approximately by Peardonville Road and Mill Lake to the south; Ware Street, Mill Lake Road and Horn Street to the east; Parkview Street and Tims Street to the west; and to the north by a line roughly parallel to George Ferguson Way and Maclure Road, centred on South Fraser Way. - Where land actually trades: there is no greenfield or acreage ground in the core. Land changes hands as (1) individual infill / teardown parcels on the older detached streets, (2) small subdivisions carved out of those streets through rezoning, and (3) development sites assembled for the plan-designated multi-family forms — the Rail District being the named example (Section 2). Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report (research file above).
- Bourquin Exchange, the central transit exchange, is in the area; BC Transit routes include 1 (Highstreet/UFV), 2 (Highstreet/McMillan), 4 (Saddle/Montrose), 5 (Clearbrook/Bourquin via Hospital), 12 (UFV/Bourquin), 17 (Industrial/Bourquin), 21 (Aldergrove/Abbotsford) and 31 (Abbotsford/Mission). ## 2. THE ACREAGE ITSELF

**The honest position, stated plainly at the top: Central Abbotsford has NO acreage market. This is built-out urban ground — there is no acreage subdivision, no hobby-acreage pocket, no working-farm ground and no bare-land strata acreage community in the urban core. Verified named acreage developments counted here: 0. What trades as "land" here is an infill parcel, a teardown lot, or a development site held for a plan-designated building form — all documented in the lots-and-land home file named in the FILE STATUS block.**

- **What an "acreage" search actually surfaces here.** Fee-simple infill parcels on the older detached streets (RS3, RS5 and infill RS3-i / RS7 zones) beside the city's deepest strata stock. A vacant or teardown parcel on those streets is a fee-simple city lot: the buyer's own title, no strata corporation, no Form B, no strata fee — zoning, the two neighbourhood plans and any registered covenants govern what can replace the existing house. None of it is acreage by any measure the research states. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58178/
- **Parcel character, as the research states it — city lots, not acreage:** RS3 minimum interior lot 540 m²; RS5 minimum interior lot 420 m²; and RS7 (Infill Residential Zone) minimum lot size 300 m² for new interior lots, with minimum lot width 10.0 m and depth 22.5 m — among the smallest detached lot minimums in the city. RS3-i (Urban Residential Zone, Infill) is named in City council material for Maple Street in the Historic Downtown plan area. These minimums are why the core's land market is an infill market: the plans contemplate existing streets being re-cut into small lots, not acreage being opened. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/52433/ and https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58178/
- **The subdivision process, documented on a real central file:** a September 16, 2025 City council report (PDS 172-2025) processes a rezoning of 2330 Fir Street (Lots 30 and 31) from RS3-i to RS7 to facilitate a three-lot subdivision, consistent with the Urban 3 – Infill designation — with the standard conditions in that report being a development agreement / cash-in-lieu for road and utility upgrades under the Development Bylaw, a Community Amenity Contribution, a s.219 covenant limiting density on the new lots, and resolution of unbudgeted funding items. That report is documented here as the area's subdivision PROCESS in action — a rezoning-led, condition-heavy route to three city lots — not as acreage and not as for-sale inventory. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **Zoning: Abbotsford Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014 governs.** The land-relevant central zones as the research states them: RS3 (minimum interior lot 540 m²), RS5 (420 m²), RS7 Infill (300 m² minimum, 10.0 m width, 22.5 m depth) and RS3-i infill. No acreage or agricultural zone is documented for this sub-area in the research. In the core, the zone plus the neighbourhood-plan designation — not the street's current houses — decides a parcel's real value. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/52433/ and https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58178/
- **Plans: two neighbourhood plans inside Abbotsforward 2050.** The City Centre Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2913-2019) and the Historic Downtown Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2920-2019) are the adopted plans for the core, under Abbotsforward 2050 — Abbotsford's CURRENT Official Community Plan, adopted December 2025. Any live-page "2500-2016" or research "2600-2016" OCP citation is STALE / conflicted: apply 2050 and flag both numbers as a Jerry sweep item. Historic Downtown designations that price land: Urban 1 – Midrise (up to 6 storeys), Urban Centre – Mixed Use, Urban 2 – Ground Oriented (rowhouses, townhouse, multiplex or duplex housing) and Urban 3 – Infill (single detached with some ground-oriented duplexes). Abbotsforward 2050 also states a new mixed-use centre between the hospital and Mill Lake Park and a Primary Transit Corridor along South Fraser Way between Clearbrook and McCallum Road. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/community-planning/neighbourhood-plans and https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/Historic%20Downtown%20Neighbourhood%20Plan%20Final%20Event%20Boards.pdf and https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/making-progress-housing-abbotsford
- **SSMUH / added units on a lot:** the bylaw's s.140 small-scale multi-unit provision allows 3 units on lots 280 m² or less and 4 units on lots greater than 280 m² in the stated conditions, and s.140.3.8 permits one secondary suite in all single detached dwellings. June 2024 Council adoption of the SSMUH amendments is City-confirmed; the SSMUH bylaw NUMBER is UNCONFIRMED ("3560-2024" is asserted in research files and must not be asserted). Abbotsford's suite cap is under 50% of the principal dwelling, and a coach/garden suite is capped at 15% of lot area to a maximum of 100 m² — Abbotsford's rules ONLY. This 3–4-unit provision is the core's main lot-value lever: a teardown parcel's price is routinely its unit count, so the s.140 conditions must be read for the specific lot before paying for potential. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400140 and https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
- **Subdivision and servicing requirements:** as the Fir Street file shows, a central subdivision typically needs rezoning to an infill zone first, then Approving Officer approval, with Development Bylaw works and services secured by agreement or cash-in-lieu, amenity contributions and s.219 covenants as conditions. The Abbotsford Development Bylaw's current number/year is HELD UNCONFIRMED in this program (a 2021 draft and 2022 references both circulate): the process is documented instead, and detailed servicing requirements for a specific parcel are UNCONFIRMED until the current bylaw text and the file's Works and Services Report are read. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
- **Flood / watercourse diligence:** Willband Creek runs through the Historic Downtown / central area and is named in City Historic Downtown plan material. The central upland is not Sumas Prairie — but parcel-level floodplain status still requires a City floodplain map check for any lot near the creek, and no lot should be priced as flood-free on the neighbourhood name alone. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- **Heritage diligence:** the Gur Sikh Temple (Abbotsford Sikh Temple) at 33089 South Fraser Way is a designated National Historic Site in the core — a reminder that Historic Downtown parcels can carry heritage and design expectations under the neighbourhood plan even where no single-lot heritage designation is documented. Parcel-level heritage status: UNCONFIRMED until the plan schedules and title are read for the lot. https://www.pc.gc.ca/culture/designation/lieu-site/temple-sikh-abbotsford-sikh-temple
- **ALR rules in full — stated so a mislabelled "acreage" listing can be tested against them (no ALR parcel is documented in this sub-area, Section 2):** ALR residences cannot be stratified (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239 deposit of a strata plan = subdivision; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)); subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval; ALC residence caps are a principal residence of 500 m² or less, a secondary suite only within the principal residence, and one additional residence of 90 m² or less on parcels of 40 ha or less / 186 m² or less on parcels over 40 ha. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/ and https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf

- **If a lot were bare-land strata (no verified case in the core):** the BC strata block would apply to the LOT — Form B diligence (fee, approved levies, CRF balance, insurance summary; provided within 7 days, max $35 plus up to 25¢/page); depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2, May 1, 2023 exemptions); a "45+" listing line is NOT a valid BC age bylaw. - **Property transfer tax (PTT) on land:** 1% on the first $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2 million, 3% above $2 million, plus a further 2% on the residential portion above $3 million. Worked anchors (inputs + tier math): $500K → $8,000; $700K → $12,000; $800K → $14,000; $900K → $16,000; $1.0M → $18,000; $1.2M → $22,000; $1.5M → $28,000; $1.8M → $34,000; $2.0M → $38,000; $3.5M → $93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M. (A townhomes sibling elsewhere in the program carries $78,000 for $3.5M — that figure is an OPEN reconciliation item; this file follows $93,000. No sibling for this area carries the $78,000 figure.) Land routes into the home-based exemptions: the First-Time Home Buyers' Program can be kept by a buyer who registers a vacant lot and builds, if lot value at registration plus construction cost is $835,000 or less (partial under $860,000), building and moving in by the first anniversary of registration; the Newly Built Home Exemption can refund PTT paid on a vacant lot once a qualifying newly built home is constructed on it ($1,100,000 full / $1,150,000 partial). An infill parcel bought to hold or to resell as land gets neither. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax and https://wowa.ca/calculators/bc-property-transfer-tax
- **GST on vacant land — stated only at the CRA GI-003 level:** CRA Info Sheet GI-003 states most sales of vacant land by individuals are exempt (land kept for personal use is the example), while an individual's sale can be taxable where the land is capital property used primarily in a business, is sold in the course of a business, or is a parcel created by subdividing another parcel into more than two parts. That last branch is the central-area trap: the Fir Street pattern — subdivide, then sell the lots — sits on the taxable side of the CRA's line when carried on as a business, while a homeowner's one-time sale of a personal-use lot generally does not. Anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED; treatment of any specific parcel is UNCONFIRMED until vendor status and parcel history are established; state the GST position in the contract. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Speculation and vacancy tax:** the Province's taxable-areas list names the City of Abbotsford — owners of residential-class property must complete the annual declaration. A vacant infill parcel's class and any under-construction exemption are parcel-specific: UNCONFIRMED for a single lot until its assessment class and the current exemption list are read. Vancouver's Empty Homes Tax (3%) is Vancouver ONLY and does not apply here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- **Federal prohibition on purchase by non-Canadians:** this file states only what its cited sources support. The March 27, 2023 regulatory amendments repealed the provision that had made vacant land zoned residential or mixed-use inside a census metropolitan area or census agglomeration "residential property" — as amended, vacant land itself can be purchased by a non-Canadian, including for residential development, per the law-firm summaries; dwellings remain governed by the prohibition and specific application is UNCONFIRMED at the official-text level in this file — the position is NOT conformed beyond that source. https://www.mross.com/what-we-think/article/update-canadian-government-prohibits-non-canadians-from-purchasing-residential-real-estate
- **First-home programs — the land boundary, stated precisely:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent MARCH 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself — 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. It becomes relevant on a central parcel only when a qualifying new home is built and bought on it. GST New Housing Rebate: 36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out, likewise housing-only. Home Buyers' Plan $60,000 per buyer; First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html

The court-sale process itself is the settled BC process (Supreme Court Civil Rules 21-7 / 13-5, Practice Direction PD-66, Form 60). Whether Form B diligence or a specific PTT/GST treatment applies to a given court sale is UNCONFIRMED in this file and is kept distinct from the CRA GI-003 vacant-land position above. ## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can I buy an acreage in Central Abbotsford?** No — there is no acreage market on this ground. The sub-area is a built-out urban core of city lots (zoning minimums run 300–540 m²). A buyer who needs acreage is looking at Abbotsford's rural sub-areas, not the core; what you can buy here is an individual infill or teardown parcel, priced on its RS3/RS5/RS7 zoning and its plan designation, or — as a developer — assembled ground like the Rail District site, which is a master-plan land play, not acreage. (Section 2 above.)
- **Q: A listing calls a central property an "acreage" — what is it really?** Almost certainly a larger city lot or a development site using the word loosely. Test it three ways: the lot area on title against the zone minimums in Section 3, the ALC map for any ALR claim (no ALR parcel is documented in this sub-area), and the plan designation — the Rail District's "19 acres," for example, is a master-plan site area, not a parcel for sale. (§2–§3 above)
- **Q: How small can a new central lot be?** RS7 (Infill Residential Zone) allows new interior lots down to 300 m², with minimum width 10.0 m and depth 22.5 m; RS3 minimum interior lot is 540 m² and RS5 is 420 m². But reaching RS7 usually takes a rezoning — the documented Fir Street route rezoned RS3-i to RS7 for a three-lot subdivision, with servicing, amenity and covenant conditions attached. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58178/ and https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
- **Q: What is an older central house actually worth as land?** Usually its unit potential: s.140 allows 3 units on lots of 280 m² or less and 4 units on lots over 280 m² in the stated conditions, one secondary suite is permitted in all single detached dwellings (s.140.3.8), and the plan designations (Urban 2 – Ground Oriented; Urban 1 – Midrise to 6 storeys) set the larger forms. Confirm the zone section and designation for the parcel before paying for any of it — the SSMUH bylaw number in some material ("3560-2024") is UNCONFIRMED. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400140
- **Q: Is the parcel serviced, and who pays for upgrades?** The core is built urban ground on municipal services, but subdivision and redevelopment files carry Development Bylaw works-and-services conditions — the Fir Street report secures road and utility upgrades and extensions by development agreement or cash-in-lieu, plus a Community Amenity Contribution on that file. The servicing bill for a specific parcel is in its Works and Services Report: UNCONFIRMED until that report is read. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
- **Q: Do I pay GST on a teardown or a subdivided lot?** It turns on the seller and the parcel's history, per CRA GI-003: an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt, but a sale in the course of a business or of a parcel created by subdividing into more than two parts can be taxable. A developer-sold newly created lot is the taxable pattern; a long-held family lot usually is not. Have the contract allocate the GST expressly. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **Q: Can PTT exemptions help on a lot purchase?** Only through building: a first-time buyer who registers a vacant lot and builds can keep the FTHB exemption if lot value plus build cost totals $835,000 or less (partial under $860,000) and they build and move in by the first anniversary; the Newly Built Home Exemption can refund lot PTT once a qualifying new home stands on it ($1.1M / $1.15M thresholds). Holding land bare earns neither. https://wowa.ca/calculators/bc-property-transfer-tax
- **Q: What are the holding costs while I assemble or permit?** Property taxes on the due-date cycle (July 2, 2026; 5% July 3; second 5% September 16, 2026), the annual speculation and vacancy tax declaration for residential-class property in Abbotsford, insurance, and any tenant/tenancy obligations on a house bought as a teardown. There is no strata fee on a fee-simple parcel. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- **Q: Is flood risk a central-area issue?** The core is upland, not Sumas Prairie — but Willband Creek runs through the Historic Downtown / central area, so parcels near the creek still need the City floodplain map check. Neighbourhood-level reassurance is not a parcel-level answer. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- **Q: Which schools serve a central lot?** Two locator chains are settled (SD34's settled runs cover all 13 addresses in its program run; these two are this area's samples, each on a single-address basis): 33130 Bevan Ave returns Godson Elementary → Abbotsford Middle → Abbotsford Senior Secondary, and 32161 Dormick Avenue returns Dormick Park Elementary → Abbotsford Middle → Abbotsford Senior Secondary (SD34 School Locator, mybaragar.com, run 2026-11-01). Any other address needs its own run. https://swift.abbyschools.ca/ourschool/kindergartenregistration/swift
- **Q: How long does a small subdivision take here?** No official timeline is published in any source checked: UNCONFIRMED. The documented sequence is rezoning (where the infill zone is not already in place), Approving Officer review, works-and-services conditions, amenity contribution and s.219 covenants before final approval — price the carrying time on that sequence, not on a promised date. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The core's smallest-lot zone is also its rezoning story.** RS7's 300 m² minimum (10.0 m × 22.5 m) is among the smallest detached lot minimums in Abbotsford, and central files reach it by application — the September 2025 Fir Street report shows Council attaching a density-limiting s.219 covenant (maximum two units per lot on the sub-540 m² lots created) to the very subdivision the rezoning enabled. Small-lot does not automatically mean maximum-density: read the covenants registered on the new titles. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/58305/
