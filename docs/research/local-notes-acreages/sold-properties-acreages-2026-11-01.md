## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **Who owns and runs the MLS® system:** at the national level, the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA), a trade association, owns the trademarks Multiple Listing Service® ("MLS®") and REALTOR®, and comprises over 100,000 real estate brokers and agents across 100 local real estate boards and associations across Canada. To use the MLS® system to facilitate real estate transactions, agents must be a member of CREA and agree to comply with CREA's rules. Agents and brokers must become members of a local real estate board and CREA in order to access the MLS® system, which the Competition Bureau describes as a comprehensive database of current and historical listing information that is crucial in the provision of residential real estate brokerage services; members must adhere to both CREA's rules, such as the REALTOR® Code, and the rules of their respective local real estate boards. For this site, the local boards named in the page's own Reciprocity notice are the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® (GVR), the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board (FVREB) and the Chilliwack and District Real Estate Board (CADREB) — the sold feed spans all three board territories, matching the Abbotsford / Surrey / Langley / Chilliwack / Port Coquitlam / Vancouver spread on its first page. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage and https://jaredgibbons.ca/sold-properties.html
- **The 2010 CREA consent agreement (Competition Bureau) — careful reading required:** in early 2010 the Commissioner of Competition brought an application to the Competition Tribunal alleging CREA was abusing its dominant position in the market for residential real estate in Canada, on the basis that some CREA rules prevented agents from offering more innovative services and pricing options to consumers. In October 2010 a consent agreement with CREA was reached; registered with the Tribunal to remain in force for a 10-year period, it required CREA not to adopt, maintain or enforce rules that deny real estate agents the ability to provide mere postings, or that discriminate against agents who might offer mere postings, and it also encompassed areas such as negotiations regarding the cooperating seller's commission and where a seller's information can be displayed. That consent agreement was about listing services and display of a seller's information — it is cited here as the documented federal milestone in how MLS® rules are governed, NOT as the source of any current BC sold-data display rule. The agreement's 10-year term as described in the Bureau's submission has run; what replaced or followed it is UNVERIFIED in this file. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage
- **Sold-data display: the documented precedent is Ontario's, not BC's:** the Competition Bureau's litigation against the Toronto Real Estate Board (TREB) concerned exactly the display of sold data online: the Commissioner argued TREB's Virtual Office Website (VOW) policies prohibited VOWs from displaying information regarding sold and "pending sold" homes, offers to the cooperating broker, and withdrawn, expired, suspended or terminated listings. The Competition Tribunal agreed with the Commissioner, categorically rejected TREB's argument that its restrictions were primarily motivated by consumer privacy concerns regarding sold information, and held those concerns were a pretext; its remedy required TREB, among other things, to provide certain data fields in VOW feeds and allow the data to be displayed on VOWs on any device. Careful reading: the TREB case is a Greater Toronto Area precedent about VOW (password-protected client website) display. It documents that sold-data display in Canada has been a contested, litigated question governed board by board — it is NOT a statement of the FVREB or GVR rule, and no BC board's sold-display rule is stated in this file, because neither board publishes its MLS® rules on a public page that could be read for this research. https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/promotion-and-advocacy/regulatory-adviceinterventions-competition-bureau/submission-ftc-and-doj-residential-real-estate-brokerage

- **The BC regulator's consumer rules around any sale on this page:** in British Columbia, real estate trading services are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA); a licensed real estate professional in BC must only work for either the buyer or the seller in a transaction, must inform consumers of the duties and responsibilities owed to clients and non-clients, and must inform unrepresented consumers of the risks of dealing with a professional who is representing another party. BCFSA also requires disclosure to consumers at the outset of dealings — whether the professional will be able to represent the consumer as a client, what the duties to clients and non-clients are, and how to file a complaint with BCFSA — and states that dual agency (representing two or more clients in the same transaction whose interests conflict) was prohibited in BC in 2018 except in the rarest of circumstances. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/protecting-real-estate-consumers
