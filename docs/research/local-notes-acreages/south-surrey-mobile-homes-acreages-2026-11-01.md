- **SSMUH / suites / short-term rentals (Surrey lots):** Surrey's SSMUH framework took effect July 8, 2024; the bands are 3 units on lots under 280 m², 4 units on larger lots, and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop. A secondary suite is capped at 40% of the principal dwelling's habitable floor area. Short-term rentals are permitted only in the operator's own principal residence, owner-only, with a City business licence at $358/year as stated on the City's licence page — DISCLOSED CONFLICT: a City of Surrey 2024 corporate report proposed $350/year; the licence page's $358 figure is carried with the $350 figure disclosed. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing and https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/permits-licences/secondary-suites and https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/permits-licences/short-term-rentals
- **Streamside / flood position (Surrey):** Surrey's streamside protection runs under Zoning Bylaw Part 7A, with setbacks set BY STREAM CLASS — there is NO single riparian figure for Surrey ground, and none is stated in this file. Parcel-level flood/streamside position: UNVERIFIED per parcel.
- **MHPTA frame — the frame that replaces land law for a park home:** manufactured home site tenancies are covered by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act; landlords and tenants must have a written tenancy agreement for the site. On a sale where the home stays in the park, assignment under MHPTA s.28 requires the landlord's prior written consent (or consent deemed given under the regulations), an order of the director, or authorization in the tenancy agreement; before asking, the seller must give the proposed buyer written copies of the park rules and the relevant parts of the tenancy agreement; the landlord cannot charge a fee for consent; and on assignment the pad rent for the new tenant stays the same, as does the scheduled date of a subsequent rent increase. The park owner must respond within 10 days of the written request (RTB Form 10) and silence counts as consent given, per the siblings' record. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- **Pad-rent increases — Act percentages ONLY; NO pad-rent dollar figure is published as fact in this file:** a park landlord may increase rent only with 3 full months' notice, only once every 12 months, and only up to the yearly limit — **2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027.** The proportional amount is the change in local government levies plus the change in regulated park utility fees, divided by the number of manufactured home sites in the park; further increases require a Residential Tenancy Branch application. Any pad-rent range or fee figure on the live page (including a feed card's monthly-fee line) is UNVERIFIED site/listing copy, flagged as such and never republished as a figure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Park closure / conversion (MHPTA s.42):** where a landlord intends to convert all or a significant part of a park to a non-residential use or a residential use other than a manufactured home park, the landlord must have all necessary government permits and approvals in place before issuing notice, must give 12 months' notice covering a full year, and must pay the tenant $20,000 compensation on or before the notice's effective date; the tenant may move out early on at least 10 days' written notice, may dispute within 15 days, may in defined circumstances apply for additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000, and if the landlord does not take steps toward the stated conversion within a reasonable period the landlord must pay the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent. Surrey's Tenant Protection Bylaw, 2025, No. 21788 does NOT apply to property governed by the MHPTA. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/end-tenancy-manufactured-homes and https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_reg_21788.pdf
- **Registration and certification (every manufactured-home sale, every tenure in this topic):** the sale, transfer or purchase of a manufactured home is only effective if the transaction is registered with the Manufactured Home Registry, through a lawyer or notary filing the original signed and witnessed Bill of Sale and Notice to Transfer or Change Ownership forms. Manufactured homes include modular and mobile homes; the CSA label states the standard — modular homes under A-277, mobile homes under Z-240. A home may not be sold without its original legible CSA decal; a lost label requires Technical Safety BC re-certification (a licensed electrical contractor inspects the wiring and an electrical approval label is prepared in place of the CSA label). A permit to move a home is issued by the Registry, a tax certificate for transport purposes is required (with exceptions including a move to a different pad within the same park), and the transport permit expires 30 days after issue. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/owning-a-home/manufactured-home-registry
- **Taxes on a home in a park:** a manufactured home owner is assessed and taxed like other residential property owners, but the assessed value will not include the value of the land if the home is in a manufactured home park not on reserve land, or on land privately owned by someone other than the home owner. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/annual-property-tax/manufactured-home
- **ALR RULES IN FULL — the governing frame wherever a parcel on this ground is in the reserve (no feed parcel's status is confirmed either way, §2):** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified — cannot be converted into strata lots and sold separately; the deposit of a strata plan is a subdivision (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). Residence caps as the ALC states them: one principal residence per parcel, maximum gross floor area 500 m²; a secondary suite permitted ONLY inside the principal residence; an additional residence capped at 90 m² on parcels of 40 ha or less and 186 m² on parcels over 40 ha. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Is a manufactured home on a pad an acreage purchase?** No — a rented pad is a tenancy, not a lot purchase: no title to land transfers, there is no Form B, no CRF and no depreciation report, and the home's assessed value excludes the land. The land questions for a park home are the park rules, the site tenancy agreement and the MHPTA assignment in §3. (§§2–3.)
- **Q: Do I own the land when I buy a manufactured home in South Surrey?** It depends on the community, and this page's feed holds both forms: in a manufactured home park you generally own the home and rent the site under a Manufactured Home Site Tenancy Agreement governed by the MHPTA, while in own-land communities you buy the land with the home — the Cranley Drive and 20 Avenue cards state the buyer owns the land, and the 160 Street cards are park-pad product. The Cranley Drive community's title form is UNVERIFIED; at Sea Ridge Bays (20 Avenue, cross-referenced in §2) the land ownership is bare-land strata title. https://jaredgibbons.ca/south-surrey-mobile-homes.html
- **Q: Is Breakaway Bays a 55+ park?** NOT verified 55+ — the directory says age-restricted (55+), the MLS listings say "NO AGE RESTRICTION" / "Family Park," and this file follows the MLS basis with the conflict flagged. Read the written park rules for the specific home before relying on any age claim. (§2.)
- **Q: What is Sea Ridge Bays, in land terms?** A bare-land strata — the program's sole bare-land-strata park variant: you own the home AND the strata lot at 15875 20 Avenue, pay a strata fee instead of pad rent (listing-stated at $210/month including water, never a fact here), and take a Form B. It is documented ONCE in this topic's lots-and-land home file and cross-referenced here only. (§2.)
- **Q: Does the park have to approve me, and can it charge for that?** Yes to approval, no to a fee: assignment of the site tenancy requires the landlord's prior written consent under MHPTA s.28 (or consent deemed given under the regulations), the seller must first give you the written park rules and the relevant tenancy agreement terms, and the landlord cannot charge a fee for giving consent. (§3.)
- **Q: How much can pad rent go up?** Up to the yearly limit — 2.3% plus a proportional amount for 2026, and 2.2% plus a proportional amount for 2027 — once every 12 months, on 3 full months' notice. On assignment to a new owner the rent and the next increase date carry over unchanged. No pad-rent dollar figure is published as fact in this file. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/manufactured-homes/rent-increases-for-manufactured-homes
- **Q: What happens if a South Surrey park closes or is converted?** Under MHPTA s.42, as the Province states it: the landlord must have all permits and approvals first, then give 12 months' notice and pay $20,000 compensation on or before the effective date; tenants may leave early on 10 days' written notice, may dispute within 15 days, and may in defined circumstances claim additional compensation of the home's assessed value minus the $20,000; if the landlord does not proceed within a reasonable period, a further payment of the greater of $5,000 or 12 months' rent applies. Surrey's 2025 Tenant Protection Bylaw expressly does not cover park residents. (§3.)
