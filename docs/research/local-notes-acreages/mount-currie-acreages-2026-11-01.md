## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES

- Community and governance: Mount Currie is the main community of the Líl̓wat Nation (Ucwalmícwts-speaking; the band was formerly styled the Mount Currie Indian Band). The Nation states the community is home to approximately 1,495 of its more than 2,275 citizens, and is named for Mount Currie, the 2,591 m (8,501 ft) mountain at the centre of Líl̓wat Traditional Territory. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- Land status: the community sits on Líl̓wat reserve lands — the Nation describes 10 reserves totalling about 2,930 ha, within a Traditional Territory of approximately 780,000 hectares extending north to Gates Lake, east to the Upper Stein Valley and west to the coastal inlets. Reserve lands are federal lands set aside for the Nation; they are not a municipality, and the surrounding unincorporated Pemberton Valley is SLRD Electoral Area C. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/ and https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf
- Neighbourhoods inside the reserve lands: the Nation's Community Housing Plan names four — Mount Currie Village (IR 1 and IR 10), the centre with the administrative and civic facilities; Xet̓ólacw Village (IR 6), also called the Upper Community, the largest residential neighbourhood; Lillooet Lake Road (IR 3 and IR 8); and Kwetsa7 (IR 2), the last two less developed and rural residential in character. These are Nation neighbourhoods, not subdivisions for sale. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf
- Location: approximately 160 km north of Vancouver and approximately 35 km north of Whistler via Highway 99, and also approachable via the Duffy Lake Road (Highway 99 east); the Village of Pemberton is the neighbouring municipality a few kilometres west — the exact road distance on an official source is UNVERIFIED. Statistics Canada treats "Mount Currie" as an Indian reserve census subdivision (code 5931843) within the SLRD. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/

## 2. THE ACREAGE ITSELF

**No acreage market in Mount Currie — and, stronger, NO bare-land market exists on Líl̓wat Nation reserve land at all, stated plainly at the top: neither fee-simple nor bare-land strata exists there. There are no acreage parcels, lots or land for a member of the public to buy, no named acreage subdivision, and no presale land offering. What exists instead — and what this section documents — is the Nation's own land-allocation system for its citizens, under which serviced lots are allocated and registered through the Nation's process. Verified acreage developments counted here: 0, as a structural zero, not a search gap.**

- **The allocation system itself — the Five Steps to Home Ownership on Reserve, as the mount-currie lots-and-land home file documents them:** (1) meet the **Housing Manager**; (2) a **land encumbrance check from the Lands Department confirming a serviced lot is available and registered to the person**; (3) a **Band Council Resolution — the Band Council acts as guarantor on the loan**; (4) possible **housing subsidies from Indigenous Services Canada**; (5) a **Ministerial Loan Guarantee** and bank financing. The serviced lot is allocated and registered through that process — it is never listed, priced or sold on an open market. Standard lot dimensions on an official source: UNVERIFIED. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf (~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/mount-currie-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md)
- **Tenure — neither freehold nor strata:** housing is Nation-administered on reserve land — band housing, and member home ownership through the Nation's process with a Band Council Resolution and Ministerial Loan Guarantee. This is neither freehold nor strata, and homes do not come to the open market for general buyers; Mount Currie housing must never be presented as freehold or strata in any page copy. Typical types are single-detached family homes in the village neighbourhoods, including newer subdivision homes (Nation subdivisions for citizens, not market subdivisions) and modular/manufactured homes built to CSA Z-240. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf and https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Lilwat-Nation-Housing-Plan-2018-2033.pdf
- **New housing, in the Nation's own terms:** the Nation is building new housing in both the Mount Currie and Xet̓ólacw communities, with a goal of 12 units per year, under its Community Housing Plan (2018–2033). This is a Nation housing program for citizens, documented as such — never as developer inventory or presale stock. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- **Building costs (Nation guidance, not land prices):** the Nation's home-ownership material puts new construction of a modest home at about $250–$300 per square foot including water, sewer and hydro connections, and modular/pre-manufactured homes built to CSA Z-240 at about $160–$180 per square foot installed and hooked up, noting modulars for Mount Currie must meet zone 6 construction standards and a roof snow load rating of 136 lb per square foot. These are the Nation's construction guidance figures for citizens building on allocated lots — never land or acreage prices. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: Can anyone buy an acreage or a lot in Mount Currie?** No. Mount Currie is the main community of the Líl̓wat Nation on reserve land; housing and serviced lots are allocated through the Nation's own process for its citizens — there is no fee-simple open market and no bare-land market at all. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Q: How does a Líl̓wat citizen get a lot and build?** Through the Five Steps: Housing Manager meeting → Lands Department land encumbrance check confirming a serviced lot is available and registered to you → Band Council Resolution (Council as guarantor) → ISC housing subsidies check → Ministerial Loan Guarantee and bank financing. (Section 2.)
- **Q: Is there bare-land strata or fee-simple land anywhere in the community?** Neither exists on the reserve lands as documented — tenure is Nation-administered, neither freehold nor strata. Do not present any Mount Currie address as freehold or strata. (Section 2.)
- **Q: I saw "Mt. Currie View Village" — is that Mount Currie land?** No. It is at 7370 Highway 99 in the Village of Pemberton — manufactured homes on rented pads in a Pemberton park, named for the view of the mountain. Different municipality, different form, pad-rental tenure, and a pad home is not land ownership anywhere. (Section 2.)
- **Q: What does building a home cost on an allocated lot?** The Nation's guidance: about $250–$300 per sq ft for a modest new build including water, sewer and hydro connections, or about $160–$180 per sq ft installed for a CSA Z-240 modular, which must meet zone 6 standards and a 136 lb per sq ft roof snow load rating for Mount Currie. https://lilwat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/LWN7610_BuildingCommunitiesFlatsheet.pdf
- **Q: Do standard taxes, PTT and mortgages work there?** No — standard municipal/regional taxes and PTT do not apply to reserve land the way they apply off reserve (the Nation's own taxation laws or per-lot fees are UNVERIFIED), and financing runs through the Band Council Resolution as guarantor plus a Ministerial Loan Guarantee, so standard mortgage and foreclosure mechanics do not apply the same way. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- **Q: Which schools serve Mount Currie?** Xet̓ólacw Community School is the Nation's own school in the community; under the Local Education Agreement with School District 48, Líl̓wat students also attend Signal Hill Elementary and Pemberton Secondary School in Pemberton, with SD48 Bus 5 serving Mount Currie (stops at IR 10 Road and Spirit Circle on Pemberton Portage Road). These are the schools named in the Nation/district sources — no catchment is inferred beyond them, and grade range/enrolment for Xet̓ólacw Community School are UNVERIFIED. https://lilwat.ca/departments/tszil-language-and-culture/advanced-education/ and https://22.files.edl.io/3fff/09/03/25/214527-07fe5ac2-2ab8-4eb4-9bfd-69ad562e6006.pdf
- **Q: Is there transit between Mount Currie, Pemberton and Whistler?** Yes — BC Transit's Pemberton Valley Transit local service connects Xit'olacw and Mt Currie to the Village of Pemberton, and a commuter service runs Pemberton–Whistler; the service is cost-shared by BC Transit, the Village of Pemberton, the SLRD and the Líl̓wat Nation. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/889/862/pem_rg_sept2020.pdf
- **Q: If I want fee-simple acreage near Mount Currie, where do I look?** Off reserve, in the Pemberton Valley / Village of Pemberton market — a different jurisdiction with municipal zoning, standard taxes and MLS sales. That ground is documented in its own Pemberton files in this program, never in this file, and no Pemberton parcel is presented here as Mount Currie stock. https://lilwat.ca/our-community/about-us/
- **Q: Why are there no market stats for Mount Currie?** Neither SnapStats report covers the Pemberton area at all, and MLS activity is not a meaningful measure of a Nation-administered housing system on reserve land. No figure is substituted. (Section 1.)
