## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **PAGE DEFECT, restated precisely (this slug only):** the live page states the premium "ranges from 2.80% to 4.00% of the loan amount (plus 0.20% for 30-year insured amortizations)." That range does not match CMHC's own published range of **0.6% to 4.5%** re-read for this file, and the 0.20% 30-year surcharge was not confirmed on a CMHC or Finance page in this pass. This file adopts CMHC's published range only. The other two approved insurers (Sagen and Canada Guaranty) are named on the live page; their premium schedules were not read in this pass and no figure from them is stated here. https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html and https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **How interest is calculated — semi-annual compounding:** under the federal Interest Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. I-15, s.6, where principal and interest secured by a mortgage on real property are payable on a blended-payment plan, no interest is chargeable unless the mortgage contains a statement showing the principal and the rate of interest chargeable on it, "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" — the statutory basis for the Canadian convention that fixed mortgage rates are stated as nominal rates compounded semi-annually, which is why a Canadian payment calculation converts the nominal rate to a monthly rate using semi-annual compounding rather than dividing by 12. The live page states the same convention for fixed rates; its variable-rate monthly-compounding statement is a lender-practice statement, not a statutory rule, and is UNVERIFIED as a rule. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html and https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **Minimum down payment (Government of Canada / CMHC):** for homes $500,000 or less, a minimum 5% down payment; for homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on any amount over $500,000; homes $1,500,000 or more require a minimum down payment of 20% and mortgage loan insurance is not available. The Department of Finance Canada confirms the same structure as the insured-mortgage rule that took effect with the December 15, 2024 cap increase. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained and https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Mortgage loan insurance (CMHC) — thresholds and premiums:** insurance is required in Canada when a homebuyer makes a down payment of less than 20% of the home's purchase price; the insurance protects the lender, not the buyer, and allows a buyer to qualify with a down payment as low as 5%. Eligibility frame as published by CMHC: insurance is needed where the down payment is less than 20% AND the home price is $1.5 million or less (equivalently, loan-to-value over 80%); it is not needed at 20% or more down, and it is not available at all on homes priced over $1.5 million. Premiums as published by CMHC: based on the size of the down payment and the loan amount — the smaller the down payment, the higher the premium rate — premium rates range from **0.6% to 4.5%** of the total mortgage amount, and the premium can be paid in full at the time of purchase or added to the mortgage. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **The mortgage stress test — OSFI Guideline B-20 / minimum qualifying rate:** OSFI's minimum qualifying rate (MQR) for uninsured mortgages is currently **the greater of the mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25%** (page date modified 2026-01-29). The buffer is currently 2% and the floor is currently 5.25%; OSFI reviews both at least annually. OSFI obliges federally regulated lenders to apply this stress test, and Guideline B-20 sets OSFI's expectation that lenders apply the MQR to most newly underwritten residential mortgagors. Insured mortgages are tested the same way: FCAC states federally regulated entities require borrowers to pass a stress test at the higher of 5.25% or the contract rate plus 2%, and that "that's the case for insured and uninsured mortgages." https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **The renewal-switch exception:** OSFI does not expect lenders to apply the MQR to an uninsured straight switch at renewal — where a borrower switches their uninsured mortgage from one federally regulated lender to another with no increase to the amortization period and no increase to the loan amount. FCAC adds the related triggers: a borrower with an existing mortgage must pass the stress test again if they refinance their home or take out a home equity line of credit. Provincially regulated lenders (e.g., credit unions) are not federally regulated; FCAC notes lenders that aren't federally regulated may also ask a borrower to pass a stress test — whether any given BC credit union applies it is lender-specific and UNVERIFIED here. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Amortization rules:** insured mortgages are those with less than 20% down, available only on homes priced at $1.5 million or less (the insured price cap, raised from $1 million effective December 15, 2024). Eligibility for 30-year insured amortizations was extended to **all first-time homebuyers** and to **all buyers of new builds**, effective December 15, 2024, building on the August 1, 2024 measure for first-time homebuyers purchasing new builds. The exact general insured maximum (25 years) for insured borrowers outside those two groups was NOT stated on a regulator page re-read in this pass and is not asserted as a verified figure here. The Department of Finance's detailed "first-time homebuyer" criteria for the 30-year measure were not re-read: UNVERIFIED — do not encode a definition from secondary sources. Effect of amortization choice (arithmetic, not a rule): a longer amortization lowers the payment at any given rate and raises total interest over the life of the loan. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Affordability ratios — GDS and TDS (FCAC):** Gross Debt Service — total monthly housing costs should not be more than 39% of gross household income; monthly housing costs are the mortgage payments, property taxes, heating costs and 50% of condo fees (if applicable). Total Debt Service — total debt load should not be more than 44% of gross income (total monthly housing costs plus all other debts, including credit card balances, car loans, lines of credit, student loans, child or spousal support and any other debts). FCAC frames 39% and 44% as guidelines, noting a borrower may still qualify with ratios slightly higher but is increasing risk — a calculator should present them as guideline thresholds, not hard approval cut-offs. FCAC's own Mortgage Qualifier Tool uses the same 39% / 44%. The live page's GDS/TDS description matches the FCAC composition (principal and interest, property taxes, heating estimate, 50% of strata fees). https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Closing costs this calculator points to — BC property transfer tax, worked examples with inputs and tier math shown:** general rates verified on the Province's page: 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% of the portion greater than $200,000 and up to and including $2,000,000; 3% of the portion greater than $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on the residential property value greater than $3,000,000. PTT is based on fair market value on the day of registration at the Land Title Office and is separate from annual property taxes; the exemptions are researched in the first-time-buyer-calculator file. Anchor checks, standard rates, no exemption unless stated: **$500K→$8,000; $700K→$12,000; $800K cap→$14,000 ($6,000 for a fully-qualifying first-time buyer after the $8,000 saving); $900K→$16,000; $1.0M→$18,000; $1.1M→$20,000; $1.2M→$22,000; $1.5M→$28,000; $1.8M→$34,000; $2.0M→$38,000; $3.5M→$93,000 INCLUDING the further 2% tier over $3M (tier math: $2,000 + $36,000 + $45,000 + $10,000)** — a townhomes sibling in this program (south-surrey-homes-over-1-8m) carries $78,000 for $3.5M; this file follows $93,000, flags the conflict wherever met, and never silently carries $78,000. A PTT row reading "Over $3M — 5%" is a compression of the real structure (3% on the portion over $2,000,000 plus a further 2% on the residential portion over $3,000,000), not a single 5% rate — flagged wherever met; this file's research does not document such a row on THIS page, and none is asserted against it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax
- **GST and closing-cost figures the page names:** GST on a newly built home is 5% in BC; the federal rebate programs that can offset it are researched in the first-time-buyer-calculator file and are not restated here beyond the standing frame below. Legal/notary fees, title insurance, home inspection and property-tax adjustments are real closing-cost categories named on the live page, but the live page's dollar range for legal/notary fees ($1,500–$2,500) is not an official figure and was not verified on any official source: UNVERIFIED as a figure — a calculator should either take these as user inputs or omit amounts. https://jaredgibbons.ca/mortgage-calculator.html
- **GST on vacant land — ONLY at CRA GI-003 level (the acreage buyer's closing-cost question):** developer/business sales and newly subdivided lots are generally TAXABLE; an individual's sale of personal-use land is generally exempt. Anything finer than GI-003 is UNCONFIRMED in this file — and no acreage purchase should have a GST figure produced by this calculator beyond that frame without advice on the specific parcel. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/gi-003/sales-vacant-land-individuals.html
- **First-home programs — the land boundary:** the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST Rebate (Bill C-4, Royal Assent MARCH 13, 2026 per CRA News No. 122) applies to new HOUSING, not bare land itself — 100% of GST to $1 million (maximum $50,000), phasing out to $1.5 million, agreements on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031; a source stating March 12 is noted in-line where met and never silently overwritten; a live page's $1.25M straight-line phase-out row is UNVERIFIED as a mechanism (CRA says only "gradually reduced"). The GST New Housing Rebate (36%, maximum $6,300, $350,000–$450,000 phase-out) is likewise housing-only. Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal limit $60,000 per buyer (plus the 2026–2028 repayment-relief deferral carried in this program); First Home Savings Account $40,000 lifetime / $8,000 per year. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/news122/news122-excise-gst-hst-news-no-122.html
- **BC strata block — where the purchase priced into the tool is strata:** Form B diligence; depreciation reports on a 5-year cycle for stratas with 5+ lots since July 1, 2024; minimum 10% CRF contribution since November 1, 2023; rental-restriction bylaws VOID since November 24, 2022; only 55+ age bylaws valid (SPA ss.123.1/123.2, May 1, 2023 exemptions). The calculator-side consequence is the GDS line above: 50% of strata/condo fees count inside monthly housing costs. A fee-simple acreage carries no strata fees to enter. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK

- **Q: What rate does the stress test use?** The greater of your mortgage contract rate plus 2% or 5.25% — OSFI's minimum qualifying rate, applied by federally regulated lenders to insured and uninsured mortgages alike; OSFI reviews the 2% buffer and the 5.25% floor at least annually. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages and https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: What is the minimum down payment in Canada?** 5% on homes of $500,000 or less; on homes between $500,000 and $1,499,999, 5% on the first $500,000 plus 10% on the portion over $500,000; at $1,500,000 or more, a minimum of 20% — and mortgage loan insurance is not available at that level. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: When do I need mortgage loan insurance, and what does it cost?** Whenever your down payment is under 20% on a home priced at $1.5 million or less. CMHC publishes premium rates ranging from **0.6% to 4.5%** of the total mortgage amount, depending on the down payment size and loan amount; the premium can be paid up front or added to the mortgage. If the live page shows 2.80%–4.00% + 0.20%, that is the page defect flagged at the top of this file — follow CMHC. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Q: Can I get a 30-year amortization?** On an insured mortgage (under 20% down), 30-year amortizations have been available to all first-time homebuyers and to all buyers of new builds since December 15, 2024; on an uninsured mortgage (20% or more down) 30 years is a lender offering rather than an insured-program rule, so it is confirmed with the lender, not the calculator. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
- **Q: How much of my income can go to housing and debt?** FCAC's guidelines: total monthly housing costs (mortgage payments, property taxes, heating, 50% of condo fees) should not exceed 39% of gross household income (GDS), and total debt load including all other debts should not exceed 44% (TDS); slightly higher ratios may still qualify but carry more risk. https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/mortgages/preparing-mortgage.html
- **Q: Why does the calculator run my payment twice?** Because the lender sizes the approval at the qualifying rate, not the contract rate: run the payment once at your contract rate (what you will actually pay) and once at the greater of contract rate plus 2% or 5.25% (the number the lender uses). https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **Q: I'm buying bare land or an acreage — do the same down-payment tiers apply to me?** UNCONFIRMED in this file's research: the CMHC and Finance material read here is framed on homes, and no official page read for this file states a land-loan down-payment tier. Do not let the calculator's home tiers stand in for land terms — confirm them with the lender for the specific parcel. (Section 2.)
- **Q: Do I face the stress test at renewal?** Not on a qualifying uninsured straight switch — OSFI does not expect the MQR on a switch to another federally regulated lender with no increase to the loan amount or amortization. A refinance or a new HELOC triggers the test again, so model the two paths separately. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **Q: Does the calculator's answer mean I'm approved?** No. A calculator computes payments and guideline ratios; the underwriting decision sits with the federally regulated lender under Guideline B-20, and the insurer sets its own eligibility on insured loans. The output is an estimate to take to a lender or broker. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **Q: What closing costs sit outside the mortgage math?** BC property transfer tax alone runs 1% to $200,000, 2% to $2,000,000 and 3% above that on fair market value before any exemption (worked anchors in Section 3) — cash needed at completion on top of the down payment. The page's legal/notary dollar range is UNVERIFIED as a figure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW

- **The page's own premium range is the outlier, not CMHC's.** CMHC's published range (0.6%–4.5%) is wider at both ends than the live page's 2.80%–4.00% — a buyer at the smallest down payment (highest premium rate, up to 4.5%) is exactly the buyer the page's range understates, and a buyer at a large down payment (lowest rate, down to 0.6%) is the buyer it overstates. The 0.20% 30-year surcharge could not be confirmed on any CMHC or Finance page in the research pass. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/observer/2025/cmhc-mortgage-loan-insurance-explained
- **Canadian fixed rates are not divided by 12.** The Interest Act's "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" statement requirement is why a Canadian calculator converts a nominal fixed rate using semi-annual compounding — a tool that simply divides by 12 overstates the monthly rate. Whether THIS live calculator implements it that way is NOT CONFIRMED, because its formulas are not in the page text and no calculation was submitted. https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-15/FullText.html
- **The renewal switch is the one stress-test-free move left.** A straight uninsured switch at renewal (same amount, same amortization, another federally regulated lender) is not expected to be stress-tested — but the moment the switch becomes a refinance or adds a HELOC, the test returns. Owners who model only one path misprice the other. https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/minimum-qualifying-rate-uninsured-mortgages
- **December 15, 2024 moved two lines at once.** The insured price cap rose from $1 million to $1.5 million and 30-year insured amortizations opened to all first-time buyers and all new-build buyers on the same effective date — material written before that date understates both. https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/remarks-by-the-deputy-prime-minister-announcing-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html
