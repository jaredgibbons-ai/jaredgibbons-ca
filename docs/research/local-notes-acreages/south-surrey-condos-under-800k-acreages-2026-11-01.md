## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT ACREAGES

- **OCP (Surrey side): Surrey 2050 OCP Bylaw No. 21900 (adopted Jun 15, 2026) is CURRENT.** Bylaw 18020 is REPLACED and is never cited as current in this file. - **Zoning (Surrey side): Zoning Bylaw No. 12000 governs.** Secondary suites: one secondary suite may be permitted per single family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit, a suite is not permitted within a coach house or garden suite, and a suite's habitable floor area may not exceed 40% of the principal dwelling unit — the suite cap is 40% of principal habitable area, one per unit, not a separate strata lot. The often-quoted "90 m²" cap is NOT current — no 90 m² suite figure was found in the current consolidated bylaw text, and it is never stated as current in this file. Coach houses and garden suites: all Surrey residential zones that allow detached houses, duplexes and houseplexes also allow a coach house or garden suite, 35 m² minimum to 75 m² maximum, rising to 120 m² for lots zoned RA, R2, R2-O and R3 where the existing house or duplex is below maximum FAR, with lane access required on smaller R3–R6 lots and one extra parking space. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/12000 and https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/residential-building/residential-building-permits/coach-houses-garden-suites
- **SSMUH (Surrey side):** Surrey's July 8, 2024 SSMUH changes allow a maximum of 3 units on lots smaller than 280 m², 4 units on lots larger than 280 m², and up to 6 units on eligible lots of at least 281 m² within 400 m of a frequent bus stop; the changes renamed the former RF-12 / RF-12C zones to the R4 Small Lot Residential Zone and RF-SD to R6 Semi-Detached. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing

- **ALR RULES IN FULL — ALR ground exists at South Surrey's farm-adjacent edges (Hazelmere / Elgin Chantrell side), so the full frame governs wherever a parcel is in the reserve:** subdivision within the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. ALR residences cannot be stratified without the ALC's permission — depositing a strata plan is a subdivision, and a person must not subdivide agricultural land unless permitted by the Commission or under the ALR General Regulation (ALC Bulletin 09 s.6; Strata Property Act s.239; Agricultural Land Commission Act s.21(1)). This is a permission rule, not an absolute ban, and ALC Information Bulletin 05 contains no stratification statement and is not cited for it. Residence caps as the ALC states them: a principal residence must have a total floor area of 500 m² or less, and no more than 1,000 m² of fill (including structural aggregate for foundations and driveways) can be placed for its construction; a secondary suite is permitted ONLY when located within the principal residence, entirely contained within it; one additional residence, detached from the principal residence, is permitted up to 90 m² on properties of 40 ha or less and up to 186 m² on properties over 40 ha, and building more than the permitted number, or larger than the size limits, requires ALC approval. City zoning and the ALC frame both apply; neither displaces the other. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Farm class (BC Assessment) — the full three-tier form, never the flat figure:** a producing farm must meet minimum gross income at farm gate prices of $2,500 if the total area of the farm operation is between 0.8 ha (1.98 acres) and 4 ha (10 acres); $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area in excess of 4 ha if the operation is greater than 4 ha; and $10,000 if the operation is less than 0.8 ha — and new applications for farm classification must be submitted by the legislated deadline of October 31.
