## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Maillardville is the City of Coquitlam's oldest and most historic neighbourhood, in the city's southwest, centred on Brunette Avenue — the commercial main street the City's neighbourhood plan works to restore — with the house streets running north of Brunette Avenue toward Austin Avenue. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- The French-language street names on the house streets are part of the record, not decoration: TransLink's route 153 stops on Brunette Avenue at Laval Street, Schoolhouse Street, Marmont Street, Nelson Street, Lebleu Street, Blue Mountain Street and Bernatchey Street. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt153.pdf
- Shared Maillardville facts — the plan's history, the French-Canadian settlement record and the area's strata stock — are carried in the maillardville-coquitlam-condos-townhomes-for-sale note; this note covers the detached-house side only. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Detached houses are the historic stock here: the Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan's stated work includes conservation of heritage homes and preservation of the existing street, block and lot patterns, which is the small-lot mill-era pattern the house streets still follow. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- Infill on house lots is plan-directed, not free-form: the plan encourages detached Housing Choices development in its Neighbourhood Attached Residential areas, designed to complement the historic and existing built-form character of the streets around it. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- The plan names two Heritage Character Areas on the house streets — Laval Square and Allard-LeBleu — where the heritage character housing is concentrated; no official source read states a typical Maillardville lot size or a build-decade split for the detached stock, so none is claimed. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide Coquitlam zoning, small-scale multi-unit housing, 2026 tax and utility figures and Speculation and Vacancy Tax applicability are stated in full in the chineside-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note; this note covers only the Maillardville house rules. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit
- Heritage buildings on house lots are conserved through Heritage Revitalization Agreements: the Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan requires the conservation of heritage buildings through the use of Heritage Revitalization Agreements as part of redevelopment for projects with heritage buildings. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- Maillardville has its own plan where Chineside and Central Coquitlam do not: the Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan, adopted as Schedule "A" to Bylaw No. 4465, 2014 and now carried in the Interim Official Community Plan's Schedule J list, guides growth and reinvestment in the neighbourhood. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17458
- The plan's design control reaches detached infill: Maillardville has its own residential design guidelines, separate from the city-wide guidelines, and the plan calls for new residential development to conserve heritage buildings and follow the Maillardville-specific design guidelines. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Can I tear down an older Maillardville house and rebuild? **A:** Not without checking the heritage file first. The Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan requires heritage buildings to be conserved through Heritage Revitalization Agreements as part of redevelopment, and the plan's stated direction is conservation of heritage homes and preservation of the existing street, block and lot patterns. A teardown candidate has to be checked against the City's heritage records and the plan's two Heritage Character Areas, Laval Square and Allard-LeBleu, before its value is priced as a bare lot. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- **Q:** Which elementary school is in Maillardville? **A:** Alderson Elementary, kindergarten to Grade 5, at 825 Gauthier Avenue, is the School District No. 43 (Coquitlam) elementary school in the neighbourhood, and École Maillard Middle, Grades 6 to 8, is at 1300 Rochester Avenue. Catchment is still assigned by the exact house address in the district's School Locator, so the school physically in the area is not automatically the assigned school for every Maillardville street. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/043
- **Q:** What growth does the plan allow around the house streets? **A:** The updated Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan was developed to guide growth and reinvestment over 20 to 25 years and to accommodate an additional 6,000 residents and increased employment in the plan area. The growth is directed mainly to the high-density, mixed-use Neighbourhood Centre on Brunette Avenue, while the house streets are handled through heritage conservation, the two Heritage Character Areas and detached Housing Choices infill. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- **Q:** Is there a bus through Maillardville to a SkyTrain station? **A:** Yes — route 153 runs through the neighbourhood on Brunette Avenue. TransLink's timetable routing takes route 153 from Braid Station via Brunette Avenue, Laurentian Crescent, Austin Avenue, Poirier Street, Foster Avenue, Linton Street and Como Lake Avenue to Coquitlam Central Station, with Brunette Avenue stops at Bernatchey Street, Blue Mountain Street, Lebleu Street, Nelson Street, Marmont Street, Laval Street and Schoolhouse Street. Both end stations are SkyTrain stations. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt153.pdf
- **Q:** Are Maillardville houses strata or freehold? **A:** The detached stock on the heritage house streets is freehold; the strata buildings and townhome complexes in Maillardville are covered in the maillardville-coquitlam-condos-townhomes-for-sale note. What a house buyer must verify instead is the heritage record: whether the specific house is a heritage building the plan expects to be conserved through a Heritage Revitalization Agreement on redevelopment. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the heritage status of the specific house before anything else: the plan requires conservation of heritage buildings through Heritage Revitalization Agreements on redevelopment, and the Laval Square and Allard-LeBleu Heritage Character Areas concentrate that risk on named streets. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- Check creek and flood setbacks on the south-slope lots: Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996 section 519 sets no building within 30 metres of the natural boundary of the Brunette, Coquitlam, Fraser or Pitt rivers, or within 15 metres of another qualifying watercourse, and no building below the flood construction level in designated floodplain. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/1170/Part-5-General-Regulations-PDF
- Check the Watercourse Protection Development Permit Area on ravine lots: the permit area covers land within 30 metres of the top of bank or top of ravine bank of any watercourse. https://www.coquitlam.ca/254/Development-Permits
