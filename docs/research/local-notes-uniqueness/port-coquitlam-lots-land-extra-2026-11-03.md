## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- This page covers the whole City of Port Coquitlam, between the Coquitlam River on the west and the Pitt River on the east, with Lougheed Highway (Highway 7) and the Mary Hill Bypass (Highway 7B) crossing it and Shaughnessy Street and Coast Meridian Road as the main north–south routes. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2026-02/3838%20-%20Official%20Community%20Plan.pdf
- The plan frame is the Official Community Plan (OCP) Bylaw No. 3838, which identifies Downtown as a mixed-use neighbourhood and sets the development permit areas, including watercourse protection, that overlay the city's lots. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2026-02/3838%20-%20Official%20Community%20Plan.pdf
- Low land is a defining fact of this city's lot fabric: a significant portion of Port Coquitlam is within the Fraser and Pitt River floodplain and is protected by dyke infrastructure, with pump stations and dyke patrols. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/media-centre/port-coquitlam-reminds-residents-prepare-possible-flooding
- Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) land sits in the south of the city, including land in and around Colony Farm Regional Park at the west edge of the central area — a land page for this city cannot treat every open parcel as development land. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2026-02/3838%20-%20Official%20Community%20Plan.pdf

- Land here trades as individual fee-simple house lots and as downtown assembly ground, not as vacant-lot inventory: no named bare-land subdivision, bare-land strata offered as lots, or presale lot offering is verified for this city in this program's research. ~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/port-coquitlam-homes-for-sale-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md

- The redevelopment form of the land is set by the apartment zones: RA1 allows a floor area ratio (FAR, floor area divided by lot area) of 1.0, increasable to 1.5 where required parking is underground or in a structure and further to 2.0 on payment of a $50 per square foot contribution to City reserve funds for community amenities, and RA2 allows FAR up to 2.5, both concentrated downtown and along main roads. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2025-08/3630%20-%20Zoning%20Bylaw%202008.pdf
- Minimum lot widths for detached lots are zone-specific: RS1 requires at least 15 metres, RS2 at least 12 metres, RS3 at least 30 metres and RS4 at least 9.5 metres, under Zoning Bylaw No. 3630. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2025-08/3630%20-%20Zoning%20Bylaw%202008.pdf
- No manufactured-home park exists in Port Coquitlam in any source this program read — the two parks that surface in searches, Hideaway Park and Wildwood Park, are both in the City of Coquitlam — and a manufactured home on a rented pad is in any case not lot stock. ~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/port-coquitlam-homes-for-sale-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- General Port Coquitlam zoning and tax facts are written in full in the mary-hill-port-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note (#6 in this program); this note covers only the land-specific rules below. ~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/port-coquitlam-homes-for-sale-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md
- Subdivision and servicing: no Port Coquitlam subdivision or servicing bylaw number is verified in this program — it is held UNCONFIRMED in the servicing-bylaw ledger, and Coquitlam's Bylaw No. 3558, 2003 is a different city's bylaw and is never a Port Coquitlam number. ~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/port-coquitlam-homes-for-sale-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md
- Small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH) changes what a house lot can hold without rezoning: RS-zoned lots across the city can hold 3 to 6 units depending on lot size, location and transit proximity, with an accessory dwelling unit capped at 90 square metres and a secondary suite capped at 40% of the principal unit's floor area in the City's amendment summary. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- A watercourse permit can come before a subdivision: in the OCP's Watercourse Protection Development Permit areas, no filling, alteration or damage to a watercourse, ditch or adjacent environmentally sensitive land can occur without a Watercourse Development Permit, and subdivision is one of the activities that requires it. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/development-permits/watercourse-development
- Trees on a vacant lot are permit-controlled: the Tree Bylaw, 2019, No. 4108 lets the City issue a tree cutting permit with conditions on the extent, timing and replacement of cutting, and refuse one where cutting would fall in the active nesting season without a biological survey. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/4108-tree-bylawpdf
- Flood position is checked lot by lot, not by neighbourhood: parts of the low city sit behind the Pitt River dike system, and flood status and any flood construction requirement are confirmed with the City for the specific parcel. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/capital-projects/cedar-drainage-pump-station-0

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is there a bare-land market in Port Coquitlam? **A:** No documented segment. Land here trades as individual fee-simple house lots on the side streets and as downtown assembly ground under the RA1 and RA2 apartment zones, whose floor area ratios run to 2.0 with an amenity contribution and 2.5 respectively. No named bare-land subdivision, bare-land strata lot offering or presale lot offering is verified for this city, so a "vacant land" search here is really a search for a teardown or assembly parcel. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2025-08/3630%20-%20Zoning%20Bylaw%202008.pdf
- **Q:** How much amenity and park charge lands on a new single-family lot? **A:** Two City charges are in motion. The City's Amenity Cost Charge (ACC) page sets a proposed ACC rate of $15,358 per single-family lot, paid at building permit issuance or subdivision. Separately, the proposed Parks development cost charge is $15,253 per dwelling unit or lot for single family, while the City maintains its utility DCC rates at the 2023 levels. Confirm which rates are in force for the lot's application date. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/amenity-cost-charge-program
- **Q:** Can a serviced house lot take more than one home without rezoning? **A:** Yes, within the small-scale rules. The City states RS-zoned lots across Port Coquitlam can hold 3 to 6 small-scale multi-unit housing units without rezoning, depending on lot size, location and transit proximity. The accessory dwelling unit cap is 90 square metres and a secondary suite is capped at 40% of the principal unit's floor area in the City's summary, and the specific lot should still be confirmed with the City before pricing. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- **Q:** Is my lot in the floodplain? **A:** It is a lot-level question in this city. The City states a significant portion of Port Coquitlam is within the Fraser and Pitt River floodplain and protected by dyke infrastructure, and its Cedar Drainage Pump Station, inside the dike system, is being rebuilt with construction scheduled from summer 2026 to summer 2027. Neither the dike nor the pump station settles a single lot's status — check the parcel with the City. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/media-centre/port-coquitlam-reminds-residents-prepare-possible-flooding
- **Q:** Does a ditch or creek at the back of the lot matter? **A:** Yes — it can trigger a permit before any other approval. In the watercourse protection areas designated in the Official Community Plan, subdivision, soil disturbance, vegetation removal and construction of roads, services or flood protection works all require a Watercourse Development Permit first, supported by an environmental study from a qualified professional. Budget the study into any creek- or ditch-edge lot. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/development-permits/watercourse-development

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The City's development application fees are charged per lot on a sliding scale for residential subdivision: the first 20 lots are charged per proposed lot at the higher rate, lots 21 to 60 at a lower rate, and remaining lots at the lowest rate, under the Fees and Charges Bylaw. https://pub-portcoquitlam.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11274
- A Watercourse Development Permit is cheap on paper but not in work: the City's 2026 fees schedule charges $500 for the permit in agricultural and residential small-scale zones, and the application itself requires an environmental study prepared by a qualified professional. http://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/4415-fees-and-charges-bylawpdf
- The current fees bylaw is Fees and Charges Bylaw, 2025, No. 4415, in effect January 1, 2026, and its Schedule K is the Subdivision and Engineering Development schedule — the schedule a land buyer prices from. http://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/4415-fees-and-charges-bylawpdf
- Utility charges for a Port Coquitlam lot are flat annual water and sewer fees, billed separately from taxes and due March 31, 2026 — a vacant lot still sits inside that billing cycle. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/2026tax-newsletter-finalpdf
- There is no SkyTrain in Port Coquitlam to price into a lot: an extension is an exploratory business case only in the Mayors' Council February 19, 2026 work plan, with no funding, route or dates. ~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/port-coquitlam-homes-for-sale-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Port Coquitlam's general tax position is written in full in the mary-hill-port-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note; the land-specific charges below are this page's costs. ~/workspace/local-notes/lots-and-land/port-coquitlam-homes-for-sale-lots-and-land-2026-11-01.md
- Development cost charges: the City's DCC page states the Parks DCC program was last updated in 1999 and the Utilities and Transportation DCC program was updated in 2023, with utility DCC rates maintained at the 2023 levels while the Parks update proceeds. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/development-cost-charges
- Proposed Parks DCC rate for a new single-family lot: $15,253 per dwelling unit or lot, city-wide, in the draft rates the City presented to Council on July 8, 2025 — proposed, not yet the in-force rate. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/development-cost-charges
- Utility DCC rates sit in the City's Development Cost Charge Bylaw 2023, No. 4320, as footnoted in the City's own Parks DCC engagement material. http://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/2025-09-15-city-port-coquitlam-engagement-sessionpdf
- Amenity Cost Charges: the City's adopted ACC program page sets a proposed rate table with single-family at $15,358 per lot, ground-oriented multi-family at $8,038 per dwelling unit and apartments at $5,454 per dwelling unit, paid at building permit issuance or subdivision. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/amenity-cost-charge-program
- Permit fees a land buyer meets: a Watercourse Development Permit costs $500 in agricultural and residential small-scale zones and $2,500 in commercial, industrial, institutional, rowhouse, townhouse, apartment and comprehensive development zones, under Fees and Charges Bylaw, 2025, No. 4415. http://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/4415-fees-and-charges-bylawpdf

- Port Coquitlam is a designated taxable area for the provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax, so a residential lot owner there must complete the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the floodplain and dike position for the specific lot with the City first: a significant portion of the city is in the Fraser and Pitt River floodplain behind dikes, and the City page does not give a lot-level flood construction level. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/media-centre/port-coquitlam-reminds-residents-prepare-possible-flooding
- Check the Watercourse Protection Development Permit layer in OCP Bylaw No. 3838 for any lot with a creek, ditch or slough: Hyde Creek and DeBoville Slough drain the city's low ground into the Pitt River, and the permit layer, not the street address, decides whether an environmental study is required. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/development-permits/watercourse-development
- Check Agricultural Land Reserve status per parcel on the Agricultural Land Commission mapping, especially south-city and Colony Farm edge parcels; a view over the ALR is not ALR status. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- Check the tree position before valuing a treed lot: Tree Bylaw, 2019, No. 4108 permit conditions can set the extent, timing and replacement planting for any clearing. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/4108-tree-bylawpdf
