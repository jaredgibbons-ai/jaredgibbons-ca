## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- An egg farm is a quota holding plus barns, not just land: BC Egg classifies producers as Backyard Flock (up to 99 hens, no quota or registration), Small Lot (100–399 hens, registration, no quota) and Registered Producers (400 or more quota hens). https://bcegg.com/on-the-farm/small-lot-farms/
- Quota is counted in layers: a BC Farm Industry Review Board (BCFIRB) decision records a national allocation increase distributed as 44,475 layers to licensed producers in good standing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/organizational-structure/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/correspondence/bc-egg-marketing-board/15_mar_16_-_emb_quota_allocation_prior_approval_decision.pdf
- The farm land under an egg operation is usually Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR = Agricultural Land Reserve) land, so the residence caps in the ALR Use Regulation also apply: 500 square metres (m²) principal, 90 m² additional. https://webfiles.islandstrust.bc.ca/islands/local-trust-areas/south-pender/current-projects/Minor%20LUB%20Amendments%20Project/8%20-%20Public%20Hearing%20Binder/05.2_SP-BL-129_Bylaw%20referral_Responses.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- You cannot legally sell regulated eggs at scale without quota: to become a Registered Producer you must be issued quota from the BC Egg Marketing Board, per BC Egg. https://bcegg.com/on-the-farm/small-lot-farms/
- Quota moves only with Board approval: BCFIRB's Cross decision records that egg quota transfers privately or through the Quota Exchange, subject to Egg Board approval and a 5 per cent assessment retained to fund the new entrant program. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/allancross_0607_dec_nov_10_06.pdf
- The Board is supervised, not self-governing: BCFIRB prior-approves the Egg Board's quota distribution policy and its New Producer Program rules under the Egg Scheme. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/16_mar_01_bcfirb_decision_bcemb_new_producer_program_2016-1.pdf
- Supply management rests on three pillars named by BCFIRB: production control, price control and import control, with quota management a core Egg Board responsibility. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/organizational-structure/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/correspondence/bc-egg-marketing-board/15_mar_16_-_emb_quota_allocation_prior_approval_decision.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Does the quota come with the farm when I buy it?
**A:** Only through a Board-approved route. BC Egg lists three ways to become a Registered Producer: purchase through the Quota Exchange, purchase a Going Concern Sale when a registered producer sells farm and quota together, or win a New Producer Program draw. A land sale alone does not transfer quota. https://bcegg.com/on-the-farm/small-lot-farms/

**Q:** How many hens can I keep without quota?
**A:** Up to 399, in two bands. BC Egg states no quota or registration is required at 99 hens or fewer, Small Lot Producers with 100–399 hens register through the Small Lot Program without quota, and 400 or more hens requires quota as a Registered Producer. https://bcegg.com/on-the-farm/small-lot-farms/

**Q:** What does a Small Lot permit cost and allow?
**A:** The permit fee is $250, with a $250 annual renewal, per BC Egg's application form. Small Lot Producers may sell ungraded eggs directly to end users, must keep eggs at 4 degrees Celsius, and may not sell eggs to a Registered Shell Egg Station. https://bcegg.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Small-Lot-Permit-Application-eForm-Oct17-2024.pdf

**Q:** Who sets how much new quota BC gets?
**A:** The national allocation comes first. BCFIRB's 2015 decision records Farm Products Council of Canada approving a national increase requested by Egg Farmers of Canada, which then issued a provincial allocation to the Egg Board for distribution to BC producers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/organizational-structure/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/correspondence/bc-egg-marketing-board/15_mar_16_-_emb_quota_allocation_prior_approval_decision.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- A quota transfer shrinks on the way through: the Egg Board retains 5 per cent of quota transferred to fund the new entrant program, per BCFIRB's Cross decision. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/allancross_0607_dec_nov_10_06.pdf
- Growth quota has strings: BCFIRB's 2020 decision records that quota holders are not eligible to receive growth quota for 12 months following a transfer of any quota, with a business-unit exception. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/2020_jan_30_emb_qate_implementation.pdf
- New entrants are rationed by draw: BCFIRB prior-approved Egg Board New Producer Program amendments for a 2016 draw for 12 new producers. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/16_mar_01_bcfirb_decision_bcemb_new_producer_program_2016-1.pdf
- Conventional housing has a deadline in the background: BC Egg's 2018 allocation rationale encourages phasing out conventional production well ahead of the July 1, 2036 Code of Practice deadline for pullets and laying hens (check current). https://bcegg.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/2018_Allocation_Rationale_Final.pdf

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- The Small Lot entry cost is fixed by the Board: $250 application fee and $250 annual renewal, per the BC Egg Small Lot Permit application. https://bcegg.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Small-Lot-Permit-Application-eForm-Oct17-2024.pdf
- The land under an egg farm is Class 9 only if it produces: BC Assessment requires a prescribed amount of qualifying primary agricultural products for sale for Class 9, Farm; farm buildings are Class 1. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/understanding-property-classes-and-exemptions?TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=582130f6-038f-4842-ada7-0166ce03a246
- Half the farm land value is school-tax exempt: BC Assessment notes 50 per cent of the assessed value of a Class 9 parcel is exempt from school taxes under section 130(1)(a) of the School Act. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/understanding-property-classes-and-exemptions?TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=582130f6-038f-4842-ada7-0166ce03a246

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Egg farms ship through graders, not farm-gate scale: Small Lot Producers are barred from selling to a Registered Shell Egg Station, which is the grading channel Registered Producers use. https://bcegg.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Small-Lot-Permit-Application-eForm-Oct17-2024.pdf
- The Board office buyers deal with is in Abbotsford at 32160 South Fraser Way, on the Highway 1 corridor (times UNCONFIRMED). https://bcegg.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Small-Lot-Permit-Application-eForm-Oct17-2024.pdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Verify the quota, not just the barns: quota transfers need Egg Board approval, and a private transfer is assessed 5 per cent, so the quota on paper after closing is less than the quota before it. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/allancross_0607_dec_nov_10_06.pdf
- Verify the producer's standing: BCFIRB's allocation decision distributes new quota pro rata only to licensed producers in good standing with the Egg Board. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/organizational-structure/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/correspondence/bc-egg-marketing-board/15_mar_16_-_emb_quota_allocation_prior_approval_decision.pdf
- Verify the land separately: ALR status, municipal zoning and water licensing each sit outside the quota system and must be checked on the parcel itself. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/application-and-notice-process/applications/application-process/
