## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The homes are large detached-form strata townhouses: listing records for the complex describe three-level, five-bedroom townhouses of roughly 2,850 to 2,990 square feet with a ground-level one-bedroom suite — listing-stated figures for individual units, not a developer specification, and flagged as such. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2011-R086.pdf
- The City's own record fixes the first phase: Corporate Report R086 (May 9, 2011) describes Phase 1 as 10 buildings of townhouse units, with Building 9 (Unit 13) and Building 10 (Unit 14) constructed in 2010 and 2011. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2011-R086.pdf
- Those two buildings reached Council because they were occupied rental townhouses being converted to strata title — the report is a Strata Title Conversion application under the Strata Property Act, so early Blu Living homes began as rentals and were strata-titled after occupancy. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2011-R086.pdf
- Later phases carry conflicting years in listing records (individual units are listed as built 2013, 2015 and 2016) — no developer or City page read in this pass states a single completion year for the whole complex, so none is printed as the complex's year.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The complex is governed by its own CD zone, By-law No. 16608, not a standard Surrey townhouse zone — what can be built, added or altered at 15988 32 Avenue runs through that bylaw and Development Permit No. 7907-0105-00. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2011-R086.pdf
- Because the first buildings were strata conversions of occupied rentals, a buyer of an early-phase unit should confirm on title that the unit sits in the larger strata the Approving Officer signed off on, exactly as the 2011 report describes the conversion path. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2011-R086.pdf
- Long-term rental restriction bylaws are unenforceable in British Columbia stratas since November 24, 2022, but the ground-level suites in these homes are part of the unit's own floor plan — renting the suite separately is a strata-bylaw and City secondary-suite question to confirm for the specific unit, not an assumption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- The strata must obtain a depreciation report every five years (stratas of five or more lots, in force July 1, 2024) — for a complex built in phases from 2010 onward, that report is where phase-by-phase envelope and roof timing first shows. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The complex's own zoning is a one-off: CD By-law No. 16608 was written for this property, so a Blu Living addition, deck or exterior change is judged against that bylaw's text, not the standard townhouse (RM) rules a Surrey buyer may know from other complexes. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2011-R086.pdf
- Phase 1 alone was 10 buildings — the complex is phased, so two units with the same floor plan can sit in buildings of different ages with different depreciation-report timelines. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2011-R086.pdf
- The golf course across 32 Avenue is Morgan Creek Golf Club — the address's defining landmark, and the reason the page files the complex as "Townhomes at Morgan Creek" even though board data files it under Grandview Surrey.
- Listing records describe a self-contained ground-level suite with its own entrance in these homes — a feature to verify unit by unit on the strata plan and the City suite record, since suite legality is per-unit paperwork, not complex marketing.
- Provisional versus final occupancy mattered here at conversion: the City withheld final occupancy approval for Buildings 9 and 10 until engineering servicing and landscaping were finished and approved — the kind of condition a buyer of a converted unit confirms was discharged. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2011-R086.pdf
