# Chilliwack Farms — Extra Local Notes
Page: chilliwack-farms (Chilliwack; farms).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Chilliwack farm ground surrounds the urban core across the valley floor and uplands. The City states agriculture accounts for 67% of the City's total land base and 29% of economic activity. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- The Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) covers about two-thirds of the city. The City's Official Community Plan material states the ALR occupies two-thirds of the municipality's land base and that farmland surrounds the city. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/pdfbook/planning/2040OCP/files/assets/basic-html/page-28.html
- Farm districts named in City and program material include Greendale (see the greendale-guide note), Yarrow, East Chilliwack, Rosedale and Fairfield Island. The City states the ALR is made up of the Fraser River valley bottom at about 14,000 hectares and Ryder Ridge at about 950 hectares. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=8630

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The farm stock here is defined by six agricultural zones, not subdivisions. Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000, Section 6 sets AL (Agricultural Lowland), AU (Agricultural Upland), AS (Agricultural Small Lot), AR (Agricultural Residential), AC (Agricultural Commercial) and AFP (Agricultural Food Processing) zones. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- Minimum lot sizes differ sharply by zone and approval route. In AL and AU the minimum is 7.5 hectares for an ALR subdivision, 1 hectare by boundary adjustment, and 0.2 hectares by Agricultural Land Commission (ALC)-approved homesite severance or boundary adjustment. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- Small-lot agriculture is a studied Chilliwack pattern. The City's Small Lot Agriculture Study states Chilliwack contains 41,862 acres in the ALR and that the City reviews applications for non-farm uses, exclusions, subdivisions and boundary adjustments. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=8630

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The Farm Home Plate limits where homes sit on farm land. In the AL, AR and AC zones the plate is capped at 4,000 square metres for the principal residential use and 1,000 square metres for an additional residential use, and Development Permit Area 4 applies where the plate rules are not met. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- An ALC application may be needed before the City can approve farm building work. The City lists a new house, coach house or suite, placing fill or removing soil, non-farm uses, and subdivision or a boundary adjustment as items that may require an ALC application. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- ALR residences follow the provincial caps. The ALC states a principal residence must be 500 square metres or less, a secondary suite is allowed only inside the principal residence, and one additional residence is allowed up to 90 square metres on parcels of 40 hectares or less or 186 square metres on larger parcels. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- Floodplain building is regulated separately from zoning. Floodplain Regulation Bylaw 2018, No. 4519 sets setbacks of 60 metres from the Fraser River natural boundary, 75 metres from the Vedder River, and 30 metres from the Chilliwack River and Vedder Canal for buildings requiring floodproofing. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=999

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** How much of Chilliwack is actually farmed? **A:** Agriculture covers most of the city's land base. The City states agriculture accounts for 67% of the total land base and 29% of economic activity, and its plan material adds that farming in Chilliwack produces 10% of British Columbia's agricultural production on about 17,000 hectares, so farm neighbours and farm traffic are the normal setting, not an exception. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- **Q:** What is the Farm Home Plate and why does it matter? **A:** The Farm Home Plate clusters the home so the rest stays farmable. The City states Development Permit Area 4 – Farm Home Plate exists to reduce the long-term impact of residential uses on agricultural land, a development permit is required when the Zoning Bylaw plate regulations are not met, and the plate caps are 4,000 and 1,000 square metres. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- **Q:** Can I subdivide a Chilliwack farm into small lots? **A:** Subdivision is tightly limited inside the ALR. The Zoning Bylaw sets a 7.5-hectare minimum for an ALR subdivision in the AL and AU zones, with 0.2 hectares reachable only by an ALC-approved homesite severance or boundary adjustment, and the City confirms subdivision or a boundary adjustment may require an ALC application first. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- **Q:** Do I need to keep farming to keep the lower tax class? **A:** Yes, farm class depends on actual production and an annual filing. BC Assessment requires minimum gross income at farm gate prices — $2,500 on an operation of 0.8 to 4 hectares, $2,500 plus 5% of actual value above 4 hectares, and $10,000 under 0.8 hectares — and applications are due by October 31 for the next tax year. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Q:** Is my farm behind a dike or in open floodplain? **A:** Most valley-floor farms sit behind the City dike system. The City states a large portion of Chilliwack lies in the Fraser River floodplain and, to a smaller extent, the Vedder River floodplain, an extensive diking system is in place, and the City is the local diking authority that maintains the dikes and related infrastructure. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2825

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The Agricultural and Rural Advisory Committee (ARAC) reviews farm applications. The City states the Agricultural Area Plan was created in 2012 with the Chilliwack Agricultural Commission and ARAC monitors its implementation by reviewing agricultural development applications. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- Check ALR status on the City Webmap before writing an offer. The City directs owners to use the City Webmap to check whether a property is in the ALR, and states ALC applications are referred to the City for review with its Letter of Authorization included. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- Yarrow sits in two floodplains at once. The Yarrow Neighbourhood Plan states Yarrow is within the floodplains of both the Fraser River and the Vedder River, is protected by an extensive City-managed diking system, and a projected average flood depth of 4 metres applies if a dike fails. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=6799
- Vedder River floods are mainly a fall and winter event. The City states larger Vedder River floods occur in fall and winter from heavy rainfall or rain-on-snow, while spring and summer rises come from snowmelt, and its Flood Response Plan is reviewed and updated every year. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2055
- Part of the ALR was underused in the last inventory cited by the City. The Small Lot Agriculture Study states an Agricultural Land Use Inventory in 2012 found over 1,400 hectares, or 8% of the ALR, underutilized and available with potential for farming. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=8630
- Soils here are alluvial valley-bottom soils. A provincial report on the Chilliwack and Agassiz areas states agricultural soils developed on alluvial, lacustrine and glacial outwash materials, and most farms lie below 20 metres above sea level. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/research-monitoring-and-reporting/reporting/reporting-documents/environmental-protection-docs/compl_manure.pdf

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- For the headline residential tax rate, see the chilliwack-houses-for-sale note. This note covers only the farm-specific charges below. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- Farm land is taxed as BC Assessment Class 9 only when it qualifies. BC Assessment states Class 9 Farm land must produce a prescribed amount of qualifying primary agricultural products for sale, such as crops or livestock, while farm buildings fall in Class 1 Residential. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/understanding-property-classes-and-exemptions?TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=582130f6-038f-4842-ada7-0166ce03a246
- A leased-out farm parcel can lose class if the lease filing lapses. BC Assessment states a leased property is disqualified if a lease expires or seven years pass and a current lease is not submitted to the assessor by October 31 before the tax year. http://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/classifying-land-leased-to-farmers
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) taxable area includes Chilliwack. The Province lists the City of Chilliwack as a designated taxable area, and only owners of residential-class property in a taxable area must file the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- Development application fees are set by bylaw. The City states its Development Application Fee Bylaw lists fees for all development applications and related services, which is the schedule to check for rezoning, development permit and ALR-related filings. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=655

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Highway 1 is the main route for farm goods and buyers. The Fraser Valley Regional District (FVRD) states the Fraser Valley Express, Route 66, connects Chilliwack and Abbotsford with Langley and Burnaby via Highway 1. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/transit.html
- Route 66 is a daily interregional service. BC Transit lists Route 66 Fraser Valley Express as serving Chilliwack, Abbotsford, Langley and Burnaby with daily service, operated by Chilliwack Transit. https://www.bctransit.com/plan-a-trip/interregional-routes/
- Route 71 links Chilliwack to Agassiz and Harrison Hot Springs. The FVRD states it administers Route 71 connecting Agassiz, Harrison Hot Springs and Chilliwack as one of its transit services delivered by BC Transit. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/transit.html

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood risk covers most of the city outside the hillsides. The City's plan states that except for the hillsides and Sardis-Vedder area, Chilliwack is situated on floodplains, sharing Fraser River flood risk, with the Chilliwack and Vedder Rivers also presenting flood risk to portions of the community. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/pdfbook/planning/2040OCP/files/assets/basic-html/page-54.html
- New building in the floodplain must meet flood construction levels. The Floodplain Regulation Bylaw requires habitable floor elevation at the applicable Flood Construction Level (FCL) for the Fraser, Chilliwack or Vedder River or Vedder Canal plus 0.6 metres freeboard in areas protected by a standard dike. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=999
- Confirm ALR status and whether an ALC application is needed for the intended use. The City states properties in the ALR are subject to the Agricultural Land Commission Act and ALR regulations, and ALC approval may be required before the City can approve related development applications. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
