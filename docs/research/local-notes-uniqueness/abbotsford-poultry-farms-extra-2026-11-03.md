# Abbotsford Poultry Farms — Extra Local Notes
Page: abbotsford-poultry-farms (Abbotsford; poultry farms).
## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Abbotsford's poultry ground is the city's Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) prairie, above all Sumas Prairie in east Abbotsford. The City states Abbotsford is the largest municipality in British Columbia (BC) at 389 square kilometres, with approximately 72% of its land in the ALR, and describes it as one of the most intensively and diversely farmed areas in Canada. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/agriculture
- Sumas Prairie alone is a 90 square kilometre area of low-lying, highly productive agricultural land in east Abbotsford. The City states its multi-generational farming operations produce approximately half of BC's dairy, chickens, turkeys and eggs, making it a critical part of the provincial food supply. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- Quota mechanics are not repeated here. For BC chicken quota, allotment and transfer rules in full, see the broiler-farms-for-sale-bc note; this note covers only how poultry buying lands in Abbotsford.

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The stock is working poultry operations on large ALR parcels, split by the City into upland and lowland patterns. The City's AgRefresh process split the Agriculture designation into Agriculture 1 – Uplands and Agriculture 2 – Lowlands to reflect existing lot patterns, retaining a minimum subdivision size of 8.0 hectares in the Agricultural One (A1) zone and 16.0 hectares in the Agricultural Two (A2) zone. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-10/AgRefresh%20St3%20-%20Parcel%20Size.pdf
- AgRefresh is the framework a buyer inherits for barns and farm buildings. The City states AgRefresh was a three-stage review of agricultural policies, bylaws and regulations for ALR land that updated Official Community Plan policy and Zoning Bylaw regulation and prepared a bylaw compliance approach for farming areas. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/agriculture
- A poultry parcel's residence position follows the provincial ALR frame, stated in the farms note, plus Abbotsford zoning — the City's own A1 report confirms an A1 property requires a minimum lot size of 8.0 hectares under the Zoning Bylaw. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/44004/

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Abbotsford zoning, not just the ALR, sets the parcel floor. A subdivision in the A1 zone must meet the 8.0 hectare minimum, and the A2 lowland zone carries a 16.0 hectare minimum, per the City's AgRefresh parcel-size work. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-10/AgRefresh%20St3%20-%20Parcel%20Size.pdf
- Subdivision inside the ALR also needs Agricultural Land Commission approval, and the City forwards applications to the Commission — a City staff report on a Taylor Road property records an ALR subdivision application being forwarded to the Commission with the parcel zoned Agricultural One (A1) at 20.6 hectares. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/44004/
- Floodplain rules are a working rule on the prairies, not a footnote. The City's flood page states Sumas Prairie is subject to Nooksack River overflow risk, with major flooding events in 1990, 2021 and again in 2025 inundating farmland, infrastructure and residential areas and causing loss of farm animals. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- Manure and bedding storage on an Abbotsford poultry farm must meet provincial setbacks: 30 metres from a drinking water source, 15 metres from a watercourse and 4.5 metres from a property boundary for permanent storage under the Code of Practice for Agricultural Environmental Management. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/waste-management/industrial-waste/agriculture/regulation-requirements/minimum-setbacks

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Why does everyone talk about flooding when I ask about Abbotsford poultry farms? **A:** Because the main production prairie flooded in 2021 and again in 2025. The City states the November 2021 event evacuated over 1,100 properties and caused significant livestock and poultry loss, and the December 2025 event put 485 properties under evacuation order and 1,069 under alert. A buyer must price dike, pump and flood-construction diligence into any Sumas Prairie purchase. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- **Q:** How small can an Abbotsford poultry parcel be if I want to subdivide later? **A:** Subdivision minimums are large and zone-specific. The City retained 8.0 hectares as the minimum new lot in the A1 upland zone and 16.0 hectares in the A2 lowland zone, and subdivision in the Agricultural Land Reserve also requires Agricultural Land Commission approval. Most poultry parcels therefore cannot be split into small acreages, whatever the purchase plan assumes. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-10/AgRefresh%20St3%20-%20Parcel%20Size.pdf
- **Q:** Is quota included with an Abbotsford poultry farm? **A:** Only if the contract says so, and the transfer still runs through the sector board. Chicken quota in BC transfers privately with no central exchange, and the full broiler transfer mechanics are in the broiler-farms-for-sale-bc note. In Abbotsford, confirm the quota position, the registered premises and the processor arrangement for the specific farm before subjects are removed. https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/
- **Q:** What is actually being built to protect the prairie? **A:** The City's preferred long-term option is a hybrid built around a new Sumas River Pump Station, improved resilience at the Barrowtown Pump Station, and replacing temporary works with permanent works along Sumas Dyke, as the first phase of a multi-year program. Council supported the option in principle and a funding submission to the Province. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/council-supports-sumas-prairie-long-term-flood-mitigation?fbclid=IwAR2_RbPlYyTlph8VIGsxGw1-NmQvK6xo0-H8B1upb-vHDlgqazRoBib_ois
- **Q:** Does irrigation water just come with a prairie farm? **A:** No, prairie irrigation runs on the City's diking and drainage system on a season. The City states it lowers irrigation water levels on the east side of Sumas Prairie from September 20, 2026 and across Sumas and Matsqui Prairies from September 28, 2026, with levels restored in May 2027 for the growing season. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/diking-drainage

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Sumas Prairie's flood source is cross-border, not local rain alone. The City states that during heavy rainfall or rapid snowmelt the Nooksack River can overtop its banks at Everson, Washington, with overflow flowing north across the Canada–United States border into Sumas Prairie. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- A flood here is also a trade and highway event. The City states flooding in Sumas Prairie threatens Highway 1, the Trans Mountain Pipeline, the Westcoast Energy natural gas pipeline, the Sumas/Huntingdon border crossing and Southern Railway service. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- The 2021 flood closed the border and the highway. The City records that the November 2021 event forced closure of the United States/Canada border and Highway 1, stopping movement of people, goods and services, alongside the livestock and poultry losses. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- A joint flood plan now exists beyond the City alone. The Sumas River Watershed Flood Mitigation Plan brings together the Province, the cities of Abbotsford and Chilliwack, and the Semá:th, Leq'á:mel and Máthxwi First Nations, with an agriculture-focused session held for Sumas Prairie operators. https://sumasfirstnation.com/departments/governance-and-natural-resources/sumas-river-watershed-flood-mitigation-planning/
- Abbotsford promoted on-farm processing and retail through AgRefresh. City remarks at the Federation of Canadian Municipalities conference describe AgRefresh as modernizing agricultural policy to enable on-farm processing, farm retail and agri-tourism opportunities. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/mayor-siemens-talks-agricultural-and-flood-mitigation-national
- Wet-season manure covering applies here by the Province's own example. The Lower Fraser Valley is named as a high precipitation area (600 millimetres or more, October to April) where field-stored solid by-products must be covered October 1 to April 1. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/waste-management/industrial-waste/agriculture/regulation-requirements/storage

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- The active project for this page is flood mitigation, in phases. Phase one, as supported by Council, is a new Sumas River Pump Station, Barrowtown Pump Station resilience work, and permanent works along Sumas Dyke, with a more detailed plan and First Nations and stakeholder engagement as the stated next steps. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/council-supports-sumas-prairie-long-term-flood-mitigation?fbclid=IwAR2_RbPlYyTlph8VIGsxGw1-NmQvK6xo0-H8B1upb-vHDlgqazRoBib_ois
- The City also adopted a new Official Community Plan in December 2025. The City states the update, after more than two years of planning, emphasized housing supply and diversity and enabled 37,185 units over 20 years under its 2024 Interim Housing Needs Report — urban growth context that makes the ALR boundary, not urban rezoning, the frame for poultry land. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/making-progress-housing-abbotsford

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Abbotsford property taxes were due July 2, 2026, and penalties cannot be waived. The City states a first 5% penalty applied July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty September 16, 2026, both including unclaimed Home Owner Grant amounts, for the January–December 2026 billing period. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice
- Registered owners are billed annually by the City for property taxes, solid waste collection, secondary suites and other charges, per the same tax notice page — a farm residence with a suite can therefore carry a suite charge line. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice
- Farm building tax relief follows the provincial assessment rule. In a municipality, farm buildings other than a farmer's dwelling are exempt at $50,000 or 87.5% of assessed value, whichever is greater, while the dwelling is fully taxable. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Highway 1 and Highway 11 are the sector's working corridors, and both are flood-exposed at Sumas. The City lists Highway 1 among the critical infrastructure threatened by Sumas Prairie flooding, and the joint watershed plan work proceeds alongside dike and Barrowtown upgrades. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- The Sumas/Huntingdon border crossing is part of daily farm logistics and part of the flood risk. The City names the crossing among the nationally important infrastructure in the prairie that remains unprotected without long-term flood mitigation. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- Highway widening west of Abbotsford affects feed and bird haul timing. The Province states the 216th Street to 264th Street phase of the Fraser Valley Highway 1 Corridor Improvement Program covers 10 kilometres, is under construction, and adds high-occupancy and electric vehicle lanes in the median. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation-projects/fraser-valley-highway1/highway1-216th-264th-widening

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood construction and dike position are the first checks for any prairie parcel. The City's whole long-term plan exists because the existing system was overtopped in 2021 and tested again in December 2025, a 1-in-35-year event by the City's description. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- Check the parcel's zone, ALR status and floodplain position in City records before offering. Earlier program work for this slug found no parcel-level verification in its own research, and nothing in the sources read for this note verifies any specific farm — see NOT CONFIRMED.
- Disease-zone exposure is high in this concentration of production. CFIA states the Fraser Valley's poultry density, rainfall and migratory pathway make it particularly susceptible to large avian influenza outbreaks, and its predetermined zones can impose movement restrictions around an infected premises. http://inspection.canada.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/latest-bird-flu-situation/control-zones/fraser-valley-zoning-project
- Verify manure storage setbacks and runoff controls barn by barn. The Province requires runoff diverted from storage and leachate kept out of watercourses, with the setbacks in section 3. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/waste-management/industrial-waste/agriculture/regulation-requirements/storage
