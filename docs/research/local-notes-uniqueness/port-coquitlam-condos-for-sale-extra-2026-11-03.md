## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Apartment zoning downtown is RA1 and RA2 under Zoning Bylaw, 2008, No. 3630: RA1 allows a floor area ratio (FAR) of 1.0, rising with structured parking and amenity contributions, and RA2 allows FAR up to 2.5, concentrated downtown and along main roads. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2025-08/3630 - Zoning Bylaw 2008.pdf
- Every new downtown condo building needs a development permit: the City designates multi-family residential development as a Development Permit Area in the Official Community Plan (OCP) for form and character, under OCP Bylaw, 2013, No. 3838. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/development-permits/development-permit
- No Port Coquitlam strata can bar long-term rentals: rental-restriction bylaws have been void province-wide since November 24, 2022, including the restriction element in Shaughnessy Square's directory line. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- A 55-plus bylaw is the only age bylaw a strata may enforce, under sections 123.1 and 123.2 of the Strata Property Act — the frame for Mayfair Terrace's registered position at 2266 Atkins Avenue. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- At Mayfair Terrace, ask for the service agreement with the Form B: the mandatory base service plan sits on top of strata fees, so the strata fee alone understates the monthly cost. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Does Port Coquitlam have a condo tower? **A:** Yes, exactly one high-rise: The Shaughnessy at 2789 Shaughnessy Street, 26 storeys and 167 units, built 2012. Everything else verified in the city is low-rise — four to six storeys — so a buyer who wants tower amenities and views has one building to watch, and a buyer who wants low-rise wood-frame or concrete has the Kelly Avenue and Atkins Avenue shelf instead. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/index.php/building/the-shaughnessy-2789-street
- **Q:** Which Port Coquitlam condo is 55-plus? **A:** Mayfair Terrace at 2266 Atkins Avenue is the city's one verified age-restricted strata — plan LMS4171, built 2000, 46 units — and it carries a mandatory service plan on top of strata fees. The 55-plus building beside Montrose Square, Kew Lifestyles at Montrose at 2430 Mary Hill Road, is purpose-built rental: there is nothing to buy in it, whatever a search result implies. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/index.php/building/mayfair-terrace-2266-atkins-ave
- **Q:** Is there SkyTrain to a downtown condo? **A:** No. Port Coquitlam has no SkyTrain station; the downtown rail link is the West Coast Express from Port Coquitlam Station, with five weekday westbound trains in the morning to Waterfront and five eastbound returns in the afternoon, and no weekend or holiday service. Buses connect downtown to Coquitlam Central Station for the Millennium Line. Price a condo on the trains that run, not on an extension. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt997.pdf
- **Q:** Can I rent out a downtown condo long-term? **A:** Yes. Strata rental-restriction bylaws have been unenforceable anywhere in British Columbia since November 24, 2022, so an older Port Coquitlam bylaw package that still prints a rental cap does not bind a new owner. Short-term rentals can still be restricted by bylaw, and at Mayfair Terrace the registered 55-plus bylaw still limits who may live in the unit even when it is rented. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- **Q:** Is a downtown condo in the floodplain? **A:** Much of the city is, so check the specific building. The City states that a significant portion of Port Coquitlam sits within the Fraser or Pitt River floodplain and is protected by dyke infrastructure, and its Zoning Bylaw, 2008, No. 3630 carries Flood Construction Level plans in Schedule B — the documents a buyer or insurer checks for a Shaughnessy Street address. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/media-centre/city-port-coquitlam-prepares-potential-flooding

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Port Coquitlam's 2026 residential municipal tax rate is 2.1985 dollars per $1,000 of assessed value, under the Annual Tax Rates Bylaw, 2026, No. 4441, with taxes due July 2, 2026. https://portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2026-05/4441%20-%20Annual%20Tax%20Rates%20Bylaw.pdf
- Condo utility charges are flat annual fees, billed separately from tax: for 2026 the City lists an apartment unit at $456 for water and $458 for sewer, with utility bills due March 31, 2026 and a 5 percent penalty after that date. http://www.portcoquitlam.ca/services/utility-bills
- Solid waste fees are billed on the annual property tax notice, and only multi-family homes that opted in to City waste collection pay them. http://www.portcoquitlam.ca/services/utility-bills
- Port Coquitlam is a Speculation and Vacancy Tax taxable area in the Metro Vancouver Regional District, so every residential condo owner must file the provincial declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Port Coquitlam Station is a West Coast Express station only: five weekday westbound trains at 6:04, 6:34, 7:04, 7:34 and 8:04 a.m. arriving Waterfront at 6:40 to 8:40 a.m., five eastbound afternoon returns, and no weekend or statutory holiday service, per TransLink's timetable. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt997.pdf
- There is no SkyTrain station in Port Coquitlam; TransLink's regional map shows the Millennium Line ending its eastward run through Coquitlam, with Port Coquitlam served by bus connections to Coquitlam Central Station. https://maps.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/transit-system-maps/system-maps/fwc26_regional_transit_connections.pdf
- Route 160 runs between Port Coquitlam Station and Kootenay Loop in Vancouver through Port Moody and Burnaby, per TransLink's route listing — the all-day bus alternative to the peak-only train. https://maps.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/transit-system-maps/system-maps/fwc26_regional_transit_connections.pdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood is the first check for a downtown condo: the City states a significant portion of Port Coquitlam is within the Fraser or Pitt River floodplain behind dyke infrastructure, and flood plain maps are posted at portcoquitlam.ca/flood. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/media-centre/city-port-coquitlam-prepares-potential-flooding
- Flood Construction Levels are in Schedule B of Zoning Bylaw, 2008, No. 3630 — the bylaw's own flood plans are where a buyer confirms the level that applied to a specific downtown building. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2025-08/3630 - Zoning Bylaw 2008.pdf
- The Cedar Drainage Pump Station upgrade, in the dike system at the northeast end of the city, was in tendering with construction set for summer 2026 to summer 2027 and completion expected late summer 2027, per the City's capital projects page. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/capital-projects/cedar-drainage-pump-station-0
- Rail noise is a station-shelf item: downtown buildings near Port Coquitlam Station sit beside the Canadian Pacific Railway line the West Coast Express runs on, and no City page read states a noise standard for a specific building — check unit orientation and strata minutes. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt997.pdf
