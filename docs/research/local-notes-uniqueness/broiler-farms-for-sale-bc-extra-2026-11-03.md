# Broiler Farms BC — Extra Local Notes
Page: broiler-farms-for-sale-bc (British Columbia; broiler chicken farms).
## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Broiler production in British Columbia (BC) is concentrated in the Fraser Valley, not spread evenly across the province. The Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) states the Fraser Valley is highly dense in poultry operations, sits under a migratory pathway, and that BC holds about 12% of Canadian poultry production while 39% of highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) infected premises in the current event were in the Fraser Valley. http://inspection.canada.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/latest-bird-flu-situation/control-zones/fraser-valley-zoning-project
- The BC Chicken Marketing Board (BCCMB) divides the province for quota purposes, and its Interior guide defines the BC Interior Region as any premises not in the BC Lower Mainland (Fraser Valley) or Vancouver Island. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/BC-Interior-Quota-Conditions-Growers-Guide.pdf
- A broiler farm may only produce on its registered premises. The BCCMB General Orders state no grower shall produce chicken except on his registered premises, and only persons holding a valid quota or permit may produce the regulated product. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The farm stock here is quota plus barns, not land alone. The General Orders cover barn space in Part 33 and require a grower to be in receipt of an allotment form before producing in a quota production period, with an 8-week cycle allotted at 1.929 kilograms per quota unit. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- Mainstream and specialty quota are different stock. The Board's specialty pilot allocates specialty quota on a 48-week, 6-cycle annualized basis starting with Period A-198 (September 21, 2025), and allows specialty holders to use Part 45 leasehold agreements on the same terms as mainstream growers from that period. https://bcchicken.ca/news/market-pilot-project-decision-august-19-2024/
- Small specialty holders under 24,000 kilograms were offered a buy-one-get-one conversion: buying mainstream quota converts it 1:1 to specialty and the Board grants matching specialty quota, subject to having barn space to accommodate it. https://bcchicken.ca/news/market-pilot-project-decision-august-19-2024/

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- You cannot grow commercial broilers without quota or a permit. General Orders Part 3 states regulated product shall be produced with a quota or permit, and no grower shall produce in excess of the quota or permit allotted. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- National allocation is set first, then provincial allotment follows. Chicken Farmers of Canada (CFC) meets every eight weeks with farmers, processors, further processors and the restaurant trade to decide how much chicken to raise, and monitors compliance with provincial quota allocations. https://www.chickenfarmers.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/2017-Annual-Report-ENG-web-2.pdf
- Period A-204 ran August 23 to October 17, 2026 as an eight-week period, which shows the production calendar a buyer steps into mid-cycle. https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/fpcc-cpac/documents/council-decisions-2026/07-decision-cfc-quota-a204-licensing-en.pdf
- There is currently no central quota exchange for chicken in BC. The Board's Quota Use and Access Consultation (QUAC) states there is no centralized platform and quota is transferred privately through hatchery-processor-grower connections, advertising, or word of mouth. https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/
- The New Entrant Grower (NEG) program is in abeyance, not open. The Board retired the previous program after its last growers started by August 2025, put a future program in abeyance pending a Quota Exchange (QEX) operating for at least one year, and noted 19 growers entered in 2019–2025 without NEG support plus 10 through NEG. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/New-Entrant-Grower-Program-Update-September-2026.pdf
- Interior quota carries relocation conditions. An Interior grower may relocate quota anywhere in BC only after operating in the Interior at least two years, moving the entire holding, arranging a processor in the new location, giving the current processor notice two allocation cycles before relocation, and obtaining Board approval; moved quota may not be transferred for two years after relocation. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/BC-Interior-Quota-Conditions-Growers-Guide.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Does quota automatically go with the land when I buy a broiler farm? **A:** No, quota does not simply run with the land. The Board's consultation confirms transfers happen privately with no central exchange, and the General Orders treat quota transactions, transfers with a registered premise, and leases of quota as separate regulated steps requiring Board processes. A buyer must confirm in the contract exactly what quota is included and complete the Board transfer for that premise before relying on production. https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/
- **Q:** Can I start as a new entrant through the Board right now? **A:** Not through a New Entrant Grower program at present. The Board put the program in abeyance in September 2026, will review the decision only after a Quota Exchange has operated for at least one year, and points to its next strategic planning process in 2027. Entry today therefore means buying an existing operation or buying quota privately, not waiting for a program allotment. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/New-Entrant-Grower-Program-Update-September-2026.pdf
- **Q:** What happens if I produce too much or too little in a period? **A:** Over- and under-marketing are adjusted six periods later, with temporary relief in place. For Periods A-204 and A-205 the under-marketing carryforward sleeve was raised from 6% to 10%, and growers are encouraged to grow up to 104% without an allotment reduction six cycles later because Chicken Farmers of Canada raised the provincial penalty limit from 102% to 104%. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Memo-2026-08-05-Ext-Temp-Measures-to-Under-and-Over-Marketing.pdf
- **Q:** Do I need a processor before I can move or place birds? **A:** Yes, a processor arrangement is part of the approval picture. The Interior guide requires a grower relocating quota to arrange agreement with a processor in the new location and to give the current processor notice two allocation cycles before relocation, on top of Board approval. A farm with barns but no processor path is not ready to produce. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/BC-Interior-Quota-Conditions-Growers-Guide.pdf
- **Q:** Are food safety and animal care audits optional extras? **A:** No, certification is mandatory for registered growers. The BC Farm Industry Review Board (BCFIRB) reporting states all chicken growers are required to be certified in Chicken Farmers of Canada's Animal Care Program and On-Farm Food Safety Program, with 314 registered growers receiving on-farm audits at multiple sites in 2024. A buyer should ask for current audit status before completion. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/2024_cmb_parp_final.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Lost production in one period cannot simply be made up later. The Board states allocation and quota are fixed each period, set by Chicken Farmers of Canada at the national level, and a carryforward depends on having barn space beyond current quota and the prorated allocation. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Memo-2026-08-05-Ext-Temp-Measures-to-Under-and-Over-Marketing.pdf
- Chick supply, not just quota, can limit a flock. The Board's December 2025 memo attributes tight chick supply to the ongoing HPAI outbreak and limited import of eggs and chicks, with the BC Broiler Hatching Egg Commission adjusting hatchery allocations on domestic egg availability. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Memo-Temp-Changes-to-Under-and-Over-Marketing-Sleeves-Dec2025.pdf
- A Chief Veterinarian general order currently covers quota holders across the poultry boards. Order AIV2026-01, issued September 17, 2026, applies to persons responsible for chickens in operations regulated by the BC Chicken Marketing Board and other poultry boards who hold quota, to prevent spread of highly pathogenic H5 and H7 avian influenza. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/chief-vet-general-orders/general_order_aiv2026-01.pdf
- The Fraser Valley now uses predetermined disease zones. CFIA will declare a Fraser Valley Primary Control Zone with movement restrictions when avian influenza is detected in poultry within the zone or within 10 kilometres of an infected premises, using 11 predetermined geographical zones. http://inspection.canada.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/latest-bird-flu-situation/control-zones/fraser-valley-zoning-project
- Manure storage has fixed setbacks a barn expansion must respect. Under the Code of Practice for Agricultural Environmental Management (AEM Code), storing manure in a permanent structure requires 30 metres from a drinking water source, 15 metres from a watercourse, and 4.5 metres from a property boundary. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/waste-management/industrial-waste/agriculture/regulation-requirements/minimum-setbacks
- Field storage rules tighten in the wet Fraser Valley. The Province states only solid by-products may be field-stored for seven months or less, and in a high precipitation area getting 600 millimetres or more from October to April, such as the Lower Fraser Valley, the pile must be covered from October 1 to April 1. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/waste-management/industrial-waste/agriculture/regulation-requirements/storage

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- The main board project is the Quota Exchange, and it is not yet operating. The Board will review its New Entrant Grower abeyance only once the Quota Exchange has been implemented and in operation for at least one year, making QEX the gating item for future entry policy. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/New-Entrant-Grower-Program-Update-September-2026.pdf
- The specialty allocation pilot is the live production change. It began with Period A-198 on September 21, 2025, moving specialty quota to a 48-week, 6-cycle annualized allocation. https://bcchicken.ca/news/market-pilot-project-decision-august-19-2024/

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Farm buildings get a specific municipal tax exemption, but the dwelling does not. BC Assessment states that in municipalities the exemption for farm buildings other than a farmer's dwelling is $50,000 or 87.5% of the assessed value of the farm buildings, whichever is greater, and a farmer's dwelling in a municipality is fully taxable. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- Over-marketing above the temporary ceiling still attracts a levy. For Periods A-199 through A-201, marketings above 110% attracted levies at $0.66 per kilogram live weight, with levies between 106% and 110% suspended for those periods. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Memo-Temp-Changes-to-Under-and-Over-Marketing-Sleeves-Dec2025.pdf
- No quota price is stated in this note. The Board publishes no quota price in the sources read, and the Quota Exchange that would make pricing transparent does not yet operate. https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Birds move by truck between registered premises, hatcheries and processors, so processor location is a transport fact. The Interior guide requires Interior growers to work with a registered processor located in the BC Interior, and a relocation requires a processor agreement in the new location. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/BC-Interior-Quota-Conditions-Growers-Guide.pdf
- Disease control zones can restrict that movement without warning. A declared Fraser Valley Primary Control Zone applies movement restrictions when avian influenza is detected in or near the zone. http://inspection.canada.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/latest-bird-flu-situation/control-zones/fraser-valley-zoning-project

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Avian influenza is the first risk to check, because the production base sits in a dense, migratory-bird area. CFIA states those factors make the Fraser Valley particularly susceptible to large HPAI outbreaks. http://inspection.canada.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/latest-bird-flu-situation/control-zones/fraser-valley-zoning-project
- Biosecurity is a legal and audit duty, not a preference. The Province's enhanced biosecurity guide states producers are responsible for reducing disease introduction and spread, and improving biosecurity protects both the farm's economic interest and the broader poultry industry. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/ai_enhanced_biosecurity_for_small_scale_poultry_producers.pdf
- Check barn space against quota before buying either one. The Board states a grower's ability to carry forward production depends heavily on available barn space beyond current quota and prorated allocation, and the specialty conversion offer is expressly subject to having barn space to accommodate the quota. https://bcchicken.ca/news/market-pilot-project-decision-august-19-2024/
- Check manure and by-product storage against the AEM Code setbacks and runoff rules for the exact barn site. The Province requires runoff to be diverted from storage and contaminated runoff and leachate to be prevented from entering watercourses or crossing the property boundary. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/waste-management/industrial-waste/agriculture/regulation-requirements/storage
