# City of Langley Homes for Sale — Extra Local Notes

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- The City of Langley is its own municipality — 10 square kilometres, surrounded by the Township of Langley on the north, east and south and by Surrey's Cloverdale across 196 Street to the west — and nothing on a Township page governs a City lot. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/docslang-207268-v2-2026election-candidateinformationguidepdf-1
- The City's own history in two sentences: the settlement began as "Innes Corners," later "Langley Prairie," a business and service centre on (Old) Yale Road drawing trade from the whole region; on March 15, 1955 the City of Langley incorporated as a separate municipality from the Township. https://www.langleycity.ca/community-culture/about-langley-city
- The diagonal is the giveaway: Fraser Highway cuts across the street grid from Cloverdale to the downtown core at Glover Road and Logan Avenue, while the Langley Bypass (Highway 10) forms the southern commercial edge above the Nicomekl River floodplain. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf
- The City names six neighbourhoods by elementary catchment: Nicomekl, Douglas, Simonds, Blacklock, Alice Brown and Uplands. https://www.langleycity.ca/community-culture/about-langley-city

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Most City households live in attached homes: the City is about 80% apartments and townhouses by household, with the detached stock concentrated in the Uplands and the streets north of Grade Crescent and south of the Nicomekl floodplain. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf
- The Official Community Plan (OCP) sorts the stock by form: high-density mixed-use in the "Core" around the planned SkyTrain stations near 196 Street and 203 Street, mid-rise to 12 storeys, low-rise to 6 storeys, townhouses along future frequent bus routes, and compact lots with secondary or garden suites north of Grade Crescent. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf
- Infill here is old for the region: townhouses and 'plexes have sat inside the detached neighbourhoods for decades, including along 203 Street, 50 Avenue and the west side of City Park — a City-acknowledged pattern, not a new experiment. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The plan is the Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 3200, adopted November 2021 and read here in its February 2026 consolidation; its Appendix A is a separate Nicomekl River District Neighbourhood Plan for the floodplain-edge lands. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf
- Small-scale multi-unit housing runs through a dedicated zone: the Residential Small Scale Multi Unit Housing (R-SSMUH) zone permits a single dwelling, duplex or townhouse as principal uses, plus a secondary suite and an accessory dwelling unit, to a maximum of four dwelling units per lot, with a 300 square metre minimum lot for a single dwelling, 60% maximum lot coverage and an 11.0 metre principal-building height limit. https://d1e1jt2fj4r8r.cloudfront.net/17e24b86-04c5-4cdd-b15f-3179cfb7c586/e0f09604-d0cc-4ba9-b693-d3b5a5c85af6/R-SSMUH Zoning.pdf
- Density near the stations is a floor, not just a ceiling: the OCP requires minimum site densities of approximately 3.0 floor area ratio (FAR) within the Transit-Oriented Core areas around the planned SkyTrain stations near 196 Street and 203 Street. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf
- Floodplain rule: the Nicomekl River floodplain crosses the city's south side — the City counts the floodplain itself among its 346 acres of parkland — and lots between 56 Avenue and the river sit in the OCP's floodplain and Nicomekl River District framework rather than ordinary residential policy. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/docslang-207268-v2-2026election-candidateinformationguidepdf-1

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is this the City or the Township — does it change anything? **A:** Yes, everything municipal. The City of Langley is a separate 10-square-kilometre municipality incorporated March 15, 1955; its tax rate, utility charges, zoning bylaw and Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 3200 are its own, and Township of Langley figures — including its much larger rural tax and utility tables — do not apply to a City address. Check the municipality on the BC Assessment and City tax records, not the postal address, which just says "Langley." https://www.langleycity.ca/community-culture/about-langley-city
- **Q:** Which schools serve a City of Langley home? **A:** School District No. 35 (Langley) runs one secondary school inside the city: Langley Secondary at 21405 56 Avenue, Grades 9–12, with H.D. Stafford Middle School as its middle school. The district groups six City elementaries in the Langley Secondary family: Blacklock Fine Arts Elementary (5100 206 Street), Douglas Park Community (5409 206 Street), Nicomekl Elementary (20050 53 Avenue), Simonds Elementary (20190 48 Avenue), Uplands Elementary (4471 207A Street) and Alice Brown Elementary (20011 44 Avenue). Catchment is by address; confirm in the district's locator. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/035
- **Q:** How many units can go on a detached lot here? **A:** Up to four, in the zone built for it. The R-SSMUH zone allows a maximum of four dwelling units per lot across the permitted forms — a principal dwelling, duplex or townhouse, plus a secondary suite and an accessory dwelling unit as secondary uses — subject to the zone's 300 square metre minimum lot area, 60% coverage cap and 11.0 metre height limit. Whether a specific lot carries that zone is a City zoning-map check. https://d1e1jt2fj4r8r.cloudfront.net/17e24b86-04c5-4cdd-b15f-3179cfb7c586/e0f09604-d0cc-4ba9-b693-d3b5a5c85af6/R-SSMUH Zoning.pdf
- **Q:** When does the SkyTrain actually arrive, and where is the station? **A:** It is under construction now, not a proposal. The provincial project site states SkyTrain is coming "between King George Station in Surrey and 203 Street in Langley City," with the Langley City Centre terminus at 203 Street and Industrial Avenue; the project's own construction pages show guideway and station construction under way along Fraser Highway and Industrial Avenue in fall 2026. The in-service date is the project's to state — check the project site for the current target before relying on one. https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/current-work?cst&query-14-page=2
- **Q:** Is the south side of the city a flood risk? **A:** The Nicomekl River floodplain is a mapped part of the city, not a rumour. The City lists the Nicomekl Floodplain among its major parklands, the OCP carries a Nicomekl River District Neighbourhood Plan as Appendix A, and the older multi-family zones sit between 53 Avenue and the floodplain's north edge. A lot south of 53 Avenue needs the City's floodplain and development permit check before a purchase or a rebuild. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Water is metered and billed once a year for most homes: the City reads residential water meters annually in March and puts the charge on the June tax notice; high-volume users are billed every two months instead. https://www.langleycity.ca/PropertyTaxes/utilities
- Sewer is not a flat guess — it is calculated at 80% of your metered water consumption, so a leaking toilet or heavy irrigation shows up in the sewer line too. https://www.langleycity.ca/PropertyTaxes/utilities
- Douglas Park, at Douglas Crescent and 206 Street, is the civic living room: the City's main events park with a permanent stage, the Douglas Recreation Centre, sports box and water park — and Douglas Park Community School (5409 206 Street) sits in the same park block. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/docslang-207268-v2-2026election-candidateinformationguidepdf-1
- The "downtown" a buyer pictures is one intersection deep: the historic core is the one-way Fraser Highway strip through Innes Corners at Glover Road, and the Langley Centre bus exchange sits at Logan Avenue and Glover Road. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf
- City Park, on the west side, already carries townhouse and 'plex infill on its edge — the OCP's own examples of missing-middle housing in this city are on 201A Street near Sendall Gardens and the west side of City Park. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- Surrey Langley SkyTrain, King George Station (Surrey) to 203 Street (Langley City), Province of British Columbia with TransLink: 16-kilometre elevated Expo Line extension along Fraser Highway with eight stations, ending at the Langley City Centre terminus at 203 Street and Industrial Avenue in the City — STATUS: major construction under way, with guideway installation, pile driving on Industrial Avenue and station construction listed in the project's fall 2026 notices, read 2026-11-03. https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/construction-update-extended-hours-fraser-highway-east-of-highway-15/
- SkyTrain power supply, Fraser Highway between 140 Street (Surrey) and 203 Street (Langley City), BC Hydro: overhead line relocation largely complete and energization work running spring 2026 to late 2027, fed from the Fleetwood (Surrey) and McLellan (Langley) substations — STATUS: under construction, read 2026-11-03. https://www.bchydro.com/energy-in-bc/projects/surrey-langley-skytrain.html

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- The City's published 2026 property tax summary sets Class 1 Residential at a total of $4.2067 per $1,000 of taxable value, of which the City of Langley general municipal portion is $2.5937 per $1,000, with the balance to Metro Vancouver (shown as GVRD), school, TransLink (GVTA), BC Assessment and the Municipal Finance Authority. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026taxrates
- Water for 2026 is $2.06 per cubic metre plus a $75 annual fee, metered; sewer is charged on 80% of water consumption at $2.41 per cubic metre plus a $75 fee; the 2026 single-family solid waste charge is $432. https://www.langleycity.ca/PropertyTaxes/utilities
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies in the City of Langley: it is a Metro Vancouver municipality on the Province's taxable-areas list, so City owners file the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- The City's 2026–2030 Financial Plan bylaw (Bylaw No. 3318) context: about 75% of operating spending is funded through property taxes and utility charges billed at the end of May each year, and the draft plan balanced on a 3.57% property tax increase — context for the rate above, not a second rate. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/docslang-204913-v2-bylaw3318-2026-2030financialplanbylaw2025no3318pdf

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The bus hub is Langley Centre exchange at Logan Avenue and Glover Road downtown; its anchor route is TransLink Route 503 Fraser Highway Express, running Surrey Central Station to Aldergrove via Langley Centre on Fraser Highway, with the Surrey Central–Langley Centre segment in the Frequent Transit Network. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/503/direction/1/schedule
- Until the SkyTrain opens, rail access is by bus: Langley Centre routes connect to Surrey Central and King George stations on the Expo Line, and Route 595 runs from Langley Centre to Maple Meadows Station for the West Coast Express corridor. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/503/direction/1/schedule
- By road, the city is crossed by Fraser Highway (to Surrey and Aldergrove), 200 Street (to Willowbrook and Highway 1) and the Langley Bypass, Highway 10 (to Cloverdale and Delta); Highway 1 itself runs through the Township north of the city, not through the City. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Start with the Nicomekl River floodplain layer: the City's own documents treat the floodplain as a defined district (OCP Appendix A, Nicomekl River District Neighbourhood Plan), and the City does not publish a lot-level flood construction level in the pages read — ask the City for the specific lot's floodplain status before subjects. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf
- Check the station-area rules before pricing a lot on potential: within the Transit-Oriented Core around 196 Street and 203 Street, the OCP sets a minimum density of about 3.0 FAR, which cuts both ways — it supports assembly value and constrains a low-density rebuild. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/ocp-bylaw-no-3200consolidation-february-2026rpdf
- Check tree and habitat assessments on older treed lots: a 2024 City file for an eight-lot subdivision at 20525 and 20541 46A Avenue shows Council referring applications back for tree and environmentally sensitive area assessments measured against the City's Urban Forest Management Plan and a 2:1 tree replacement expectation. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/bylaw-3295-explanatory-memo-citiwest-8-lot-subdivision-application-20525-20541-46a
- Construction exposure is a current, dated risk: the SkyTrain project's weekly advisories list single-lane traffic in effect 24/7 along most of the Fraser Highway corridor with pedestrian and cyclist detours — a Fraser Highway or Industrial Avenue purchase closes into an active construction zone. https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/current-work?cst&query-14-page=2
