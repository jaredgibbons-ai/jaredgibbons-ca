# West End Neighbourhood — Extra Local Notes
Page: west-end-new-westminster (West End; neighbourhood).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- The West End is the City of New Westminster's western residential neighbourhood: the City's west-side study area pairs the West End with Connaught Heights, bordered by Tenth Street to the north, Fenwick Avenue to the west, Stewardson Way and Sixth Avenue to the south, and Twelfth Street to the east. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/rte/files/Westside%20Child%20Development%20Hub%20New%20West.pdf
- Twelfth Street is the neighbourhood's commercial spine and its eastern edge: the City commissioned a Twelfth Street Heritage Inventory in 2003 because the street was expected to see new commercial and residential development. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/CNW_DOCS_201027_v2_Twelfth_Street_Heritage_Inventory(1).pdf
- The ground slopes: the City's Street Tree Master Plan notes the West End and Connaught Heights value views sloping down south and west to the Fraser River. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/220121_CNW_Street_Tree_Master_Plan_Final_reduced(1).pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The West End is predominantly single-family in character, with commercial and multi-family uses only on sections of Twelfth Street, Sixth Street and Eighth Street, per the City's Street Tree Master Plan planting area D. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/220121_CNW_Street_Tree_Master_Plan_Final_reduced(1).pdf
- The build era is early: the same plan records that the West End developed in the late 1800s and early 1900s, and many residential streets have rear laneways. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/220121_CNW_Street_Tree_Master_Plan_Final_reduced(1).pdf
- The Twelfth Street corridor stock is older still in places: the City's heritage inventory documents the street's older buildings, including the Boughen-designed houses in the 400 block of Twelfth Street. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/CNW_DOCS_201027_v2_Twelfth_Street_Heritage_Inventory(1).pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The West End has its own zoning family: the Neighbourhood Single Detached Residential District (NR-1) was designed for single detached dwellings in Moody Park, the West End and Connaught Heights, and its stated intent is to allow single detached dwellings, secondary suites, and laneway or carriage houses in the West End, Kelvin and Connaught Heights neighbourhoods. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/300_One_and_Two_Unit_Residential___As_of_June_8_2026.pdf
- Small lots have their own district here too: NR-5 (Small Lot Dwelling Districts, implemented 1997) sits alongside NR-1 and the RT-1A duplex districts in the City's NR zoning guide. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NR_Zoning_FAQs.pdf
- Infill rules changed city-wide in June 2026: Council adopted Official Community Plan and Zoning Bylaw amendments permitting up to six units of infill housing — secondary suites, laneway and carriage houses, triplexes, quadplexes and other multiplex forms — on single detached and duplex properties across most of the city, without a site-specific rezoning. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects
- Whether a given West End lot is zoned RGO-A (up to four units) or RGO-B (up to six units) under the infill program is shown on the City's infill map and property list, not settled by the neighbourhood name. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Which elementary school serves the West End? **A:** Lord Kelvin Elementary, Kindergarten to Grade 5, at 1010 Hamilton Street, is the neighbourhood school building; School District No. 40 (SD40) assigns by home address, so confirm the exact address with the district. The district's long-range plan also lists a Lord Kelvin Elementary replacement at increased capacity as a future capital project. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in/new-westminster https://newwestschools.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/SD40-LRFP-Final-26.09.25.pdf
- **Q:** Can I build a laneway house behind a Twelfth Street house? **A:** The zoning intent says yes in principle. The NR-1 district that covers the West End allows single detached dwellings with secondary suites and laneway or carriage houses, many West End streets have rear laneways, and the June 2026 infill amendments removed the need for a site-specific rezoning on most single detached properties — the lot's own RGO-A or RGO-B designation sets the unit count. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/300_One_and_Two_Unit_Residential___As_of_June_8_2026.pdf https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects
- **Q:** Is the West End in the Fraser River floodplain? **A:** The West End sits on the slope above the river, not on the flat. The City's flood page states the city covers twelve square kilometres of which five are within the provincial floodplain boundaries, and development in flood-susceptible lands is controlled through the subdivision and development process — a buyer checks the specific lot against the City's Flood Susceptibility Map rather than the neighbourhood name. http://www.newwestcity.ca/services/public-safety/flood-management
- **Q:** What is the Twelfth Street strip? **A:** It is the West End's commercial street and a documented heritage corridor. The City prepared a Twelfth Street Heritage Inventory to augment its heritage and geographic information system records and inform community planning for the street, ahead of expected commercial and residential development, and the street's commercial district stayed active through the 1950s and 1960s on the inventory's account. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/CNW_DOCS_201027_v2_Twelfth_Street_Heritage_Inventory(1).pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The West End's first school predates most of its houses: West End School opened in 1889 on a small site on Twelfth Street at Nanaimo Street and was immediately found too small for the population. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/CNW_DOCS_201027_v2_Twelfth_Street_Heritage_Inventory(1).pdf
- A landmark sits at the corner of Sixth Avenue and Twelfth Street: the West End Methodist Church, built in 1891 to a late Victorian Gothic design by architect George W. Grant, per the City's heritage inventory. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/CNW_DOCS_201027_v2_Twelfth_Street_Heritage_Inventory(1).pdf
- West End streets are in the City's tree-planting Area D, where 2016 canopy cover was about 21 percent — the City's own figure for this group of neighbourhoods. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/220121_CNW_Street_Tree_Master_Plan_Final_reduced(1).pdf
- The City treats the west side as under-served for family facilities: its west-side child development hub needs assessment covers the West End and Connaught Heights together and records a current lack of facilities, programs and services on the west side. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/rte/files/Westside%20Child%20Development%20Hub%20New%20West.pdf

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- Lower Twelfth Street land assembly, City of New Westminster: the City purchased three properties — 235 Thirteenth Street, 240 Twelfth Street and 221 Twelfth Street — taking possession on September 15, 2026, for a total of $28.5 million from City reserves; STATUS — land held, existing uses continue, and the City states there is currently no timeline for developing the properties; read 2026-11-03. https://www.newwestcity.ca/2026/09/16/september-2026-lower-12th-area-purchase.php
- Infill Housing Program, City of New Westminster: Official Community Plan and Zoning Bylaw amendments adopted June 2026 permitting up to six infill units on most single detached and duplex properties; STATUS — adopted and in effect, with the RGO-A/RGO-B property map published; read 2026-11-03. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- New Westminster's 2026 total residential property tax rate is 4.79312 per $1,000 of assessed value in the City's 2026 property tax model, all taxing authorities combined. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/26_CNW_Mill_Rates_.pdf
- Utilities are a separate flat-rate bill: for a single-family dwelling in 2026 the net charges after the 5 percent discount are water $881.20, sewer $1,364.21 and solid waste (120-litre cart) $508.32, a subtotal of $2,753.73, with the statement mailed in late February and full payment due Monday, May 4, 2026 for the discount. https://www.newwestcity.ca/propertytaxes-utilities/sb_expander_articles/1271.php
- A house with a secondary suite pays more: the 2026 net utility subtotal for a single-family dwelling with a secondary suite is $4,143.46, because the suite adds its own water and sewer charges and a 240-litre solid waste cart. https://www.newwestcity.ca/propertytaxes-utilities/sb_expander_articles/1271.php
- New Westminster is a Speculation and Vacancy Tax taxable area, so every residential owner in the West End must file the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Route 112 is the West End's spine bus: New Westminster Station/Edmonds Station via Twelfth Street, Bay 10 at New Westminster Station. https://buzzer.translink.ca/2026/04/42-bus-routes-with-more-service-starting-april-20-find-yours/
- Route 106 runs the Sixth Street side, New Westminster Station/Edmonds Station, and route 123 runs Eighth Street to Brentwood Station; TransLink increased service on all three routes from April 20, 2026. https://buzzer.translink.ca/2026/04/42-bus-routes-with-more-service-starting-april-20-find-yours/
- No SkyTrain station sits in the West End; the nearest stations are New Westminster Station (Expo Line) downtown and 22nd Street Station west in Connaught Heights' station area — walking times from a given West End address are UNCONFIRMED.

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood is checked lot by lot, not by neighbourhood: the City created a Flood Susceptibility Map from the provincial Fraser River Flood Profile showing areas that can flood during spring freshet, and 170 hectares of the mainland floodplain are unprotected by dyke. http://www.newwestcity.ca/services/public-safety/flood-management
- Heritage is the second check on Twelfth Street: confirm whether a house is on the City's Heritage Register or in the Twelfth Street Heritage Inventory before planning a teardown, because the inventory was prepared to document older buildings ahead of development pressure. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/CNW_DOCS_201027_v2_Twelfth_Street_Heritage_Inventory(1).pdf
- The City is updating its Flood Management Strategy to current climate science and newer regulations, so flood construction requirements for low lots may change; the strategy page does not give a lot-level flood construction level. https://www.newwestcity.ca/planning-building-and-development/projects-on-the-go/articles/7966.php
