# Mission Court-Ordered Sales — Extra Local Notes
Page: missionforeclosuresandcourtsales (Mission; court-ordered sales).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- A Mission foreclosure is not filed in Mission: no Supreme Court registry sits in Mission, and section 21 of the Law and Equity Act says a foreclosure must start at the registry in the municipality where the land is, or, if there is no registry there, at a registry in the same judicial district. https://ltpm.ltsa.ca/21-venue-foreclosure-proceedings
- A 2025 BC Supreme Court case naming the City of Mission, Herar v. Mission (City), 2025 BCSC 1553, was filed in the Abbotsford Registry and heard in Abbotsford. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/sc/25/15/2025BCSC1553.htm
- The Abbotsford Supreme Court registry is at 32375 Veterans Way, Abbotsford; that courthouse opened to the public on January 18, 2021, and Supreme Court trials began there February 1, 2021. https://www.bccourts.ca/Supreme_Court/scheduling/lists/Abbotsford/Notice_re_Abbotsford_Courthouse.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Any mortgaged Mission property can be the subject of this process: Rule 21-7 of the Supreme Court Civil Rules covers a proceeding for foreclosure of the equitable right to redeem mortgaged property, or for redemption, and it must be started by petition. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo80/loo80/168_2009_03
- Court Services Online sorts Supreme Court civil files by class, and "Foreclosure" is one of the classes a searcher can select when looking for a file. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/CSO_Search_Tips.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The BC court-ordered-sale process starts with a petition: Rule 21-7 requires a foreclosure proceeding to be started by petition in the Supreme Court of British Columbia. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/loo80/loo80/168_2009_03
- The petitioner must serve an information sheet with the petition: Rule 21-7 (4.1) requires a petitioner who serves a foreclosure petition to include the informational notice for foreclosure proceedings in Form 125. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/oic/arc_oic/0701_2021
- The first court order is the Order Nisi, which fixes the amount owing and the redemption period; in 1048977 B.C. Ltd. v. Aviva, 2025 BCSC 1532, the court granted an order nisi with a six-month redemption period and later extended that period by two months. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/sc/25/15/2025BCSC1532.htm
- If the owner does not redeem, the court can give a party conduct of sale and later approve a sale; the Land Title Practice Manual's case notes describe files moving from order nisi, to an order for sale with conduct of sale, to a court order approving the sale. https://ltpm.ltsa.ca/32-enforcement-personal-covenant
- A sale is not final until the court approves it, and foreclosure hearings follow Practice Direction 66 (PD-66); BC Courts announced on March 31, 2026 that PD-66 was updated so foreclosure applications other than approval-of-sale applications may be heard in virtual chambers. https://www.bccourts.ca/supreme_court/archived_announcements/2026/Jan_Mar_26.aspx
- The last step, where the lender takes the property itself, is the Order Absolute; the Land Title Act, section 271, lets the registrar act on certified copies of the order nisi and the final order of foreclosure and presume the regularity of the steps between them. https://ltpm.ltsa.ca/271-presumption-regularity-foreclosure-and-cancellation-proceedings

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Where is a foreclosure of a Mission home actually heard? **A:** Not in Mission, because Mission has no Supreme Court registry. Section 21 of the Law and Equity Act sends the file to a registry in the judicial district where the land sits, and a 2025 BC Supreme Court case involving the City of Mission itself was filed in the Abbotsford Registry and heard in Abbotsford, at 32375 Veterans Way. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/sc/25/15/2025BCSC1553.htm
- **Q:** How long does the owner get to pay out the mortgage and stop the sale? **A:** The Order Nisi sets that redemption period, and the court controls its length. In one BC Supreme Court case the order nisi set a six-month redemption period, and the court later granted a two-month extension, which shows the period is fixed by the court in each file rather than by the lender. https://www.bccourts.ca/jdb-txt/sc/25/15/2025BCSC1532.htm
- **Q:** Can I check whether a Mission property is already in foreclosure? **A:** Yes, through Court Services Online, the Province's court records service. Its eSearch lets you search Supreme Court civil files and select "Foreclosure" as the file class; searching the database is free, and viewing the details of one file costs $6.00. https://justice.gov.bc.ca/cso/help/faq.do
- **Q:** Is a City of Mission tax sale the same thing as a foreclosure? **A:** No, they are different processes run by different bodies. A tax sale is the City selling a property whose taxes have not been paid for three years, under sections 645 to 672 of the Local Government Act. A foreclosure is a lender's Supreme Court proceeding under Rule 21-7, started by petition, with an Order Nisi and a court-set redemption period. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-sale-information

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Mission's tax sale runs on a fixed date: the City holds a public auction at 10:00 a.m. on the last Monday in September each year in Council Chambers at City Hall, and bidders must register by 9:45 a.m. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-sale-information
- Tax sale questions go to a named City line: the City lists Tax Sale Inquiries at 604-820-3717 on its tax sale page. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-sale-information
- Court file documents are not emailed: the Province's court records page says requests for court documents cannot be emailed, and a file is accessed at the registry where the matter was heard or online through Court Services Online. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/justice/courthouse-services/documents-forms-records/access-court-records

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- A tax sale purchase is not final for a year: the City states the original owner has one year after a tax sale to pay the taxes and buy the property back, which is the tax sale redemption right. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-sale-information
- A tax sale strips the owner's other rights at once: the City, citing section 665 of the Local Government Act, states that on a tax sale all rights of the owner immediately cease except the right of redemption, the right to bring an action to set aside the tax sale, and the right to possession. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-sale-information
- Tax sale properties are sold without any City warranty: the City states all property sales at tax sale are "as is" without warrant or guarantee by the City of Mission. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-sale-information
- On a foreclosure, title moves on the court orders, not on a normal transfer: the Land Title Practice Manual notes the registrar works from certified copies of the order nisi and the final order of foreclosure under section 271 of the Land Title Act. https://ltpm.ltsa.ca/271-presumption-regularity-foreclosure-and-cancellation-proceedings
