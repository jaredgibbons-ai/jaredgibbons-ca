# Chicken Farms — Extra Local Notes
Page: chicken-farms (British Columbia; chicken farms).
## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- This page covers chicken farms province-wide in British Columbia (BC), with production concentrated in the Fraser Valley. The Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) states the Fraser Valley is highly dense in poultry operations and particularly susceptible to large highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) outbreaks, and the BC Chicken Marketing Board treats the Lower Mainland (Fraser Valley), Vancouver Island and the BC Interior as distinct regions for quota purposes — see the broiler-farms-for-sale-bc note for the regional quota conditions in full. http://inspection.canada.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/latest-bird-flu-situation/control-zones/fraser-valley-zoning-project
- Chicken in BC is not one sector with one board. The Province's Chief Veterinarian order AIV2026-01 lists the regulated poultry bodies together: the BC Broiler Hatching Egg Commission, the BC Chicken Marketing Board, the BC Egg Marketing Board and the BC Turkey Marketing Board, each covering persons who hold quota from their relevant board or commission. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/chief-vet-general-orders/general_order_aiv2026-01.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- On the ground, a chicken farm is one of two different operations: a broiler operation growing meat birds in cycles, or a layer operation keeping hens for table eggs. For board mechanics for each, see the broiler-farms-for-sale-bc note (broiler quota in full) and the egg-farms-for-sale-bc note (layer quota in full); this note covers only what differs when you walk the farm.
- A broiler operation runs on short production periods set nationally. Chicken Farmers of Canada and industry meet every eight weeks to decide how much chicken to raise, and Period A-204 ran August 23 to October 17, 2026, so barns cycle, empty and refill on that calendar rather than holding a standing flock. https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/fpcc-cpac/documents/council-decisions-2026/07-decision-cfc-quota-a204-licensing-en.pdf
- Both operation types must be certified, which is part of the stock a buyer inherits. BC Farm Industry Review Board reporting states all chicken growers are required to be certified in the national Animal Care Program and On-Farm Food Safety Program, with on-farm audits performed by BC Chicken Marketing Board field service representatives. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/2024_cmb_parp_final.pdf
- Manure and bedding storage is built infrastructure on either type. The Province's Agricultural Environmental Management (AEM) Code guidance lists agricultural by-products as including manure, soiled animal bedding, and dropped or spoiled feed, all of which need compliant storage on the farm. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/waste-management/industrial-waste/agriculture/regulation-requirements/storage

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The first rule is that each chicken sector has its own quota and its own board, so quota from one sector does not authorize production in another. The Chief Veterinarian order treats broiler hatching egg, chicken, egg and turkey quota holders as separate regulated groups under separate boards and commissions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/chief-vet-general-orders/general_order_aiv2026-01.pdf
- A chicken farm may only produce on its registered premises under its own board's orders — for the broiler production, allotment and transfer rules in full, see the broiler-farms-for-sale-bc note. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/BCCMB-GENERAL-ORDERS-Aug-26-2011.pdf
- Disease orders can apply to every chicken operation at once, whatever the sector. Order AIV2026-01, effective on its September 17, 2026 issue, requires compliance by quota-holding chicken and turkey operations across all four poultry boards to prevent spread of highly pathogenic H5 and H7 avian influenza, with exemptions only through the board or commission after review by a Ministry of Agriculture and Food poultry veterinarian. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/chief-vet-general-orders/general_order_aiv2026-01.pdf
- Manure storage setbacks apply to chicken farms in every sector. Permanent storage requires 30 metres from a drinking water source, 15 metres from a watercourse other than a drinking water source, and 4.5 metres from a property boundary under the AEM Code summary. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/waste-management/industrial-waste/agriculture/regulation-requirements/minimum-setbacks

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is a chicken farm a broiler farm or an egg farm — and why does it matter at purchase? **A:** It matters because the quota, board and production cycle are different assets. A broiler farm sells meat birds grown in eight-week allocation periods set through Chicken Farmers of Canada, while a layer farm's value sits in egg quota under a different board. Confirm which board's quota is actually included in the sale, in writing, before valuing barns or land. https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/fpcc-cpac/documents/council-decisions-2026/07-decision-cfc-quota-a204-licensing-en.pdf
- **Q:** Can I buy one chicken farm and switch it from meat to eggs later? **A:** Not on the quota you bought. The Province's own disease order lists the BC Chicken Marketing Board and the BC Egg Marketing Board as separate regulators with separate quota holders, and each sector's transfer rules live with its own board. A switch means acquiring the other sector's quota through that board's process — see the egg-farms-for-sale-bc note — not converting the existing holding. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/chief-vet-general-orders/general_order_aiv2026-01.pdf
- **Q:** What certification paperwork should I ask the seller for? **A:** Ask for current Animal Care Program and On-Farm Food Safety Program certification and the last audit result. Board reporting confirms all chicken growers must retain annual certification in both national programs and that audits are performed on-farm for registered growers, with 314 registered growers audited at multiple sites in 2024. Gaps found after purchase become the buyer's compliance problem. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/2024_cmb_parp_final.pdf
- **Q:** How does avian influenza affect a purchase closing in the Fraser Valley? **A:** It can restrict bird and product movement around the farm during an event. CFIA now uses 11 predetermined Fraser Valley zones and declares a Primary Control Zone with movement restrictions when avian influenza is detected in poultry within the zone or within 10 kilometres of an infected premises. Check the farm's zone status and any active orders before removing subjects. http://inspection.canada.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/latest-bird-flu-situation/control-zones/fraser-valley-zoning-project
- **Q:** Where do I find broiler quota transfer rules without reading a whole guide? **A:** The full broiler mechanics are written once, in the broiler-farms-for-sale-bc note: allotment per period, the absence of a central quota exchange, the New Entrant Grower program's abeyance, and Interior relocation conditions. This page deliberately does not repeat them, because a combined chicken search mixes broiler and layer farms whose boards and transfers must not be blurred together in a purchase. https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- A combined chicken listing feed mixes two businesses that share almost no paperwork. Broiler quota moves privately through hatchery-processor-grower connections with no central exchange, as the Board's consultation states, while egg quota moves through its own board's exchange — see the egg-farms-for-sale-bc note for that system. https://bcchicken.ca/quota-use-and-access-consultation/
- Barn space can be the binding constraint, not quota. The Board states a grower's ability to carry forward under-marketed production depends heavily on available barn space beyond current quota and the prorated allocation, which applies when comparing two farms with similar quota. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Memo-2026-08-05-Ext-Temp-Measures-to-Under-and-Over-Marketing.pdf
- Temporary marketing relief has an expiry built in. The raised 10% under-marketing sleeve and eased over-marketing levies were extended only through Period A-205, so a buyer should not price a farm assuming those sleeves are permanent. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Memo-2026-08-05-Ext-Temp-Measures-to-Under-and-Over-Marketing.pdf
- Wet-season manure rules are stricter in the Lower Fraser Valley. Field-stored solid by-products in an area with 600 millimetres or more of precipitation from October to April must be covered from October 1 to April 1, and only solid by-products may be field-stored, for seven months or less. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/waste-management/industrial-waste/agriculture/regulation-requirements/storage
- Wild birds are the stated disease pathway for every farm here. The Chief Veterinarian order records that wild birds, particularly waterfowl, are natural reservoirs of avian influenza and spread the virus through migration and daily movements to domestic poultry. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/chief-vet-general-orders/general_order_aiv2026-01.pdf
- Small-scale biosecurity guidance exists separately from commercial rules. The Province's guide for small-scale producers covers outdoor, pasture-based and multi-species operations and states every producer is responsible for reducing disease introduction and spread to other producers. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/ai_enhanced_biosecurity_for_small_scale_poultry_producers.pdf

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Farm classification, not the chicken sector, drives the land tax position. BC Assessment classifies farm land in Class 9 only where land produces a prescribed amount of qualifying primary agricultural products for sale, while farm buildings fall in Class 1 — see the farms note for the classification framework in full. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/understanding-property-classes-and-exemptions?TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=582130f6-038f-4842-ada7-0166ce03a246
- Municipal farm building relief is the same across chicken types. In a municipality, farm buildings other than a farmer's dwelling are exempt at $50,000 or 87.5% of their assessed value, whichever is greater, and the dwelling itself is fully taxable. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Feed, chicks and finished birds all move by road, and movement can be zoned in a disease event. CFIA's Fraser Valley zones apply movement restrictions by geography, so a farm's highway access matters less, in an outbreak, than which control zone it sits in. http://inspection.canada.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/diseases/reportable/avian-influenza/latest-bird-flu-situation/control-zones/fraser-valley-zoning-project
- Processor distance is a real operating fact for broilers. Board relocation rules require a processor agreement in the new location before quota moves, which ties a farm's practical market to the processors willing to take its birds — see the broiler-farms-for-sale-bc note. https://bcchicken.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/BC-Interior-Quota-Conditions-Growers-Guide.pdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Confirm the sector first, then the quota, then the barns — in that order. A layer barn cannot produce broilers on egg quota, and the boards, quota and audits differ by sector, as the Province's four-board listing shows. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/chief-vet-general-orders/general_order_aiv2026-01.pdf
- Check for active disease orders covering the specific premises before completion. The September 2026 Chief Veterinarian order applies to quota holders under all four poultry boards and allows exemptions only through the board after veterinary review. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/animal-health/chief-vet-general-orders/general_order_aiv2026-01.pdf
- Inspect manure storage and runoff controls against the AEM Code for the exact site. The Province requires runoff to be diverted away from storage and contaminated runoff and leachate kept out of watercourses and off neighbouring land. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/waste-management/industrial-waste/agriculture/regulation-requirements/storage
- Verify audit and certification standing with the board, not just the seller. Annual certification in the national animal care and food safety programs is mandatory and audited on-farm, per BC Farm Industry Review Board reporting. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/2024_cmb_parp_final.pdf
