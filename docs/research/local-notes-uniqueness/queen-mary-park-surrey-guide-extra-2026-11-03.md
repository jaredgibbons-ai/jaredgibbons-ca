# Queen Mary Park Surrey Guide — Extra Local Notes
Page: queen-mary-park-surrey-guide (Queen Mary Park, Surrey; Guide).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Queen Mary Park is a Fraser Valley board sub-area in central Surrey, west of King George Boulevard and south of 96 Avenue, named for the City park at its centre — it is a board and local name, not a City of Surrey plan area. https://www.surrey.ca/activities-parks-recreation/parks/queen-mary-park
- The City's own park page places Queen Mary Park north of 88 Avenue and west of 132 Street, and the City files the park under its "Whalley and City Centre" community grouping even though the surrounding blocks sit at the meeting of the Whalley side (north) and the Newton side (south) of central Surrey. https://www.surrey.ca/activities-parks-recreation/parks/queen-mary-park
- Bear Creek Green Timbers is east across King George Boulevard, Cedar Hills and West Newton are south, Whalley is north, and Scottsdale / North Delta lie across Scott Road (120 Street) to the west. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/bear-creek-park
- No City neighbourhood or plan-area boundary for a "Queen Mary Park" residential district was confirmed on an official source: do not treat any street line quoted elsewhere as a City boundary.

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The stock is detached houses — older basement-entry homes, half duplexes and newer custom builds — with low-rise condos and townhomes at the edges and at least one manufactured-home park address on 134 Street. (program research file: batch-10/queen-mary-park-surrey.md, read 2026-11-01)
- Suite use is a defining feature of the detached stock here: houses are commonly described with basement suites, side suites and multi-suite half duplexes used for multi-generational living and rental income, which is why suite legality matters more in this sub-area than in most of Surrey. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- Queen Elizabeth Meadows Park, 9400 134 St, anchors the sub-area's east edge, across from a townhome complex on 134 Street. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/queen-elizabeth-meadows

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- One secondary suite is the Surrey limit: the City permits only one suite per single-family, semi-detached or duplex dwelling unit, at most 40% of the principal unit's habitable floor area, and never inside a coach house or garden suite — a multi-suite listing here needs a legality check, not just a rent estimate. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- Added-unit (small-scale multi-unit housing, SSMUH) allowances and the full zoning treatment are Surrey-wide and are written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note; the local angle is that this sub-area sits between two RapidBus corridors, so edge lots are the ones most likely to fall within 400 m of a frequent bus stop. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- Healy Creek runs through Queen Mary Park and the park's natural areas exist to protect its ecosystem, so lots backing onto the park or the creek can carry riparian setback and tree rules — check the parcel in the City's COSMOS map. https://www.surrey.ca/activities-parks-recreation/parks/queen-mary-park

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is there a Queen Mary Park Elementary School? **A:** No school by that name exists in Surrey. The British Columbia Ministry K-12 list for School District 36 has no Queen Mary Park Elementary — the Queen Mary elementaries in the provincial list are in Vancouver and North Vancouver. This sub-area is named for the City park, not a school, and its elementaries are David Brankin, Senator Reid, Betty Huff, Kennedy Trail and Kirkbride. (BC Ministry K-12 schools file, 2025/2026)
- **Q:** Which elementary school will my child attend? **A:** It depends on the exact address, because SD36 catchments are address-based and no locator result for a Queen Mary Park address is on record. Schools confirmed by address in or beside the sub-area are David Brankin Elementary (9160 128 St), Senator Reid Elementary (9341 126 St), Betty Huff Elementary (13055 Huntley Ave), Kennedy Trail Elementary (8305 122a St) and Kirkbride Elementary (12150 92 Ave). Run the address in the SD36 School Locator before relying on one. (BC Ministry K-12 schools file, 2025/2026)
- **Q:** Are the basement suites in these houses legal? **A:** Not always — verify each one with the City. Surrey permits one secondary suite per dwelling unit at most 40% of the principal unit's habitable floor area, and homes in this sub-area are openly described in listings as having unauthorized suites. A suite's permit status affects financing, insurance and whether the rental income can be counted at all. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
- **Q:** How do I get downtown without a car? **A:** Two RapidBus corridors frame the sub-area: the R1 King George Blvd runs on King George Boulevard at the east side and the R6 Scott Road runs on Scott Road (120 Street) at the west side, both connecting to Expo Line stations. Local route 324 also runs via 132 Street between Surrey Central Station and Newton Exchange. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt324.pdf
- **Q:** How close is the hospital? **A:** Surrey Memorial Hospital, 13750 96 Ave, sits at the sub-area's north edge and is the area's hospital; the Surrey-Newton Urgent and Primary Care Centre is at 6830 King George Blvd further south. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Surrey/surrey-newton-urgent-and-primary-care-centre

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Queen Mary Park's trails start at two unmarked-feeling points: the intersection of Queen Mary Boulevard and 130B Street, and the end of Lanark Place east of Queen Mary Boulevard, with the playground reached from the trails in the middle of the park. https://www.surrey.ca/activities-parks-recreation/parks/queen-mary-park
- The park is mostly protected natural area, not sports fields: the City states the majority of the park is natural areas established to protect the sensitive ecosystem of Healy Creek, an important tributary to Bear Creek and the Serpentine River. https://www.surrey.ca/activities-parks-recreation/parks/queen-mary-park
- The two secondary schools beside the sub-area are Queen Elizabeth Secondary (9457 King George Blvd) and L.A. Matheson Secondary (9484 122 St) — a Queen Mary Park address can fall to either side of that line, so the locator run matters. (BC Ministry K-12 schools file, 2025/2026)
- Route 321 runs the length of King George Boulevard past the sub-area's east edge, from Surrey Central Station via Newton Exchange to White Rock Centre — the one-seat bus ride to White Rock. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt321.pdf
- The nearest full grocery anchor is at Central City, 10153 King George Blvd in City Centre, where T&T Supermarket anchors the mall. https://centralcity.ca/
- Measured from the park (8972 Queen Mary Boulevard) in the What's Nearby pass: King George Station is a 4.7-minute drive (3.00 km) and Surrey Central Station a 6.4-minute drive (3.93 km), OSRM routing measured 2026-11-02. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt321.pdf

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Queen Mary Park pays the city-wide Surrey rate: the 2026 total residential (Class 1) rate is $3.42731 per $1,000 of assessed value, with the full schedule in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies: the Province lists the City of Surrey as a designated taxable area, so owners here file the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Healy Creek is the local constraint layer: the creek is a tributary to Bear Creek and the Serpentine River and its ecosystem is the reason most of Queen Mary Park is protected natural area, so any lot draining to or backing onto the creek corridor should be checked for riparian setbacks in COSMOS. https://www.surrey.ca/activities-parks-recreation/parks/queen-mary-park
- Surrey's mapped floodplain lowlands — the Nicomekl and Serpentine coastal floodplain and the Little Campbell River area — are south of this upland sub-area, but the Serpentine system is the same watershed Healy Creek feeds, so a low lot near the creek is a parcel-level check, not a neighbourhood assumption. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- Suite legality is the financial due-diligence item here: an unauthorized suite can fail financing and insurance checks, and the City's suite page — one suite per unit, 40% maximum — is the standard to test the property against. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite
