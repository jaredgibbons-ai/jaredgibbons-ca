# Maple Ridge Lots and Land — Extra Local Notes
Page: maple-ridge-lots-land (Maple Ridge; lots and land).
## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Maple Ridge land buyers face two different fabrics: serviced urban lots inside the sewer area and large rural parcels outside it. A City report distinguishes Suburban Residential, which can only be serviced by on-site septic, from Estate Suburban, which is located within the Fraser Sewer Area and can be serviced by municipal sewer. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/september-19-2016-suburban-residential-report
- The rural lot fabric runs through the RS-3 (One Family Rural Residential) areas in the north and east of the city, toward Silver Valley, Thornhill and Whonnock. The City report treats RS-3 and RS-2 as the suburban and rural residential zones for subdivision policy. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/september-19-2016-suburban-residential-report
- Shared Maple Ridge civic and acreage-general facts are owned by the earlier maple-ridge-acreages-for-sale note; this note takes only the land, subdivision and charges angle.

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Typical rural lot size depends on water, not just zoning. The City's zoning table in its suburban report gives RS-3 a minimum parcel size of 0.8 hectares (2 acres) with community water and 2.0 hectares (5 acres) without community water, and RS-2 a minimum of 0.4 hectares (1 acre). https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/september-19-2016-suburban-residential-report
- Suburban lot creation has been slow by design. The same City report states just over 300 new lots were created in the Suburban and Estate Suburban designations since 2009, and that water servicing had since been extended through many of those areas. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/september-19-2016-suburban-residential-report
- No named bare-land strata land subdivision was confirmed from a City page read for this note, so none is named here; subdivision servicing for any such proposal is governed by Bylaw No. 4800-1993. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/subdivision-development-bylaw-no-4800-1993

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Subdivision and Development Servicing Bylaw No. 4800-1993 is the servicing rulebook for a Maple Ridge lot. The City states it regulates subdivision and development of land and prescribes servicing standards according to each zone. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/september-19-2016-suburban-residential-report
- The full bylaw text is published by the City as Subdivision and Development Bylaw No. 4800-1993, which is the document a buyer's engineer should price road, water, drainage and frontage works from. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/subdivision-development-bylaw-no-4800-1993
- An ALR lot cannot be subdivided on City zoning alone. A City council report for 24689 124 Avenue notes the RS-3 minimum is 0.8 hectares, the property was 1.5 hectares, subdivision would still need rezoning, and because it is in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) an application to the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) would also be required. https://pub-mapleridge.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=6908
- City policy discourages splitting farm land. The same report cites Official Community Plan (OCP) Policy 6-12 discouraging subdivision of agricultural land to minimise land speculation and barriers to entry for farming. https://pub-mapleridge.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=6908
- A detached garden suite on ALR land is treated differently from subdivision. The report states a detached garden suite on ALR land does not require ALC approval because the ALR Use Regulation permits an additional residence up to 90 square metres on parcels under 40 hectares. https://pub-mapleridge.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=6908

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** What does a vacant Maple Ridge lot actually cost to make buildable? **A:** Budget for Development Cost Charges (DCCs), servicing works and frontage upgrades on top of the purchase. Maple Ridge adopted an updated DCC Bylaw on September 24, 2024, its first DCC revision since 2017, to fund sewer, water, drainage, parks and roads for growth, and rezoning files require road dedication and utility servicing to Bylaw 4800-1993 standards. https://www.mapleridge.ca/build-do-business/construction-development-permits/development-cost-charges
- **Q:** Can I split a 1.5-hectare RS-3 acreage into two lots? **A:** Not as of right, even though the numbers look close. The City told Council for a 1.5-hectare RS-3 property on 124 Avenue that subdivision would require a rezoning application, and if the land is in the ALR, an ALC application as well, because the RS-3 minimum lot area is 0.8 hectares and OCP policy discourages agricultural subdivision. https://pub-mapleridge.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=6908
- **Q:** Will a rural lot have sewer? **A:** Often no, and that changes the minimum lot size and the build cost. The City's suburban report states Suburban Residential land can only be serviced through on-site septic disposal, while Estate Suburban land inside the Fraser Sewer Area can use municipal sewer, and RS-3 minimums rise from 0.8 hectares with community water to 2.0 hectares without it. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/september-19-2016-suburban-residential-report
- **Q:** When are DCCs and the new Amenity Cost Charges paid? **A:** At subdivision if subdivision applies, otherwise at building permit, under the City's proposed payment timing. Its Amenity Cost Charges (ACC) material states ACC and DCC payment timing is the same, and gives draft ACC rates at first reading on May 26, 2026 of $1,169 per unit for duplex, triplex, fourplex and townhouse homes and $552 per unit for a secondary suite or detached garden suite, with a target bylaw effective date of January 1, 2027. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/acc-open-house

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- DCC rates changed recently, so old pro formas are stale. The City states its updated DCC Bylaw was adopted September 24, 2024, the first revision since 2017, because development and infrastructure costs had risen. https://www.mapleridge.ca/build-do-business/construction-development-permits/development-cost-charges
- DCC instalment rules change on January 1, 2026. The City's DCC page flags changes to the Development Cost Charges (Instalments) Regulation starting that date, which matters for cash flow on a phased subdivision. https://www.mapleridge.ca/build-do-business/construction-development-permits/development-cost-charges
- The 2025 recycling levy disappeared from the tax bill. The City states the previously planned $43 recycling levy was removed after curbside recycling moved to Recycle BC at no cost to residents. http://www.mapleridge.ca/news/2025-budget-invests-community-priorities-while-reducing-tax-rate-increase
- Lot grading is a completion gate, not paperwork. City Bulletin 2015-002-BG requires a BC Land Surveyor statement that subdivision grades are within 150 millimetres of design before a Certificate of Completion or building permits. http://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/finalgrade-surveybulletin201501231545150275pdf
- School catchment for a new lot is checked through School District 42's locator, not by nearest school on a map. https://www.sd42.ca/school-locator/

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- The land-relevant City project read for this note is the Abernethy Way extension from 232 Street to 240 Street along 124 Avenue. The City states Council approved that alignment in 2025, it has the least impact on the environment and the ALR among options studied, design and property acquisition run in 2025–2026, and construction is anticipated to start in 2027. https://www.mapleridge.ca/your-government/city-projects/abernethy-way-corridor-improvements-232-street-240-street
- A future phase from 240 Street to 256 Street is in the long-term plan with timing not yet confirmed, according to the same City project page. https://www.mapleridge.ca/your-government/city-projects/abernethy-way-corridor-improvements-232-street-240-street

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Maple Ridge's total residential mill rate for 2026 is 4.53578 per $1,000 of assessed value in the City's tax-rate summary. The municipal general portion is 2.86430, with the school levy for Class 1 at 1.21480. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/2026-property-tax-rates
- Water is a flat annual charge for an unmetered single-family lot in that summary. The City's 2026 summary shows a flat fee of $859.25 or a parcel charge of $191.65, and a multi-residential flat fee of $815.62. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/2026-property-tax-rates
- Sewer and water utility increases are a carrying cost for a serviced lot. For 2025 the City stated sewer rates rose 23.5%, about $136 more for an average household, driven by Metro Vancouver wastewater costs, and water rose 4.5%, about $35 more. http://www.mapleridge.ca/news/2025-budget-invests-community-priorities-while-reducing-tax-rate-increase
- DCCs are the main land-specific charge, as set out in section 3 and section 4 above. https://www.mapleridge.ca/build-do-business/construction-development-permits/development-cost-charges
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) applies in Maple Ridge. The Province lists the City of Maple Ridge as a taxable area in the Metro Vancouver Regional District. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- A rural Maple Ridge lot is car-dependent, but the West Coast Express (WCE) is the commuter anchor for the city. TransLink lists Maple Meadows Station park and ride at 20010 Dunn Avenue with 435 spaces, open 7 days a week from 4 a.m. to 2 a.m. https://www.translink.ca/parkandride?utm_source=direct&utm_medium=shorturl&utm_campaign=parkandride
- Lougheed Highway and Dewdney Trunk Road carry east-west traffic that the Abernethy Way extension is meant to relieve. The City states the extension will provide an alternate east-west route and support future transit including Bus Rapid Transit. https://www.mapleridge.ca/your-government/city-projects/abernethy-way-corridor-improvements-232-street-240-street

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Floodplain construction rules apply to low Maple Ridge lots, especially near the Fraser and Alouette rivers. The City publishes a floodplain construction document that a buyer should read against the specific parcel before designing a build. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/floodplain-construction-0
- Septic feasibility is a due-diligence item on Suburban Residential land, because the City states that designation can only be serviced by on-site septic disposal methods. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/september-19-2016-suburban-residential-report
- Watercourse protection affects lot grading and buildable area. The City's grading bulletin cites the Maple Ridge Watercourse Protection Bylaw 6410-2006 together with Subdivision Control Bylaw 4800-1993 as governing subdivision grades. http://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/finalgrade-surveybulletin201501231545150275pdf
- Check ALR status before assuming a garden suite or subdivision path. The ALC permits one principal residence up to 500 square metres and an additional residence in defined cases, and local bylaws may be stricter. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
