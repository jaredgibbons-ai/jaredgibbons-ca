# Westwood Plateau Neighbourhood — Extra Local Notes
Page: westwood-plateau-coquitlam (Westwood Plateau; neighbourhood).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Westwood Plateau is the planned residential plateau on Eagle Mountain in north Coquitlam, reached from below by Johnson Street and by Plateau Boulevard and Parkway Boulevard climbing the slope. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt187.pdf
- The City's own history in the Interim Official Community Plan (OCP), Bylaw No. 5511, 2026 records that the Westwood Racing Circuit opened in 1959 at the end of Johnson Street on Eagle Mountain and closed in 1990 to make way for residential development on Westwood Plateau. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17000/Official-Community-Plan---Schedule-A-PDF
- Scott Creek and Eagle Ridge sit below the plateau to the south, Port Moody is the municipal neighbour to the west, and Anmore lies beyond the city boundary to the northwest — adjacency as shown on City and TransLink route mapping for the Johnson Street corridor. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt187.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide zoning and Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) rules are in full in the scott-creek-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note; the plateau-specific check is which development permit areas touch the lot.
- The Interim OCP carries four development permit areas city-wide: Urban Design, Watercourse Protection, Wildfire Hazard and Unstable Slopes, with maps in Schedule G and guidelines in Schedule H. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17000/Official-Community-Plan---Schedule-A-PDF
- A Watercourse Protection Development Permit is required before altering land within 30 metres of the top of bank or top of ravine bank of a watercourse in this part of Coquitlam — the 50-metre rule applies only in Northeast Coquitlam. https://www.coquitlam.ca/254/Development-Permits
- Single-family dwellings do not require a form and character development permit, but a permit is still required before subdividing, constructing or adding to a building on land inside a development permit area. https://www.coquitlam.ca/254/Development-Permits

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is there a SkyTrain station on Westwood Plateau? **A:** No, there is no rapid transit on the plateau. The transit link is TransLink route 187, which runs from Coquitlam Central Station up Johnson Street, then Plateau Boulevard, Paddock Drive and Plateau Boulevard again to Parkway Boulevard at Plateau Boulevard. Every plateau trip to the Millennium Line or the West Coast Express starts with that bus leg or a drive down Johnson Street. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt187.pdf
- **Q:** Which elementary schools serve plateau addresses? **A:** Catchment is by home address in School District No. 43 (SD43), and the plateau's named elementary building is Bramblewood Elementary at 2875 Panorama Drive. Other plateau addresses can fall to neighbouring catchments, so run the exact address in the SD43 School Locator instead of assuming the closest school is the assigned one. https://www.sd43.bc.ca/bramblewood/ https://mybaragar.com/index.cfm?event=page.SchoolLocatorPublic&DistrictCode=bc43
- **Q:** Does the wildfire interface affect a plateau lot? **A:** It can, because the Interim OCP designates a Wildfire Hazard Development Permit Area and maps it in Schedule G, and the plateau's forest-edge and greenbelt lots are the ones to check against that map. The permit area does not ban building; it sets conditions through the Schedule H guidelines, and the map — not the street name — decides whether a specific lot is inside it. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17000/Official-Community-Plan---Schedule-A-PDF
- **Q:** What happened to the land before the houses? **A:** It was a racetrack. The City records that the Westwood Racing Circuit opened in 1959 at the end of Johnson Street on Eagle Mountain, was the first purpose-built permanent road course in Canada, and closed in 1990 to make way for residential development on Westwood Plateau. That history explains the plateau's single-era, master-planned street pattern. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17000/Official-Community-Plan---Schedule-A-PDF
- **Q:** Can I rely on a view from an upper plateau lot? **A:** No official source read for this note states a view-corridor or view-protection rule for Westwood Plateau lots. View depends on the lot's elevation, the slope development permit conditions and what can be built or grown between the house and the outlook, so treat any view as a lot-by-lot check of zoning height, the Unstable Slopes map and neighbouring parcels, not a neighbourhood promise. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17000/Official-Community-Plan---Schedule-A-PDF

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The Coquitlam Crunch Trail tops out on Westwood Plateau: it climbs 2.2 kilometres on the British Columbia Hydro right-of-way with 894 steps and 242 metres of elevation gain, and its access points include Bramble Park on Panorama Drive and David Avenue. https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/coquitlamcrunchtrail-27
- Route 187's plateau loop is one-directional in effect: outbound buses climb Johnson Street to Plateau Boulevard and Paddock Drive, and the return runs from Parkway Boulevard at Plateau Boulevard back down Johnson Street to Coquitlam Central Station. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt187.pdf
- Route 185 also touches the plateau's south edge, running Lansdowne Drive to Panorama Drive below the plateau before continuing as the 184 toward Moody Centre Station. http://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/192/tt185.pdf
- The City tells small-scale housing applicants to contact Development Services before designing if there is a creek on or next to the lot or a steep slope on or next to it — both are common conditions on plateau edge lots. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Route 187, Coquitlam Central Station to Parkway, is the plateau's bus: via Johnson Street, Plateau Boulevard and Paddock Drive to Parkway Boulevard at Plateau Boulevard, effective September 7, 2026 on the timetable read. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt187.pdf
- There is no SkyTrain or West Coast Express station in or beside the plateau; both are reached at Coquitlam Central Station, the route 187 terminus. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt187.pdf
- Johnson Street is the main road off the plateau, connecting south to Guildford Way and the City Centre; Plateau Boulevard and Parkway Boulevard are the internal plateau arterials the bus loop uses. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt187.pdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the Wildfire Hazard and Unstable Slopes development permit maps in OCP Schedule G first for any forest-edge or slope lot; the schedules show the layers, not a lot-level geotechnical conclusion. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17000/Official-Community-Plan---Schedule-A-PDF
- Check the City environmental development permit mapping, Schedule B, which maps environmentally sensitive areas, steep slopes and the interface wildfire boundary for the City Centre and Northeast areas. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/1527/Schedule-B---Environmental-Development-Permit-Areas-PDF?bidId=
- Check watercourse distance before planning any addition: within 30 metres of top of bank or ravine bank in this part of the city, a Watercourse Protection Development Permit is required before land is altered. https://www.coquitlam.ca/254/Development-Permits
