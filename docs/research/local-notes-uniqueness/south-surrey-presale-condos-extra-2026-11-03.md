## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Presales in British Columbia are regulated under the Real Estate Development Marketing Act (REDMA), and the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) is the regulator; seek advice from a lawyer, notary or licensee before signing, per BCFSA's own guide. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/consumer-resources/consumer-guide-pre-sale-real-estate-purchases
- A developer must give a buyer a disclosure statement, with a reasonable opportunity to read it before signing, and material facts about the development go in that statement, not the brochure. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/consumer-resources/consumer-guide-pre-sale-real-estate-purchases
- Effective April 1, 2025, disclosure statements filed under REDMA Policy Statement 14 carry a completed "Summary of Pre-Sale Risks and Buyer Rights" form at the front. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/consumer-resources/consumer-guide-pre-sale-real-estate-purchases
- BCFSA runs a pilot that lets projects of 100 or more units extend the early marketing period from 12 to 18 months; whether a South Surrey project is in the pilot is stated in its disclosure statement. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/consumer-resources/consumer-guide-pre-sale-real-estate-purchases

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** How long do I have to cancel a South Surrey presale contract?
**A:** Seven days, with no penalties, counted from the later of two dates: the date you sign the pre-sale contract, and the date you give the developer an acknowledgement that you had an opportunity to read the disclosure statement. That right is in REDMA and is explained in BCFSA's consumer guide. Use those seven days for a lawyer's review of the disclosure statement. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/consumer-resources/consumer-guide-pre-sale-real-estate-purchases

**Q:** What if the project changes after I sign?
**A:** The developer's disclosure duty continues after signing: when a material fact is omitted, changed or misrepresented, the developer must file an amendment to the disclosure statement and give a copy to every buyer who has not yet closed. A rescission right can attach to an amendment, so read each amendment on arrival rather than filing it away. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/consumer-resources/consumer-guide-pre-sale-real-estate-purchases

**Q:** How do I check whether a White Rock presale site is actually approved?
**A:** Use the City of White Rock Development Application Information Portal, which is updated daily and lists active and completed applications since 2023, sorted by file number in the format 23-001. Projects stay on the portal for about two years after a Council decision. Marketing material is not an approval; the portal status is the check. https://www.whiterockcity.ca/512/Development

**Q:** How do I check a Surrey-side presale's status?
**A:** Read the City of Surrey planning report for the application number, which states the stage in plain terms. The 20 Avenue report, for example, describes a 56-unit townhouse development as "currently at Third Reading" and lists the addresses, the Sunnyside Heights NCP designation and the permits still outstanding. Ask for the application number from the developer and match it to the report. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7922-0363-00-and-7922-0363-01

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Surrey's 2026 all-in Class 1 residential tax total on the City schedule is 3.42731 per $1,000 of assessed value; a presale buyer is taxed on assessed value after completion, not on the contract figure. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Both Surrey and White Rock are designated taxable areas for the provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax, so the annual declaration applies to a completed presale unit in either city. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
