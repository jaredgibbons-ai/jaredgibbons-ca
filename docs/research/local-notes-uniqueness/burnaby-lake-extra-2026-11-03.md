# Burnaby Lake Neighbourhood — Extra Local Notes
Page: burnaby-lake (Burnaby Lake, Burnaby; neighbourhood).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Burnaby Lake's housing sits on the streets around a regional park, not inside it: the residential edges are the Sperling Avenue and Kensington Avenue side to the west and north-west and the Government Road and Beaverbrook side to the east, wrapped around Burnaby Lake Regional Park. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park
- Two different things share this name: Burnaby Lake Regional Park is a Metro Vancouver regional park, while "Burnaby Lake" is also the Greater Vancouver Real Estate Board (GVR) sub-area name for the housing around it. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park
- Lougheed Highway runs along the north side and Highway 1 along the south side of the lake, and the City has delivered a pedestrian and cycling overpass across Highway 1 at the lake. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2026-05-28/how-burnaby-funding-growth-through-responsible-use-reserves
- The area's named park entrances are Piper Spit and Avalon, and its working streets for address searches are Sperling Avenue, Kensington Avenue, Winston Street and Government Road. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The housing here is detached houses on the park's edges, because the centre of the sub-area is public park, sports and civic land, not housing land. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park
- The west-edge anchor is the Burnaby Lake Sports Complex, reached from Kensington Avenue and Sperling Avenue, with 5 artificial turf fields with lights plus grass fields, diamonds and a fieldhouse. https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/burnaby-lake-sports-complex
- Bill Copeland Sports Centre, 3676 Kensington Avenue, sits inside that complex and is a 2,000-seat venue (1,984 permanent seats) with a National Hockey League (NHL)-sized rink. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/venue-rentals/bill-copeland-sports-centre

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The lake and park lands are park and conservation land under Burnaby's plan framework, so a home here borders protected parkland rather than future housing land. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park
- The City of Burnaby and Metro Vancouver have signed a 25-year lease agreement for the park (signed February 2025 — check current), under which Metro Vancouver funds maintenance, natural resource protection and capital improvements and will develop a Park Management Plan with the City within five years. http://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-02-05/city-burnaby-and-metro-vancouver-sign-burnaby-lake-regional-park
- Lake-edge lots sit beside a wildlife sanctuary shoreline, so watercourse-setback and habitat conditions are parcel-level checks, not assumptions. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is Burnaby Lake a neighbourhood or a park? **A:** It is both, for different things. Burnaby Lake Regional Park is the Metro Vancouver regional park around the lake, managed by Metro Vancouver since 1978 and now under a 25-year lease agreement with the City, while "Burnaby Lake" is also the board sub-area name for the houses on the streets around the park, so a listing's "Burnaby Lake" label describes the housing edge, not the park itself. http://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2025-02-05/city-burnaby-and-metro-vancouver-sign-burnaby-lake-regional-park
- **Q:** Can I paddle on the lake from my side of the park? **A:** Yes, from the west side. Metro Vancouver describes a rowing course with a rowing pavilion and a public canoe launch at the Burnaby Lake Sports Complex, while the former kayak and canoe launch at Piper Spit on the north shore can no longer be used because of siltation, so launch access depends on which entrance a home is near. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/Documents/burnaby-lake-regional-park-draft-management-plan.pdf
- **Q:** Which SkyTrain station serves this area? **A:** Sperling–Burnaby Lake Station, at 2800 Sperling Avenue. It is an elevated Millennium Line station at Sperling Avenue and Lougheed Highway on the area's north-west side, you can walk into the park directly from the station, and TransLink's station map shows bus routes 110, 134 and 144 connecting there. http://infomaps.translink.ca/system_maps/skytrain_station_maps/sperling_burnaby_lake_station.pdf
- **Q:** What is being built beside Bill Copeland Sports Centre? **A:** A new Burnaby Lake Recreation Complex. The City states it replaces the aging CG Brown Memorial Pool and Burnaby Lake Ice Rink with a 10-lane 50-metre pool, a 6-lane 25-metre pool and an NHL-sized rink, and the City's Fall 2025 update put it at 33 percent complete with an estimated opening in 2028, so nearby streets carry construction traffic in the meantime. http://www.city.burnaby.bc.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2025-11/City-Connect-Fall-2025.pdf
- **Q:** Does living by the lake mean lake frontage rights? **A:** No. The shoreline is a protected regional park managed by Metro Vancouver under its lease with the City, the park is described as a wildlife sanctuary, and its rules — including no wildlife feeding at Piper Spit — are part of living beside it; a home borders or overlooks the park, it does not come with private lake frontage. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/burnaby-lake-regional-park

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Still Creek is the lake's primary inflow, entering from the west, and the Brunette River drains the lake at its eastern end toward the Fraser River. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/Documents/burnaby-lake-regional-park-draft-management-plan.pdf
- The park protects 137 hectares of wetland and lowland forest around the lake, inside a Brunette River watershed that drains about 7,200 hectares of mostly urbanized land. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/Documents/burnaby-lake-regional-park-draft-management-plan.pdf
- The park's Sprott Street sports-field entrance on Sperling Avenue is served by bus routes 110 and 144, not by a station-side stop. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt110.pdf
- City construction notices through 2026 advised lane closures on Kensington Avenue between Sprott Street and Joe Sakic Way for the recreation complex work, with the southbound bus stop at Joe Sakic Way moved north but kept in service. https://www.burnaby.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2026-07/Burnaby-Lake-Recreation-Centre-Construction-Notification-July-13-2026.pdf
- The nearest hospital is Burnaby Hospital, 3935 Kincaid Street (Fraser Health), immediately south-west of the sub-area. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Burnaby/burnaby-hospital
- Schools differ by which edge of the lake an address is on: School District 41 (Burnaby) lists École Seaforth Elementary (Kindergarten to Grade 7) at 7881 Government Road on the east side and Sperling Elementary at 2200 Sperling Avenue on the west side. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/041

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Sperling–Burnaby Lake Station is this area's rapid-transit stop, an elevated Millennium Line station at Sperling Avenue and Lougheed Highway with buses 110, 134 and 144 at its bays. http://infomaps.translink.ca/system_maps/skytrain_station_maps/sperling_burnaby_lake_station.pdf
- Route 110 runs past the lake's north and west sides between Lougheed Station and Metrotown Station via Government Road, Winston Street, Sperling Station, Kensington, Sprott Street and Deer Lake Parkway. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt110.pdf
- Route 144 runs between Metrotown Station and Simon Fraser University (SFU) and connects at Sperling–Burnaby Lake Station, linking the lake's west side to both. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt144.pdf
- Highway 1 runs along the lake's south side, with the City's new pedestrian and cycling overpass crossing it at the lake. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2026-05-28/how-burnaby-funding-growth-through-responsible-use-reserves

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check Still Creek flood exposure for west-side lots: Metro Vancouver has coordinated watershed and floodplain studies for the Still Creek–Brunette River drainage area, and the City of Vancouver's 2025 water strategy notes seasonal flooding already occurs downstream in Burnaby. https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/rupert-renfrew-integrated-water-management-strategy.pdf
- Check the draft park plan before assuming a shoreline lot's setting stays unchanged: Metro Vancouver's draft management plan records siltation at the Piper Spit launch and proposes facility and trail changes over a 20-year horizon. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/Documents/burnaby-lake-regional-park-draft-management-plan.pdf
- Check Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) status for a specific parcel in the Agricultural Land Commission's ALR Property and Map Finder rather than from the sub-area name. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
