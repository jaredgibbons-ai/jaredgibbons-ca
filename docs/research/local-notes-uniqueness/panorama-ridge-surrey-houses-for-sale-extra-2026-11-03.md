# Panorama Ridge Houses — Extra Local Notes
Page: panorama-ridge-surrey-houses-for-sale (Panorama Ridge; detached houses).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Panorama Ridge, on the City's own definition, is bounded by Highway 10 (58 Avenue), Colebrook Road, 120 Street and King George Boulevard. A City of Surrey park engagement document for Joe Brown Park states that definition word for word and places the park, at 125A Street and 54 Avenue, in Newton's Panorama Ridge neighbourhood. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/JoeBrownOffleashAreaPublic EngagementSummary.pdf
- This is the Surrey side of the ridge only: the neighbourhood sits in Surrey's Newton community, south of Highway 10 and west of King George Boulevard, sloping down toward Colebrook Road. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/JoeBrownOffleashAreaPublic EngagementSummary.pdf
- The wider ratepayers' area is drawn larger — Highway 10 north, King George Boulevard east, Highway 99 south and Scott Road west — but that community-association boundary is not the City's; the City definition above governs this note. https://www.panoramaridge.org/wp-content/uploads/WPRRA-2013-Fall.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Houses on the west ridge are deliberately large-lot and low-density. A 2026 City planning report for 13438 and 13458 Highway 10 describes the West Panorama Ridge Local Area Plan designation as Suburban Residential (half-acre) and the Official Community Plan (OCP) designation as Suburban, inside a Suburban Density Exception Area capped at 5 units per hectare. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7921-0094-00
- Infill here means splitting acreage, not replacing small lots: that same application proposed subdividing two suburban residential lots totalling about 1.17 hectares into nine lots, on land zoned Acreage Residential (RA) and Suburban Residential (R1). https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7921-0094-00
- East of about 128 Street the pattern tightens to standard urban lots around Panorama Ridge Secondary and 64 Avenue, where a City report describes an R3-zoned site at 13314 64 Avenue beside the school — the ridge's two house patterns meet along 64 Avenue. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7922-0112-00.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The density cap is the rule that protects the ridge's lot sizes. The OCP Suburban Density Exception Area limit of 5 units per hectare applies to west-ridge lots, and the 2026 Highway 10 subdivision report states the nine-lot proposal did not comply with that maximum even though it complied with the broader Suburban designation — a buyer counting on a split must check the exception area first. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7921-0094-00
- Watercourse setbacks can cut into a ridge lot: the same report records a green-coded watercourse in the Highway 10 right-of-way north of the site, classed as a Class C feature under the zoning bylaw's watercourse rules. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7921-0094-00
- Surrey-wide suite, coach house and small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH) rules are written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note; on RA and R1 ridge lots the zone minimums and the density exception, not the city-wide unit count, are usually the binding limit. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7921-0094-00

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Which secondary school serves a Panorama Ridge house? **A:** École Panorama Ridge Secondary at 13220 64 Avenue, Grades 8 to 12, is the ridge's secondary school. Its feeder elementary schools, listed in the school's own registration handout, are Colebrook, Henry Bose (regular and French Immersion), North Ridge and Panorama Park, plus Woodward Hill French Immersion — Grade 7 students at a feeder school are registered automatically. https://media.surreyschools.ca/media/Default/medialib/2024-2025-registration-handout.442f63164880.pdf
- **Q:** Can a half-acre ridge lot be subdivided? **A:** Sometimes, but the density exception area is the gate. The City capped this part of the OCP at 5 units per hectare, and staff reported in 2026 that a proposal to turn two lots of about 1.17 hectares into nine lots did not comply with that cap — so the lot count a seller imagines may be higher than the plan allows. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7921-0094-00
- **Q:** Is the secondary school full? **A:** It is over its stated capacity on the City's most recent figure. A 2026 planning report records School District 36 advising that Panorama Ridge Secondary was operating at 108% capacity as of September 2025 — a reason to confirm catchment enrolment, not just catchment boundaries, before buying for the school. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7921-0094-00
- **Q:** Where is the elementary school for the west ridge? **A:** Colebrook Elementary at 5404 125A Street, Kindergarten to Grade 7, is the west-ridge elementary school on the provincial list, and the City's Highway 10 report names Colebrook as the expected elementary school for new homes at 13438 Highway 10, with Panorama Ridge Secondary for secondary students. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/036

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Joe Brown Park is the ridge's big park, and it is a working park file, not just green space. The City describes Joe Brown Park as a 27.5-hectare (68-acre) community park at 125A Street and 54 Avenue, and consulted on its dog off-leash area — the closest active park named for new homes at the ridge's east end is West Newton Community Park, 750 metres away. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/JoeBrownOffleashAreaPublic EngagementSummary.pdf
- Truck traffic is formally restricted through the ridge: the ratepayers' newsletter records City "No Truck" signs at 128 Street, Scott Road and Highway 10 after complaints about trucks passing Colebrook Elementary — through-truck routes are not the residential ridge streets. https://www.panoramaridge.org/wp-content/uploads/WPRRA-2013-Fall.pdf
- 64 Avenue, the ridge's northern east–west road, has just been rebuilt east of the ridge: the City widened 64 Avenue from 152 Street to 164 Street to four lanes with a multi-use pathway, completing a continuous four-lane corridor from 120 Street to 196 Street, with completion in 2026. https://www.surrey.ca/news-events/news/surrey-breaks-ground-final-phase-of-64-avenue-road-widening
- The East Panorama Ridge Plan is not a housing plan: the City lists it under South Newton as the plan guiding a small business park and light industrial area south of Highway 10, so the industrial pocket below the ridge is planned, not accidental. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/newton-land-use-plans

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Surrey-wide property tax rates, utility billing and the suite fee are written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- The ridge-specific cost point is servicing on large lots: the City's 2026 utilities financial plan keeps water, sewer, drainage and solid waste as self-funded, user-pay utilities, so a metered half-acre lot's bill follows its own consumption rather than a flat neighbourhood rate — confirm metered versus flat status for the specific lot. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/f001-2026-five-year-2026-2030-financial-plan-self-funded-utilities
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) applies here because the City of Surrey is a designated taxable area, so every residential owner files the yearly declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The ridge is a car-first area with two highway edges. Highway 10 runs along the north side and King George Boulevard along the east, and the City frames 64 Avenue as the east–west connector linking Surrey residents to the City of Delta and the Township of Langley. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/capital-projects/64-avenue-improvements
- The hospital and SkyTrain planning east of the ridge will change its bus pattern: the City is investigating a direct bus connection linking the new Cloverdale hospital, the 184 Street Surrey–Langley SkyTrain station and Campbell Heights, with final routing to be set by TransLink — no route is in service on that concept yet. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2025_R264.pdf
- Measured drive times and a confirmed in-ridge bus route list are UNCONFIRMED in this note (see NOT CONFIRMED); do not rely on a frequency figure that no operator page in this pass stated.

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Slope and watercourse checks come first on a ridge lot. The Highway 10 report shows the pattern: a classified watercourse beside the site, watercourse setback rules in the zoning bylaw, and a local area plan holding density down — check the lot in the City's COSMOS map for watercourse and development permit layers before assuming a buildable footprint. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7921-0094-00
- Capacity, not catchment, is the school risk: Panorama Ridge Secondary was reported at 108% capacity in September 2025, so a catchment address alone does not guarantee a seat pathway — confirm with School District 36. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7921-0094-00
- No City floodplain guide specific to Panorama Ridge was found in this pass, unlike Bridgeview's named guide; a lot-level flood, liquefaction or steep-slope status is UNCONFIRMED here and must be checked on the City's hazard maps for the specific lot.
