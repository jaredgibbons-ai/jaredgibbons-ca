# Abbotsford Houses — Extra Local Notes
Page: abbotsford-houses-for-sale (Abbotsford; detached houses).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Abbotsford is one city, not a set of separate towns, and its detached houses sit inside the City of Abbotsford in the Fraser Valley Regional District (FVRD). The City's Official Community Plan (OCP) is Bylaw No. 2600-2016, as amended. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2025bschedulea
- Highway 1 (Trans-Canada Highway) cuts the city east–west, with house areas reached from exits including Mount Lehman Road, Clearbrook Road, McCallum Road, Sumas Way (Highway 11) and Whatcom Road. Highway 1 widening in the Fraser Valley is a provincial project. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/transportation-infrastructure-projects/highway-1-fraser-valley/documents/2026-09-22-september-fraser-valley-hwy1-fact-sheet.pdf
- The city's detailed house-area rules sit in neighbourhood plans, not in the OCP alone. The City lists its neighbourhood plans on one page, including the City Centre Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2913-2019), the Historic Downtown Neighbourhood Plan (Bylaw 2920-2019) and the McKee Neighbourhood Plan added in 2023. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/community-planning/neighbourhood-plans

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Detached houses are the form this page covers, from older central streets to acreage houses on the rural edges. Acreage parcels with houses occur on the rural west and southwest edges, serviced by wells and onsite sewage where outside the urban boundary. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400210
- New detached supply is charged as a distinct class in the City's development cost charge (Development Cost Charge, DCC) summary: "single detached" is charged per lot, separate from duplex, townhouse and apartment. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2026-04/DCC-ACC%20Rates%20Summary%20Bylaw%20No.%203633-2025_0.pdf
- The McKee area on Sumas Mountain is the city's last large greenfield house area. The City's McKee profile describes the plan area before most building took place. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/2020%20McKee%20Profile%20-%20spreads%20final.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- One zoning bylaw governs every Abbotsford house lot: Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014. The City's zoning page states the bylaw sets zones and rules for each property. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/bylaw-services/bylaw-listings/zoning-bylaw-2014
- Added homes on a house lot follow section 140 of that bylaw. Section 140's small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH) provision allows 3 units on lots 280 square metres or less and 4 units on lots greater than 280 square metres in the stated conditions, and section 140.3.8 states one secondary suite is permitted in all single detached dwellings. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400140
- Farm-edge houses can sit in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR). The OCP includes an ALR map (Map 16), and housing on ALR land also follows the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) rules. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2025bschedulea
- Houses near Abbotsford International Airport (YXX) can face federal height limits. The City's YXX development guidelines tie building height to Airport Zoning Regulations and drawing M-500. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/docs/business-development/Development%20Guidlines%20for%20YXX%202020.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Can I add a suite to an Abbotsford house? **A:** A secondary suite is permitted in all single detached dwellings under section 140.3.8 of the zoning bylaw. That is a City bylaw permission, not a guarantee for one address: the lot still has to meet the bylaw's other conditions, parking and building-code rules, so confirm the exact address with the City before counting suite income in an offer. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400140
- **Q:** How many homes can go on one Abbotsford house lot? **A:** Up to four in the stated small-scale multi-unit conditions. Section 140 allows 3 units on lots 280 square metres or less and 4 units on lots greater than 280 square metres where the conditions are met. Zone, servicing and development permit areas can still limit a specific lot, so the City's WebMap and a zoning confirmation come before any rebuild plan. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400140
- **Q:** Which school does an Abbotsford address feed into? **A:** The catchment is set by street address, not by neighbourhood name. Abbotsford School District No. 34 (SD34) states its School Locator portal determines the catchment school, and its catchment policy is Policy 18. Check the exact address in the locator before removing subjects, because two houses on the same street can sit in different catchments. https://www.abbyschools.ca/schools/catchments
- **Q:** Is Abbotsford in the Speculation and Vacancy Tax area? **A:** Yes, Abbotsford is a taxable area, so an owner must declare each year even when exempt. The Province lists the taxable areas on its Speculation and Vacancy Tax pages, and reserve and treaty lands are not part of the taxable areas. A house left empty can be taxed even though a principal residence is normally exempt. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- **Q:** Do I check flood risk the same way everywhere in Abbotsford? **A:** No, the low prairie areas and the hillside areas carry different checks. The City runs a flood response page for the Sumas and Matsqui prairie dike areas, while hillside lots are checked against slope and creek rules in the OCP. Start with the City's WebMap for the parcel, then the flood page if the lot is on the prairie floor. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Your water bill cycle depends on where the house sits. The City bills water and sewer bi-monthly, except in the Clearbrook Waterworks District, where sewer is billed quarterly. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/utility-billing/current-utility-bill
- Paying the utility bill on time cuts it by 5 percent. The City states a 5 percent discount applies when payment is received by the due date printed on the bill. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/utility-billing/current-utility-bill
- Unpaid utility balances do not stay separate from the house. The City states any utility account balance outstanding at December 31, 2025 was transferred to the property tax account and is subject to daily interest. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/utility-billing/current-utility-bill
- The Gur Sikh Temple on South Fraser Way is a National Historic Site of Canada. Parks Canada lists the temple as a designated place, in the central part of the city. https://www.pc.gc.ca/culture/designation/lieu-site/temple-sikh-abbotsford-sikh-temple
- Abbotsford Regional Hospital and Cancer Centre is the city's hospital, listed by Fraser Health in Abbotsford. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Abbotsford/abbotsford-regional-hospital-and-cancer-centre
- The City's parcel lookup is its WebMap, not a listing site. The City provides WebMap under mapping, data and analytics for property information. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/mapping-data-analytics/webmap

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Abbotsford property tax rates are set each year in a Tax Rates Bylaw, expressed per $1,000 of taxable value. The 2025 bylaw is Bylaw No. 3643-2025, read a first and second time May 6, 2025. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2025-05/Bylaw%203643-2025%20Tax%20Rates%20Bylaw%202025.pdf
- Water and sewer are charged by use, not as one flat city-wide fee. Under the Fees and Charges Bylaw amendment for 2025 (Bylaw No. 3598-2025), residential water was $1.42 per cubic metre and sanitary sewer $1.30 per cubic metre, with a 5 percent discount for payment by the due date. https://pub-abbotsford.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=693
- Those utility rates rose again in 2026. The City states Council approved a 4 percent increase to water and sewer rates in January 2026, effective March 1, 2026: residential water $1.46 per cubic metre and sewer $1.35 per cubic metre. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/utility-billing/water-sewer-rates
- Solid waste is an annual cart-based fee. The 2025 fees bylaw example for a serviced property with a 240-litre garbage cart, 240-litre recycling cart and 240-litre compostables cart is a basic annual fee of $407. https://pub-abbotsford.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=693
- A new detached house carries a single-detached DCC total of $64,860 per lot in the City summary effective February 1, 2026, including school site acquisition charges. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2026-04/DCC-ACC%20Rates%20Summary%20Bylaw%20No.%203633-2025_0.pdf
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies in Abbotsford, which the Province lists as a taxable area. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Abbotsford has no SkyTrain; buses are run by BC Transit in the Central Fraser Valley system. Route 66, the Fraser Valley Express, links Abbotsford with Langley, Chilliwack and the SkyTrain connection to the west. https://www.bctransit.com/central-fraser-valley/schedules-and-maps/route-overview/?route=66
- Route 1 (Highstreet / University of the Fraser Valley, UFV) and Route 2 (Highstreet / McMillan) are the cross-town GoLines named in BC Transit's route list — see the route pages for current routing. https://www.bctransit.com/central-fraser-valley/schedules-and-maps/route-overview/?route=2
- Route 21 connects Abbotsford with Aldergrove and Langley, with peak trips to Abbotsford International Airport (YXX) in the BC Transit route list carried in the Nov 1 research — check current. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/cfv_map_21.pdf
- Highway 11 (Sumas Way) runs south from Highway 1 to the Sumas–Huntingdon border crossing with the United States, through the Poplar side of the city — see the poplar-abbotsford-houses-for-sale note.

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the dike and flood layer first for any prairie-floor house. The City's flood response pages cover the Sumas Prairie and Matsqui Prairie dike areas and evacuation information. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- Check the Natural Environment Development Permit rules before planning work near a creek or slope. The City's Natural Environment Development Permit (NEDP) guide ties environmental assessment reports to the OCP and neighbourhood plans. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2026-09/NEDP%20Application%20Guide_Appendix%20A_August%202026%20%5BFinal%5D.pdf
- Check wells and septic where a house is outside the urban boundary. The Province's groundwater pages cover well records and registration for property owners. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/groundwater-wells-aquifers/information-for-property-owners/well-records-registration
- Check onsite sewage rules with Fraser Health for a house on a septic system. Fraser Health publishes the regulatory requirements for onsite sewerage systems. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/health-topics-a-to-z/onsite-sewerage-systems/regulatory-requirements-for-onsite-sewerage-systems
- Check the parcel itself on the City WebMap; the map shows the lot, it does not replace a title search or a survey. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/mapping-data-analytics/webmap
