## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Bridgeview is a low Fraser River bench neighbourhood in North Surrey, and almost everything about buying attached housing here is set by the river, the dike and the Pattullo Bridge approach. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/south-westminster
- The City groups Bridgeview's riverfront into the South Westminster plan area: Fraser River to the west, King George Boulevard to the north and 96 Avenue to the south, largely industrial along the river between Scott Road Station and the Surrey Fraser Docks. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/south-westminster
- The residential core is the grid around Bridgeview Park at 12560 115 Avenue and the Bridgeview Community Centre at 11475 126A Street — the streets a townhome buyer here will actually see. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/bridgeview-park
- The City calls Bridgeview Park's setting "the historic neighbourhood of Bridgeview" but publishes no boundary for the board sub-area — the boundary is NOT CONFIRMED and is flagged again below. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/bridgeview-park

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Bridgeview's housing is detached houses on small floodplain lots beside light-industrial land, and no named condo or townhome complex in Bridgeview was confirmed on a municipal, strata or developer source read in this pass — the thin attached shelf is documented honestly in NOT CONFIRMED. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/south-westminster
- The zoning tells the same story: residential lots sit in the Single Family Residential (RF) pattern while the riverfront carries Light Impact Industrial (IL) and Special Industry (I-4) zones, so a "townhome" search here mostly returns houses and duplex-form product. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7917-0186-00.pdf
- The South Westminster plan, approved December 2003, directs a business park and mixed use to the north end around Scott Road Station and keeps Brownsville Bar Park as the river beach — attached housing growth is planned at the station end, not mid-Bridgeview. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/south-westminster
- Bridgeview Elementary at 12834 115A Avenue is the in-neighbourhood school, with Kwantlen Park Secondary at 10441 132 Street named as the family's secondary school in district material. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/036

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Floodproofing is the first rule: in Bridgeview's designated floodplain, the RF zone's special regulations allow the listed residential uses only on lots with at least 15 metres of frontage and 464 square metres of area, and Part 8 (Floodproofing) of Zoning By-law No. 12000 sets the building elevation rules. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7916-0352-00.pdf
- A current City report for 11444 and 11426 124 Street states the Fraser River floodplain here requires a Minimum Building Elevation (MBE) of 4.8 metres geodetic — roughly three metres of fill on a typical lot to found a building at that elevation. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7924-0145-00.pdf
- Development in the 200-year floodplain needs a Hazard Lands (Flood Prone Areas) Development Permit under the Official Community Plan (OCP), with a geotechnical report addressing the hazard-lands guidelines — the pattern the City applied at 13099 116 Avenue. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7917-0186-00.pdf
- Lot consolidation in Bridgeview's designated floodplain areas has its own minimums in the RF zone — 464 square metres and 15 metres frontage, with no minimum depth — which is the rule that shapes any townhome assembly here. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7916-0457-00.pdf
- Truck parks are a live land-use fight: the November 17, 2025 report on 124 Street records that Bridgeview's roads were built on soft base material not intended for heavy trucks, and that temporary use permits for truck parks in Bridgeview tend to become permanent. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7924-0145-00.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** What does the 4.8-metre Minimum Building Elevation mean for a Bridgeview townhome or house? **A:** It means the building's floor must sit at 4.8 metres geodetic in the Fraser River floodplain, which a 2025 City report says takes about three metres of fill on the 124 Street lots it studied. Any attached project here carries fill, geotechnical and development-permit costs a Guildford townhome does not, and an older home may pre-date the standard entirely. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7924-0145-00.pdf
- **Q:** Is Bridgeview behind a dike, or just low? **A:** Both — it is low land in the Fraser River floodplain that the City treats as flood-prone hazard land. City reports place Bridgeview lots inside the 200-year floodplain, require a Hazard Lands development permit for new development, and record the wider Surrey floodplain system of dikes and pump stations as the protection works. A specific lot's status is read in the City mapping, not from the neighbourhood name. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7917-0186-00.pdf
- **Q:** Why is there so little condo or townhome stock to choose from here? **A:** Because the plan and the ground both push against it. South Westminster is designated largely industrial along the river, the residential lots carry floodplain minimum lot sizes of 464 square metres with 15-metre frontages, and each new project needs floodproofing and a hazard-lands permit. The station-end mixed use around Scott Road Station is where the plan expects attached growth instead. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/south-westminster
- **Q:** Which bus actually runs through Bridgeview? **A:** Route 371, on an operator timetable: Surrey Central Station to Scott Road Station via Bridgeview Drive, 115 Avenue, 128 Street, 132 Street, 114 Avenue, 112 Avenue and Gateway Station. The R6 Scott Road RapidBus runs on Scott Road at the area's west edge, linking Scott Road Station to Newton Exchange — it does not enter the Bridgeview grid. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt371.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Brownsville Bar Park and Sandbar, in the 1100 block of Portage Road, is registered heritage site No. 223 (registered January 12, 2004), marking the early Fraser River ferry crossing at Browns Landing — the river beach at the end of Bridgeview's streets is a protected heritage place. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/heritage-conservation/heritage-sites/whalley/brownsville-park-and-sandbar
- Bridgeview Park at 12560 115 Avenue packs in a water park, two softball diamonds, a multi-use field and tennis, pickleball and basketball courts — the recreation load of the whole bench lands on this one park. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/bridgeview-park
- The Bridgeview Community Centre at 11475 126A Street has one gymnasium — the only City recreation facility inside the neighbourhood. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/recreation-facilities/bridgeview-community-centre
- Heavy trucks on soft local roads are a documented complaint, not local colour: the City's 2025 truck-park report records trucks damaging ditches and roads and vibrating neighbouring homes on 124 Street. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7924-0145-00.pdf
- There is no library branch in Bridgeview; the City Centre Branch at 10350 University Drive is the Whalley branch serving it. https://surreylibraries.ca/locations/city-centre-branch

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Bridgeview pays the City of Surrey rate: total 2026 residential (Class 1) $3.42731 per $1,000 of assessed value, broken down in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Floodplain ownership has a carrying cost beyond tax: a new build or major renovation must meet the 4.8-metre Minimum Building Elevation and carry a geotechnical report for the hazard-lands permit — costs to price before comparing a Bridgeview lot with a Guildford lot. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7917-0186-00.pdf
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) applies in Surrey, a Province-listed designated taxable area, so even a vacant floodplain holding property needs the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood is the headline risk: look the exact lot up in the City of Surrey Mapping Online System (COSMOS) flood and hazard layers, then confirm whether it sits in the 200-year Fraser River floodplain and what Minimum Building Elevation applies — the neighbourhood-wide figure found is 4.8 metres geodetic. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7924-0145-00.pdf
- A Hazard Lands (Flood Prone Areas) Development Permit is required for development in the floodplain, supported by a geotechnical report — the City report for 13099 116 Avenue shows the permit also bundled streamside setbacks (a 10-metre setback plus a 15-metre no-build covenant for an A/O class ditch). https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7917-0186-00.pdf
- Soft ground is a building fact here: City reporting describes Bridgeview roads as historically built on softer base materials, and foundations in the floodplain need the geotechnical work the permit requires — the flood map does not tell a buyer what an existing older home was actually built on. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7924-0145-00.pdf
- For any strata unit, check the depreciation report for flood-related insurance and deductible exposure through the Form B Information Certificate — provincial law requires stratas of five or more lots to obtain a depreciation report every five years. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
