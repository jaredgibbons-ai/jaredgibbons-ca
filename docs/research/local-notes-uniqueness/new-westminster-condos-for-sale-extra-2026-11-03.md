# New Westminster Condos — Extra Local Notes
Page: new-westminster-condos-for-sale (New Westminster (city-wide); condos).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- New Westminster's condo stock is organized around its five SkyTrain stations, because the City states Transit Oriented Development (TOD) Areas enable density within 800 metres of the City's five SkyTrain stations. Downtown, the Quay, Sapperton and Uptown each sit in relation to one of those mainland station areas, while Queensborough is planned under its own community plan. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects
- Downtown's condo core clusters around New Westminster Station and Columbia Station, because developer EDGAR's own project writing states New Westminster already has many transit-oriented developments primarily clustered around New Westminster Station and Columbia Station in the downtown core, such as the Plaza 88 mixed-use complex built around New Westminster Station. https://edgardevelopment.com/transit-oriented-developments-set-to-usher-in-a-new-new-westminster/
- Sapperton's condo node is the Brewery District on Keary Street, because Wesgroup states its Brewery District is in the Sapperton neighbourhood, on the former Labatt brewery site, steps from the Sapperton SkyTrain Station. Keary Street and Brunette Avenue are the streets that node is addressed from. https://wesgroup.ca/thekeary/neighbourhood/
- Queensborough is planned under its own community plan, because the City states the Queensborough Community Plan is the key guiding document for new development in Queensborough, with its own land use designations and development permit guidelines. The Queensborough Eastern Neighbourhood Node inside that plan is defined by Stanley Street to the west, Duncan Street to the north and east, and Ewen Avenue to the south. https://www.newwestcity.ca/planning-building-and-development/projects-on-the-go/articles/3475.php?status=complete

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Downtown holds the city's tower stock around its two stations, because EDGAR states transit-oriented developments in New Westminster are primarily clustered around New Westminster Station and Columbia Station in the downtown core, with Plaza 88 built around New Westminster Station as the named example. Tower condos elsewhere in the city are the exception to that downtown pattern. https://edgardevelopment.com/transit-oriented-developments-set-to-usher-in-a-new-new-westminster/
- Sapperton's stock is a single master-planned district's buildings, because Wesgroup states the Brewery District is a completed community of residential highrises, shops and office buildings on the former brewery site, and that The Keary is the final ownership opportunity in that district. A Sapperton condo search is therefore largely a search within one developer's district on Keary Street. https://wesgroup.ca/residential/page/2/
- Queensborough's multi-family stock is governed by named development permit areas, because the City lists Queensborough residential Development Permit Areas including Ewen Avenue Multi-Family, Compact Lot, East Queensborough, Port Royal and Queensborough Eastern Node. The form of a Queensborough condo or multi-family building is set by which of those permit areas the parcel falls in. https://www.newwestcity.ca/development-policies-and-process/development-permit-areas/sb_expander_articles/1184.php
- Uptown and the Brow of the Hill carry older mid-rise strata stock alongside the tower districts, but individual Uptown building names could not be verified from a municipal or strata source read for this note, so they are listed under NOT CONFIRMED rather than named here. https://www.newwestcity.ca/zoning-bylaw/sb_expander_articles/1618.php

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The zoning document is Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001, consolidated as of June 8, 2026, according to the City's zoning bylaw page. The City states amendment bylaws have been combined with the original bylaw for convenience only and the consolidation is not a legal document, so a condo buyer checking a building's zone should cite the consolidation date, not treat the web copy as the legal text. https://www.newwestcity.ca/zoning-bylaw/sb_expander_articles/1618.php
- Station-area density still requires a rezoning, because the City states that in February 2026 Council amended the Official Community Plan (OCP) designations in the Transit Oriented Development Areas to allow the minimum provincial densities, but no changes were made to zoning, meaning a rezoning application is still required before the new heights and densities can be built. An OCP designation on a condo site is not a building approval. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects
- New multi-unit buildings must include in-unit or common storage, because the City states Council in May 2025 approved requirements that residential storage space of at least 3.7 square metres (39.8 square feet) be provided for all new dwelling units, within the unit or in a common storage room, applying to applications received on or after May 5, 2025. That rule changes what a new condo unit includes compared with the existing stock. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects
- Family-sized units are now a bylaw requirement in new multi-unit buildings, because the City states the updated Family-Friendly Housing Policy, implemented by Zoning Bylaw changes in November 2024, increases the minimum number of two- and three-bedroom units required and establishes minimum unit sizes. For rental projects, after a two-year transition, a minimum of 35 percent two- and three-bedroom units, with at least 10 percent three-bedroom or more, is required effective November 18, 2026. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects
- Townhouse zoning now has named districts, because the City's Interim Development Review Framework, updated June 22, 2026, states townhouses are expected to rezone to Residential Townhouse (RT)-A or RT-B zoning districts, and infill housing to Residential Ground-Oriented Infill (RGO)-A or RGO-B districts. The Zoning Bylaw's multi-unit residential volume states the intent of RT-A (district 405, Bylaw 8524, 2025) and RT-B (district 406) directly, which settles the district names in the current bylaw text. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/400_Multi_Unit_Residential___As_of_June_8_2026.pdf
- New Westminster-wide tax and utility basics: see the the-heights-new-westminster-houses-for-sale note. The batch's townhouse pipeline detail is in the new-westminster-townhomes-for-sale note; the condo and apartment pipeline is in section 6 of this note.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Does provincial transit-oriented density mean a tower can be built beside my condo without a rezoning?
**A:** No, because the City states it amended the Official Community Plan designations in the Transit Oriented Development Areas in February 2026 to allow the provincial minimum densities, but made no changes to zoning, so a rezoning application is still required before those heights and densities can be built. The City also cautions against speculation based on anticipating Council's decisions on such applications. https://www.newwestcity.ca/new-provincial-housing-legislation/transit-oriented-development-areas/sb_expander_articles/6750.php

**Q:** What is actually proposed at Columbia Square, beside New Westminster Station?
**A:** EDGAR Development proposes eight towers at 88 Tenth Street, on the 7.1-acre Columbia Square site it states it acquired in 2021, with 3,800 homes in its current project description, plus commercial, childcare, public space and space for a new school. EDGAR's earlier June 2023 unveiling described approximately 2,400 homes for the same site, so the home count has changed between the developer's own releases and should be rechecked against the current rezoning file. https://edgardevelopment.com/transit-oriented-developments-set-to-usher-in-a-new-new-westminster/

**Q:** Is Sapperton finished building, or is more coming?
**A:** The Brewery District itself is presented by its developer as complete, with The Keary at 230 Keary Street as the final ownership opportunity in Wesgroup's master-planned community there. Wesgroup states homeowners at The Keary are steps from the Sapperton SkyTrain Station, so Sapperton's remaining condo supply, on the developer's own account, is that final Keary Street building rather than a further phase of the district. https://wesgroup.ca/residential/page/2/

**Q:** Do new condos here have to include family-sized units and storage?
**A:** Yes, under two stated City rules. The Family-Friendly Housing Policy's Zoning Bylaw changes, adopted November 2024, raise the minimum share of two- and three-bedroom units and set minimum unit sizes in new multi-unit developments, and the May 2025 storage rule requires at least 3.7 square metres of storage for every new dwelling unit, in-unit or in a common room, for applications from May 5, 2025. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects

**Q:** What extra charges will a new condo project here carry?
**A:** Two City charges were adopted in late 2025. The City states Council adopted Amenity Cost Charge Bylaw No. 8540, 2025 on December 1, 2025, requiring development applications to help fund amenities such as community centres, libraries and child care, and adopted Development Cost Charge Bylaw Amendment Bylaw No. 8539, 2025 on January 12, 2026, adding fire protection and police facility categories. Both are in force on the City's Development Updates page. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Plaza 88 is built around New Westminster Station itself, because EDGAR describes it as the Plaza 88 mixed-use complex built around New Westminster Station in the downtown core. A downtown condo's station access can therefore be internal to its own complex, which is a different condition from the walk-to-station access in Sapperton or Uptown. https://edgardevelopment.com/transit-oriented-developments-set-to-usher-in-a-new-new-westminster/
- The Keary's name is a Sapperton street history, because Wesgroup states Royal Engineer James Keary and his family arrived in New Westminster, his son William later became Mayor of New Westminster, and Keary Street and The Keary continue to honour the family name. The building at 230 Keary Street is named for the street's own family, not a marketing invention. https://wesgroup.ca/thekeary/neighbourhood/
- A riverfront connection is proposed between the city's two river parks, because the City states it proposes completing the Brunette-Fraser Regional Greenway with a floating greenway between Westminster Pier Park and Sapperton Landing Park, passing under the New Westminster Rail Bridge and the new Pattullo Bridge. Quay and Sapperton condos sit at the two ends of that proposed link. https://www.newwestcity.ca/riverfrontvision/articles/5969.php
- Queensborough condo parcels can carry a flood permit layer the downtown towers do not, because the City lists a Flood Hazard Development Permit Area among Queensborough's Development Permit Areas, and states single detached dwellings on lots of 371.6 square metres or more are exempt from the other Queensborough guidelines but not from the Flood Hazard guidelines. https://www.newwestcity.ca/development-policies-and-process/development-permit-areas/sb_expander_articles/1184.php
- The 22nd Street station area is under a City-led planning hold, because the City states that while the reGENERATE Vision planning work continues, expected through 2025 and into mid-2027, most development applications around the SkyTrain station and Transit Village will be considered premature. West End and 22nd Street area condo proposals therefore face a stated pause that downtown proposals do not. https://www.newwestcity.ca/planning-building-and-development/new-westminsters-official-community-plan/ocp-implementation-projects/22nd-street-station-area-bold-vision/sb_expander_articles/7272.php
- The City is under a provincial housing target, because the City states the Province issued a Housing Target Order in July 2024 requiring 4,432 net new units over five years, the background count against which the section 6 pipeline is reviewed. https://www.newwestcity.ca/new-provincial-housing-legislation
- School District No. 40 (New Westminster) has exactly one secondary school for the whole city, New Westminster Secondary School at 820 6th Street, so a condo buyer's secondary catchment does not change between Downtown, the Quay, Sapperton, Uptown and Queensborough. Elementary and middle catchments do change, with Fraser River Middle School at 800 Queens Avenue and Queensborough Middle School at 833 Salter Street serving different parts of the city. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in/new-westminster

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- New Westminster-wide property tax, utility billing and Speculation and Vacancy Tax treatment: see the the-heights-new-westminster-houses-for-sale note.
- New condo projects carry the Amenity Cost Charge, because the City states Amenity Cost Charge Bylaw No. 8540, 2025, adopted December 1, 2025, requires development applications to assist in funding new amenities such as community centres, libraries and child care. The City directs readers to its Financing Growth webpage for current rates, which are the rates a presale buyer should check against the developer's disclosure. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects
- Development Cost Charges were widened in 2026, because the City states Development Cost Charge Bylaw Amendment Bylaw No. 8539, 2025, adopted January 12, 2026, updated the charges to include additional categories for fire protection and police facilities permitted by provincial legislation, and that updated rates are in effect. A project approved before that date and a project approved after it do not carry the same charge categories. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects
- Inclusionary housing is a stated cost of added density, because the City states its Inclusionary Housing Policy, updated September 8, 2025, sets the expectation that applicable multiple unit residential or mixed-use applications seeking increases in development entitlements will contribute towards below market housing units. That contribution is part of a new condo project's economics in New Westminster, separate from the two charge bylaws above. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Five SkyTrain stations serve the city, because the City states its Transit Oriented Development Areas enable density within 800 metres of the City's five SkyTrain stations. TransLink's Expo Line timetable lists the in-city sequence as 22nd Street, New Westminster and Columbia on the King George branch, and Columbia, Sapperton and Braid on the Production Way–University branch. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt991.pdf
- 22nd Street Station is addressed at 649 22nd Street, because TransLink's station map for 22nd Street Station gives that address in New Westminster, with bus bays for routes including the 410 to Richmond-Brighouse, the 101 to Lougheed Station, the 104 to Annacis Island and the 100 to Marpole Loop. A West End condo's transit is that station's bus grid as much as the Expo Line itself. http://infomaps.translink.ca/system_maps/skytrain_station_maps/22nd_street_station.pdf
- Queensborough has no station in TransLink's in-city Expo Line list, which names 22nd Street, New Westminster, Columbia, Sapperton and Braid stations, so its condo transit runs to those mainland stations. The City's Queensborough planning documents treat Queensborough under its own community plan, separate from the mainland station areas, which is why Queensborough development is reviewed against the Queensborough Community Plan rather than a station-area designation. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt991.pdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Do not price a site on provincial TOD heights alone, because the City's speculation disclaimer states the transit oriented development area tiers and maps do not create development rights, that development rights are determined by the Zoning Bylaw, and that the City cautions against speculation based on anticipating Council's decisions. A rezoning is the step that changes a condo site's rights. https://www.newwestcity.ca/new-provincial-housing-legislation/transit-oriented-development-areas/sb_expander_articles/6750.php
- Check whether an existing rental building is in the file, because the City states properties containing existing rental units are required to comply with the Council-endorsed Rental Replacement Policy, and that all development applications currently in progress will be expected to meet the new Tenant Relocation Policy being modelled on Burnaby's regulation. A redevelopment assembly with sitting rental units carries both policies. https://www.newwestcity.ca/devprojects
- Verify project unit counts against the current City file, not the launch release, because EDGAR's own releases for Columbia Square at 88 Tenth Street state approximately 2,400 homes in June 2023 and 3,800 homes in its later release, so the rezoning application's current figure is the one to rely on. https://edgardevelopment.com/edgar-unveils-new-pedestrian-only-comprehensive-planned-community-in-new-westminster/
- For Queensborough purchases, read the Development Permit Area for the parcel, because the City lists six categories of Queensborough Development Permit Areas, including the Flood Hazard area, and states that where a parcel falls within more than one area, one Development Permit may be issued if the guidelines for each area are addressed. A Queensborough condo's flood and form rules are parcel-specific. https://www.newwestcity.ca/development-policies-and-process/development-permit-areas/sb_expander_articles/1184.php
