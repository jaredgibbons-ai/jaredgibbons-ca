# BC 55+ Strata Rules — Extra Local Notes
Page: bc-55-plus-strata-rules (British Columbia; 55+ strata rules).
## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- These rules are province-wide strata rules, not a neighbourhood rule, and they apply only where a strata corporation has an age-restriction bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- The Province's age-restriction page states strata corporations in British Columbia (BC) can have bylaws that restrict the age of one or more residents to age 55 or older. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- A manufactured-home park, a co-op, a rental building and a freehold house are not governed by this strata bylaw rule merely because they use a 55+ label, and no Province page read here extends the strata rule to them.

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The housing this page is about is strata lots in a strata corporation with a 55+ bylaw, in any building form that corporation takes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- People younger than 55 can own a strata lot in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live in it, because the bylaw applies to residents, not ownership. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- The Province uses the term specified resident for an owner, tenant or occupant who lives in the lot and either meets the age requirement or has a legacy exemption. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Only a 55+ age bylaw is allowed now, and any bylaw restricting residents to a younger age such as 19+, 25+ or 40+ is no longer permitted after November 24, 2022. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- The Province's legislation-changes page states that on November 24, 2022, only bylaws limiting occupants to age 55+ remained allowed, any age bylaw below 55 became invalid, and the change applies regardless of existing bylaws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- No strata corporation may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw after November 24, 2022, and all such bylaws are invalid, while a strata may still have or create a bylaw banning or limiting short-term rentals. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation
- A bylaw amendment is made by a 3/4 vote of owners and filed at the Land Title Office, and new or changed bylaws do not take effect until filed, except pet bylaws as the Province notes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/amending-bylaws-and-rules
- A bylaw cannot contravene the Strata Property Act, the Strata Property Regulation, the Human Rights Code or any other law, and bylaws restricting occupants to age 55+ are expressly permitted within that limit. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/amending-bylaws-and-rules
- The age-restriction page cites Strata Property Act Part 7, sections 123.1 and 123.2, and Strata Property Regulation 7.01 as its references — those are the only section numbers cited in this note, because they are the numbers that page itself states. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** My spouse is under 55 — can we both live in a 55+ strata? **A:** Yes, a younger spouse or partner is exempt when the other resident is a specified resident. The Province lists an exemption for a younger spouse married to, or living in a marriage-like relationship with, a specified resident who meets the age requirement or holds a legacy exemption. That exemption was part of the additional family exemptions that took effect May 1, 2023, alongside the children cases described on the same page. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q:** What happens to a partner or spouse who survives the 55+ resident? **A:** The Province pages read in this pass do not state a separate surviving-spouse rule in those words, so that situation is UNCONFIRMED here and must not be guessed. What the page does state is that exemptions exist in relationship to a specified resident, that a corporation can specify further exemptions in its own bylaws, and that legacy residents keep their own caregiver and family exemptions. Read the specific corporation's filed bylaws and get legal advice for a survivorship case. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q:** Can a live-in caregiver under 55 live in the unit? **A:** Yes, caregiver exemptions apply to live-in caregivers who provide care to another resident who is dependent on them due to illness, disability or frailty. The Province gives the example of an 87-year-old resident living with a 44-year-old caregiver. The caregiver exemption existed with the November 24, 2022 changes, before the wider family exemptions were added on May 1, 2023. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q:** I was living there before the 55+ bylaw passed — must I leave? **A:** No, legacy exemptions protect a resident who was lawfully residing in the lot when the bylaw was passed and who continues to reside there. The Province gives a 36-year-old resident, and two sisters aged 22 and 25, as examples of residents who may continue living in the unit despite a new 55+ bylaw. Legacy residents are also entitled to the caregiver and family exemptions in the future. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q:** Can my adult child move back in with me? **A:** Sometimes, and the test is caregiving before age 19, as the Province states it. An exemption covers a person aged 19 or older, such as an adult child, if the specified resident was one of that person's caregivers before the person reached 19. A separate exemption covers a child under 19 if the specified resident is a caregiver to that child. Both sit inside the May 1, 2023 family exemptions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q:** Can I buy at 50 and rent it out until I turn 55? **A:** Ownership is allowed under 55, and a rental-restriction bylaw cannot stop a long-term rental, but the resident still must meet the age rule or an exemption. The Province states under-55 people can own in a 55+ corporation but may not be able to live there, and separately states rental-restriction bylaws are invalid while short-term rental bylaws remain allowed. A tenant who is under 55 and not exempt does not become lawful merely because renting is allowed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- A strata corporation's own bylaws can specify further exemptions beyond the provincially required ones, so two 55+ buildings can lawfully differ. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- Strata councils must comply with the BC Human Rights Code when administering the corporation and applying bylaws, and the Province advises councils to seek a knowledgeable strata lawyer for drafting, interpreting and enforcing an age bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- The Land Title Office does not review filed bylaw documents, so a filed bylaw is not automatically an enforceable bylaw. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/amending-bylaws-and-rules
- Before the second annual general meeting, a unanimous vote is required to change bylaws for wholly residential strata corporations, while at the second annual general meeting or later the threshold is a 3/4 vote. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/amending-bylaws-and-rules
- A rule is not a bylaw: rules govern use, safety and condition of common property and cannot govern the use of a strata lot, which only a bylaw can do. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/amending-bylaws-and-rules
- The maximum fine a strata may set is $200 for each contravention of a bylaw other than a short-term rental bylaw, and $50 for each contravention of a rule, as the Province states. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/amending-bylaws-and-rules

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- No municipal tax rate exists for this province-wide rules page, and none is stated here.
- The provincial item the Province lists is the Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT), which applies only in designated taxable areas the Province lists by municipality and regional district, including the City of Surrey, the City of Abbotsford and the District of Mission. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- Reserve lands, treaty lands and lands of self-governing Indigenous Nations are not part of the SVT taxable areas, as the Province states. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- No SVT rate figure is stated in this note, because the taxable-areas page read in this pass lists areas and exclusions and does not state a rate.

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Read the filed bylaws, not the listing, because a bylaw change takes effect only when filed at the Land Title Office and the office does not review what is filed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/amending-bylaws-and-rules
- Check for a mistaken repeal, because the Province warns strata corporations reviewing bylaws to take care not to mistakenly repeal an age-restriction bylaw when filing a consolidated set. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/amending-bylaws-and-rules
- The Province's own disclaimer states its strata housing information is a basic starting point and is not a substitute for legal advice, which is the correct posture for any exemption that turns on a family's facts. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
