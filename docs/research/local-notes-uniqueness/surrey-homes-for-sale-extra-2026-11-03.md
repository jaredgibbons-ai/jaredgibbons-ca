# Surrey Homes for Sale — Extra Local Notes
Page: surrey-homes-for-sale (City of Surrey; Homes for Sale).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Surrey is structured as six City communities — Cloverdale, Fleetwood, Guildford, Newton, South Surrey and Whalley — and "City Centre" is a downtown planning core inside Whalley, not a seventh community or a board sub-area. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities
- The city's north edge is the Fraser River and Highway 1 and its south edge is the United States border at 0 Avenue, with Delta to the west and Langley to the east, so a "Surrey" search spans riverfront, farmland and downtown tower ground in one municipality. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities
- Guildford, in the northeast corner, shows the pattern: the Fraser River borders its northern edge, Whalley is west, Langley is east, and its southern border runs from 96 Avenue to 84 Avenue. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- The Fraser Valley board reports Surrey as one community split into 15 sub-areas (Whalley, Guildford, Queen Mary Park, Bear Creek Green Timbers and others), so board sub-area names and City community names do not match one-to-one. (as of September 2026, Source: SnapStats FVR September 2026 report)

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The housing stock follows the six-community structure: high-density towers only in the City Centre core, and detached, townhouse and low-rise stock across the other five communities. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- Guildford's stock is mature neighbourhoods that grew up around the Guildford Town Centre shopping mall, plus the newer Fraser Heights subdivision, which slopes down toward the Fraser River. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- City Centre's post-2017 growth has come as high-density residential, commercial, mixed-use and institutional development under the City Centre Plan, which is why the downtown is the one part of Surrey where detached houses are the exception. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- Zoning, small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH) allowances and lot rules for Surrey houses are covered in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Every Surrey property carries a zone under Surrey Zoning By-law No. 12000, shown parcel by parcel in the City's COSMOS map; the city-wide zoning, SSMUH and suite rules are written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- About one-third of Surrey is designated Agriculture in the Official Community Plan (OCP), and most of it sits in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), so a rural-looking Surrey address can carry farm rules rather than residential ones — confirm the OCP designation and ALR status for the parcel in COSMOS. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0236-00.pdf
- Inside City Centre, the plan layer that governs what can be built next to a home is the City Centre Plan (approved 2017, update underway), not the base zone alone. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is Surrey one housing market or several? **A:** Several markets share one city hall. The City plans Surrey as six communities — Cloverdale, Fleetwood, Guildford, Newton, South Surrey and Whalley — each with its own town centre, and the board further splits Surrey into 15 sub-areas. A city-wide average mixes downtown towers, Guildford subdivisions and south Surrey farmland-edge lots, so compare a home against its own community and sub-area first. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities
- **Q:** Is "Surrey City Centre" a separate area from Whalley when I search? **A:** No — City Centre is the downtown core inside Whalley. The City lists the community as "City Centre (Whalley)," and board reporting files the downtown under the Whalley sub-area; there is no "City Centre" sub-area in board data. The City Centre Plan (2017) is the planning document for the core. Search and compare under Whalley, then check the plan. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- **Q:** When does SkyTrain reach the rest of Surrey? **A:** The Surrey Langley SkyTrain is a 16-kilometre Expo Line extension along Fraser Highway from King George Station to 203 Street in Langley City, with eight stations, and TransLink states an anticipated in-service date of 2029. Six stations are in Surrey: Green Timbers, 152 Street, Fleetwood, Bakerview-166 Street, Hillcrest-184 Street and Clayton. Until it opens, King George Station is Surrey's only rail terminus outside the Whalley core stations. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/rapid-transit-projects/surrey-langley-skytrain
- **Q:** Does a Surrey home always come with city water and sewer? **A:** Not on the agricultural edge. Surrey's SSMUH exemptions include properties not connected to community water and sewer, and about one-third of the city is OCP-designated Agriculture, mostly in the ALR. For any acreage or lowland address, confirm servicing and ALR status for the exact parcel in the City's COSMOS map before assuming residential servicing. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Simon Fraser University's Surrey campus has operated in City Centre since 2002 and sits inside the Central City complex above the mall at 250 - 13450 102 Avenue, next to Surrey Central Station — a university campus inside a shopping centre. https://www.sfu.ca/surrey/about/our-locations/sfu-at-central-city.html
- SFU's first Surrey building opened in fall 2006 above Central City mall, designed by architect Bing Thom, and the campus has since expanded to a second building for its Sustainable Energy Engineering program. http://www.sfu.ca/surrey/about/our-story.html
- Surrey's organics go to the Surrey Biofuel Facility, which the City describes as the first closed-loop organic waste facility in North America, turning organic waste into renewable natural gas and compost — the facility behind Surrey's curbside organics program. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Green Timbers Urban Forest, at the 14600 block of 100 Avenue, is called British Columbia's "Birthplace of Reforestation": logged completely by 1929, replanted, run as a forestry school and nursery, and made a park in 1974. https://www.surrey.ca/activities-parks-recreation/parks/green-timbers-urban-forest
- Holland Park, 13428 Old Yale Road, is the downtown's festival green space at 10 hectares, walking distance from Surrey City Hall at 13450 104 Avenue. https://www.surrey.ca/activities-parks-recreation/parks/holland-park
- Chuck Bailey Recreation Centre, 13458 107A Avenue, is the City Centre recreation facility and is being expanded, per the City's Whalley community page. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- Surrey Langley SkyTrain (Province of BC / TransLink) — 16-kilometre elevated Expo Line extension along Fraser Highway from King George Station to 203 Street in Langley City, eight stations and three transit exchanges; Surrey stations are Green Timbers (140 Street and Fraser Highway), 152 Street, Fleetwood (160 Street), Bakerview-166 Street, Hillcrest-184 Street and Clayton (190 Street); STATUS: in delivery, anticipated in-service date 2029, read 2026-11-03. https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/stations/
- King George Boulevard Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) — approximately 19 kilometres and 12 stations from Surrey City Centre to Semiahmoo Town Centre in South Surrey along King George Boulevard; STATUS: design stage advanced by Council, read 2026-11-03. https://www.surrey.ca/news-events/news/council-approves-next-stage-of-king-george-boulevard-brt
- City Centre Plan update (City of Surrey) — the update to the 2017 plan establishes a Central Business District, identifies a future Entertainment District (endorsed by Council in October 2025), enables more transit-oriented development around SkyTrain stations and allows growth in Bolivar Heights; STATUS: plan update underway, read 2026-11-03. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- Guildford Plan (City of Surrey) — the current land use plan for Guildford Town Centre and the 104 Avenue corridor; STATUS: approved by Council October 30, 2023, read 2026-11-03 (check current). https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/GuildfordPlanFinal.pdf

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Surrey's 2026 total residential (Class 1) property-tax rate is $3.42731 per $1,000 of assessed value — $1.84251 municipal plus $1.58480 for other authorities — and the full rate schedule, parcel taxes and utility charges are written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note; see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies in Surrey: the Province lists the City of Surrey as a designated taxable area, so owners file the annual declaration; see the surrey-houses-for-sale note for the full treatment. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Surrey's only SkyTrain service today is in Whalley/City Centre: the City's transit page lists four Expo Line stations in Surrey — Scott Road, Gateway, Surrey Central and King George. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey
- The R1 King George Blvd RapidBus runs from Guildford Exchange via Surrey Central Station and King George Station to Newton Exchange, tying the Guildford, City Centre and Newton town centres together on one route. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/214/ttR1.pdf
- The Surrey Langley SkyTrain will add a second rail corridor along Fraser Highway with an anticipated in-service date of 2029; until then the Fraser Highway corridor is bus-served. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/rapid-transit-projects/surrey-langley-skytrain
- Guildford's northern edge gives the city its Highway 1 and Port Mann Bridge access, with views of the bridge and the Fraser River along that edge. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Surrey's flood exposure sits in its lowlands, not its upland town centres: the City's floodplain pages cover the coastal floodplain along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers toward Cloverdale and Newton and the Little Campbell River area in the south — check any low-lying parcel against the City floodplain mapping. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- Riparian rules follow Surrey's creeks city-wide: Queen Mary Park exists largely to protect Healy Creek, a tributary to Bear Creek and the Serpentine River, so lots backing onto creeks can carry setback and tree rules a listing will not show — check the parcel in COSMOS. https://www.surrey.ca/activities-parks-recreation/parks/queen-mary-park
- ALR status is the third city-wide check: most of Surrey's OCP Agriculture designation is in the Agricultural Land Reserve, which limits non-farm use and subdivision regardless of the zone name on a listing. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0236-00.pdf
