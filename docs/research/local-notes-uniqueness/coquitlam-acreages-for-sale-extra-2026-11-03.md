# Coquitlam Acreages — Extra Local Notes
Page: coquitlam-acreages-for-sale (Coquitlam; acreages).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Coquitlam's acreage ground is its northeast: the large rural parcels on upper Burke Mountain and along Coast Meridian Road and Hazel Drive, east of the Coquitlam River and below Pinecone Burke Provincial Park. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/790/Northwest-Burke-Vision-PDF
- The park is the hard northern boundary: the Northwest Burke Vision records the 38,000-hectare (94,000-acre) Pinecone Burke Provincial Park to the northeast, with primary access through the vision area off Harper Road via Coast Meridian Road. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/790/Northwest-Burke-Vision-PDF
- The board sub-area name for most of this ground is Burke Mountain; the planning frame is the Northeast Coquitlam Area Plan and its Partington Creek Neighbourhood Plan, which covers the eastern Burke Mountain lands between Cedar Drive and Victoria Drive. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2009

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Northeast Coquitlam acreage is old large-lot stock, not a subdivision product: individual rural parcels on the Burke Mountain slopes that predate the neighbourhood plans, with no named acreage subdivision and no bare-land strata acreage community verified for this ground. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/790/Northwest-Burke-Vision-PDF
- Servicing is the dividing line between parcels: the Northwest Burke Vision identifies "Water Service Constrained Lands" inside its roughly 400-hectare vision area, so two similar-sized parcels can differ completely on whether City water reaches them. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/790/Northwest-Burke-Vision-PDF
- Where a Partington Creek parcel is designated in the neighbourhood plan, the planned form is ground-oriented housing, not acreage retention: the plan designates land for townhousing and envisions about 10,000 to 16,500 residents across the plan area. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2009
- Coquitlam's general land rules — Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996, the small-scale residential zones, and the Subdivision and Development Servicing Bylaw No. 3558, 2003 — are covered in full in the coquitlam-lots-land note; this note keeps only the acreage angle.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The Partington Creek wildlife corridor is now a real constraint on Burke Mountain land: the City is securing a 50-metre-wide corridor along Partington Creek, expanding to a 100-metre buffer at a wildlife "hot spot," to protect the movement connection between Pinecone Burke Provincial Park, Minnekhada Regional Park and Burke Mountain green spaces. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2009
- The corridor land is being transferred to City ownership and designated as parkland as development proceeds, so a creek-side acreage's developable area can shrink at rezoning, not just at building permit. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2009
- Coquitlam's planning frame changed in 2026: the streamlined Interim OCP (Bylaws 5511/5512, adopted April 27, 2026) supersedes OCP No. 3479, which is not current — see the coquitlam-lots-land note for the shared land rules under the new plan. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/1932
- Small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH = small-scale multi-unit housing) still applies on qualifying low-density lots (up to four units in the City's small-scale residential zones), but it does not override the corridor, creek setbacks or a parcel's neighbourhood-plan designation — see the coquitlam-lots-land note. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/15337/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-Summary-Guide?bidId

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Can I subdivide a northeast Coquitlam acreage now?
**A:** Not on a rural timeline of your own choosing. The Burke Mountain lands are planned through the Northeast Coquitlam Area Plan and neighbourhood plans such as Partington Creek, and the Northwest Burke Vision phases future neighbourhood planning over 30-plus years. A parcel develops when its neighbourhood plan and servicing reach it; until then it is a holding parcel, and the City's current development applications page is where its status shows. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2009

**Q:** Is a Burke Mountain acreage on City water and sewer?
**A:** Check the specific parcel, because the answer splits by street. The Northwest Burke Vision maps "Water Service Constrained Lands" inside the vision area, and servicing extensions are being secured project by project through development approvals in Partington Creek. Assume nothing from a neighbouring parcel's services; confirm water and sewer with the City for the exact lot. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/790/Northwest-Burke-Vision-PDF

**Q:** What does the Partington Creek wildlife corridor do to my land?
**A:** It can take the creek strip out of the developable area permanently. The City is securing a 50-metre-wide corridor along Partington Creek, widening to 100 metres at a wildlife hot spot, with surrounding lands transferred to City ownership as parkland through the development approval process. Price a creek parcel on its area outside the corridor and creek setbacks. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2009

**Q:** How close is this ground to the provincial park, and does that matter?
**A:** It borders it. Pinecone Burke Provincial Park (38,000 hectares) sits to the northeast and its primary access runs through this area off Harper Road via Coast Meridian Road. For a buyer that means trail access and a permanent green boundary — and it is also why the City is protecting the wildlife corridor across the private parcels below the park. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/790/Northwest-Burke-Vision-PDF

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The wildlife corridor is being bought with development, not taxes: the City states the first phase is secured through three related Burke Mountain development projects at no cost to taxpayers, using the development approval process. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2009
- The corridor followed a 15-month City-commissioned wildlife monitoring study, and the first development proposal inside it received first reading on May 25, 2026 and second and third readings after a June 22, 2026 public hearing — about 30% of the identified corridor would be protected if that project proceeds. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2009
- The three corridor projects together include more than 400 townhouse units, transportation improvements and added parkland, and the project also expands Knoll Park. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2009
- Minnekhada Regional Park, in Port Coquitlam, is the second anchor of the corridor system, east of the Burke Mountain lands across the Pitt River lowlands. http://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2009
- School District No. 43 (SD43) catchments are address-based up here too: Smiling Creek Elementary at 3456 Princeton Avenue serves the Burke Mountain side, but run the exact acreage address in the SD43 locator before marketing a school. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/15337/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-Summary-Guide?bidId

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Coquitlam's 2026 Class 1 Residential total municipal rate is $2.2756 per $1,000 of assessed value ($2.1993 municipal services plus $0.0763 drainage), and the grand total with other governments is $3.8520 per $1,000. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17264/2026_Tax-Rates
- 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty after that date and a further 5% after September 15, 2026; utility bills were due March 31, 2026. https://coquitlam.ca/544/Property-Taxes
- Coquitlam is a designated taxable area for the provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax, so a vacant acreage held by a non-exempt owner is inside the declaration system. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- Coquitlam's development cost charge (DCC = development cost charge) figures for a northeast subdivision: see the coquitlam-lots-land note.

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Coast Meridian Road and David Avenue are the through-routes for the Burke Mountain acreage ground, and Harper Road off Coast Meridian Road is the primary access to Pinecone Burke Provincial Park. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/790/Northwest-Burke-Vision-PDF

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Creek and corridor risk is mapped, not guessed: Partington Creek, Hyde Creek and Smiling Creek watercourses cross the vision area, and the City warns its watercourse information may not show every watercourse — a parcel survey and riparian check decide the real setbacks. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/790/Northwest-Burke-Vision-PDF
- Servicing risk is the second map: the "Water Service Constrained Lands" in the Northwest Burke Vision tell you where City water does not yet reach, but the map does not price an extension — that figure comes from the City for the specific parcel. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/790/Northwest-Burke-Vision-PDF
- Whether any northeast Coquitlam parcel is in the Agricultural Land Reserve is a parcel check on the Agricultural Land Commission maps; no ALR parcel for this ground is confirmed in this program. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
