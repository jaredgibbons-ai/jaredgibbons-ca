# Simon Fraser University Area Neighbourhood — Extra Local Notes
Page: simon-fraser-university-area-burnaby (SFU / UniverCity, Burnaby; neighbourhood).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- This area is the top of Burnaby Mountain: the Simon Fraser University (SFU) Burnaby campus and the UniverCity residential community beside it, reached by Burnaby Mountain Parkway and Gaglardi Way. https://univercity.ca/about-us/history/
- UniverCity occupies about 65 hectares south and east of the campus, in neighbourhoods the developer materials name West Highlands, East Highlands, High Street and the Slopes. https://intergulf.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/B1709_1060_Arbour_Feature-Sheet_v4.pdf
- The campus and community are ringed by the Burnaby Mountain Conservation Area, whose trails climb from about 25 metres above sea level to the 366-metre summit. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/walking-hiking-and-biking/burnaby-mountain-conservation-area-trails
- The board sub-area name is printed in full as "Simon Fraser University SFU"; the neighbouring sub-area "Simon Fraser Hills" covers the Forest Grove side of the mountain and is a different area. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in/burnaby

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Almost every home here is a strata condominium or townhome in UniverCity, because the community was planned as a dense, mixed-use development rather than a lot subdivision. https://univercity.ca/about-us/history/
- There are no conventional fee-simple residential lots inside UniverCity itself: buyers own their strata unit on SFU's prepaid ground lease, so comparisons between buildings come down to unit size, floor and outlook rather than land. https://univercity.ca/about-us/history/
- An early anchor project is The Hub at UniverCity, completed in spring 2009 with 146 condominium units and 23,000 square feet of retail space including the grocery store on University High Street. https://www.univercity.ca/upload/LibertyRelease.pdf
- Intergulf Development Group's own materials list its earlier mountain buildings as Novo 1 and Novo 2, The Peak (2019) and Terraces at The Peak (2020), before its current Arbour project in the Slopes. https://intergulf.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/B1709_1060_Arbour_Feature-Sheet_v4.pdf
- Detached houses are rare in this sub-area and sit mainly in pockets at the mountain's edges. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in/burnaby
- For building-by-building strata detail, see the simon-fraser-university-area-burnaby-houses-for-sale note and the simon-fraser-university-area-burnaby-condos-townhomes-for-sale page.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Tenure is the rule that defines buying here: the land remains under SFU ownership, and SFU Community Trust leases fully serviced, subdivided parcels to developers through 99-year lease agreements, so UniverCity homes are leasehold, not freehold. https://univercity.ca/about-us/history/
- The community's density is set by its own Official Community Plan (OCP) framework: the City of Burnaby approved the OCP and zoning amendments in 1996, allowing up to 4,536 residential units housing up to 10,000 residents at build-out. https://univercity.ca/about-us/history/
- As part of that arrangement, SFU transferred more than 320 hectares of university land to the City of Burnaby for the Burnaby Mountain Conservation Area, which is why the built area is small and the protected forest around it is permanent. https://univercity.ca/about-us/history/
- Burnaby-wide zoning, tax and utility rules are treated in full in the vancouver-heights-burnaby-houses-for-sale note — see the vancouver-heights-burnaby-houses-for-sale note; the leasehold structure above is the part that differs here.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Are UniverCity homes freehold or leasehold? **A:** Leasehold. SFU Community Trust's history states the land remains under SFU ownership and parcels are leased to developers through 99-year lease agreements, so a buyer purchases a leasehold strata interest in the home, not the land under it. Read the ground lease for the building — its start date, remaining term and end-of-term provisions — before subject removal, because the lease is what a lender and a lawyer will examine first. https://univercity.ca/about-us/history/
- **Q:** Is the Burnaby Mountain Gondola built, or even approved? **A:** Neither. TransLink states the gondola is not yet approved or funded: it must be included in a future investment plan and approved by the TransLink Board and the Mayors' Council on Regional Transportation. The preferred route is a straight line from Production Way–University Station to a terminal near the SFU bus exchange, with trips of about seven minutes for the roughly 25,000 daily trips the mountain generates. https://www.translink.ca/news/2023/november/translink%20seeking%20feedback%20on%20burnaby%20mountain%20gondola
- **Q:** Which elementary school serves a UniverCity address? **A:** University Highlands Elementary, 9388 Tower Road, is the School District 41 (Burnaby) school inside the community, and SFU's schools page notes most homes were located so families can walk to it in under five minutes. Catchments are still address-based, and the district locator returned Cameron Elementary for the sample address at Burnaby Mountain Secondary, so run the exact building address in the locator before relying on a catchment. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/school/04141069
- **Q:** How do residents get downtown without a car? **A:** By RapidBus and bus-to-SkyTrain connections. TransLink's R5 Hastings Street RapidBus terminates at the SFU Exchange and links the mountain to Burrard Station in downtown Vancouver, running at least every 10 minutes at peak and every 15 minutes off-peak, and the 145 bus connects SFU Exchange to Production Way–University Station on the Millennium Line. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- **Q:** What is the elementary school building itself? **A:** A converted university building. SFU's schools page states University Highlands Elementary is a conversion of an existing office building, leased to the school district by SFU Community Trust, with a new gymnasium and seismic upgrades, Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design (LEED) Gold certification, and capacity for 40 kindergarten and 275 elementary students; it opened in September 2010. https://liveatsimonfraser.ca/simon-fraser-schools.html

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The conservation area trail network is 34 multi-use trails over 28 kilometres across 578 hectares, and closures happen: the City's trails page read on 2026-11-03 lists the Velodrome Trail closed for dirt erosion and aging wood steps, and a November 9–13 closure of Centennial Way and its parking lot for upgrades, weather permitting. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/walking-hiking-and-biking/burnaby-mountain-conservation-area-trails
- Burnaby Mountain Park at the summit holds the Centennial Rose Garden, the Kamui Mintara (Playground of the Gods) sculptures, a playground and picnic areas. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/parks/burnaby-mountain-conservation-area
- Childcare is on the mountain: SFU Community Trust's community contact list confirms the UniverCity Childcare Centre and the Verdant Early Childhood Centre operate in UniverCity alongside SFU Childcare services. https://univercity.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/UniverCity-Contacts-.pdf
- The public secondary school on the mountain is Burnaby Mountain Secondary, 8800 Eastlake Drive, operated by School District 41. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in/burnaby
- City recreation programs run out of a school here: the community association lists City of Burnaby fitness and recreation programs for all ages at University Highlands Elementary, with occasional events at Richard Bolton Park. https://www.univercityca.com/copy-of-connect
- Shopping is centred on University High Street, where The Hub's retail includes the community grocery store, pharmacy and café space beside the residential buildings. https://www.univercity.ca/upload/LibertyRelease.pdf

STATUS: developer's own project materials current as read on 2026-11-03; the developer source read does not state a completion date. https://intergulf.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/B1709_1060_Arbour_Feature-Sheet_v4.pdf

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The R5 Hastings Street RapidBus terminates at SFU Exchange, linking the mountain to Burrard Station downtown at least every 10 minutes at peak and 15 minutes off-peak. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- The 145 bus is the SkyTrain link, running between SFU Exchange and Production Way–University Station on the Millennium Line. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Route_Diagrams/213/rR5.pdf
- The 144 bus connects the mountain to Metrotown Station via Sperling–Burnaby Lake Station, and the 143 connects SFU to Burquitlam Station, per TransLink's Burnaby system map. https://translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/transit-system-maps/regional-maps/2026/september-2026/burnaby-and-new-westminster-map.pdf
- There is no SkyTrain station on the mountain itself; every rail trip starts with a bus down Burnaby Mountain Parkway or Gaglardi Way. https://www.translink.ca/news/2023/november/translink%20seeking%20feedback%20on%20burnaby%20mountain%20gondola

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Read the building's ground lease before anything else: confirm the lease commencement date, the remaining term and any end-of-term provisions in the SFU Community Trust lease structure, because tenure, not the building, is the long-term risk in UniverCity. https://univercity.ca/about-us/history/
- Check current trail and slope conditions on the City's conservation area page: the page read on 2026-11-03 lists an erosion closure on the Velodrome Trail, on a mountain whose grade climbs about 341 metres from base to summit. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/walking-hiking-and-biking/burnaby-mountain-conservation-area-trails
- Do not price a unit as if the gondola exists: TransLink states it is not approved or funded, so current transit value is the R5 and bus network only. https://www.translink.ca/news/2023/november/translink%20seeking%20feedback%20on%20burnaby%20mountain%20gondola
