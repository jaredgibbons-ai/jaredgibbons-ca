# Sumas Prairie Neighbourhood — Extra Local Notes
Page: sumas-prairie (Sumas Prairie; neighbourhood).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Sumas Prairie is a flat, drained lakebed in the City of Abbotsford, running east from the city toward the Vedder Canal and the City of Chilliwack boundary; it is the former Sumas Lake, drained in the 1920s, and Highway 1 (the Trans-Canada Highway) crosses the old lakebed through it. https://hdp-ca-prod-app-abbotsford-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/3617/8514/9747/825d811c3be89a72a69be8d9da9a6980_Storyboards_v7_lower_res.pdf
- The prairie is split by the City of Abbotsford's own land-use policy into the Inner Sumas Prairie, protected by the Sumas Dike, and the Outer Sumas Prairie outside that dike line; the Sumas Dike runs about 16.8 kilometres along the south-eastern portion of the prairie. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coaother/c00410
- The watercourses that bound and drain the area are named in City of Abbotsford Policy C004-10: the Vedder Canal, the Sumas Drainage Channel, the Sumas River, and Marshall, Saar and Arnold Creeks. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coaother/c00410
- The northeasternmost part of the prairie around Yarrow is inside the City of Chilliwack, not Abbotsford, so two municipalities and two tax bills apply across one flat plain — the Abbotsford portion was the former District of Sumas, amalgamated into Abbotsford in 1972. https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026EMCR0002-000081.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The housing stock is farmhouses and rural homes on working farm parcels, not subdivisions: the prairie is designated Agricultural in Abbotsford's Official Community Plan (OCP) and zoned mainly Agricultural One (A1) and Agricultural Two (A2). https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/55263/
- Barrowtown Elementary, at 5137 Tolmie Road, is the prairie's own school — Abbotsford School District (SD34) describes it as a small, rural K-5 school "in the heart of a thriving agricultural community in Eastern Abbotsford," on its Tolmie Road site since 1966. https://barrowtown.abbyschools.ca/school-name-history-4
- Homes here sit on the lakebed behind the dike system, so the Barrowtown Pump Station — not a municipal storm sewer alone — is what keeps the prairie dry day to day. https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026EMCR0002-000081.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Most of the prairie is in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), so farm use governs and subdivision or non-farm use needs Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) approval on top of City zoning. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/55263/
- Abbotsford's A1 zone sets a minimum new lot size of 8.0 hectares (about 19.8 acres), and the A2 zone sets 16.0 hectares; the City's AgRefresh work retained those 8 ha and 16 ha minimums rather than lowering them. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-10/AgRefresh%20St3%20-%20Parcel%20Size.pdf
- Under the A1 zone a lot can carry one secondary suite of up to 100 square metres, plus one accessory farm dwelling unit on lots of at least 0.4 hectares where ALR rules allow it — a second full house for non-farm family is not a right. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/55263/
- City Policy C004-10 restricts what can be built on City-owned land along the Vedder Canal, Sumas Drainage Channel and Sumas River: no permanent buildings are permitted on that City land unless Council approves them. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coaother/c00410

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is Sumas Prairie protected from flooding now? **A:** It is protected by dikes and the Barrowtown Pump Station, not by high ground. The Province states the prairie flooded in November 2021 when Nooksack River water crossed from Washington State, and again saw flooding in December 2025; the dike and pump system is the protection, and the Province is funding design work to strengthen it. https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026EMCR0002-000081.pdf
- **Q:** Can I subdivide a Sumas Prairie farm into smaller lots? **A:** Generally no. Abbotsford's zoning sets minimum new lots of 8.0 hectares in the A1 zone and 16.0 hectares in the A2 zone, and because the land is in the Agricultural Land Reserve, a subdivision also needs Agricultural Land Commission approval. Existing smaller lots are historic parcels, not a pattern a buyer can repeat. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-10/AgRefresh%20St3%20-%20Parcel%20Size.pdf
- **Q:** Do I pay extra tax for the dike system? **A:** Yes. Abbotsford's Tax Rates Bylaw 3643-2025 levies a separate Sumas Prairie Dyking and Drainage Area charge on parcels inside that area: a residential rate of 0.54199 per $1,000 plus a parcel tax of $31.95 per acre in Zone A or $20.10 per acre in Zone B. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2025-05/Bylaw%203643-2025%20Tax%20Rates%20Bylaw%202025.pdf
- **Q:** Which school do prairie children attend? **A:** Barrowtown Elementary on Tolmie Road is the local catchment school for the eastern prairie, and it is a small rural K-5 school; Abbotsford School District (SD34) tells families to confirm the catchment for their exact address in the district's School Locator before relying on it. https://barrowtown.abbyschools.ca/ourschool/registration/barrowtown
- **Q:** Is there bus service on the prairie? **A:** No fixed-route bus runs through the farm roads of Sumas Prairie. BC Transit's Route 66 Fraser Valley Express runs on Highway 1 between Chilliwack, Abbotsford, Langley and Lougheed Station, and its Abbotsford stops are at Highstreet and McCallum Road, west of the prairie — a drive from any prairie address. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/chw_map_66.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The prairie only stays dry because the Barrowtown Pump Station runs: without pumping, the old lakebed would refill, which is why the Province put $76.6 million into upgrades to protect the station itself from Sumas and Nooksack River floodwater. https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026EMCR0002-000081.pdf
- Floodwater here can come from the United States: the Province states that during heavy rain the Nooksack River can overtop its banks at Everson, Washington, and flow north across the border into Sumas Prairie — a source no Abbotsford bylaw controls. https://hdp-ca-prod-app-abbotsford-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/3617/8514/9747/825d811c3be89a72a69be8d9da9a6980_Storyboards_v7_lower_res.pdf
- Barrowtown Elementary is named for Edward Barrow, the provincial agriculture minister who directed the draining of Sumas Lake; the first school opened in 1952 at Interprovincial Highway and Number 2 Road. https://barrowtown.abbyschools.ca/school-name-history-4
- Powerboats over 10 horsepower are discouraged on the Sumas River upstream of the Barrowtown Floodbox under City Policy C004-10, and the river speed limit there is 10 km/h — the river beside the dike is a working drainage channel, not a recreational lake. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coaother/c00410
- The flood plan for the watershed is being written jointly by the Province, the City of Abbotsford, the City of Chilliwack and the Semá:th, Máthxwi and Leq'á:mel First Nations under the Sumas River Watershed Flood Mitigation Planning Initiative, so prairie land rules may change with that plan. https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026EMCR0002-000081.pdf

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- Sumas River Watershed Flood Mitigation Planning Initiative — watershed-wide, Sumas Prairie between Abbotsford and Chilliwack; partners are the Province of B.C., the City of Abbotsford, the City of Chilliwack and the Semá:th, Máthxwi and Leq'á:mel First Nations; not a housing project. STATUS: planning and design studies under way, with $3.3 million in provincial funding conveyed December 3, 2025, read 2026-11-03. https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026EMCR0002-000081.pdf
- Barrowtown Pump Station upgrades — Barrowtown, Sumas Prairie; City of Abbotsford project with provincial funding of $76.6 million to better withstand floodwaters from the Sumas and Nooksack rivers. STATUS: funded and in progress as the Province reported on January 28, 2026, read 2026-11-03. https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026EMCR0002-000081.pdf
- No residential subdivision applications on the prairie itself were found in the Abbotsford material read on 2026-11-03; the land is Agricultural Land Reserve farm land, and the active files are flood works, not housing. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/55263/

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Abbotsford's total combined residential tax rate for 2025 was 4.12415 per $1,000 of assessed value, in the Province of B.C.'s municipal tax-rate statistics (Schedule 702 series). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/local-governments/facts-framework/statistics/tax-rates-tax-burden
- On top of the city-wide rate, parcels in the Sumas Prairie Dyking and Drainage Area pay the dyking levy in Tax Rates Bylaw 3643-2025: residential 0.54199 per $1,000, plus $31.95 per acre (Zone A) or $20.10 per acre (Zone B) parcel tax. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2025-05/Bylaw%203643-2025%20Tax%20Rates%20Bylaw%202025.pdf
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) does apply here: the Province's taxable-areas list names the City of Abbotsford, so a residential property on the prairie is in a designated taxable area and the owner files the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Driving is the only practical way in or out: Highway 1 crosses the prairie on the old lakebed, and farm roads such as Tolmie Road, Sumas Way and No. 3 Road connect to Abbotsford's urban area to the west and to Chilliwack via the Vedder Canal bridges to the east. https://hdp-ca-prod-app-abbotsford-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/3617/8514/9747/825d811c3be89a72a69be8d9da9a6980_Storyboards_v7_lower_res.pdf
- The nearest transit is BC Transit Route 66 Fraser Valley Express on Highway 1, stopping in Abbotsford at Highstreet and McCallum Road; the Fraser Valley Regional District states the route connects Chilliwack and Abbotsford with Langley and Burnaby (Lougheed Station). https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/transit.html
- Drive times from the prairie to Abbotsford's urban core or to Chilliwack are UNCONFIRMED — no operator or municipal page read states a measured time.

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood is the defining risk: the Province records major Nooksack-overflow flooding of the prairie in 1990, 2021 and again in 2025, and the watershed plan is being designed to a minimum provincial standard of a 1-in-200-year event. https://hdp-ca-prod-app-abbotsford-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/3617/8514/9747/825d811c3be89a72a69be8d9da9a6980_Storyboards_v7_lower_res.pdf
- A buyer should ask which side of the Sumas Dike the parcel sits on — Inner Prairie (behind the dike) or Outer Prairie — because City Policy C004-10 and the court record of the 2021 flood treat the two sides differently; the policy and dike maps are the lookup, not the listing. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coaother/c00410
- Check the parcel in the Agricultural Land Reserve on the Agricultural Land Commission's mapping before assuming any non-farm use, home business or subdivision: ALR status, not the asking use, governs what the land can do. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/55263/
- What the City policy page does not tell a buyer: whether a specific house flooded in 2021 or December 2025, or its individual flood depth — that is a lot-by-lot question for the City's flood records and a property disclosure statement, and it is UNCONFIRMED for any single parcel from the sources read.
