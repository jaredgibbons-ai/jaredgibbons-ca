# Chilliwack River Valley Neighbourhood — Extra Local Notes
Page: chilliwack-river-valley (Chilliwack River Valley; neighbourhood).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- The Chilliwack River Valley is unincorporated Fraser Valley Regional District (FVRD) Electoral Area E: the FVRD describes this North Cascade area as "known as the Chilliwack River Valley," at 652 km² with a 2021 Census population of 1,568. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
- Settlement runs along Chilliwack Lake Road on both sides of the Chilliwack River, from Vedder Crossing at the west end to Chilliwack Lake at the east end. http://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/parks-recreation/parks-trails/thompson.html
- The FVRD's slope assessment names the valley's communities: Baker Trails, Bell Acres, Slesse Park and Post Creek, plus the Ranger Run area. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/crvssra
- Baker Trails, the first named community up the valley, is a manufactured home subdivision 2 km east of the Vedder Crossing Bridge on Chilliwack Lake Road. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/flood-protection.html

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The FVRD Housing Needs Report for Electoral Areas E and H records the housing as single detached homes and movable dwellings, and records single-family residential as the only permitted residential use under the Official Community Plan designations. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Document~Library/EA Housing Needs Report.pdf
- Baker Trails Village is a manufactured home subdivision protected by FVRD debris control works — three training berms, two debris basins and a stabilization slope — and the Williamsburg development on Hirschman Road is approximately 30 residential lots, 4.5 km east of the Vedder Crossing Bridge, protected by the Rexford Creek debris basin. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/flood-protection.html
- No strata condominium building or townhome complex in the valley was named on an official or developer source in this pass.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Land use is under the FVRD Official Community Plan for Electoral Areas E and H, Bylaw No. 1115 — the FVRD titles it for "Columbia Valley, Lindell Beach and Chilliwack River Valley" — as amended by Bylaw No. 1533, 2019; City of Chilliwack zoning does not apply. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/government/bylaws/planning-land-use.html
- Zoning is the FVRD Consolidated Zoning Bylaw No. 1638, 2021; the zone and minimum lot area for a specific valley parcel were not confirmed and are per-parcel FVRD checks. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/zoning
- An Official Community Plan amendment implemented an Erosion Setback Line for the Wilson Road to Baker Trails area and the Slesse Park area, which constrains building near the river in those stretches. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Home/First Quarter Update 2021.pdf
- The FVRD Floodplain Management Bylaw No. 1669, 2022 applies in the electoral areas, with alluvial-fan and hazard-area development set by a site-specific geohazard assessment. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Bylaws/Planning~and~Land~Use/Floodplain%20Management%20Bylaw%20No.%201669.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is there any bus service in the valley? **A:** No fixed-route bus enters the valley. BC Transit's Chilliwack and FVRD Transit Future Action Plan places fixed-route service inside the City of Chilliwack, on routes 51 to 59, none of which runs up Chilliwack Lake Road. BC Transit's Chilliwack Rider's Guide states residents of the Chilliwack River Valley may be eligible for handyDART, the door-to-door service for people who cannot use fixed-route transit without assistance. Daily life here is by car. https://www.bctransit.com/chilliwack/schedules-and-maps/
- **Q:** Are valley homes on water and sewer, or well and septic? **A:** It depends on the service area, and most of the valley is outside one. The FVRD's 2026 Area E rates sheet lists exactly two residential utility services: Water – Bell Acres, metered and billed quarterly, and Baker Trails Sewer, a flat annual charge. A parcel not in the Bell Acres water area or the Baker Trails sewer area is on private well and septic arrangements, so confirm the specific parcel's services with the FVRD before writing. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Services/Documents/Utility~Billing/Electoral Area E - Residential Rates 2026.pdf
- **Q:** Where do valley children go to school? **A:** There is no School District 33 school located in the valley. Catchments are address-based, and the one locator check on record for a valley address — 48600 Chilliwack Lake Road, run in the earlier program research — returned no result, because no Chilliwack Lake Road range in the locator contains that number. Run the exact address of the property in the SD33 School Locator and treat any school named without that check as unconfirmed. https://sd33.bc.ca/index.php/find-your-school
- **Q:** How far up the valley does cell service reach? **A:** Not far. BC Parks states there is no cell phone reception past the On The Way store on Chilliwack Lake Road, and advises making park reservations before the trip. For a home buyer that means the upper valley — past the store toward Chilliwack Lake — is off the mobile network, which affects work-from-home plans, emergency calling and listing-day communications alike. https://bcparks.ca/sxotsaqel-chilliwack-lake-park/
- **Q:** What do the dike and debris works in the valley actually protect? **A:** Named properties, not the whole valley. The FVRD operates the 900-metre Wilson Road dike along the Chilliwack River's north bank, about 3.5 km upstream of Vedder Crossing; debris control works at Baker Trails Village; and the Rexford Creek debris basin protecting the Williamsburg lots. The Wilson Road dike was damaged in the 2021 Atmospheric River Event and recovery planning is underway. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/flood-protection.html

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Thompson Regional Park, 48600 Chilliwack Lake Road, is the valley's FVRD community park, with a picnic shelter, playground and loop trail — and its land was donated by the Chilliwack River Valley Ratepayers Association. http://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/parks-recreation/parks-trails/thompson.html https://bcparks.ca/sxotsaqel-chilliwack-lake-park/
- Sx̱ótsaqel/Chilliwack Lake Provincial Park at the head of the valley has 182 frontcountry campsites and 40 km of trails, and its gates are locked between 11 p.m. and 7 a.m. daily — summer park traffic shares Chilliwack Lake Road with residents. https://bcparks.ca/sxotsaqel-chilliwack-lake-park/
- The FVRD asks valley visitors to "pack in, pack out" and runs community cleanups with residents; illegal dumping is reported at 1-877-952-7277. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
- There is no Fraser Valley Regional Library branch in the valley; the nearest named in the earlier program research is Sardis Library, 5819 Tyson Road, Chilliwack. https://www.fvrl.bc.ca/

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- Chilliwack River Valley Slope Stability Risk Assessment: the FVRD, working with BGC Engineering and funded by the provincial Ministry of Emergency Management and Climate Readiness, is assessing slope hazards — landslides, debris flows, rotational slumps, rockfall and rockslides — on both sides of the valley from Baker Trails to the Slesse Park landslide, plus Ranger Run and Post Creek; a community workshop in the valley is planned for this winter. STATUS: assessment in progress, project page last updated December 6, 2025, read 2026-11-03. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/crvssra
- Wilson Road Dike recovery: the FVRD states the 900 m dike was damaged during the 2021 Atmospheric River Event and planning for the recovery work is currently underway. STATUS: recovery planning, read 2026-11-03. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/flood-protection.html
Read: 2026-11-03

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- The FVRD-wide tax and utility structure — provincial rural property tax instead of a municipal tax, payment only for the services an electoral area receives, and utility billing outside taxation — is in full in the bridal-falls note; see that note.
- Area E utilities are billed every three months, in January, April, July and October: Water – Bell Acres (service 480) is metered at an $86.00 quarterly minimum including up to 50 m³, with usage over that at $1.77 per m³, plus property taxes. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Services/Documents/Utility~Billing/Electoral Area E - Residential Rates 2026.pdf
- Baker Trails Sewer (service 446) is a flat annual charge of $1,021.78 with no property tax component, per the FVRD's 2026 Area E residential rates. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Services/Documents/Utility~Billing/Electoral Area E - Residential Rates 2026.pdf

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Chilliwack Lake Road is the only road through the valley, running from Vedder Crossing in Chilliwack to Chilliwack Lake — there is no second exit from the upper valley. http://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/parks-recreation/parks-trails/thompson.html
- No fixed-route BC Transit service runs in the valley; handyDART eligibility is the only transit provision BC Transit names for valley residents. https://www.bctransit.com/chilliwack/schedules-and-maps/
- BC Parks warns of severe afternoon winds on Chilliwack Lake and dangerous currents at the lake outflow, and advises boaters and rafters to avoid that area — relevant to any property marketed on river or lake recreation access. https://bcparks.ca/sxotsaqel-chilliwack-lake-park/
Read: 2026-11-03

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Slope hazards are the valley's defining diligence item: the FVRD's risk assessment covers landslides, debris flows, rotational slumps, rockfall and rockslides on both valley sides, and its mapping and mitigation work is still in progress. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/crvssra
- Check any fan or creek lot against Floodplain Management Bylaw No. 1669, 2022, which requires a site-specific geohazard assessment to set the Flood Construction Level and setback. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Bylaws/Planning~and~Land~Use/Floodplain%20Management%20Bylaw%20No.%201669.pdf
- Dike protection is parcel-specific in effect: the Wilson Road dike protects the land behind it on the river's north bank, and it was damaged in the 2021 Atmospheric River Event, with recovery planning underway — do not assume a valley lot is dike-protected without checking its position against the FVRD's works. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/flood-protection.html
- Wildfire is a named planning layer: the FVRD's Area E page links a Community Wildfire Resiliency Plan and an area Hazard Report. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
