# Cloverdale Farms — Extra Local Notes
Page: cloverdale-farms (Cloverdale, Surrey; farms).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Cloverdale's farm land is the lowland east and south of the town centre, in the City of Surrey, around 176 Street (Highway 15) and Colebrook Road. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/minutes/MIN_AEIAC_2021-02-23.pdf
- The Serpentine River lowland is the named farm landscape here: Surrey's 72 Avenue project crosses the Serpentine River and Bear Creek to reach agricultural land in this belt. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/capital-projects/72-avenue-improvements-extensions?check_logged_in=1
- Cloverdale began at Five Corners, the historic intersection of 60 Avenue and Old McLellan Road, established in 1879 in the Clover Valley agricultural area. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2023-R176_0.pdf
- Surrey-wide farm rules: see the surrey-farms note. This note covers only the Cloverdale angle.

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The farm zone here is General Agriculture Zone (A-1) in Surrey Zoning By-law No. 12000, applied lot by lot: a 3073 – 184 Street file is described by the City as about 1.8 ha, designated Agricultural, zoned A-1 and in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR = Agricultural Land Reserve). https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/minutes/MIN_AEIAC_2021-02-23.pdf
- A second Cloverdale-edge file, 4966 – 176 Street, is about 39 acres, designated Agricultural and zoned A-1, for a farm machinery and equipment storage building. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/minutes/MIN_AEIAC_2021-02-23.pdf
- New lots in A-1 are large: within the ALR the subdivision minimum is 4 ha, and outside the ALR it is 2 ha (Surrey-wide rule, stated in full in the surrey-farms note).

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The house size cap is provincial, not just Surrey's: a May 12, 2026 Surrey Agricultural and Food Policy Committee file states a proposed dwelling under 500 square metres (m²) is compliant with Agricultural Land Commission (ALC = Agricultural Land Commission) regulations. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/council-minutes/agricultural-and-food-policy-committee-minutes-may-12-2026
- An additional dwelling over 90 m² does not fit the ALR allowance: the same Surrey file notes the ALC allows an additional dwelling unit of 90 m² or less, and an existing larger home would have to be reduced to 90 m² to be kept as that unit. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/council-minutes/agricultural-and-food-policy-committee-minutes-may-12-2026
- Sensitive-ecosystem review can apply on a farm lot: the 3073 – 184 Street proposal paired its setback variance with a Development Permit for Sensitive Ecosystems. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/minutes/MIN_AEIAC_2021-02-23.pdf
- Surrey-wide A-1 footprint, soil and subdivision rules: see the surrey-farms note.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Is a Cloverdale farm lot also in the ALR?
**A:** Usually yes in the lowland, but check the lot. City files describe 3073 – 184 Street and 4966 – 176 Street as designated Agricultural, zoned A-1 and located within the ALR, which means both Surrey zoning and ALC rules apply to building, subdivision and non-farm use. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/minutes/MIN_AEIAC_2021-02-23.pdf

**Q:** Can I keep the old farmhouse and build a new one?
**A:** Only if the old house fits the 90 m² additional-dwelling limit. Surrey's May 2026 committee file states the ALC allows an additional unit of 90 m² or less, and that an existing home over that size would need to be reduced to qualify as the additional unit. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/council-minutes/agricultural-and-food-policy-committee-minutes-may-12-2026

**Q:** Will the 72 Avenue extension change farm access here?
**A:** Yes, on the north edge of the farm belt. Surrey states the Fraser Highway to 184 Street extension includes improved agricultural ditches, full-movement access to farm properties and wide shoulders for larger farm vehicles, with a bridge across the Serpentine River. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/capital-projects/72-avenue-improvements-extensions?check_logged_in=1

**Q:** Can I build a large machinery shed set back less than the zone requires?
**A:** Not without a variance. At 4966 – 176 Street the owner needed a Development Variance Permit to reduce the front yard setback from 30 metres to 9 metres for a farm machinery and equipment storage building on A-1 land. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/minutes/MIN_AEIAC_2021-02-23.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Farm ditches are part of the road design here: Surrey's 72 Avenue extension promises improved agricultural ditches and full-movement farm access, not just lanes. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/capital-projects/72-avenue-improvements-extensions?check_logged_in=1
- Barn Owl habitat is a named constraint east of the Serpentine River: Surrey's environmental assessment for 72 Avenue identifies Barn Owl, a species at risk, with critical habitat near the corridor where barns provide suitable habitat. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/capital-projects/72-avenue-improvements-extensions?check_logged_in=1
- Salmon-bearing water runs through the farm belt: Bear Creek, Fleetwood Creek and the Serpentine River are all identified as salmon-bearing in the same City assessment. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/capital-projects/72-avenue-improvements-extensions?check_logged_in=1
- Normal farm practice protection applies to the working farms around the town centre, through the BC Farm Industry Review Board (BCFIRB) complaint process. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/agriculture-seafood/agricultural-land-and-environment/farm-practices-protection/resolving-farm-concerns-and-complaints

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Surrey's 2026 Municipal Total tax rate is 1.84251 per $1,000 for Class 1 Residential and 4.48750 per $1,000 for Class 9 Farm, per the City's 2026 property tax rate schedule. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Class 9 is earned, not automatic: BC Assessment requires land to produce a prescribed amount of qualifying primary agricultural products for sale to be Class 9, Farm. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/understanding-property-classes-and-exemptions?TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=582130f6-038f-4842-ada7-0166ce03a246
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) taxable areas list includes the City of Surrey. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- Surrey also charges a Capital Parcel Tax of $300.00 for Class 1, 8 and 9 properties, collected with property taxes, per the 2026 schedule. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the ditch and creek lines on the lot: the City's own assessment names the Serpentine River, Bear Creek and Fleetwood Creek as salmon-bearing, with bridges and large culverts planned where 72 Avenue crosses them. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/capital-projects/72-avenue-improvements-extensions?check_logged_in=1
- Check whether a Sensitive Ecosystems Development Permit Area touches the lot: the 3073 – 184 Street A-1 file required one alongside a setback variance. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/minutes/MIN_AEIAC_2021-02-23.pdf
- The ALR check does not settle tax class: BC Assessment classifies on production for sale, not on ALR status alone. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/understanding-property-classes-and-exemptions?TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=582130f6-038f-4842-ada7-0166ce03a246
