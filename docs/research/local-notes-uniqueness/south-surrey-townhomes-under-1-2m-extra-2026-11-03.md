## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- South Surrey townhouses cluster in Grandview Heights, between 16 Avenue and 28 Avenue, with the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) to the east, under the Grandview Heights General Land Use Plan. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/north-grandview-heights
- North Grandview Heights alone covers about 339 hectares south of 32 Avenue, west of Highway 15, east of 156 Street and north of 28 Avenue, bordered by Rosemary Heights to the north. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/north-grandview-heights

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The townhouse form here is set by plan, not by chance: the Orchard Grove plan puts townhouses and rowhouses at 15 to 30 units per acre in the plan interior, as a transition between multi-family housing toward 24 Avenue and single-family houses toward 26 Avenue. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/OrchardGroveNCPStage2Report2012.pdf
- Newer Grandview townhouse sites are zoned Multiple Residential 30 Zone (RM-30) after rezoning from One-Acre Residential Zone (RA), the pattern in the City report for 16468 and 16488 20 Avenue, 1909 165A Street and 16469 19 Avenue. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7922-0363-00-and-7922-0363-01
- A second current site, 1935 165A Street, sits across from Ta'talu Elementary School in the Sunnyside Heights plan, with townhouses zoned RM-30 to its north. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7924-0086-00-and-7924-0086-01

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- RM-30 setbacks are the rule a buyer sees on foot: the 20 Avenue application varied the minimum side yard setback in Part 22 RM-30 from 6.0 metres to 3.0 metres for four buildings, by Development Variance Permit. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0153-00.pdf
- The Sunnyside Heights Neighbourhood Concept Plan (NCP) designation for the 20 Avenue site is Multiple Residential 30 to 45 units per acre, plus the Grandview Ridge Trail corridor. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7922-0363-00-and-7922-0363-01
- North Grandview's 2005 plan amendments were made expressly to allow smaller single-family lots and townhouse development, so townhouse zoning there traces to that amendment, not the 1999 plan. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/north-grandview-heights

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Are South Surrey townhouses strata or freehold?
**A:** The newer Grandview complexes are strata developments approved as a single townhouse project under RM-30 zoning, not fee-simple lots. The City reports describe each site as one townhouse development with shared setbacks and a development permit, for example 56 units at 16468 20 Avenue. A buyer therefore reviews strata documents, not just title, before removing subjects. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7922-0363-00-and-7922-0363-01

**Q:** What is the Grandview Ridge Trail and does it affect a townhouse lot?
**A:** It is a City trail corridor running through Sunnyside Heights, and townhouse applications beside it convey trail land to the City. The 7918-0153-00 report states the trail will now be acquired at fair market value because no bonus density is proposed. Units fronting the trail face a public corridor, which affects privacy and landscaping at purchase. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0153-00.pdf

**Q:** Which elementary school do new Grandview townhouses feed into?
**A:** Ta'talu Elementary is the school the City's own impact analysis uses for these sites, and it was well under operating capacity in September 2025 because it was built for the growing Grandview area. The 54-townhouse analysis projects 48 school-aged children. Catchments are address-based, so confirm the unit address with School District No. 36 (Surrey) before relying on one school. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0153-00.pdf

**Q:** Why do some Grandview townhouses sit closer to the lane than others?
**A:** Because Council supported setback relaxations by Development Variance Permit on several sites. The 1935 165A Street project received conditional approval on February 24, 2025 with a variance permit for setback relaxations. Two complexes built a few years apart can therefore have different yard depths under the same RM-30 zone, which is visible in garden and parking space. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7924-0086-00-and-7924-0086-01

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Surrey's 2026 municipal total for Class 1 residential property is 1.84251 per $1,000 of assessed value, and the all-in total shown on the City rate schedule is 3.42731 per $1,000. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Surrey is a designated taxable area for the provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax, so an owner files the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the NCP designation before assuming a neighbouring acreage lot stays as-is: the 20 Avenue site's neighbours include acreage single-family lots designated Multiple Residential 10 to 15 units per acre with a townhouse application already at Third Reading. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7924-0086-00-and-7924-0086-01
- Check grading on sloped Grandview lots in the strata documents: staff required the applicant to refine grading so units step with natural grade. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0153-00.pdf
