# Mountain Meadows — Extra Local Notes
Page: mountain-meadows-port-moody (Mountain Meadows; neighbourhood page).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Mountain Meadows is a north-slope Port Moody neighbourhood built around one street, Noons Creek Drive: TransLink's route 184 timetable lists its stops along Noons Creek Drive at Falcon Drive, Thurston Terrace, Maude Court, Barberry Place, David Avenue, Shale Court and Sandstone Crescent. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt184.pdf
- The City plans it as part of a five-neighbourhood group, not on its own: Chapter 14 of Port Moody 2050, the Official Community Plan (OCP) Bylaw, 2026, No. 3522, groups Heritage Mountain, Twin Creeks, Noons Creek, Mountain Meadows and Heritage Woods in one section. https://www.portmoody.ca/media/ctlfdx01/chapter-14-port-moody-2050-official-community-plan.pdf
- David Avenue is the top-edge street: the 184 turns from Noons Creek Drive onto Panorama Drive at the David Avenue end, tying the pocket to the Coquitlam side of the ridge. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt184.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The stock is a planned mix, and the City says so in the plan: the five north-slope neighbourhoods, Mountain Meadows included, currently contain a mix of single detached homes and townhouses, plus a small number of apartment units. https://www.portmoody.ca/media/ctlfdx01/chapter-14-port-moody-2050-official-community-plan.pdf
- The plan's stated direction is gentle change on the same lots: building on the present character, the future vision includes Small-Scale, Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) developing over time, leading to gentle densification along the existing lot patterns. https://www.portmoody.ca/media/ctlfdx01/chapter-14-port-moody-2050-official-community-plan.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Creek-side lots carry an environmental permit rule: the City's environmental page states the Environmentally Sensitive Areas Development Permit Area (DPA4) guides development in and near environmentally sensitive areas, restricts tree removal, vegetation removal and site grading, and can be checked on Official Community Plan Map 13 or the ViewPort map's "ESA & Watercourses" layers. http://www.portmoody.ca/business-development-and-planning/development/environmental-protection-and-requirements/
- Watercourse setbacks apply on top of zoning: the City states its Zoning Bylaw identifies riparian management zones intended to keep development activities away from sensitive environments around streams and watercourses, including Noons Creek. http://www.portmoody.ca/business-development-and-planning/development/environmental-protection-and-requirements/
- Any ground disturbance needs sediment control first: under the City's Stream and Drainage Bylaw, an Erosion and Sediment Control (ESC) permit must be issued before clearing, excavation or grading. http://www.portmoody.ca/business-development-and-planning/development/environmental-protection-and-requirements/
- Port Moody-wide tax, zoning and Small-Scale, Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) facts — see the heritage-mountain-port-moody-houses-for-sale note. https://www.portmoody.ca/media/ctlfdx01/chapter-14-port-moody-2050-official-community-plan.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Which elementary school is in the neighbourhood? **A:** Mountain Meadows Elementary is in the pocket itself, at 999 Noons Creek Drive, kindergarten to Grade 5, in School District No. 43 (Coquitlam). It is the only Port Moody elementary school on Noons Creek Drive. A Noons Creek Drive address does not by itself prove assignment, because the district assigns by home address, so run the specific address through the district's school locator before relying on this school. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in/port-moody
- **Q:** Where is the salmon connection buyers hear about? **A:** Noons Creek is a working salmon creek, and its hatchery sits downstream at 300 Ioco Road. The Port Moody Ecological Society, a volunteer organization operating since 1991, runs the Noons Creek Hatchery and a water quality laboratory, works with Fisheries and Oceans Canada (DFO) to enhance coho and chum salmon stocks, and tests Noons Creek and its tributaries twice a week. Creek health is monitored, not assumed. https://www.noonscreek.org/about
- **Q:** Can I clear trees or grade a sloped, creek-side lot freely? **A:** No. The City states the Environmentally Sensitive Areas Development Permit Area, DPA4, restricts tree removal, vegetation removal and site grading in and near environmentally sensitive areas, and an Erosion and Sediment Control permit under the Stream and Drainage Bylaw must be issued before any planned ground disturbance such as clearing, excavation or grading. Check DPA4 on the ViewPort map before pricing any lot work. http://www.portmoody.ca/business-development-and-planning/development/environmental-protection-and-requirements/
- **Q:** How do residents get to SkyTrain without a car? **A:** By the 184 bus, which is the neighbourhood's one-seat link to two stations. TransLink's timetable runs the 184 from Moody Centre Station via St. Johns Street and the Barnet Highway frontage road at Inlet Centre Station, then Falcon Drive and Noons Creek Drive to Panorama Drive, where it continues as the 185 toward Coquitlam Central Station. There is no SkyTrain station on the north slope itself. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt184.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The City treats Noons Creek as a light-sensitive area: its streetlight upgrade page lists Noons Creek as an example location adjacent to an environmentally sensitive area, where it will consider lower colour temperature 3000 Kelvin (K) warm-white lighting to reduce light pollution. http://www.portmoody.ca/city-government/city-projects/led-streetlight-upgrades/
- The hatchery's year has a fixed public moment: the Port Moody Ecological Society releases coho and chum fingerlings each May at its Fingerling Festival at the Noons Creek Hatchery and the adjacent Port Moody Recreation Complex at 300 Ioco Road, after a four-year ocean journey for returning fish. https://www.noonscreek.org/about
- City crews work on this street by name: the City's roadwork page lists sidewalk repairs at 1309 Noons Creek Drive and 1005 Noons Creek Drive, with parking-lane and sidewalk closures and pedestrian detours while the work runs. http://www.portmoody.ca/resident-services/roads-traffic-and-construction/roadwork-and-construction/
- The neighbourhood has its own section in the City's Parkland Strategy: the June 2025 strategy treats "Mountain Meadows-Noons Creek" as one park-planning unit, separate from Heritage Mountain. https://api.ghdcdn.com/portmoody-edocs/v1/eDocs/PortMoody?docnumber=655901

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Port Moody-wide tax rates, utility rates and billing dates apply here as across the city — see the heritage-mountain-port-moody-houses-for-sale note. https://www.portmoody.ca/utilities
- There is no separate Mountain Meadows rate: the City states utility rates are set annually and the utility bill covers water and sewer services plus recycling, green waste, glass and garbage collection, the same bill structure for every Port Moody neighbourhood. https://www.portmoody.ca/utilities
- The one date a new owner should mark is the utility due date: the 2026 utility bill was due Friday, February 27, 2026, with a 5% penalty after that date and a second 5% after March 31, 2026. https://www.portmoody.ca/utilities

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Route 184 is the pocket's bus: Moody Centre Station to Noons Creek and Panorama Drive, via Inlet Centre Station, Falcon Drive and the full length of Noons Creek Drive, continuing as the 185 to Coquitlam Central Station. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt184.pdf
- David Avenue is the ridge road out: it runs east into Coquitlam's Panorama Drive corridor and west toward Heritage Mountain Boulevard, and the 184's David Avenue stops serve the top of the pocket. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt184.pdf
- The creek trail system is a City planning item, not just informal paths: the City's Trail Network Plan 2025 names the Noons Creek Trail and plans proposed trail extensions for the Heritage Mountain, Twin Creeks and Heritage Woods side of the north slope. https://portmoody-edocs.ghdcdn.com/v1/eDocs/PortMoody?docnumber=662006

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check DPA4 before buying any lot near the creek or a ravine: the City directs owners to Official Community Plan Map 13 or the ViewPort "ESA & Watercourses" layers, and a DPA4 application can require an environmental report, a construction environmental management plan and watercourse setback confirmation — the map shows the area, not a lot-level clearance. http://www.portmoody.ca/business-development-and-planning/development/environmental-protection-and-requirements/
- Check steep-slope mapping as well as creek mapping: the Official Community Plan Bylaw, 2026, No. 3522 map set includes Map 14, Development Permit Area 5 Hazardous Lands, and Map 16, Development Permit Area 5 Steep Slopes, both relevant on this hillside. https://media-002-ca.cdn.govstack.com/portmoody-014-ca/media/ic4jfx1b/draft-city-of-port-moody-official-community-plan-bylaw-2026-no-3522.pdf
- Check soil history on any non-residential lot: the City states properties with a history of commercial or industrial use may be deemed contaminated under the Environmental Management Act and Contaminated Sites Regulation, and permit applicants for such land must submit a Site Disclosure Statement. http://www.portmoody.ca/business-development-and-planning/development/environmental-protection-and-requirements/
