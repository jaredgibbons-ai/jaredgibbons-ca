## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Upper Eagle Ridge is the top end of the Eagle Ridge slope: Falcon Drive, Charter Hill Drive and the upper Lansdowne Drive streets, above Eagle Ridge Park and below David Avenue and the Westwood Plateau edge. https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/coquitlamcrunchtrail-27
- Shared neighbourhood facts — schools, Eagle Ridge Park facilities, the slope and Crunch Trail figures — are in full in the upper-eagle-ridge-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note; this page adds the street-level detail below. https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/Eagle-Ridge-Park-36
- The park campus at the foot of the pocket is addressed off the side streets, not Lansdowne itself: Eagle Ridge Park's entrance is 2635 Runnel Drive, and its outdoor pool subfacility is 2689 Guildford Way. https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/Eagle-Ridge-Outdoor-Pool-91
- The west edge is Johnson Street, where Route 183 runs; the east side falls away toward Pipeline Road and the Scott Creek green corridor. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/183/direction/1/schedule

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The stock pattern is set by the plan map: the City Centre Area Plan Schedule A designates the Falcon Drive and upper Lansdowne lands "One Family Residential" and "Compact One Family Residential," with the apartment and townhouse designations held down-slope toward City Centre — full treatment in the upper-eagle-ridge-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/3487/City-Centre-Area-Plan-Servicing-Assessment-PDF
- The street-level fact the plan map does not show is the BC Hydro right-of-way: the Coquitlam Crunch Trail climbs the hydro corridor through the pocket, so the upper streets back onto either the Scott Creek green corridor, the school-and-park campus, or the cleared Crunch corridor — three different rear-lot conditions on adjoining streets. https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/coquitlamcrunchtrail-27

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide zoning, small-scale multi-unit housing and tax facts point to the scott-creek-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note; the slope, creek and wildfire permit rules for Falcon Drive and Charter Hill Drive are in full in the upper-eagle-ridge-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit
- The street-level trigger to add here is the creek system behind the slope: Scott Creek's headwater tributaries drain the Eagle Ridge slope, and the City classifies every watercourse by environmental value and monitors creek flow and level — a rear-lot ditch on an upper street can be a classified watercourse carrying the 30-metre Watercourse Protection Development Permit distance used in the City Centre Area Plan. https://coquitlam.ca/217/Creeks-and-Streams
- The City operates flow, level and water quality monitoring on various creeks and uses it for hydraulic modelling — drainage behaviour on the upper streets is measured by the City, and a buyer can ask Engineering and Public Works what the nearest Scott Creek tributary monitoring shows. https://coquitlam.ca/217/Creeks-and-Streams

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Which bus actually serves the upper streets — is it only the Lansdowne bus? **A:** No, three routes ring the pocket. Route 185 runs Lansdowne Drive itself; Route 183 (Moody Centre Station / Coquitlam Central Station) runs the west side via Johnson Street, Panorama Drive, David Avenue and Heritage Mountain Boulevard; and Route 160 runs Guildford Way at the foot of the slope via Pinetree Way. A Falcon Drive or Charter Hill Drive address is a walk downhill to one of the three, not a single-route neighbourhood. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/183/direction/1/schedule
- **Q:** What is the cleared corridor behind the upper houses? **A:** It is the British Columbia Hydro right-of-way carrying the Coquitlam Crunch Trail. The City lists Crunch access points at Eagle Ridge Park, on Lansdowne Drive, at David Avenue and at Bramble Park on Panorama Drive, and the trail climbs 242 metres from the Lansdowne side to the top of Westwood Plateau — so corridor lots get trail foot traffic and a maintained clearing, not a private forest edge. https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/coquitlamcrunchtrail-27
- **Q:** Where do the creeks behind these streets go? **A:** They feed Scott Creek. The City counts over 300 kilometres of creeks and streams in Coquitlam, each classified for environmental value, and Scott Creek runs through the wider Eagle Ridge area in a green corridor below the upper streets before crossing the City Centre lands. That is why the watercourse permit check in the houses note is measured from the top of bank of even small tributaries, not just the named creek. https://coquitlam.ca/217/Creeks-and-Streams
- **Q:** What sits at the top of the slope — is Westwood Plateau a different world? **A:** It is a different plan area sharing one trail. The Interim Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 5511, 2026 carries the Northwest Coquitlam Area Plan in as a Schedule J schedule for the Westwood Plateau lands immediately above this pocket, while the pocket itself sits in the City Centre Area Plan mapping — the boundary between the two plan regimes runs along the top of this neighbourhood. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1436/Official-Community-Plan-Review

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Route 160, Port Coquitlam Station to Kootenay Loop, runs Guildford Way at the foot of the pocket via Pinetree Way — a second rail connection (Braid Station side, via Port Coquitlam) that the Lansdowne-only description misses. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/210/tt160.pdf
- The Scott Creek Community Garden sits between Scott Creek Middle and Eagle Ridge Elementary inside the park campus — garden plots at the foot of Falcon Drive, administered through the City's park system. https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/Eagle-Ridge-Park-36
- Eagle Ridge Outdoor Pool at 2689 Guildford Way is seasonal: the City lists its 2026 season as May 30 to September 7, so "pool at the end of the street" is a summer-only amenity. https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/Eagle-Ridge-Outdoor-Pool-91
- The City has installed real-time water quality monitoring devices on four named creeks — Hoy, Stoney, Partington and Nelson — and Scott Creek is not among the four, so creek data for this slope is the rotating monitoring cycle, not a live feed. https://coquitlam.ca/217/Creeks-and-Streams
- Shopping gravity is downhill: Coquitlam Centre at 2929 Barnet Highway is the mall at the foot of the Johnson Street side, and the City Centre library branch is at 1169 Pinetree Way — nothing commercial sits on the upper streets themselves. https://www.coqlibrary.ca/

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the City's environmental development permit mapping (Schedule B) for the two layers that touch these streets — unstable slopes and the Interface Wildfire Risk Management boundary for the City Centre and Northeast areas; the map shows boundaries, not a lot-level conclusion. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/1527/Schedule-B---Environmental-Development-Permit-Areas-PDF?bidId=
- Check tributaries, not just the named creek: the City's creeks page confirms watercourses are classified individually and monitored for flow and level, so a walk of the rear lot line in wet weather plus the City map check is the due diligence for any lot draining toward Scott Creek. https://coquitlam.ca/217/Creeks-and-Streams
- Check the hydro corridor on title: lots backing onto the Coquitlam Crunch corridor sit beside a British Columbia Hydro right-of-way with public trail access at Eagle Ridge Park, Lansdowne Drive and David Avenue — the right-of-way, not the trail, is the title item. https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/coquitlamcrunchtrail-27
