# Bolivar Heights Area Guide — Extra Local Notes
Page: bolivar-heights-guide (Bolivar Heights; area guide).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Bolivar Heights has no City-published boundary of its own — it is a district inside Surrey's City Centre Plan area, tagged "Whalley and City Centre" on City park pages, sitting on the bench above the Fraser River between the Scott Road side and Green Timbers. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/bolivar-park
- The ground is defined by its two parks: Bolivar Park at 13591 Crestview Drive in the south and Invergarry Park at 11285 Surrey Road in the north, with the Bon Accord Creek ravine running through Invergarry. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/invergarry-park
- The City Centre Plan update treats Bolivar Heights as one of the plan's growth districts, separate from the downtown core: growth is planned here as a neighbourhood with its own mixed-use centre, not as tower land. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- History in two sentences: Invergarry Park dates to 1913, when the Surrey Municipality began consolidating land to conserve the Bon Accord ravine and creek system. The park now protects over 37.5 hectares (92.5 acres) of natural forest and riparian creek habitat at the area's north end. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/invergarry-park

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The planned stock change is written into the City Centre Plan: the July 11, 2022 land use changes for Bolivar Heights introduce a range from single family homes and multiplexes to townhouses and low rise apartments, plus a new urban, mixed-use village with shops and services. https://engage.surrey.ca/city-centre-plan/news_feed/council-approves-land-use-designation-changes-in-bolivar-heights-and-green-timbers
- Part of the existing detached stock is a protected enclave: the Bolivar Park neighbourhood and Extension Area was downzoned by resident petition (adopted April 18, 2016) to limit house size and height — one of five such downzoned areas in Surrey. Strata and condo stock detail is in the bolivar-heights-surrey-condos-townhomes-for-sale note — see the bolivar-heights-surrey-condos-townhomes-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf
- The recreation stock shapes the lots around it: Bolivar Park holds multiple baseball fields, a field house, playground and a two-acre fenced dog off-leash area east of the main parking lot. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/bolivar-park

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide Surrey zoning and Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) rules are written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. The Bolivar Heights differences follow.
- The governing plan is the City Centre Plan (approved January 2017), and its update is still underway: the update allows growth in Bolivar Heights including a mixed-use neighbourhood centre, establishes a Central Business District, and adds transit-oriented development around SkyTrain stations. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- Inside the Bolivar Park enclave, the 2016 downzoning caps still frame rebuilds: the Comprehensive Development zones created by the downzoning petitions generally limited the maximum size and height of houses, and Bolivar Park's was adopted April 18, 2016. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf
- Slope lots carry a permit layer: Surrey's Hazard Lands (Steep Slopes) Development Permit Area (DPA) applies to parcels containing land over 20 percent gradient, or within 10 metres downslope or 30 metres of the top of such a slope — the test for bench and ravine-edge lots above the Fraser River flats. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/surrey-official-community-plan/development-permit-areas

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is Bolivar Heights becoming high-rise like downtown Surrey? **A:** No — the plan assigns it ground-oriented growth. Council's July 11, 2022 City Centre Plan changes give Bolivar Heights single family homes, multiplexes, townhouses and low rise apartments, a mixed-use village with shops and services, community amenities and neighbourhood parks within a short walk, and a finer-grained, better-connected road network. The tower forms in the same update went to Green Timbers around the future 140 Street Station, not to Bolivar Heights. https://engage.surrey.ca/city-centre-plan/news_feed/council-approves-land-use-designation-changes-in-bolivar-heights-and-green-timbers
- **Q:** Why do some Bolivar Park streets have smaller houses than the lots suggest? **A:** Because those owners petitioned for a cap. The Bolivar Park neighbourhood and Extension Area was downzoned in 2016, one of five Surrey areas where a petition process rezoned lots into Comprehensive Development zones that generally limit maximum house size and height to preserve character. A lot inside that enclave rebuilds under the cap; a lot outside it follows the standard zone — the boundary matters more than the street name. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf
- **Q:** What schools serve the area? **A:** The named in-area elementary schools on the Ministry list are James Ardiel Elementary, Kindergarten to Grade 7, at 13751 112 Avenue, and Surrey Traditional, Kindergarten to Grade 7, at 13875 113 Avenue, both in School District 36 (Surrey). Catchments are set by address in the district's school locator, so a Crestview Drive address and a Surrey Road address should each be confirmed before an offer depends on a school. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/036
- **Q:** How do I reach Surrey Central Station from Bolivar Heights? **A:** Route 373 is the through route. TransLink's route 373 runs from Surrey Central Station to Guildford via Gateway Station, 112 Avenue, 136 Street, 113 Avenue, Surrey Road, Wallace Drive, 116A Avenue, 148 Street and 150 Street — it crosses Bolivar Heights on the 112 Avenue and Surrey Road streets rather than skirting it. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt373.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Bolivar Park's forested south and east areas sit at higher elevation with views of New Westminster and the Fraser River, and parking is from 115 Avenue — the view lots are the streets around Crestview Drive above the park, not a separate subdivision. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/bolivar-park
- Invergarry's eastern half, around Wallace Drive and Surrey Road, holds the Invergarry Mountain Bike Park, with progressive downhill trails up to 25 metres of vertical and expert terrain with drops up to 3 metres — expect riders and a gravel parking lot on Wallace Drive. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/invergarry-park
- Invergarry's walking trails enter at the ends of Gladstone Drive, Currie Drive, 111 Avenue, 114 Avenue and Surrey Road — five dead-end street entries, so trail-adjacent lots have public access at the property line. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/invergarry-park
- There is no library branch in Bolivar Heights: the City Centre Branch at 10350 University Drive lists the routes serving it, and it sits about 150 metres from Surrey Central Station. https://surreylibraries.ca/locations/city-centre-branch

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Route 373 is the spine route, running Surrey Central Station to Guildford through Gateway Station and the area's own 112 Avenue, Surrey Road and Wallace Drive streets. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt373.pdf
- The SkyTrain edge is west: Gateway Station and Surrey Central Station serve the City Centre side, and the City Centre Plan update adds transit-oriented development around the stations rather than inside Bolivar Heights itself. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- Driving, King George Boulevard and 108 Avenue are the west exits to the Fraser Highway and Guildford, and 120 Street (Scott Road) is the east exit — no highway crosses the neighbourhood, which is why the plan's finer-grained road network is a stated 2022 update item. https://engage.surrey.ca/city-centre-plan/news_feed/council-approves-land-use-designation-changes-in-bolivar-heights-and-green-timbers

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Run the slope test first on any bench or ravine-edge lot: the Steep Slopes Hazard Development Permit Area (DPA) captures parcels with land over 20 percent gradient, or within 10 metres downslope or 30 metres of the top of a slope over 20 percent, and the DPA's purpose is protecting people, property and the environment from development on unsafe, unstable slopes. Look the lot up in the City's mapping (COSMOS) against Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 18020, Sub Section VI. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/surrey-official-community-plan/development-permit-areas
- Bon Accord Creek lots add a second layer: Invergarry Park protects the Bon Accord ravine and creek system as riparian habitat, so a lot draining toward that ravine should also be checked against the Sensitive Ecosystems (Streamside) DPA before a building envelope is assumed. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/invergarry-park https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/surrey-official-community-plan/development-permit-areas
- The DPA map does not tell you a lot's stability — the City's Development Permit Areas page summarizes the DPA objectives and where they apply, so a geotechnical assessment of the specific lot is the buyer's own due diligence step, not a City answer. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/surrey-official-community-plan/development-permit-areas
