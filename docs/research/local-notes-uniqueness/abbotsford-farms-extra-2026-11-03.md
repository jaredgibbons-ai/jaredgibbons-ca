# Abbotsford Farms — Extra Local Notes
Page: abbotsford-farms (Abbotsford; farms).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Abbotsford farm ground covers the whole city, not one neighbourhood. The City is 389 square kilometres and about 72% of its land is in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR). https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/agriculture
- Sumas Prairie is the main eastern farm block. The City describes it as a 90 square-kilometre area of low-lying, highly productive agricultural land in East Abbotsford. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- Matsqui Prairie and Bradner-Mt. Lehman are the other named prairie farm areas in City material. AgRefresh background work grouped contraventions by Bradner-Mt. Lehman, Matsqui Prairie, Aberdeen-South Poplar and Sumas Prairie. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/agriculture

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The farm stock here is working farms, not houses on lots. The City calls Abbotsford one of the most intensively and diversely farmed areas in Canada, supporting a wide range of crop and livestock production. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/agriculture
- Farm dwellings sit inside that working fabric. The Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) frame allows generally one principal residence per ALR parcel, plus one additional residence only where the parcel qualifies. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The ALR rules govern most Abbotsford farm purchases. Subdivision in the ALR needs ALC approval, and a principal residence must be 500 square metres or less with no more than 1,000 square metres of fill for its construction. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- A secondary suite is allowed only inside the principal residence. One detached additional residence is allowed up to 90 square metres on parcels of 40 hectares or less and up to 186 square metres on larger parcels. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- City zoning still applies on top of the ALR frame. The City directs buyers to check current zoning, future land use and development permit areas through its property information search or WebMap. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/development-planning
- Outside the ALR, a secondary suite must be inside the principal unit, registered, and less than 50% of the principal unit, and a coach or garden suite may use up to 15% of the lot to a maximum of 100 square metres. https://www.abbotsford.ca/buildingpermits/secondary-suites

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** What did AgRefresh change for farm buyers? **A:** AgRefresh rewrote the farm rules buyers now work under. The City says it completed AgRefresh, a three-stage process that reviewed agricultural policies, bylaws and regulations and set a compliance framework for ALR land, updating Official Community Plan policy and Zoning Bylaw regulation for farming areas, so a buyer should check the current bylaw position, not an older listing description. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/agriculture
- **Q:** Why is Sumas Prairie flood risk different from other Abbotsford farms? **A:** Sumas Prairie floods from across the border, not just local rain. The City states overflow from the Nooksack River at Everson, Washington flows north into Sumas Prairie, with major events in 1990 and 2021, so buyers there need the dike, pump station and flood construction position for the specific parcel, not a city-wide assumption. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- **Q:** Can I add a second home for farm help? **A:** A second home is limited by size and parcel area. The ALC allows one additional detached residence of up to 90 square metres on parcels of 40 hectares or less, or up to 186 square metres above that size, and City zoning must also permit it, so both approvals need checking before an offer relies on that dwelling. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q:** Does buying ALR land give me farm tax class automatically? **A:** No, farm class must be applied for and earned. BC Assessment requires a producing farm to meet minimum gross income at farm gate prices — $2,500 on an operation of 0.8 to 4 hectares, $2,500 plus 5% of actual value above 4 hectares, and $10,000 under 0.8 hectares — with applications due October 31. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
- **Q:** What happened in the November 2021 flood? **A:** The 2021 flood evacuated more than 1,100 properties. The Province states the Nooksack River flooded into the Sumas River system, dikes failed, Highway 1 and the Sumas border crossing closed, and flooding across farmland caused significant loss of crops and livestock, which is why flood history is a core diligence item here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/public-safety-and-emergency-services/emergency-preparedness-response-recovery/embc/reports/abbotsford-community_summary.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Flood recovery is nearly complete but long-term protection is not finished. The City states all major recovery projects are complete or underway, including the Cole Road Dike, Sumas River bank stabilization and North Parallel Road Dike repairs, while phase two design for the Barrowtown Pump Station floodwall is underway. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/flood-recovery-nears-completion-four-years-later
- The Barrowtown Pump Station matters to Sumas Prairie farms. The City states phase one of its new floodwall is complete, and the wall is intended to reduce the risk of the station not operating during a major Nooksack overflow event. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/flood-recovery-nears-completion-four-years-later
- A joint flood plan is being built with neighbours and First Nations. The Province states the Sumas River Watershed Flood Mitigation Planning Initiative brings together Semá:th and Leq'á:mel First Nations, Abbotsford, Chilliwack and British Columbia to reduce flood risk in the watershed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/drought-flooding-dikes-dams/integrated-flood-hazard-management/governance/sumas-river-watershed-flood-mitigation-planning-initiative
- Highway 1 runs through the flood area. The City states flooding in Sumas Prairie threatens Highway 1, the Trans Mountain Pipeline, the Westcoast Energy pipeline, the Sumas-Huntingdon border crossing and Southern Railway service. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- Farm income proof must be kept. BC Assessment recommends keeping receipts, sales logs or other proof of farm sales for at least three years to support farm classification. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- The main agriculture-relevant program is long-term flood mitigation for Sumas Prairie. The City states that after the 2021 flood it commissioned a full post-flooding analysis of Sumas Prairie to protect First Nations communities, businesses, residents and agricultural properties, forming the foundation of its Long-Term Flood Mitigation Plan. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- The City has applied to exclude ALR land for a sports park, with ALR land offered in return. The City states it submitted an exclusion application to the ALC for a recreation and sports park at King and McKenzie Roads, and offered two City-owned parcels totalling about 50 acres on LeFeuvre Road for inclusion in the ALR. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-hall/news-media/council-submits-alc-application-future-recreation-and-sports-park

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- For the headline residential tax rate, see the abbotsford-houses-for-sale note. This note covers only the farm-specific charges below. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice
- Farm land is taxed as BC Assessment Class 9 only when it qualifies. BC Assessment states Class 9 Farm land must produce a prescribed amount of qualifying primary agricultural products for sale, such as crops or livestock, while farm buildings fall in Class 1 Residential. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/understanding-property-classes-and-exemptions?TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=582130f6-038f-4842-ada7-0166ce03a246
- Leased farm land has its own filing rule. BC Assessment states a copy of a current lease must be submitted, a new lease is required on expiry, and resubmission is required every seven years to keep farm classification on leased land. http://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/classifying-land-leased-to-farmers
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) taxable area includes Abbotsford. The Province lists the City of Abbotsford as a designated taxable area, and only owners of residential-class property in a taxable area must file the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
- City property taxes were due July 2, 2026. The City states a first 5% penalty applied July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on September 16, 2026. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/property-taxes/your-current-tax-notice

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Highway 1 is the main farm-to-market road. The Fraser Valley Express, Route 66, connects Chilliwack and Abbotsford with Langley and Burnaby via Highway 1, as the Fraser Valley Regional District (FVRD) describes it. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/transit.html
- Route 66 is a daily interregional service. BC Transit lists Route 66 Fraser Valley Express as serving Chilliwack, Abbotsford, Langley and Burnaby with daily service, operated by Chilliwack Transit. https://www.bctransit.com/plan-a-trip/interregional-routes/
- The Sumas-Huntingdon border crossing is inside the farm area's risk picture. The City names that crossing among the critical infrastructure threatened by Sumas Prairie flooding. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood is the first check on any Sumas Prairie parcel. The City states Sumas Prairie has a long history of flooding from Nooksack River overflow, most notably in 1990 and 2021, so buyers should confirm dike protection and flood construction requirements for the lot. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- Check the parcel's zone and plan designation before assuming a farm use is allowed. The City states current zoning, future land uses and development permit areas can be found through its property information search or WebMap. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/development-planning
- Check ALR status and residence limits on the ALC record. The ALC states local governments may further regulate or restrict residences in the ALR, so City zoning and the ALC frame both need reading for the same parcel. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
