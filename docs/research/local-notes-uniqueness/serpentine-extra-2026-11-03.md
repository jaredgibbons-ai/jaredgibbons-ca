# Serpentine Sub-Area — Extra Local Notes
Page: serpentine (Surrey; sub-area — acreages, farms and homes).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Serpentine is a board name, not a city neighbourhood. "Serpentine" is a Fraser Valley Real Estate Board / Paragon sub-area, one of three sub-areas of the board community "Cloverdale" (Clayton, Cloverdale, Serpentine), and the City of Surrey publishes no boundary or neighbourhood profile for it. https://news.fvreb.bc.ca/member-updates/tech-tip-18-map-of-the-bc-boards-areas-subareas/
- The ground the label covers is Surrey's southern lowland: acreages and farm parcels along 40 Avenue, 176 Street (Highway 15), 168 Street, 184 Street, 188 Street and 196 Street, toward Morgan Creek, Campbell Heights and the South Surrey border country. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- The name matches the land: Surrey's coastal floodplain stretches along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers toward Cloverdale and Newton, and most of it is protected by river dykes along those rivers. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- For 55+ housing specifics under this label, see the serpentine-55-plus-homes-for-sale note; this note covers the general sub-area only. https://news.fvreb.bc.ca/member-updates/tech-tip-18-map-of-the-bc-boards-areas-subareas/

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The stock here is acreage-led, not strata-led: estate houses on multi-acre parcels, working blueberry farms, and vacant land, rather than named condo or townhome complexes. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- At the eastern edge, Campbell Heights adds industrial-side parcels: the South Campbell Heights Plan area alone is about 249 hectares centred on the Little Campbell River around 16 Avenue and 192 Street. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/SouthCampbellHeightsLocalAreaPlan.pdf
- Farm parcels dominate the lowland: most of Surrey's Serpentine and Nicomekl floodplain is designated agricultural and held in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR). https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2007-C013.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- On ALR parcels, the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) housing frame governs, not city zoning: one principal residence capped at 500 square metres, any secondary suite inside that residence, and at most one additional residence of 90 square metres on parcels of 40 hectares or less, or 186 square metres on larger parcels. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- Subdividing ALR land requires Agricultural Land Commission permission outside limited regulation exceptions, so a large parcel is not automatically a multi-lot parcel. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/application-and-notice-process/applications/required-documents/
- Surrey's urban small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH) numbers do not apply to ALR parcels; on urban lots Surrey allows 3 units under 280 square metres, 4 units over 280 square metres, and up to 6 units on eligible lots within 400 metres of a frequent bus stop — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note for the headline city treatment. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations/zoning-small-scale-multi-unit-housing
- Where a Surrey lot does allow a suite, only one secondary suite is permitted per dwelling unit, no suite is permitted inside a coach house or garden suite, and the suite's habitable floor area may not exceed 40 percent of the principal dwelling unit. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/building-or-legalizing-secondary-suite

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is Serpentine a real Surrey neighbourhood? **A:** No, not in city planning terms. Serpentine is a Fraser Valley board sub-area name inside the board's Cloverdale community, alongside Clayton and Cloverdale. The City of Surrey has no Serpentine boundary, community plan or neighbourhood profile, so any street-level boundary a seller describes is a board label, not a municipal line, and should be checked against the actual parcel. https://news.fvreb.bc.ca/member-updates/tech-tip-18-map-of-the-bc-boards-areas-subareas/
- **Q:** Can I build a second house on a Serpentine acreage? **A:** Only within the ALC frame if the parcel is in the Agricultural Land Reserve. That frame allows one principal residence up to 500 square metres with any suite inside it, plus at most one additional residence of 90 square metres, or 186 square metres on parcels over 40 hectares. Anything beyond that needs a non-adhering residential use application to the Commission. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q:** Does this lowland flood? **A:** The lowland is floodplain by design and by history. Surrey states its coastal floodplain covers about 20 percent of the city's land area, follows the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers, and is largely protected by dykes, with sea dams on both tidal rivers first built in 1912 and 1913 to keep salt water out of farm irrigation. Check the individual parcel's flood and dyke status before offering. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- **Q:** Which schools serve this ground? **A:** Schools confirmed for this part of south Surrey include Morgan Elementary at 3366 156A Street and Earl Marriott Secondary at 15751 16 Avenue. School District 36 (Surrey) catchments are address-based, however, and no district locator result for a specific Serpentine parcel is on record here, so run the exact address through the district before relying on either school. ~/workspace/area-research-2026-11/education/work/bc_k12_schools.csv

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The Serpentine and Nicomekl sea dams exist for farming, not just flood control: Surrey states they keep saline water from moving upstream where it could harm agricultural irrigation. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- Servicing varies parcel by parcel on farm ground: city water, wells, and ditch or irrigation water all appear in this area, and no single servicing answer covers the sub-area — verify the specific parcel. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- A registered secondary suite adds its own utility bill in Surrey: the 2026 flat rate for a suite is $454.00 for water plus $821.71 for sewer, on top of the single-family rate. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/utility-billing-services/understanding-your-utility-bill
- Board and feed figures for this label do not reconcile (see NOT CONFIRMED), so no Serpentine median, pace or supply figure should be quoted from either source. https://news.fvreb.bc.ca/member-updates/tech-tip-18-map-of-the-bc-boards-areas-subareas/

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Surrey's 2026 municipal tax rate for Residential (Class 1) is 1.84251 per $1,000 of assessed value, and the Capital Parcel Tax collected with property taxes is $300.00 for Classes 1, 8 and 9 (Farm). https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- The 2026 annual flat-rate utility total for a single-family dwelling is $2,856 — $1,195 water plus $1,661 sewer — and metered water and sewer bills are mailed every four months. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/utility-billing-services/understanding-your-utility-bill
- Waste collection is charged on the property tax notice: the 2026 single-family garbage, recycling and organics rate is $344.08 for the calendar year, due July 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/utility-billing-services/understanding-your-utility-bill
- Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5 percent penalty after July 2 and a further 5 percent after September 2 — check current. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- Surrey is a listed taxable area for the provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax, so an owner here files the annual declaration unless an exemption applies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Highway 15 (176 Street) is the sub-area's main north–south provincial route, running through the farm ground toward the border; the City's watershed mapping shows Highway 15, Highway 99 and Highway 17 framing this lowland. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/surrey-ismp-map.pdf
- 16 Avenue is the east–west road the Campbell Heights work is built around, with planned improvements at 192 Street and along 24, 28 and 32 Avenues. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/Campbell-Heights-Info-Boards-Open-House-Final-web.pdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood is the first check: Surrey's own floodplain reporting attributes Serpentine, Nicomekl and Little Campbell lowland flooding to local winter rainstorms, and most of that floodplain is agricultural and in the ALR. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2007-C013.pdf
- Check ALR status parcel by parcel on Agricultural Land Commission mapping before assuming any house size, suite or subdivision right; a listing description is not an ALR determination. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- Check the title at the Land Title and Survey Authority of British Columbia (LTSA) for flood covenants, dyke rights of way and building schemes, which can restrict a parcel beyond zoning. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
