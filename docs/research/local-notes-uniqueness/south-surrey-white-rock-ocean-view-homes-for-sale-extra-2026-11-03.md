## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Steep-slope rules are the main Surrey control: the Hazard Lands Development Permit Area (DPA) covers parcels with slopes over 20% gradient, or within 10 metres downslope or 30 metres of the top of such a slope. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/surrey-official-community-plan/development-permit-areas
- Its stated purpose is to protect people, property and the environment from development on unsafe slopes, and prevent soil erosion there. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/surrey-official-community-plan/development-permit-areas
- A tree can decide whether a view survives: Surrey Tree Protection Bylaw, 2006, No. 16100 requires a permit for protected trees, including trees over 30 cm diameter at breast height. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ResidentialTreeCuttingApplicationGuidelines_0.pdf
- White Rock hillside lots follow different documents: Zoning Bylaw, 2024, No. 2506, the OCP Schedule C Environmental DPA map, and Tree Protection Bylaw 2407. https://www.whiterockcity.ca/514/Resources-Documents

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Does buying a view home protect the view? **A:** No view right is confirmed in any source read for this note. What is regulated is building and trees: Surrey controls siting on slopes through the Hazard Lands DPA, and its Tree Protection Bylaw, 2006, No. 16100 requires a permit before a protected tree — including one blocking a view — can be cut. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/surrey-official-community-plan/development-permit-areas
- **Q:** Can I build or add on an Ocean Park bluff lot? **A:** Expect a geotechnical step first. The City's 2026 report for 12588 16 Avenue required a Development Permit for Hazard Lands (Steep Slopes), and the geotechnical report submitted was peer reviewed before staff found it conformed to the Official Community Plan. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7925-0012-00.pdf
- **Q:** Which secondary school serves an upland address here? **A:** Check the exact address. Surrey School District No. 36 (SD36) states the catchment school is determined by where the address falls within the district's School Boundaries Map, using its School Locator tool; Semiahmoo Secondary, one of the South Surrey secondaries, is at 1785 148 Street. https://www.surreyschools.ca/chs/registration-1
- **Q:** What does Surrey charge to remove a protected tree on a building site? **A:** The City's building-site page states a tree cutting permit fee of $108 plus $42 per tree removed, replacement tree securities of $550 per replacement tree, and tree protection securities of $3,000 per tree up to $15,000 per single-family lot, paid before the building permit issues. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/trees-yard-garden/trees-landscape-building-site-requirements

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Surrey requires two replacement trees for every permit-sized tree removed, and states fines for removing a tree can be up to $20,000. https://www.Surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/trees-yard-garden/protecting-surreys-urban-forest
- An arborist report is required with a Surrey tree cutting application for five or more trees, and the permit must be posted on site before removal. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ResidentialTreeCuttingApplicationGuidelines_0.pdf
- SD36 elementary schools run kindergarten to Grade 7 and secondary schools Grades 8 to 12, so an upland address changes schools at Grade 8. https://www.surreyschools.ca/semiahmoo/_ci/p/174781

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
