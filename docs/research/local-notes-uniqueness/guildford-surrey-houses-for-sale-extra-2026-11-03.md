# Guildford Houses — Extra Local Notes

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Guildford is in Surrey's northeast corner, with the Fraser River along its northern edge, Whalley to the west, Langley to the east, and a southern border that stretches from 96 Avenue to 84 Avenue. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Development in Guildford has predominantly centred on 152 Street and 104 Avenue around Guildford Town Centre Mall — that intersection is the community's practical centre for a house search. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Along the northern edge there are views of the Port Mann Bridge and the Fraser River, and the newer Fraser Heights subdivision slopes down toward the river on that side. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Early settlement here was in the Township of Port Kells, established by Irish pioneers and connected by rail to New Westminster in 1891 — the Port Kells name still marks Guildford's northeast corner. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Guildford's detached stock is mostly mature urban neighbourhoods that grew up around Guildford Town Centre mall, plus the newer Fraser Heights subdivision — two visibly different house patterns in one community. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- The Guildford Plan is a complete land-use plan for parts of North Surrey including Guildford, approved by Council on October 30, 2023, so the mature stock around the town centre now sits inside a plan that designates the 104 Avenue corridor for more density. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/GuildfordPlanFinal.pdf
- The opening of the Port Mann Bridge in 1964 brought increased development and people to the area, which dates much of the mature subdivision pattern a buyer sees around the town centre. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The Guildford Town Centre – 104 Avenue Corridor Plan (Stage 1) designates land along the corridor Low to Mid Rise Apartment, so a house on or near 104 Avenue can sit under a multi-family designation even while it is still a detached house. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7923-0308-00.pdf
- Sites inside the corridor can also sit within a Frequent Transit Development Permit Area, where the City's reports tie higher-density development to the frequent transit service along 104 Avenue. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7923-0308-00.pdf
- Surrey-wide zoning, small-scale multi-unit housing, suite and coach-house rules are carried in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note; the Guildford difference is the corridor plan layered over the houses near 104 Avenue and 152 Street. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Why are apartment buildings appearing among houses near 104 Avenue? **A:** The Guildford Town Centre – 104 Avenue Corridor Plan designates that corridor for low to mid rise apartment use, and City reports approve six-storey apartment buildings on lots that currently hold single family dwellings. One report for 14308 103A Avenue describes the existing use as single family dwellings under a Low to Mid Rise Apartment designation, roughly 150 metres from a rapid bus stop. A house bought on the corridor is bought inside that transition, so check the plan designation for the exact lot. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7923-0308-00.pdf
- **Q:** How do I get downtown or to SkyTrain from a Guildford house? **A:** Guildford has no SkyTrain station; its anchor is Guildford Exchange, which the City lists among Surrey's bus exchanges. TransLink's R1 King George Boulevard RapidBus runs from Guildford Exchange to Newton Exchange through the Surrey Central and King George SkyTrain stations, so the standard transit commute is a bus to the exchange, then the R1 to a station. Drivers use the highway access the City notes for Guildford, with the Port Mann Bridge on the community's northern edge. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- **Q:** Is Fraser Heights part of Guildford or its own area? **A:** Fraser Heights is a subdivision inside Guildford, not a separate community. The City describes Guildford as made up of mature urban neighbourhoods around the town centre mall as well as the newer Fraser Heights subdivision, which slopes down toward the Fraser River. Board listings may carry Fraser Heights as its own label, so a buyer searching only "Guildford" can miss Fraser Heights houses, and a buyer searching only "Fraser Heights" can miss the mature town-centre stock. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- **Q:** What is at the Tynehead side of Guildford? **A:** Tynehead Regional Park, a Metro Vancouver regional park at the headwaters of the Serpentine River, borders Guildford's east side. Metro Vancouver describes trails including the 5-kilometre Tynehead Perimeter Trail (east) and the 1.7-kilometre Serpentine Loop Trail, a fish hatchery raising coho, chum and chinook salmon, and a leash-optional dog area that is not fenced. Houses near the park gain the trails but also the park's gate hours, which run 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. from November 3 to December 31 per the park page. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/tynehead-regional-park
- **Q:** Are Guildford houses in the floodplain? **A:** The City's coastal floodplain stretches along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers toward Cloverdale and Newton, which is south of most Guildford streets, but the Serpentine headwaters sit in Tynehead Regional Park at Guildford's east side. A Fraser Heights house on the slope toward the Fraser River and any low lot near the Serpentine headwaters deserve the lot-level check on City mapping, because the floodplain page describes areas, not individual parcels. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The City is running a planning study for Guildford Town Centre and the 104 Avenue Corridor to support enhancement of the commercial hub and surrounding areas — the plan work around the mall is still active, not finished. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- The Surrey Biofuel Facility, which the City presents on its Guildford page, is described by the City as the first closed-loop organic waste facility in North America, processing organic waste into renewable natural gas and compost. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- The barn at the eastern section of Tynehead Regional Park is a designated heritage building and one of the last surviving regional examples of a building made with mortise and tenon joints — a landmark at Guildford's east edge. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/tynehead-regional-park
- The City's Guildford gallery includes Port Kells Park, the Port Kells outdoor pool and the Port Kells Library branch — the old Port Kells township corner in the northeast still carries its own named civic facilities. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Guildford's named recreation anchor on the City page is the Guildford Aquatics Centre, alongside the Fraser Heights Recreation Centre — two separate recreation centres serve the one community. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Guildford houses pay the city-wide Surrey tax — there is no Guildford rate. The full 2026 rate, parcel taxes and utility figures are carried in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5 percent penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5 percent penalty after September 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies in Guildford because the Province lists the City of Surrey as a designated taxable area in the Metro Vancouver Regional District. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Guildford Exchange is the community's transit anchor, listed by the City among Surrey's bus exchanges, and it is the eastern terminus of TransLink's R1 King George Boulevard RapidBus. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- The R1 runs from Guildford Exchange west along 104 Avenue to Surrey Central and King George stations, then south to Newton Exchange — the route that connects a Guildford house to SkyTrain. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- Guildford has highway access and access to Surrey City Centre, per the City's community description, with the Port Mann Bridge and Highway 1 along its northern edge by Fraser Heights. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the Guildford Town Centre – 104 Avenue Corridor Plan designation for any house near 104 Avenue, because Low to Mid Rise Apartment designations and Frequent Transit Development Permit Areas can change what is built beside or instead of a detached house there. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7923-0308-00.pdf
- For a Fraser Heights lot, confirm slope and river-edge conditions on the City's mapping — the subdivision slopes down toward the Fraser River, and the City's flood material describes areas rather than clearing individual sloped parcels. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- For lots near Tynehead Regional Park, note the habitat rules that come with the Serpentine River headwaters: Metro Vancouver states salmon live in the river in most life stages and that people and dogs should stay out of the water and away from the banks. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/tynehead-regional-park
