# Abbotsford Mobile Homes — Extra Local Notes
Page: abbotsford-mobile-homes (Abbotsford; mobile homes).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Abbotsford's manufactured-home parks sit in the built-up west, central, and Poplar areas of the city, not on the Sumas or Matsqui Prairie floodplains to the east and north. The City's flood response page describes Sumas Prairie as a 90-square-kilometre low-lying area in East Abbotsford, which is a different part of the city from the named parks below. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- The zoning that holds the parks is the Manufactured Home Park Zone (RH1) of the Abbotsford Zoning Bylaw 2014, a zone for manufactured homes on individual pads inside one unsubdivided park property; the RH1 provisions were renumbered to section 491 by Bylaw 3427-2023. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/57708
- A second, different form exists on ordinary city streets: the Infill Manufactured Home Zone (RSMH) covers fee-simple manufactured homes on individual urban lots, with a minimum interior lot size of 495 square metres. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400388

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Abbotsford offers three distinct forms: rented pads in RH1 parks, a bare-land strata community, and fee-simple manufactured homes on RSMH lots. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400388
- Paradise Park, 31313 Livingstone Avenue in Abbotsford West, is a pad-rental park; City public hearing records list the address with RH1 zoning, and 2010 hearing minutes record Paradise Park residents at units in 31313 Livingstone Avenue, with one resident stating the park is for residents 55 years and older. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/49370 https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/17134/
- Georgian Park, 3300 Horn Street in Central Abbotsford, is a pad-rental park carried from the earlier local-notes program; its 55-plus rule is listing-stated only, and no official-source page confirming the address was found in this pass. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/development-planning
- Maplewood Estates, also called Maplewood Mobile Home Park, 2035 Martens Street in Poplar, is likewise a pad-rental park carried from the earlier program, with its 55-plus rule listing-stated only and no official confirmation found in this pass. https://www.abbotsford.ca/business-development/development-planning
- Downes Road Estates, 32250 Downes Road, is not a pad tenancy at all: it is a bare-land strata (Strata Plan LMS2380 in the earlier program), so buyers there own a strata lot and pay strata fees instead of pad rent. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2014b2400388

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- On a rented pad in Abbotsford, the buyer owns the home as a chattel and rents the pad under the provincial Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA) — see the surrey-mobile-homes note for the full framework and pad-rent math. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/assigment_of_mhp_tenancy_agreements.pdf
- Abbotsford has a Manufactured Home Park Redevelopment Policy, No. 400-3-07, adopted November 3, 2003 and later amended; the adopted text shows it did not apply to Areas 2, 3, and 4 of the former Allwood park, and its current status was not confirmed on a current City policy page in this pass. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/5994/
- The City's Building Bylaw 2018 deals with manufactured buildings in section 41, which references the Canadian Standards Association (CSA) A277 and Z240.10.1 standards that a manufactured home must meet. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2018b2883
- The City's updated Official Community Plan, Abbotsforward 2050, approved at the end of 2025, creates new opportunities for non-market housing on land designated Urban Area Manufactured Home Park, which signals the City treats park land as a housing resource rather than only a redevelopment reserve. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2025-11/Council%20Brief%20Oct%202025.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Does Abbotsford have a manufactured home park redevelopment policy? **A:** Yes, one was adopted, but check its current status before relying on it. Council adopted Manufactured Home Park Redevelopment Policy No. 400-3-07 on November 3, 2003, and later amendments deleted its section 5 and renamed relocation wording as compensation; no current City policy listing confirming it remains in force was found in this pass, while the provincial act's closure rules apply regardless. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/5994/
- **Q:** What is the difference between a pad at Paradise Park and a lot at Downes Road Estates? **A:** At Paradise Park you rent the pad and own only the home, under a Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act tenancy that needs the park owner's consent to assign. At Downes Road Estates you buy a bare-land strata lot as well as the home, so title is registered land ownership, strata bylaws and fees apply, and there is no pad tenancy to assign. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/49370
- **Q:** Are Abbotsford's parks in the Sumas Prairie floodplain? **A:** The named parks are not in Sumas Prairie, but do not assume any address is flood-free. The City describes Sumas Prairie as a 90-square-kilometre low area in East Abbotsford flooded in 1990, 2021, and December 2025, while Paradise Park, Georgian Park, and Maplewood Estates sit in the urban west, central, and Poplar areas; the flood status of a specific park address was not confirmed in this pass and should be checked on the City's property tools. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- **Q:** How much can pad rent go up in an Abbotsford park? **A:** Only by the provincial percentage, stated here as a percentage and never as a dollar figure. For 2026 the limit for a manufactured home site is 2.3 percent plus the proportionate increase in local government levies and regulated utility fees on the park's common property, with three full months' notice and only one increase in any 12 months. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/rent-rtb/rent-increases
- **Q:** What construction standard should a manufactured home here meet? **A:** It should carry a recognized Canadian Standards Association (CSA) label. Abbotsford's Building Bylaw 2018, section 41 on manufactured buildings, references the CSA A277 and Z240.10.1 standards, so a buyer should look for the home's CSA label and registration details and have the home inspected, since a sale of a manufactured home is only effective once it is registered in the provincial Manufactured Home Registry. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2018b2883

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The Allwood park precedent shows redevelopment is not theoretical here: 2800 Allwood Street was rezoned from Mobile Home Park (RH1) to a Comprehensive Development zone for townhouses, recorded in 2013 Council minutes. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/30782/
- Policy 400-3-07 expressly did not apply to Areas 2, 3, and 4 of the Allwood park, a carve-out a buyer would only find by reading the adopted policy text. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/5994/
- Abbotsforward 2050, the City's updated Official Community Plan, is now being implemented, and the City brief on it names Urban Area Manufactured Home Park land as a place for new non-market housing opportunities. https://www.abbotsford.ca/council/your-council-community/blog/city-begins-implementing-updated-official-community-plan
- Council approved a 4 percent water increase and a 4 percent sewer increase in January 2026, effective March 1, 2026, which flows into park operating costs and, through the provincial formula, into allowable pad-rent pass-throughs. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/utility-billing/water-sewer-rates

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood is the city's defining risk, but it is location-specific: the City's flood page records major floods in 1990, 2021, and December 2025, when 485 properties were under Evacuation Order and Highway 1 closed for three days — impacts centred on the prairie lowlands, not the urban park districts. https://www.abbotsford.ca/floodresponse
- Park closure risk follows the provincial framework summarized in the surrey-mobile-homes note — permits first, 12 months' notice, and compensation — layered over the City's 2003 Policy 400-3-07, whose current status a buyer should confirm with the City before relying on it. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/5994/
- Because a manufactured home is a chattel on a rented pad, due diligence is double: confirm the home's CSA label and Manufactured Home Registry registration, and confirm the pad tenancy can be assigned, which needs the park owner's consent on Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB) Form 10 with a 10-day deemed-consent rule. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- The Allwood rezoning shows an RH1 park can be rezoned to another use through a public process, so a buyer should search the City's development planning records for the park's address before buying. https://abbotsford.civicweb.net/document/30782/
