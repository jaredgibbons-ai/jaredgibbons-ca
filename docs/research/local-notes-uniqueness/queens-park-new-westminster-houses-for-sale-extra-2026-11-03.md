# Queen's Park Houses — Extra Local Notes
Page: queens-park-new-westminster-houses-for-sale (Queen's Park, New Westminster; houses).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Queen's Park is a roughly 118-hectare neighbourhood in central New Westminster, between Royal Avenue and Sixth Avenue and between Sixth Street and McBride Boulevard. That boundary is stated in the Statement of Significance appended to the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area amendment, Official Community Plan (OCP) Bylaw No. 7926, 2017. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Schedule_B_to_Official_Community_Plan___SOS.pdf
- The neighbourhood is predominantly single detached residential, with some multi-family buildings, commercial and institutional buildings inside the same boundary. The Statement of Significance states that mix directly, so a Queen's Park house search also crosses apartment and institutional parcels on the same streets. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Schedule_B_to_Official_Community_Plan___SOS.pdf
- Queen's Park itself, the park, occupies the eastern portion of the neighbourhood and comprises nearly 30 hectares of it. The Statement of Significance separates the park from the residential streets this way, which is why the park edge on McBride Boulevard is a boundary, not part of the housing stock. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Schedule_B_to_Official_Community_Plan___SOS.pdf
- The neighbourhood dates to the first survey of the city, because the Statement of Significance states it was included in the 1859 survey for the capital city of the Colony of British Columbia chosen by Colonel Richard Moody, and its street pattern is a grid with planted boulevards. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Schedule_B_to_Official_Community_Plan___SOS.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Queen's Park's stock is valued as a collection, not house by house, because the Statement of Significance describes the neighbourhood as valued in particular for its large collection of grand houses and their associated gardens. That collection is the reason the whole area, not only listed houses, carries heritage rules. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Schedule_B_to_Official_Community_Plan___SOS.pdf
- Every property in the area is classified Protected or Non-protected, regardless of construction age, and the City's Queen's Park HCA guide (last updated July 2023) states the provisions for Protected properties apply to the exterior, not the interior. The City also states Queen's Park is currently the only Heritage Conservation Area (HCA) in New Westminster. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- A Heritage Alteration Permit (HAP) is the central approval here, because Building Permit applications for new buildings, demolition, or changes affecting the front, sides or visible roofline of Protected properties, and subdivision applications, require an HAP and are reviewed for design guideline compliance. The City states that rule on its HCA page for the Queen's Park HCA. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- On a Protected property, an HAP can be required even when no Building Permit is needed, because the City states an HAP and additional review is also given for exterior changes that do not require a Building Permit. Standard maintenance, such as replacement of materials in-kind, does not require an HAP. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- Protected properties also carry a maintenance duty, under the City's Heritage Property Maintenance Standards Bylaw. The City states that bylaw requires an owner of protected property to keep the historic building, and identified architectural and landscape features, in good repair, and that it applies to properties protected in the Queen's Park HCA. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/1627.php
- The HCA also carries stated incentives for Protected properties: additional density, special provisions in the British Columbia Building Code, density transfer, relaxed design guidelines for laneway and carriage houses, and no heritage permit fees. The City lists those incentives on its HCA page, so they are part of a Protected house's file, not a separate negotiation. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- Before the HCA existed, houses built before 1967 in Queen's Park were subject to a one-year Heritage Control Period from June 2016 to June 2017, whose purpose the City states was to minimize loss of houses and street character while the HCA was explored — the origin of the 1967 date in older Queen's Park files. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/queens-park-heritage-conservation-area/how-the-hca-was-developed/sb_expander_articles/1268.php

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Is every Queen's Park house heritage-protected, even a newer one?
**A:** Every property sits inside the Heritage Conservation Area, but protection is split into two categories, Protected and Non-protected, regardless of construction age. The City states the HCA places a layer of protection over all properties in the area, while the permit triggers differ by category, so a buyer must confirm which category the specific house holds before assuming demolition or alteration is open. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php

**Q:** What permit do I need to change the front of a Protected house?
**A:** A Heritage Alteration Permit is required for Building Permit work affecting the front, sides or visible roofline of a Protected property, and the application is reviewed for compliance with the Queen's Park HCA design guidelines. The City also states an HAP and additional review apply to exterior changes on Protected properties that do not need a Building Permit, while in-kind maintenance does not require one. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php

**Q:** Which elementary school is in the Queen's Park area?
**A:** Herbert Spencer Elementary, at 605 Second Street, is the School District No. 40 (New Westminster) elementary school listed by the Province for this part of the city, at Kindergarten to Grade 5. The district also offers Early French Immersion at Herbert Spencer, with intake at Kindergarten or Grade 1, subject to catchment placement. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in/new-westminster

**Q:** Where do Queen's Park students go after elementary school?
**A:** New Westminster has one secondary school, New Westminster Secondary School, listed by the Province at 820 6th Street for Grades 9 to 12. For middle school, École Glenbrook Middle School at 701 Park Crescent serves Grades 6 to 8, and the district states all secondary French Immersion students attend New Westminster Secondary School, so a Queen's Park student's secondary destination does not change by street. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in/new-westminster

**Q:** What does the park itself add to a house purchase on its edge?
**A:** Queen's Park is a 75-acre park established June 13, 1887, which the City calls New Westminster's flagship park. It holds the Queen's Park Arena, the Queen's Park Sportsplex, sports fields and a stadium holding 2,000 spectators, so a house facing the park faces a working sports complex with booked field use, not only green space. https://www.newwestcity.ca/parks-and-recreation/parks/community-parks/articles/5437.php

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Queen's Park Arena, inside Queen's Park, hosts 3,500 seats and is home to ice hockey, lacrosse, minor sports associations, tournaments and special events. The City states the arena received the 2020 Bill Woycik Outstanding Facility Award from the Recreation Facilities Association of British Columbia. https://www.newwestcity.ca/parks-and-recreation/facilities/queens-park-arena
- The Queen's Park stadium holds 2,000 spectators and, with the park's artificial turf field, has lighting, and all field use requires a booking. The City lists soccer, field lacrosse, football, baseball and softball fields in the same park. https://www.newwestcity.ca/parks-and-recreation/parks/community-parks/articles/5437.php
- The Queen's Park Sportsplex is a 12,000-square-foot multi-sport facility beside the stadium, arena and skatepark, built after the collapse of the Queen's Park Arenex in 2016, and the City states it is the City's first net zero carbon facility, with solar panels and reclaimed Arenex timbers in its design. https://www.newwestcity.ca/2020/11/02/city-of-new-westminster-announces-opening-queens-park-sportsplex.php
- The park's bandshell is mid-renovation, not simply closed: the City states the Queen's Park Bandshell, a wood frame structure built in 1969, was assessed at the end of its service life, its roof and stage-level storage were removed in Phase 1, and Phase 2 restoration is in the design phase. https://www.newwestcity.ca/planning-building-and-development/projects-on-the-go/articles/8285.php?status=active
- The park's off-leash dog area is an accessible off-leash area, listed separately by the City from the sports fields, and its facilities also include Centennial Lodge and the Rose Garden, the Greenhouse, picnic shelters, a spray park and a bike skills park. https://www.newwestcity.ca/parks-and-recreation/parks/community-parks/articles/5437.php

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Queen's Park has no SkyTrain station inside its Royal Avenue to Sixth Avenue boundary, so its rail access is the Expo Line stations south of it. TransLink's Expo Line timetable lists, in order, 22nd Street, New Westminster, Columbia and then Scott Road stations on the Waterfront to King George line, which places New Westminster and Columbia stations as the downtown pair serving the Queen's Park streets. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt991.pdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Confirm the property's HCA category before subjects come off, because Protected and Non-protected properties face different permit triggers in the Queen's Park HCA. The City states Building Permit work and subdivision in the area require a Heritage Alteration Permit and design guideline review, with added review for exterior work on Protected properties even without a Building Permit. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/582.php
- Check the maintenance record on a Protected house, because the Heritage Property Maintenance Standards Bylaw requires the owner to keep the historic building and its identified architectural and landscape features in good repair. Deferred maintenance on a Protected Queen's Park house is therefore a bylaw matter, not only a condition item. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-protection-and-development/sb_expander_articles/1627.php
- Read the July 2023 Queen's Park HCA guide against the official documents, because the guide states it does not replace official documents and the City disclaims liability from its use; the OCP Schedule B and the HCA provisions are the documents to cite. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/QPHCA_Guide___JULY_2023.pdf
- For a house beside the park, check the Queen's Park Outdoor Stage Renovation timing, because the City states the bandshell roof structure was removed for safety and Phase 2 work runs, on the estimate read, from October 2025 to Summer 2027. Construction near the bandshell is a current condition of the park edge, not a completed project. https://www.newwestcity.ca/planning-building-and-development/projects-on-the-go/articles/8285.php?status=active
