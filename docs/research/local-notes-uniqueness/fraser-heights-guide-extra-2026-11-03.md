# Fraser Heights Guide — Extra Local Notes
Page: fraser-heights-guide (Fraser Heights, Surrey; area guide).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Fraser Heights sits north of Highway 1 (the Trans-Canada Highway), with the Fraser River and the South Fraser Perimeter Road (Highway 17) on its north side. A City of Surrey artist-call page for the area states the northern border is the South Fraser Perimeter Road and the Fraser River and the southern border is Highway 1; the City publishes no official boundary for the whole sub-area. https://www.surrey.ca/business-economy/tenders-rfqs-rfps/artist-call-expressions-of-interest-fraser-heights-recreation
- Its east end has a precise official frame: Abbey Ridge, the neighbourhood in East Fraser Heights, is about 175 hectares (430 acres) north of Highway 1, between 172 Street in the west and the Port Kells Industrial Area and the Golden Ears Connector in the east. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/guildford-land-use-plans/abbey-ridge-land-use-plan
- Surrey Bend Regional Park lies east of the residential area, along the Fraser River, which is the area's eastern green edge. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/surrey-bend-regional-park
- History in brief: East Fraser Heights was originally suburban acreage lots, and the Abbey Ridge plan, approved in 2017, designated it to support urban development instead. The rest of Fraser Heights has no equivalent single plan — it built out under the general Official Community Plan (OCP). https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/guildford-land-use-plans/abbey-ridge-land-use-plan

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The east end's stock story is acreage lots converting to urban housing: the City states Abbey Ridge was originally characterized by its suburban acreage lots and is now designated to support urban development, largely residential, with industrial designations along the Golden Ears Connector and a small neighbourhood commercial area on Barnston Drive. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/guildford-land-use-plans/abbey-ridge-land-use-plan
- The recreation anchor is Fraser Heights Recreation Centre, 10588 160 Street, with two gymnasiums, a fitness studio and a weight room of about 5,000 square feet, plus two preschool rooms. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/recreation-facilities/fraser-heights-recreation-centre
- Fraser Heights Park, on the same site at 10588 160 Street, carries tennis courts, turf fields, an all-weather gravel field, a skateboard plaza and natural-area forest trails. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/fraser-heights-park
- No Surrey Libraries branch sits in Fraser Heights; Surrey Libraries' Mobile Library makes scheduled stops at the recreation centre, and the nearest branches are Port Kells at 18885 88 Avenue and Guildford at 15105 105 Avenue. https://surreylibraries.ca/locations/port-kells-branch

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The Abbey Ridge Land Use Plan is the plan that governs East Fraser Heights: Council approved the final plan on February 6, 2017 (Corporate Report R032), after Stage 1 approval on December 14, 2015. Its land use designations have since been superseded by the Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan update, while the plan still guides public amenities and infrastructure. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/guildford-land-use-plans/abbey-ridge-land-use-plan
- For the rest of Fraser Heights there is no neighbourhood plan: no other City plan specific to the area was found on the City's Guildford land use plan pages, so most lots are governed by the OCP and their zoning alone. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/guildford-land-use-plans/abbey-ridge-land-use-plan
- City-wide Surrey zoning, secondary suite and Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) rules are written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/provincial-housing-financial-regulations
- Every Surrey property carries a zone under Zoning Bylaw No. 12000, and the City's COSMOS mapping is where a buyer confirms the zone and plan designations for a specific Fraser Heights address. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/zoning

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Which secondary school serves Fraser Heights? **A:** Fraser Heights Secondary, Grades 8–12, at 16060 108 Avenue. The provincial school list for School District 36 (Surrey) gives that address and grade range, and the district's Families of Schools grouping places Bothwell, Dogwood, Erma Stephenson and Fraser Wood elementaries in its family. Catchments remain address-based, so confirm the exact house address in the district school locator before relying on a school. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/036
- **Q:** Which elementary schools are in the area? **A:** Four Kindergarten to Grade 7 schools sit inside Fraser Heights in the provincial list: Bothwell Elementary at 17070 102 Avenue, Dogwood Elementary at 10752 157 Street, Erma Stephenson Elementary at 10929 160 Street and Fraser Wood Elementary at 10650 164 Street. Port Kells Elementary at 19076 88 Avenue serves the far east side in Port Kells. Which one a house belongs to is decided by the address, not the street name alone. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/036
- **Q:** Is East Fraser Heights still acreage country? **A:** No — it is a plan area mid-conversion. The City states Abbey Ridge was originally suburban acreage lots and is now designated to support urban development under a plan approved February 6, 2017, largely residential with industrial land along the Golden Ears Connector and a small commercial area on Barnston Drive. An acreage-priced lot there carries urban servicing and plan expectations, not farm rules. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/guildford-land-use-plans/abbey-ridge-land-use-plan
- **Q:** How do I get out of Fraser Heights by car? **A:** Through the Highway 1 interchanges — there is no other way across. A City transportation report records that Fraser Heights residents access areas outside via the 152 Street, 160 Street and 176 Street interchanges, and that traffic routinely backed up from Highway 1 onto 152 Street and 160 Street at various times of day. Golden Ears Way serves the east end through the Abbey Ridge / Port Kells side. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2003-R084.pdf
- **Q:** Is there a SkyTrain or a direct downtown bus? **A:** No SkyTrain reaches Fraser Heights, and the buses run to exchanges, not downtown. TransLink route 337 is named Fraser Heights / Surrey Central Station and runs to Surrey Central Station via Guildford Exchange, and route 338, a community shuttle launched in January 2025, loops Guildford Exchange to East Fraser Heights at 177A Street and 101A Avenue via the Golden Ears Connector. Rail trips start with a bus to a station. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/337/direction/0/schedule

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Harold Bishop Elementary at 15670 104 Avenue sits at the area's south edge but is grouped with Johnston Heights Secondary, not Fraser Heights Secondary — a south-edge address does not guarantee the Fraser Heights school family. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/036
- The riverfront park floods seasonally by design: Metro Vancouver states sections of the Parsons and Pacific trails in Surrey Bend Regional Park may be flooded and closed seasonally during high tides or heavy rainfall, and swimming is not recommended because of swift currents. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/surrey-bend-regional-park
- Erma Stephenson Park at 15920 110 Avenue — water park, baseball diamond, soccer field and picnic shelter — is listed by the City under the Guildford community even though its address is on the Fraser Heights side of Highway 1, so City park searches by community can miss it. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/erma-stephenson-park
- Route 338 is new and easy to miss: the East Fraser Heights community shuttle began January 6, 2025, running every 30 minutes between Guildford Exchange and 177A Street at 101A Avenue via 104 Avenue, 160 Street and the Golden Ears Connector. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt338.pdf
- A pedestrian and cycling bridge crosses Highway 1 at 112 Avenue as part of the City Centre to Fraser Heights cycling route, linking the Fraser Heights Greenway south across the highway corridor — the non-car crossing a buyer will not find on a road map. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2014-R105.pdf

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Route 337 is the main bus: TransLink names it Fraser Heights / Surrey Central Station, and its timetable runs from Surrey Central Station to a Fraser Heights terminus at 168 Street and 104 Avenue via Guildford Exchange. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/337/direction/0/schedule
- Route 338 covers the east end: the TransLink timetable shows it running from Guildford Exchange via Lincoln Drive, 105 Avenue, 150 Street, 104 Avenue, 160 Street and the Golden Ears Connector to 177A Street at 101A Avenue, returning via Barnston Drive East and 168 Street. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt338.pdf
- Highway access is the area's defining constraint: exits from Fraser Heights are the Highway 1 interchanges at 152 Street, 160 Street and 176 Street, and Highway 17 (South Fraser Perimeter Road) runs along the north side. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2003-R084.pdf
- Golden Ears Way is the east-end connector named in the Abbey Ridge plan area boundary and in the 338 route, linking East Fraser Heights toward the Golden Ears Bridge corridor; measured drive times to Guildford, Surrey Central or the bridge are UNCONFIRMED. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/guildford-land-use-plans/abbey-ridge-land-use-plan

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the lot's elevation against the river, not the street: the residential area sits on the upland south of the Fraser River, while the low riverfront land to the east in Surrey Bend Regional Park is floodplain forest and marsh — lots at the north and east edges deserve a floodplain check in City COSMOS mapping. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/surrey-bend-regional-park
- City-wide, the Fraser River floodplain covers 776 hectares of developed land behind dykes and four pump stations; the City page does not state a Fraser Heights-specific flood level, so a lot's status must come from the City's mapping for that address. https://surrey.ca/city-services/3655.aspx
- Highway noise is a named planning issue for this area: a City corporate report on the Port Mann / Highway 1 project listed Fraser Heights among the communities most impacted by construction noise and required noise monitoring and contour drawings to guide redevelopment near the corridor. (check current — report dated 2007) https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2007_R233.pdf
- Confirm Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) status per property on the far east acreage edge before assuming a use; no source read confirms or clears ALR for Fraser Heights as a whole, and the check is parcel-level through Agricultural Land Commission mapping. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
