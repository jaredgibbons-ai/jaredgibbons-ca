## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Coquitlam's condo stock sits in three different transit corridors, not one downtown: City Centre on Pinetree Way and Barnet Highway, Burquitlam and Lougheed on North Road, and the low-rise benches above them — and the City's own growth plan splits those corridors from the existing neighbourhoods. https://www.coquitlam.ca/320/How-Development-Happens
- The City states new growth is focused in transit-oriented areas near SkyTrain, such as City Centre, Burquitlam and Lougheed, taking 50% of growth, and in existing neighbourhoods such as Austin Heights and Maillardville, taking 29% of growth. https://www.coquitlam.ca/320/How-Development-Happens

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Coquitlam's zoning is Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996, and on June 9, 2025 Council adopted zoning changes for Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH), with supporting amendments June 23, 2025 — the bylaw frame every condo-adjacent lot in the city sits under. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit
- The SSMUH numbers are: up to 3 units on lots of 280 square metres or smaller, and up to 4 units on lots larger than 280 square metres but under 4,050 square metres; the 6-unit tier near frequent-service bus stops does not apply because the City states Coquitlam currently has no bus stops meeting the provincial frequent-service criteria. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit
- Forms allowed under the small-scale rules include secondary suites, carriage houses, laneway homes, garden cottages, duplexes, triplexes and fourplexes, and units may be stratified and sold or kept as rentals, subject to the zone — the pipeline that competes with resale condos at the low end. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit
- The whole city is now covered by the Interim Official Community Plan (OCP), Bylaw No. 5511, 2026, adopted April 27, 2026, which replaced Citywide OCP Bylaw No. 3479, 2001 and carries the former area plans in as Schedule J schedules — check current, as plan references in older strata and developer documents are superseded. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1436/Official-Community-Plan-Review
- The Southwest Shoulders and Corridors amendments, adopted May 27, 2026, updated land use around the Burquitlam and Lougheed Town Centre SkyTrain stations and along Austin Avenue, creating opportunities for medium-density housing and townhousing in Southwest Coquitlam. https://letstalkcoquitlam.ca/TransitOrientedAreas

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Where in Coquitlam should a condo buyer look first? **A:** Start with the corridor, not the city. The City's How Development Happens page states growth is focused in transit-oriented areas near SkyTrain — City Centre, Burquitlam and Lougheed take 50% of planned growth — while existing neighbourhoods such as Austin Heights and Maillardville take 29%. That split is why most new condo supply is on Pinetree Way and North Road, and why bench neighbourhoods offer mainly older low-rise stock. https://www.coquitlam.ca/320/How-Development-Happens
- **Q:** How many homes are in The Burquitlam Capital, really? **A:** Count it the developer's way, then check the strata plan. The developer, Magusta, records The Burquitlam Capital as 189 condominiums plus four townhomes, 193 homes in total. Earlier program research found a building source giving a different split of the same total; the strata plan governs unit entitlement and fees, so a buyer should reconcile the Form B and strata plan documents rather than either marketing count. https://magusta.com/our-portfolio/the-burquitlam-capital/
- **Q:** What will my Coquitlam condo's city tax rate be in 2026? **A:** The municipal part is $2.2756 per $1,000 of assessed value. The City's Summary of 2026 Tax Rates sets total municipal Class 1 Residential at $2.2756 per $1,000, made up of $2.1993 for municipal services plus $0.0763 for drainage, with the grand total including other governments at $3.8520 per $1,000. The rate applies the same way to a condo as to a house; the assessed value is what changes the bill. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17264/2026_Tax-Rates
- **Q:** When are Coquitlam taxes and utilities due? **A:** Taxes first, utilities earlier in the year. The City states 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026 for the January 1 to December 31, 2026 period, with a 5% penalty after July 2 and a further 5% after September 15, 2026. Utility bills were due March 31, 2026. A condo buyer closing mid-year should check both dates on the seller's tax and utility notices, not just the tax certificate. https://coquitlam.ca/544/Property-Taxes
- **Q:** Is new low-rise condo product still being built, or only towers? **A:** Low-rise is still in the permit stream. The City's current multiple-family building-permit list, updated to October 1, 2026, records a single new six-storey apartment building of 94 units at 727 North Road, applied for August 17, 2026, and 16 townhouses at 3469 Baycrest Avenue, applied for August 19, 2026. Neither entry names a developer on the City list, so developer identity for both is unconfirmed here. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/193/Current-Multiple-Family-Commercial-Industrial-and-Institutional-Building-Permit-Applications-PDF

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The City expects 90,000 new residents over the next 25 years, as stated on its How Development Happens page — the demand backdrop behind the City Centre and Burquitlam tower pipeline a condo buyer is purchasing beside. https://www.coquitlam.ca/320/How-Development-Happens
- A rezoning of 374 properties in the Southwest Shoulder and Corridor sub-areas, from R-3 Transitional Small-Scale Residential to R-1 Small-Scale Residential under Zoning Amendment Bylaw No. 5522, 2026, was before Council on July 6, 2026 — a live zoning change beside existing Southwest Coquitlam condos. https://letstalkcoquitlam.ca/TransitOrientedAreas
- The City's 2026–2030 Financial Plan estimates total municipal taxes and utilities for an average residential home at about $4,558, and limits 2026 utility increases to 2% for water, 4% for sewer and drainage and 1% for solid waste. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17424/2026_Financial_Plan_Public_Book
- Coquitlam's 2026 budget raised property tax by $93 a year for the average household (3.37%), and by $137 a year with utilities included, as stated in the City's December 9, 2025 budget release. https://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/1849
- To find the land-use designation of a specific condo-adjacent property, the City directs residents to its QtheMap viewer or to DevInfo@coquitlam.ca, as stated on the Transit-Oriented Areas project page. https://letstalkcoquitlam.ca/TransitOrientedAreas

- 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026 for January 1 to December 31, 2026, with a 5% penalty after July 2, 2026 and a further 5% after September 15, 2026. https://coquitlam.ca/544/Property-Taxes
- The 2026–2030 Financial Plan limits 2026 utility increases to 2% for water, 4% for sewer and drainage and 1% for solid waste, estimates total municipal taxes and utilities for an average residential home at about $4,558, and utility bills were due March 31, 2026. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17424/2026_Financial_Plan_Public_Book

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Coquitlam Central Station is the city's rail hub, served by the Millennium Line Evergreen Extension and the West Coast Express, and is the terminus named on the TransLink timetables for routes 153, 185, 186 and 187 that feed the condo corridors. http://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/192/tt185.pdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check flood siting under Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996 section 519 and Schedule G, the designated 200-year floodplain of the Brunette, Coquitlam, Fraser and Pitt rivers, for any low-lying condo near those river corridors. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/1170/Part-5-General-Regulations-PDF
- Check the City's floodplain mapping report for the Coquitlam, Fraser and Pitt rivers before buying near a river edge; the report is the City's mapping work, not a lot-level flood determination for a specific strata lot. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/182/Coquitlam-Fraser-and-Pitt-Rivers-Floodplain-Mapping-Report-PDF
- Check slope, watercourse and wildfire-interface layers on the Schedule B Environmental Development Permit Areas map, which maps unstable slopes and the Interface Wildfire Risk Management boundary for the City Centre and Northeast areas. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/1527/Schedule-B---Environmental-Development-Permit-Areas-PDF?bidId=
- Check Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) status per parcel on Agricultural Land Commission mapping for any land or new-build purchase at the city edge; no ALR clearance for a specific parcel is given here. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca
