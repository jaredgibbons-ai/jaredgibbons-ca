## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Jacobsen was permitted as 65 townhouse units, per the City's March 12, 2012 planning report for the site. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7910-0247-00.pdf
- The 2012 proposal required an Official Community Plan (OCP) amendment from Suburban to Multiple Residential, plus rezoning and a development permit, before the townhouses could proceed. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7910-0247-00.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Jacobsen's zone change was from A-2 and RA (Acreage Residential) to RM-30, the townhouse residential zone the project was built under — not a site-specific Comprehensive Development zone like Greenway's. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7910-0247-00.pdf
- As a strata complex with five or more lots, Jacobsen's strata must obtain a depreciation report on a five-year cycle and can no longer defer it by an annual vote. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- A development variance permit formed part of the 2012 approval package, so the built form includes varied items — confirm any alteration against the permit and the strata bylaws, not the standard RM-30 text alone. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7910-0247-00.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Who built Jacobsen? **A:** The City file names owners, not a brand-name developer. The March 12, 2012 planning report for 2687 158 Street and part of 2660 Croydon Drive lists Croydon Ventures Inc. and Norma Jean Svab as owners, with the project proceeding as a 65-unit townhouse rezoning — so a buyer who wants a builder name should ask for it in the disclosure and strata records rather than rely on marketing material. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7910-0247-00.pdf
- **Q:** How is Jacobsen different from Greenway, one street over? **A:** The approvals are different in kind. Jacobsen was rezoned to the standard RM-30 townhouse zone at a plan density of 20 to 30 units per acre for 65 homes, while Greenway was rezoned to a site-specific CD zone at 15 to 25 units per acre for 67 homes — the two complexes follow different bylaws even though they share the Grandview bench. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7910-0247-00.pdf
- **Q:** Was Jacobsen always planned as townhouses? **A:** No — the land started as suburban acreage. The 2012 application had to amend the OCP designation from Suburban to Multiple Residential and rezone from A-2 and RA before any townhouse could be built, which is why the complex sits among later Grandview subdivisions rather than in an older townhouse cluster. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7910-0247-00.pdf
- **Q:** Which schools serve 2687 158 Street? **A:** Catchments in School District 36 (SD36) are address-based, so run the exact address in the district locator before relying on a school. Grandview Heights Secondary at 16987 25 Avenue serves the wider Grandview Heights area; the elementary catchment for this specific Croydon Drive edge address must be confirmed for the unit being bought, not assumed from the neighbourhood name. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/036
