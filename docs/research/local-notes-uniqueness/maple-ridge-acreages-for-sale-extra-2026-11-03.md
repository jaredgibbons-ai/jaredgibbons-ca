## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Maple Ridge Zoning Bylaw No. 7600-2019 has agricultural zones A-1 through A-5: the City's own Shipping Container Guide (revised August 14, 2026) lists agricultural zones A-1 thru A-5 and names RS-1, RS-2 and RS-3 as rural residential zones that can also permit agriculture. That City guide settles the zone names, which the earlier acreages program could not confirm. https://www.mapleridge.ca/sites/default/files/2026-08/Shipping-Container-Guide_0.pdf
- The City's compliance checklist covers only A1, A2 and A3 for one-family residential use, and records for each property whether it is in the ALR, in a flood plain, its minimum basement elevation and any geotechnical flag. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/a1-a2-a3-agr-zoning-checklist202103081624103844
- Acreage here is whole fee-simple parcels, in and out of the ALR; no new named acreage subdivision or bare-land strata farm community was verified, matching the earlier acreages program. Rural homes not on community sewer use an onsite system under the Sewerage System Regulation, per Fraser Health. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/health-topics-a-to-z/onsite-sewerage-systems/regulatory-requirements-for-onsite-sewerage-systems

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- ALR rules: subdivision needs Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) approval, an ALR residence cannot be stratified, a principal residence built without an ALC application is capped at 500 square metres (m²) with no more than 1,000 m² of fill, a suite is allowed only inside it, and one additional residence is capped at 90 m² (40 ha or less) or 186 m² (over 40 ha), with City bylaws also required to permit it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- Floodplain Management Bylaw No. 8121-2026 now designates floodplain across the city, including any land less than 1.5 m above the natural boundary of an adjacent watercourse, lake, pond, swamp, marsh or reservoir, and land inside the bylaw's floodplain setbacks. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/maple-ridge-floodplain-management-bylaw-no-8121-2026pdf
- Tree clearing for farming is under the Tree Protection and Management Bylaw No. 8045-2026: removing fewer than ten protected trees needs no permit where the land is in active agricultural use with active farm status, and that exemption does not apply to construction. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/bulletin-trees-removals-agriculturepdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Which Maple Ridge acreage pockets are not the named rural pages? **A:** Albion, Whonnock, Ruskin and the northwest lowlands have no separate acreage page in this program, so this city-wide note covers them. They differ mainly by ground: Whonnock and Ruskin are Dewdney Trunk Road bench and creek country in the east, Albion is the Kanaka Creek and ferry-corridor side, and the northwest is flat river lowland where the floodplain bylaw matters most. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/maple-ridge-floodplain-management-bylaw-no-8121-2026pdf

**Q:** Can I clear trees to put an acreage back into farming? **A:** Partly as of right. Under Bylaw No. 8045-2026, land in active agricultural use with active farm status can remove fewer than ten protected trees without a permit. Removing ten or more needs a permit and a Tree Management Plan, and land near a riparian or ecologically sensitive area needs a Qualified Environmental Professional to set the farming setbacks first. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/bulletin-trees-removals-agriculturepdf

**Q:** Does the new floodplain bylaw change an existing farmhouse? **A:** The bylaw's work is forward-looking: it designates floodplain, sets flood construction levels and setbacks for new construction, regulates fill in flood-prone areas and creates a site-specific exemption process. The City adopted it July 28, 2026 for risks from the Fraser River, the Alouette River system, steep creeks and groundwater flooding. Ask the City how it treats your planned addition before designing it. https://www.mapleridge.ca/news/city-advances-climate-action-flood-resilience-environmental-protection-and-mckenney-creek

**Q:** Can I irrigate a Maple Ridge acreage from its well? **A:** Only with a licence if the use is non-domestic. Under the Water Sustainability Act, groundwater used for irrigation needs a water licence and payment of water fees and rentals before lawful use; domestic groundwater use needs no authorization. A well on the property is therefore not, by itself, an irrigation right, and the licence record is part of the purchase diligence. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-rights

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The City's A1 to A3 checklist sets agricultural buildings back 30 m from the front lot line and 15 m from rear and side lines, and mushroom buildings 60 m from the front line, distances an urban buyer does not expect. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/a1-a2-a3-agr-zoning-checklist202103081624103844
- The same checklist caps all agricultural buildings and structures at 20% lot coverage and greenhouse structures at 50%, with agricultural building height at 15 m. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/a1-a2-a3-agr-zoning-checklist202103081624103844
- A shipping container is allowed on an A-1 to A-5 lot only as an accessory to an agricultural use, and on an RS-1, RS-2 or RS-3 lot only where the lot is classified Farm under the Assessment Act and the container supports a farm operation. https://www.mapleridge.ca/sites/default/files/2026-08/Shipping-Container-Guide_0.pdf
- Clearing more than 500 m² over a vulnerable aquifer while removing ten or more protected trees triggers a Groundwater Impact Assessment in the farm tree plan. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/bulletin-trees-removals-agriculturepdf
- Farm class is income-tested in three tiers ($10,000 under 0.8 ha; $2,500 at 0.8 to 4 ha; $2,500 plus 5% of value over 4 ha), with applications by October 31, so a small bench acreage carries the strictest per-hectare test. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification
Read: 2026-11-03

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Look the parcel up first in Bylaw No. 8121-2026 Schedule A and its setback rules: the bylaw designates floodplain by hazard area and by the 1.5 m natural-boundary test, and its map does not state a parcel's ALR status or farm-class record. https://www.mapleridge.ca/media/file/maple-ridge-floodplain-management-bylaw-no-8121-2026pdf
- Check ALR status separately on the ALC maps by parcel identifier (PID) or civic address; official confirmation comes from the ALC Mapping team, and the ALC map does not state a flood construction level. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- On bench and upland parcels, add slope and watercourse checks: the City's Hazards Framework names slope instability and wildfire alongside flooding for Maple Ridge's upland and forested ground. https://www.mapleridge.ca/news/city-advances-hazards-framework-strengthen-climate-resilience-and-public-safety
