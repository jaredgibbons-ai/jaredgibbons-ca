## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- The District of West Vancouver stretches along 28 kilometres of shoreline and up the slopes of Hollyburn Ridge. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
- The District was incorporated on March 15, 1912, taking jurisdiction over what had been part of the District of North Vancouver. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
- Ambleside is the Municipal Town Centre; the other local commercial centres are Dundarave, Caulfeild and Horseshoe Bay, plus the Park Royal regional shopping centre. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
- Marine Drive is the east-west waterfront road linking Ambleside, Dundarave and Horseshoe Bay; Highway 1 (Trans-Canada Highway) runs on the upper bench above it. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The first Official Community Plan (OCP), under the Town Planning Act of 1926, banned new industry and called for building lots larger than elsewhere in the Lower Mainland. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
- Ambleside was an early subdivision built on a gridiron plan with a commercial strip along Marine Drive. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
- In 1959, 20 hectares were rezoned permitting dozens of high-density apartment buildings in Ambleside and Hollyburn, which is why apartments concentrate there. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
- Altamont, built as car ownership spread after 1945, has large lots, mature trees and narrow roads giving homes the feel of country estates. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
- Caulfeild was laid out as an English-style village following the contours of nature, including paths of animals. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** What is the difference between Ambleside and Dundarave? **A:** Ambleside is West Vancouver's Municipal Town Centre, with the gridiron subdivision, Marine Drive commercial strip and the 1959 apartment zone in Ambleside and Hollyburn. Dundarave is a smaller local commercial centre west on Marine Drive, named for the Scottish castle that was home to the clan of early resident R.E. Macnaghten. Both are served by local area planning in the OCP. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
**Q:** Why are West Vancouver lots larger than in Vancouver? **A:** The size is a deliberate 1926 planning choice. The District's history states the first OCP under the Town Planning Act of 1926 banned any new industry and called for building lots larger than elsewhere in the Lower Mainland, a pattern later neighbourhoods such as Altamont kept with large lots and mature trees. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
**Q:** Is Horseshoe Bay part of West Vancouver? **A:** Yes, Horseshoe Bay is a local commercial centre inside the District, at its western end. Before 1931, when Dan Sewell opened a marina and Whytecliff Lodge there, only a few families lived there year-round. Today it holds the BC Ferries terminal for Departure Bay (Nanaimo), Langdale (Sunshine Coast) and Bowen Island routes. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
**Q:** Does West Vancouver have SkyTrain or rapid transit? **A:** No, West Vancouver has no SkyTrain. Its transit is bus service on Marine Drive and Highway 1, plus the Horseshoe Bay ferry terminal. The OCP maps a Marine Drive Transit Corridor, and the Province's 2025 directive requires a Park Royal-Taylor Way local area plan enabling housing aligned with transit-oriented development best practices, which is a planning direction, not a rapid transit line. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025HMA0062-000698

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Dundarave's name and Caulfeild's unusual spelling both come from early residents: Caulfeild is named for Francis William Caulfeild, with that spelling. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
- John Lawson Park in Ambleside is named for one of West Vancouver's founders. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
- Property tax notices are mailed in May and due the first business day in July; the District states this year's payment is due Thursday, July 2, 2026. https://westvancouver.ca/services/taxes-utility-fees
- Metered utility statements are mailed four times a year for water, sewer (sanitary and storm) and curbside recycling, yard trimmings and garbage. https://westvancouver.ca/services/taxes-utility-fees
- West Vancouver water is metered, so water and sewer charges depend on how much water a home uses. https://westvancouver.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/2025%20Utilities%20Insert%20-%20digital.pdf
- A separate North Shore Wastewater Treatment Plant levy appears on the property tax bill from 2025, about $177 for a single-family home assessed at $3.66 million. https://westvancouver.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/2025%20Utilities%20Insert%20-%20digital.pdf

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- District property taxes pay for parks and streets, library, police and fire services, per the District. https://westvancouver.ca/services/taxes-utility-fees
- Utility fees are billed quarterly for water, sewer and curbside pickup; the District's 2025 insert states projected water funding needs rose 4.5 percent and sewer and storm drainage needs rose 9.6 percent in 2025. https://westvancouver.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/2025%20Utilities%20Insert%20-%20digital.pdf
- The District of West Vancouver is a designated taxable area for the provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Lions Gate Bridge connects West Vancouver to Vancouver via Park Royal and Taylor Way; the Spirit Trail greenway is planned as a continuous flat connection between Lions Gate Bridge and Horseshoe Bay. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
- Horseshoe Bay terminal serves BC Ferries routes to Departure Bay, Langdale and Bowen Island — confirm sailings on the BC Ferries schedule pages before travel. https://www.bcferries.com/schedules/mainland/hbna-current.php
- Highway 1 westbound ends at Horseshoe Bay, so ferry traffic and local traffic share that corridor.

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- The OCP schedules map natural environmental resources, steep Upper Lands and creek areas; check a lot against OCP Maps 13 (Natural Environmental Resources) and 8 (Upper Lands Planning Framework). https://westvancouver.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/OCP%20Bylaw%204985%2C%202018%20-%20Maps_revisedFeb2022.pdf
- Homes up Hollyburn Ridge sit on slopes above Highway 1, so slope and drainage review is part of due diligence for upper lots — the District history describes development up those slopes. https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-vancouver-yesterday-and-today
