# Guildford Guide — Extra Local Notes
Page: guildford-guide (Guildford, Surrey; Guide).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Guildford is Surrey's northeast community: the Fraser River borders its northern edge, Whalley is to the west, Langley is to the east, and the southern border stretches from 96 Avenue to 84 Avenue. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- The community has two distinct parts: the mature town-centre neighbourhoods around 152 Street and 104 Avenue, and Fraser Heights, the newer subdivision in the north that slopes down toward the Fraser River. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- "Guildford" is both a City of Surrey community name and a Fraser Valley board sub-area name, so the City community page and board statistics describe the same ground here — unlike City Centre, which the board files under Whalley. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Development in Guildford has predominantly centred on 152 Street and 104 Avenue around Guildford Town Centre Mall, so the attached stock clusters at the town centre and detached subdivisions fill the blocks behind it. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Fraser Heights, in the community's north, is the newer subdivision stock, on streets sloping toward the Fraser River with views of the Port Mann Bridge along the northern edge. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- The 104 Avenue corridor west of the mall is where the form is changing: council files show low-rise apartment and mixed-use rezonings replacing single-family lots along the corridor, under the Guildford Plan. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/council-minutes/regular-council-land-use-minutes-january-26-2026

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The Guildford Plan is the plan that governs what can be built here: approved by Council on October 30, 2023, it sets the land use designations for Guildford Town Centre and the 104 Avenue corridor (check current). https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/GuildfordPlanFinal.pdf
- The 104 Avenue corridor sits inside a Frequent Transit Development Area (FTDA) between City Centre and Guildford Town Centre, which is the designation the City's planning reports use when supporting higher-density apartment forms on corridor lots. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0425-00_1.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** What is the Guildford Plan and does it affect my street? **A:** Yes, if you are near the town centre or 104 Avenue. The Guildford Plan, approved by Council on October 30, 2023, sets the land use designations for Guildford Town Centre and the 104 Avenue corridor, and development applications on corridor lots are being processed against it. A detached lot inside the plan area can carry an apartment or mixed-use designation even though the house on it today is single-family. Check the parcel in COSMOS. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/GuildfordPlanFinal.pdf
- **Q:** Does Guildford have SkyTrain? **A:** No — Guildford's rapid transit today is the R1 King George Blvd RapidBus, which terminates at Guildford Exchange and runs via Surrey Central Station and King George Station to Newton Exchange. The Surrey Langley SkyTrain runs along Fraser Highway south of Guildford, not through the town centre, with an anticipated in-service date of 2029. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/214/ttR1.pdf
- **Q:** Which secondary schools are in Guildford? **A:** Two School District 36 secondaries sit in the community: Johnston Heights Secondary at 15350 99 Ave and Guildford Park Secondary at 10707 146 St. Bonaccord Elementary is at 14986 98 Ave. Catchments are address-based in SD36, so confirm the exact address in the district school locator rather than assuming the nearest school. (BC Ministry K-12 schools file, 2025/2026)
- **Q:** How does Guildford connect to the rest of the region by road? **A:** By Highway 1 and the Port Mann Bridge on its northern edge. The City's Guildford page notes residents' access to the highway and to Surrey City Centre, and the community's northern streets look directly onto the Port Mann Bridge and the Fraser River — the bridge crossing is part of daily life for the Fraser Heights side. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Guildford Town Centre is described by the City as the region's largest shopping centre, and the community grew up around it — the mall at 152 Street and 104 Avenue is the anchor the rest of the town centre is measured from. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- The Guildford Recreation Centre and Guildford Aquatic Centre sit in the town centre cluster by the mall, along with the Guildford branch library — recreation, pool and library are co-located with the retail core. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Guildford began as part of the Township of Port Kells, established by Irish pioneers and connected by rail to New Westminster in 1891; the Port Kells name survives in the community's northwest corner, with Port Kells Park, the Port Kells Outdoor Pool and the Port Kells Library branch. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- The opening of the Port Mann Bridge in 1964 is what brought Guildford's main wave of development, per the City's community history. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Surrey's Biofuel Facility — the closed-loop organics plant that processes the city's curbside organics into renewable natural gas and compost — is a Guildford-area City facility. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Tynehead Regional Park sits on Guildford's eastern side, and its overpass crosses Highway 1 at the community's edge. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Guildford pays the city-wide Surrey rate: the 2026 total residential (Class 1) rate is $3.42731 per $1,000 of assessed value, with the full schedule in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5% penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5% penalty after September 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies: the Province lists the City of Surrey as a designated taxable area, so Guildford owners file the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Guildford Exchange is the eastern terminus of the R1 King George Blvd RapidBus, which runs via 104 Avenue, City Parkway (Surrey Central Station) and King George Boulevard (King George Station) to Newton Exchange. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/214/ttR1.pdf
- Highway 1 runs along Guildford's northern edge and the Port Mann Bridge crosses the Fraser River from it — the community's regional road link is the bridge, per the City's community page. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- There is no SkyTrain station in Guildford and none is planned through the town centre: the Surrey Langley SkyTrain follows Fraser Highway to the south, with an anticipated in-service date of 2029. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/rapid-transit-projects/surrey-langley-skytrain

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- The Fraser Heights slope is the local check: the subdivision slopes down toward the Fraser River, so a north Guildford lot should be checked against the City's slope and floodplain mapping for its exact elevation and any dike or flood construction level that applies. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Surrey's mapped floodplain lowlands are the Nicomekl and Serpentine coastal floodplain and the Little Campbell River area, not Guildford's upland town-centre blocks — but the riverfront edge is parcel-specific, so confirm in the City's floodplain pages and COSMOS rather than by neighbourhood name. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- Corridor lots carry a plan risk in both directions: a 104 Avenue lot's Guildford Plan designation may allow far more than the house on it, or the plan's servicing stage may hold a project back — read the designation and the application's status, not the listing's "potential" line. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/GuildfordPlanFinal.pdf
