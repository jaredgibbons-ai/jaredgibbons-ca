# Farms — Extra Local Notes
Page: farms (British Columbia; farms, all types).
## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- This page covers farm purchases province-wide in British Columbia (BC), under one provincial purchase framework. BC Assessment, the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) and the Province's water licensing system apply in every region, while zoning and tax rates change by municipality — the sections below cover only the province-wide pieces no earlier farm page owns.
- All water in BC is owned by the Crown, which is why water is a purchase item, not an assumption. The Province states a water right is the authorized use of surface water or groundwater, and an owner with access to either must in most cases apply for the right to use it and pay an annual rental fee. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-rights

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The farm stock for tax purposes splits land from buildings. BC Assessment states land can be Class 9, Farm, where it produces a prescribed amount of qualifying primary agricultural products for sale, while farm buildings come within Class 1, Residential. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/understanding-property-classes-and-exemptions?TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=582130f6-038f-4842-ada7-0166ce03a246
- Land that contributes to production can qualify, not just cropped land. BC Assessment lists as qualifying: land used for a qualifying agricultural use, land contributing to one (for example irrigation, access to farm outbuildings, shelter belts), land for a farmer's dwelling, Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) land for a retired farmer's dwelling, and horse training and boarding land run with horse rearing. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- A single property can carry split classification. BC Assessment states a property may be split into two or more classes where it has multiple uses, with only the portion in production eligible for farm classification — including where part is leased to a farmer. http://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/classifying-land-leased-to-farmers

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Farm classification (Class 9) is voluntary and application-based, with a hard deadline. BC Assessment states owners must apply using the General Application for Farm Classification (or the Retired Farmer's Dwelling Land Application), applications and any lease must be submitted by October 31 for the following taxation year, and late new applications are not accepted. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- The income test has three tiers, by operation size. A producing farm must show gross income of $2,500 where the operation is 0.8 hectares (1.98 acres) to 4 hectares (10 acres); $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the area over 4 hectares where it is larger; and $10,000 where the operation is under 0.8 hectares. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- An additional residence for farm use is allowed without an ALC application only within set sizes. The ALR Use Regulation, section 34.3, permits an additional residence where the parcel then holds one residence of 500 square metres or less plus one of 90 square metres or less on parcels of 40 hectares or less, or one of 186 square metres or less on parcels over 40 hectares. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019?bcgovtm=CSMLS
- The ALC states the same frame in plain terms for buyers. Land in the ALR may generally have no more than one residence per parcel unless it qualifies for an additional residence, a principal residence must be 500 square metres or less with no more than 1,000 square metres of fill placed for its construction, a secondary suite is permitted only inside the principal residence, and local governments may be more restrictive but not more permissive. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- Non-domestic groundwater use has required a licence since 2016. The Province states anyone diverting or using groundwater for non-domestic purposes such as irrigation, commercial or industrial use must apply for and obtain a water licence and pay water fees and rentals before the water can be lawfully used, a change in force February 29, 2016 under the Water Sustainability Act. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-rights
- Irrigation specifically needs a licence for agricultural use. The Ministry of Agriculture and Food's irrigation factsheet states the Water Sustainability Act requires a water licence to divert, store or use water from a surface or groundwater source for non-domestic purposes including agricultural use, and that work in and about streams may also require authorization. http://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/agricultural-land-and-environment/strengthening-farming/farm-practices/2026/irrigation_2026.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** I bought a farm that already has farm classification — do I need to do anything? **A:** Yes, you must re-apply in your own name by October 31. BC Assessment states that after purchasing a farm-classified property, a buyer who plans to continue farming must submit a General Application for Farm Classification by October 31, and a buyer who will not continue farming should notify BC Assessment of the plan to cease agricultural activity. Missing the deadline loses the classification for the next tax year. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- **Q:** How much does my small farm actually have to sell to keep farm status? **A:** It depends on the size of the whole operation, in three tiers. An operation under 0.8 hectares must show $10,000 in gross farm income — the highest bar — while one between 0.8 and 4 hectares must show $2,500, and a larger one $2,500 plus 5% of the actual value of the land over 4 hectares. Keep receipts and sales logs, as BC Assessment may require proof. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- **Q:** Can I add a second home for farm help on ALR land without an ALC application? **A:** Yes, within the regulation's size limits, if local zoning also allows it. The ALR Use Regulation permits one additional residence of up to 90 square metres on a parcel of 40 hectares or less, or up to 186 square metres above 40 hectares, alongside a principal residence of 500 square metres or less. Anything larger, or more residences, requires ALC approval. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/30_2019?bcgovtm=CSMLS
- **Q:** Does the farm's well or irrigation water transfer automatically with the land? **A:** No, lawful non-domestic water use needs its own licence. The Province states the right to divert and use surface or groundwater is authorized by a licence or approval under the Water Sustainability Act, and non-domestic groundwater use — irrigation is the stated example — requires a licence and payment of fees and rentals. Confirm the licences on the specific farm and their transfer position before completion. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-rights
- **Q:** What federal financing exists besides a bank mortgage? **A:** Two federal routes are documented. Farm Credit Canada (FCC) offers a Young Farmer Loan of up to $2 million for qualified producers under 40, with no loan processing fees and up to 18 months to purchase agriculture-related assets, https://www.fcc-fac.ca/en/financing/agriculture/young-farmers and the Canadian Agricultural Loans Act (CALA) program guarantees 95% of a lender's loss on eligible loans, with limits of $500,000 per farm operation and $3 million for agricultural co-operatives. https://agriculture.canada.ca/en/node/685

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Losing farm classification changes the land's value on the roll, not just its label. BC Assessment states it will remove a property from farm classification where requirements stop being met, apply the appropriate other classification, and the land valuation will change when it is removed. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- Leased farm land needs a filed, current lease to stay classified. BC Assessment requires a copy of the current lease with the assessor, a new lease on expiry, resubmission every seven years, and states the owner bears the onus of keeping a current lease filed; rent paid is not qualifying farm income. http://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/classifying-land-leased-to-farmers
- Proof of income must be kept for years, not just the application year. BC Assessment recommends keeping receipts, sales logs or other proof of farm income for at least three years, and states it may ask for information to support continued classification at any review. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- Domestic groundwater is treated differently from farm groundwater. The Province states groundwater for domestic purposes, including basic household needs and domestic animals and livestock, does not require authorization — but irrigation and other non-domestic use does, so the same well can sit on both sides of the line. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-rights
- During drought, licensed rights can be temporarily suspended. The Province states that during a water shortage or drought, some water rights may be temporarily suspended or altered to provide for essential needs and healthy aquatic ecosystems and fish habitat. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-rights
- A developing farm has its own application path. BC Assessment states a developing-farm applicant must show sufficient detail to confirm the land will meet income requirements at the proposed harvest date, using the same General Application and the same October 31 deadline. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- FCC bundles farm software with the Young Farmer Loan. FCC states the loan includes one year of AgExpert Accounting Premium and AgExpert Field Premium, and preferential variable and five-year fixed rates are available. https://www.fcc-fac.ca/en/financing/agriculture/young-farmers

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Farm building relief is the main quantified tax item at province level. BC Assessment states that in municipalities, farm buildings other than a farmer's dwelling are exempt at $50,000 or 87.5% of assessed value, whichever is greater (a $500,000 building is taxed on $62,500), and in rural taxation areas farm buildings are entirely exempt from general provincial tax, with the school-tax exemption capped the same way. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- Water use carries an annual provincial rental. The Province states most owners must apply for the right to use surface or groundwater and pay an annual rental fee for that use; no rental rate is stated in the page read, so no figure is given here. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-rights
- CALA financing carries a registration fee structure set in the federal program, but the fee percentage was not confirmed on the Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada page read for this note, so no figure is stated — see NOT CONFIRMED.

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Farm freight in the Fraser Valley runs on Highway 1, which is being widened in phases. The Province states the current 216th to 264th Street phase is under construction with new high-occupancy and electric vehicle lanes, and a new Glover Road crossing opened in spring 2024 with 3-metre multi-use paths in both directions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation-projects/fraser-valley-highway1/highway1-216th-264th-widening
- FCC is a lender, not a transport service, and is noted here only because its agriculture financing page is the federal entry point most farm buyers use alongside a bank — it lends to farmers, ranchers, growers and other primary food producers across Canada. https://www.fcc-fac.ca/en/financing/agriculture

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Verify farm classification can survive the change of owner before pricing the tax benefit in. BC Assessment must confirm a farm continues to meet income thresholds, may demand supporting information, and states failure to provide it results in removal of farm classification. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- Search the water rights databases for the farm's licences before completion. The Province provides a water rights database search from its licensing page, and licences on sensitive streams may carry strict terms and conditions to protect fish populations and habitat. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-rights
- Check any dugout or storage works for licensing. The Ministry's irrigation factsheet states authorization is required if any water in a dugout comes from a stream or aquifer including groundwater seepage, while a dugout filling entirely from precipitation or runoff generally needs none. http://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/agricultural-land-and-environment/strengthening-farming/farm-practices/2026/irrigation_2026.pdf
- Confirm residence plans against both the ALC frame and local zoning. The ALC states local or First Nation governments may apply more restrictive total floor area rules and cannot be more permissive than the ALC's requirements, so an ALC-compliant plan can still fail locally. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
