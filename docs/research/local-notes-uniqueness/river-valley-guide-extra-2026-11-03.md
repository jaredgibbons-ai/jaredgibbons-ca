# Chilliwack River Valley Guide — Extra Local Notes
Page: river-valley-guide (Chilliwack River Valley; area guide).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- The Chilliwack River Valley is not in the City of Chilliwack: it is Fraser Valley Regional District (FVRD) Electoral Area E, the settled rural valley along Chilliwack Lake Road on both sides of the Chilliwack River, east and southeast of the city. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
- The FVRD describes Electoral Area E as "nestled in the North Cascade mountains" and known as the Chilliwack River Valley, with a population of 1,568 (2021 Census) across 652 square kilometres. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
- Chilliwack Lake Road is the valley's only spine: addresses in the valley are on Chilliwack Lake Road, for example Thompson Regional Park at 48600 Chilliwack Lake Road. http://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/parks-recreation/parks-trails/thompson.html
- The named communities in the valley, from the FVRD's slope assessment, are Baker Trails, Bell Acres, Slesse Park and Post Creek, spread along the slopes on both sides of the river from Baker Trails in the east to the Slesse Park landslide in the west. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/crvssra
- No official document publishes street-by-street boundaries for the "Chilliwack River Valley" as a neighbourhood; the Electoral Area E boundary is the governing line. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The valley's stock is rural and detached: the FVRD's Housing Needs Report for Electoral Areas E and H reports most housing units are single detached (78%) and movable dwellings make up 21% of dwelling units, figures that cover Electoral Areas E and H combined, not the valley alone. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Document~Library/EA%20Housing%20Needs%20Report.pdf
- The same report records that the only permitted residential use under the Official Community Plan's designations for these areas is single-family, so there is no strata or multi-family product planned into the valley's plan frame. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Document~Library/EA%20Housing%20Needs%20Report.pdf
- Servicing follows the settlement pattern, not a city grid: the FVRD's 2026 Area E utility rates list exactly one water service area (Bell Acres) and one sewer service area (Baker Trails) in the whole electoral area, so most addresses are outside both systems and service is a property-by-property question. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Services/Documents/Utility~Billing/Electoral%20Area%20E%20-%20Residential%20Rates%202026.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The governing plan is the FVRD's, not the City's: the Official Community Plan for Electoral Areas E and H, Bylaw No. 1115, 2011, was adopted by the FVRD Board on August 13, 2013, and Amendment Bylaw No. 1533, 2019 renamed the plan for Electoral Areas E and H after the former Area E was split in 2014. https://pub-fvrd.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11249
- River erosion has its own legal line here: an OCP amendment adopted in 2021 implemented an Erosion Setback Line in the Wilson Road to Baker Trails area and the Slesse Park area, based on two reports on the Chilliwack River. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Home/First%20Quarter%20Update%202021.pdf
- The FVRD judges new building in hazard areas against its Hazard Acceptability Thresholds for Development Approvals, which include a specific table for Chilliwack River Valley erosion or avulsion measured against the erosion setback line and the 100-year erosion limit line. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Services/Documents/Planning~and~Development/Application~Forms~and~Resources/2020%20Hazard%20Acceptability%20Thresholds.pdf
- Where the erosion or avulsion threat is immediate and extreme, the thresholds state a building permit may not be available until approved bank protection is provided. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Services/Documents/Planning~and~Development/Application~Forms~and~Resources/2020%20Hazard%20Acceptability%20Thresholds.pdf
- City of Chilliwack bylaws — its zoning bylaw, suite rules and small-scale multi-unit position, written in full in the chilliwack-houses-for-sale note — do not govern here; the FVRD is the local government for Electoral Area E and provides building inspection, planning and development approvals for the electoral areas. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Which schools serve a Chilliwack Lake Road address? **A:** No School District 33 (SD33) public school is located in the valley: the BC Ministry of Education's list of SD33 schools shows no school with a Chilliwack Lake Road or valley address, with district schools instead in Chilliwack, Sardis, Cultus Lake, Yarrow, Greendale and Rosedale. Catchments are address-based and were not confirmed school-by-school for valley addresses, so the exact address has to be confirmed with SD33 before a buyer relies on any school claim. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/033
- **Q:** Is there bus service in the valley? **A:** No fixed-route bus runs into the valley: BC Transit's Chilliwack and FVRD Transit Future Action Plan states the Chilliwack Conventional Transit System provides fixed-route, fixed-schedule service within the City of Chilliwack, and none of its nine listed routes enters the Chilliwack River Valley. The one exception is accessible transit — BC Transit's Chilliwack Rider's Guide states residents of Chilliwack River Valley and Cultus Lake North may be eligible for handyDART, its door-to-door service for people who cannot use fixed-route transit without assistance. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024-05-21-Chilliwack-and-FVRD-TFAP_final.pdf https://lite.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/chw_rg_june2026-1.pdf
- **Q:** Will my phone work at the property? **A:** Not at the upper end of the valley: BC Parks states there is no cell phone reception past the On The Way store on Chilliwack Lake Road, in Sx̱ótsaqel/Chilliwack Lake Provincial Park. That dead zone is a practical fact for showings, inspections and emergency calls for properties toward the lake end of the road, and it is one reason the FVRD pushes residents to sign up for Alertable emergency notifications. https://bcparks.ca/sxotsaqel-chilliwack-lake-park/ https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/emergency-management/emergency-notifications.html
- **Q:** Is the property on water and sewer, or on a well and septic? **A:** It depends on which named settlement the property is in, and most addresses are on neither FVRD system: the FVRD's 2026 Electoral Area E rates list one water service area, Bell Acres (metered), and one sewer service area, Baker Trails (flat annual charge). A property outside those two service areas is not on FVRD water or sewer, and its well and septic arrangements are a property-file question — the rates page does not describe private systems. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Services/Documents/Utility~Billing/Electoral%20Area%20E%20-%20Residential%20Rates%202026.pdf
- **Q:** What is the slope risk above the valley floor? **A:** It is an active, funded study area: the FVRD is running a Chilliwack River Valley Slope Stability Risk Assessment with BGC Engineering, fully funded by the BC Ministry of Emergency Management and Climate Readiness, covering landslides, debris flows, rotational slumps, rockfall and rockslides on the slopes on both sides of the valley. A buyer should ask whether the specific property falls inside the assessment area and what the mapping shows for its slope. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/crvssra

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Thompson Regional Park at 48600 Chilliwack Lake Road is the valley's community park: a 3-hectare FVRD park donated by the Chilliwack River Valley Ratepayers Association, with a covered picnic shelter, a playground, an easy 1-kilometre loop trail along a salmon enhancement channel, flush toilets and parking, open 7 a.m. to sunset. http://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/parks-recreation/parks-trails/thompson.html
- The valley's upper end is a provincial park, not a townsite: Sx̱ótsaqel/Chilliwack Lake Provincial Park has 182 frontcountry campsites, 40 kilometres of trails and backcountry camps at Greendrop, Lindeman, Flora and Radium Lakes, and its gates are locked between 11 p.m. and 7 a.m. daily. https://bcparks.ca/sxotsaqel-chilliwack-lake-park/
- Chilliwack Lake has severe afternoon winds and dangerous currents at its outflow, on BC Parks' safety information — a fact that matters for any property pitched on lake or river recreation. https://bcparks.ca/sxotsaqel-chilliwack-lake-park/
- Chilliwack River Provincial Park, on the river along Chilliwack Lake Road, was established in 1961 and has floodplain characteristics, and BC Parks states the river supports steelhead and chum, coho and sockeye salmon. https://bcparks.ca/chilliwack-river-park/
- The FVRD's own service list for Area E is short and specific: Area E Community Parks, Fire Protection, Dyke Operations and Maintenance, Storm Drainage, Street Lighting, Transit and Water — a buyer should check which of these a specific property actually receives. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
- There is no library branch and no recreation centre in the valley: the nearest Fraser Valley Regional Library branch is Sardis Library at 5819 Tyson Road in Chilliwack. https://www.fvrl.bc.ca/locations/sardis-library
- SD33's Long Range Facilities Plan notes the district does not bus students from south to north or vice versa given the geography of some schools, especially at the periphery of the district — the valley is exactly that periphery case. https://www.sd33.bc.ca/sites/sd33.bc.ca/files/2022-03/SD33%20LRFP%202021-2025.pdf
- The valley's history runs through it: BC Parks records that the Hudson's Bay Company cut the first recorded trail up the Chilliwack River in 1855, improving the Indigenous trail that joined the nine Indigenous villages between Vedder Crossing and Chilliwack Lake. https://bcparks.ca/sxotsaqel-chilliwack-lake-park/

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- Chilliwack River Valley Slope Stability Risk Assessment — Fraser Valley Regional District with BGC Engineering, fully funded by the BC Ministry of Emergency Management and Climate Readiness: a technical assessment, mapping and modelling project for slope hazards from Baker Trails in the east to the Slesse Park landslide in the west, plus the Ranger Run area and the Post Creek subdivision, intended to assess the safety of existing lands, infrastructure, development and residents and to identify mitigation options, including under future climate conditions. STATUS: assessment underway, with a community survey that received 108 responses and a community workshop planned in the valley this winter; page last updated December 6, 2025, read 2026-11-03. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/crvssra
- Wilson Road Dike flood recovery repairs — Fraser Valley Regional District: flood recovery repairs along the Wilson Road Dike, with pedestrian access restricted during the work. STATUS: repairs scheduled August 17 to September 25, 2026, read 2026-11-03. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
- No subdivision, rezoning or building project inside the valley was found on the FVRD pages read on 2026-11-03; the active files here are the hazard assessment and dike works above, which is the normal pattern for an electoral area whose OCP permits single-family use only. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Government/Documents/Document~Library/EA%20Housing%20Needs%20Report.pdf

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- There is no City of Chilliwack property-tax rate here: the valley is unincorporated FVRD Electoral Area E, so there is no municipal rate per $1,000 to quote — the City rate in the sibling Chilliwack notes does not apply to a Chilliwack Lake Road address. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
- The FVRD's 2026 Electoral Area E residential utility rates are narrow and named: Water — Bell Acres, billed quarterly, metered, minimum $86.00 with usage over 50 cubic metres at $1.77 per cubic metre, and Baker Trails Sewer, a flat $1,021.78 billed annually. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Services/Documents/Utility~Billing/Electoral%20Area%20E%20-%20Residential%20Rates%202026.pdf
- FVRD utility bills are issued every three months in January, April, July and October, as user fees in addition to taxation, and the FVRD notes not all taxpayers in the same electoral area are included in the same service. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/utility-bills.html
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax does not list this area: the Province's taxable-areas list names the City of Chilliwack but does not name Fraser Valley Regional District Electoral Area E, so a valley property is not in a listed taxable area on the page read — confirm a specific property on the Province's interactive location map. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- There is no fixed-route transit in the valley: BC Transit's plan states fixed-route, fixed-schedule service runs within the City of Chilliwack, and the valley's only transit listing is the FVRD's Area E "Transit" service entry, which is the handyDART eligibility described in section 4. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024-05-21-Chilliwack-and-FVRD-TFAP_final.pdf https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
- Road access is the single road: Chilliwack Lake Road runs the length of the valley from the Vedder Crossing side to Chilliwack Lake, and every named community, park and address in the official sources read is on it. https://bcparks.ca/sxotsaqel-chilliwack-lake-park/
- The Trans Canada Trail winds through Sx̱ótsaqel/Chilliwack Lake Provincial Park at the upper end of the valley. https://bcparks.ca/sxotsaqel-chilliwack-lake-park/
- Drive times from a valley address to Vedder Crossing, Highway 1 or Chilliwack's services are UNCONFIRMED — no source read states a measured time, and times vary by how far up Chilliwack Lake Road the property sits.

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Slope hazards are the headline risk and they are named individually: the FVRD assessment covers landslides, debris flows, rotational slumps, rockfall and rockslides, and it expressly focuses on geohazards rather than flooding or wildfire, so a buyer needs separate checks for those. https://haveyoursay.fvrd.ca/crvssra
- River erosion is a permit-level constraint, not background information: the FVRD's hazard thresholds score new building, subdivision and rezoning against the erosion setback line and the 100-year erosion limit line for the Chilliwack River Valley, and a covenant acknowledging future erosion hazard is implied in approvals in the middle band. https://www.fvrd.ca/assets/Services/Documents/Planning~and~Development/Application~Forms~and~Resources/2020%20Hazard%20Acceptability%20Thresholds.pdf
- Wildfire and evacuation planning are FVRD documents a buyer can read before offering: the Area E page links a Community Wildfire Resiliency Plan, an Emergency Management Plan, an Evacuation Guide for Area E and a Hazard Report, and the FVRD runs emergency notifications through Alertable. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
- Dike protection here is an FVRD service, not a city one: Dyke Operations and Maintenance is listed among Area E's local services, and the Wilson Road Dike needed flood recovery repairs in 2026 — ask which dike, if any, protects the specific property. https://fvrd.ca/EN/main/about-the-fvrd/electoral-areas/electoral-area-e.html
- What the pages read do not tell a buyer: the condition or capacity of a specific property's well or septic system, and whether its slope is inside the assessment's hazard mapping — both are property-level reports, not area-page facts.
