# Surrey Open Houses — Extra Local Notes
Page: surrey-open-houses (Surrey; open houses).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- This page covers public open houses across the whole City of Surrey, in the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board (FVREB) area: the board states its REALTORS® serve Surrey, Langley, White Rock, North Delta, Abbotsford and Mission, and that it supports more than 5,000 members. https://www.fvreb.bc.ca/about/about-fvreb/
- Surrey open houses split into the same six City communities a touring buyer drives between — Cloverdale, Fleetwood, Guildford, Newton, South Surrey and Whalley — so a Saturday route is planned by community and by the Fraser Highway, King George Boulevard and Highway 99 corridors, not by postal code. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities
- The signage rules on this page are Surrey's alone: they come from Surrey Sign By-law, 1999, No. 13656, a City bylaw, so the same open house run in Langley or Delta follows a different sign bylaw. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_reg_13656.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The stock this page shows is whatever Surrey sellers are opening that week, across the city's own housing forms: detached houses in Fleetwood and South Surrey, strata townhome clusters in Clayton and Fleetwood, and City Centre condos around King George Station in Whalley. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities
- Townhome open houses in Surrey are usually one unit inside a single rezoned cluster — for example the RM-30 and MR4 clusters on 74 Avenue in West Clayton — so parking, visitor stalls and strata rules are set by that complex, not by the street. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7925-0268-00

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- An open house directional sign needs no sign permit in Surrey, but only during the open house: Surrey Sign By-law, 1999, No. 13656 lists a "Real Estate open house directional sign for the duration of open house hours" among the signs exempt from the permit requirement. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/bylaws/BYL_reg_13656.pdf
- The City's real estate sign guide tightens the timing: open house signs, including directional and A-frame signs, are only permitted on the day of an open house, during the open house hours. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/SignBylawRealEstate.pdf
- On-site real estate signage is capped: the City's real estate guide states a maximum of one sign per lot frontage for a sale or rental sign, with a maximum sign area of 0.75 square metres (8 square feet) for the on-site sign along each adjacent road. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/SignBylawRealEstate.pdf
- Temporary signs must come down after the event: proposed 2026 amendments to Sign By-law No. 13656, in a Council report dated June 10, 2026, restate that real estate signs and similar temporary signs displayed on any lot shall be removed by the owner within 14 days of the termination of the event identified in the sign or the completion of the sale. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/r126-proposed-amendments-surrey-sign-law-1999-no-13656
- Prohibited sign types apply to open house marketing too: the City's business signage page lists flashing signs (except in Special Sign Areas), roof signs, billboard signs, inflatable signs, flags as signs, painted window signs and electronic message board signs as prohibited on all properties. https://www.surrey.ca/business-economy/business-services/business-signage-bylaw
- The provincial rule that follows a Surrey open house is the Home Buyer Rescission Period: the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) states a buyer may rescind a contract to purchase residential real property up to three business days after an offer is accepted, paying the seller 0.25 percent of the purchase price, and the right cannot be waived. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/home-buyer-rescission-period-information

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Can a seller in Surrey leave open house signs up all week? **A:** No. The City's real estate sign guide states open house signs, including directional and A-frame signs, are only permitted on the day of an open house and only during the open house hours, and Surrey Sign By-law No. 13656 exempts an open house directional sign from a permit only "for the duration of open house hours." A sign standing on a Tuesday for a Saturday open house is outside both the guide and the bylaw exemption. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/SignBylawRealEstate.pdf
- **Q:** If I make an offer after a Surrey open house, can I change my mind? **A:** Yes, within the provincial rescission window. BCFSA states the Home Buyer Rescission Period lets a buyer rescind a contract for residential real property up to three business days after acceptance, on written notice to the seller, for a fee of 0.25 percent of the purchase price. The period starts the day after acceptance is signed, and Saturdays, Sundays and holidays do not count as business days. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/frequently-asked-questions/home-buyer-rescission-period-frequently-asked-questions
- **Q:** Does the rescission right apply to every home I might see at a Surrey open house? **A:** No. BCFSA lists exemptions: residential leasehold properties, property sold at auction, and property sold under a court order or court supervision are exempt, and purchases where a disclosure statement is provided under the Real Estate Development Marketing Act carry that Act's rescission rights instead. A court-ordered sale toured at an open house in Surrey is therefore outside the three-day right. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/frequently-asked-questions/home-buyer-rescission-period-frequently-asked-questions
- **Q:** Who regulates the REALTOR® hosting a Surrey open house? **A:** The Province, through BCFSA. The provincial buying-and-selling page states the real estate industry is regulated under the Real Estate Services Act and the Real Estate Development Marketing Act, and that the BC Financial Services Authority ensures the industry complies with provincial legislation. The local board, FVREB, is the member association for Surrey REALTORS®, not the regulator. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home
- **Q:** Can I get an inspection done during the rescission period? **A:** Not automatically. BCFSA states access to the property is addressed in the contract of purchase and sale: if the contract is subject to inspection or has a term permitting access, the seller must abide by it, but the buyer has no automatic right to enter the property during the rescission period. A buyer who needs an inspection should write the access or subject clause into the offer made after the open house. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/frequently-asked-questions/home-buyer-rescission-period-frequently-asked-questions

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- A sign permit is required before installing permanent real estate signage in Surrey: the City's real estate sign guide states "You need a sign permit prior to installing signage" and gives the Planning and Development contact as 604-591-4370 — the exemption covers the temporary open house directional sign, not a developer's project sign. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/SignBylawRealEstate.pdf
- Surrey amended its sign rules again in 2026: a Council corporate report dated June 10, 2026 proposes targeted amendments to Sign By-law, 1999, No. 13656 to improve clarity and reduce ambiguity, noting the bylaw was adopted in 1999 and has had housekeeping amendments since — a seller should check the current consolidated bylaw, not an old brokerage handout. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/corporate-reports/r126-proposed-amendments-surrey-sign-law-1999-no-13656
- Home inspectors a buyer books after an open house must be licensed in BC: the provincial buying-and-selling page states home inspectors must be licensed by Consumer Protection BC and are required to follow certain laws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/buying-selling/buying-and-selling-home
- Deposits after an accepted offer sit in brokerage trust: BCFSA states that unless the contract says interest on a deposit is payable to the seller or the buyer, interest is payable to the Real Estate Foundation of BC — a detail most open house visitors never hear. https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/selling-home/deposits
- Touring farm open houses in the lowlands means dyke country: the Serpentine and Nicomekl sea dams close at high tide to keep brackish water out of farm land, so a wet field at a showing can be the drainage system working as designed — see the surrey-farms note for the full picture. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- An open house itself carries no City fee for a buyer; the costs that follow a Surrey purchase are the city-wide ones written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note for the 2026 tax rate, parcel taxes and utility charges. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- The one fee an open house buyer can meet directly is the rescission fee: 0.25 percent of the purchase price, payable to the seller if the buyer rescinds within the three-business-day Home Buyer Rescission Period, per BCFSA. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/home-buyer-rescission-period-information
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies to a Surrey home bought and left empty: the Province lists the City of Surrey as a designated taxable area, so every residential owner must file the annual declaration — relevant to a buyer touring open houses for a part-time residence. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- A Surrey open house run is a driving run between six communities, anchored by transit corridors a buyer can also test on the day: City Centre in Whalley is serviced by four Expo Line SkyTrain stations ending at King George Station. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities
- The R1 King George Boulevard RapidBus runs from Newton Exchange to Guildford Exchange through Surrey Central and King George stations, linking the two communities where weekend open houses cluster most densely in North Surrey. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- The R6 Scott Road RapidBus runs from Scott Road Station to Newton Exchange along Scott Road and 72 Avenue, the route a buyer without a car would use to reach Bridgeview, Scottsdale and Newton open houses. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- Highway access for a cross-city tour is Highway 1 at Guildford and Fraser Heights, Highway 99 through South Surrey, and Highway 15 (176 Street) through Cloverdale — the City notes South Surrey alone has two border crossings to the United States. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Do not treat an open house viewing as due diligence on flood risk: for any Surrey home in Bridgeview, Crescent Beach, Mud Bay or the Serpentine and Nicomekl lowlands, the flood checks in the surrey-houses-for-sale note apply in full — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities
- For a strata open house, the documents matter more than the viewing: rental-restriction bylaws are no longer allowed in BC stratas as of November 24, 2022, but short-term rental bylaws still apply and strata corporations can fine up to $1,000 a day for breaching one, per the Province's strata pages — read the bylaws before offering. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/short-term-rental-bylaws
- For a farm open house, confirm Agricultural Land Reserve status and the one-residence and 500 square metre principal-residence limits with the Agricultural Land Commission before offering — see the surrey-farms note. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- A "For Sale" sign that does not comply with Sign By-law No. 13656 is a bylaw-enforcement matter, not a title issue: the City states it is the responsibility of each business or operation to comply with the sign bylaw, and enforcement focuses first on new developments and signage already illegal under the former bylaw. https://www.surrey.ca/business-economy/business-services/business-signage-bylaw
