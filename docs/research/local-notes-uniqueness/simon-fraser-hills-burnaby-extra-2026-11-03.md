# Simon Fraser Hills Neighbourhood — Extra Local Notes
Page: simon-fraser-hills-burnaby (Simon Fraser Hills; neighbourhood).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Simon Fraser Hills sits on the lower slopes of Burnaby Mountain below Simon Fraser University (SFU), around Forest Grove Drive and Beaverbrook Crescent, north of the Lougheed Highway. School District 41 Burnaby's (SD41) Cameron-area catchment map shows Forest Grove, Stoney Creek Community School, Simon Fraser Hills Park, Broadway and the Lougheed Highway in this one pocket. https://burnabyschools.ca/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/cameron_2006.pdf
- The area takes its name from the university above it, not from a separate municipality; it is City of Burnaby, in the Cariboo-Lougheed school zone. SD41 lists Forest Grove, Stoney Creek and Cameron among that zone's elementary schools and Burnaby Mountain and Cariboo Hill as its secondary schools. https://mountain.burnabyschools.ca/blog/2017/11/07/sogi-education-in-burnaby-schools-parent-info-session-november-22nd/
- Stoney Creek runs through the area's parkland, and the City Archives' Burnaby Mountain oral history ties Simon Fraser Hills Park — once called "The Peanut" in a 1970s park-protection protest — to the Stoney Creek neighbourhood. https://search.heritageburnaby.ca/link/oralhistory681

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The stock here is ground-oriented homes on Forest Grove Drive, Beaverbrook Crescent and the courts off them, below the Burnaby Mountain Conservation Area treeline. The City's outdoor fitness circuit list names Simon Fraser Hills Park as a neighbourhood park serving these streets. https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/outdoor-fitness-circuits
- Forest Grove Park, beside Forest Grove Elementary at 8525 Forest Grove Drive, carries 3 tennis courts and 9 pickleball courts (8 shared, 1 stand-alone) — the recreation anchor for the townhouse and house streets around it. https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/outdoor-courts
- Stoney Creek Park adds 2 tennis and 2 pickleball courts on the area's south side, near Stoney Creek Community School at 2740 Beaverbrook Crescent. https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/outdoor-courts
- Named strata complexes and their build years for this page's streets are covered in the simon-fraser-hills-burnaby-condos-townhomes-for-sale note; this note does not repeat them.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Detached lots here are in Burnaby's R1 Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) District — see the suncrest-burnaby note for the full SSMUH rules; the lot-by-lot unit count shows on BurnabyMap under "Maximum SSMUH Residential Units Per Legal Lot." https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- Lots backing onto Stoney Creek can carry a Streamside Protection and Enhancement Area (SPEA) setback. The City's stream guide says Planning review is triggered when development is proposed within 30 metres of the top of bank of a stream or ravine. https://burnaby.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2021-07/Guide%20for%20Developing%20Near%20Streams.pdf
- Burnaby's rules now sit in the Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026, adopted June 9, 2026 and in effect July 1, 2026, replacing the Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 1965. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/bylaws/zoning-bylaw
- The Burnaby 2050 Official Community Plan (OCP), adopted December 9, 2025, now takes precedence over older neighbourhood community-plan maps where the two conflict. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/strategies-and-plans/official-community-plan/community-plans

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Which elementary school does a Forest Grove Drive home feed into? **A:** It depends on the exact street, because two SD41 schools sit inside this small area. Forest Grove Elementary is at 8525 Forest Grove Drive and Stoney Creek Community School is at 2740 Beaverbrook Crescent, and each has its own attendance-area map on the district site. Check the civic address in SD41's School Locator before writing an offer that depends on one school. https://burnabyschools.ca/elementary-school-addresses/
- **Q:** Is Burnaby Mountain Secondary the high school for this area? **A:** Burnaby Mountain Secondary, at 8800 Eastlake Drive, is one of the two secondary schools SD41 assigns to the Cariboo-Lougheed zone, alongside Cariboo Hill Secondary. The district groups Forest Grove, Stoney Creek and Cameron elementaries in that same zone, but the catchment line is drawn by address, so confirm the specific street in the School Locator rather than assuming the closer building applies. https://mountain.burnabyschools.ca/blog/2017/11/07/sogi-education-in-burnaby-schools-parent-info-session-november-22nd/
- **Q:** Will the proposed gondola pass over Simon Fraser Hills homes? **A:** The endorsed Route 1 is a straight line from Production Way–University Station to the SFU bus exchange, and TransLink's engagement material says cabins would pass about 50 metres above the Forest Grove neighbourhood with no towers proposed inside the neighbourhood. The project is not yet approved or funded, so treat it as a proposal to monitor, not a built change. https://translinklistens.ca/burnaby-mountain-gondola/widgets/59764/faqs
- **Q:** Can I build right up to Stoney Creek behind a house here? **A:** No — a Streamside Protection and Enhancement Area setback can apply. Burnaby's 2026 environmental assessment guide says a Qualified Environmental Professional must confirm the SPEA boundary for parcels subject to a Streamside Development Permit, using the method in Part 4 of the Zoning Bylaw. Ask the City's Planning Department whether the lot is flagged before pricing a laneway or addition. https://www.burnaby.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2026-06/Preparing-Environmental-Assessment-Report.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The Burnaby Mountain Conservation Area trail network starts effectively at this area's back fence: 34 multi-use trails over 28 kilometres across 578 hectares, climbing from about 25 metres above sea level to the 366-metre summit. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/walking-hiking-and-biking/burnaby-mountain-conservation-area-trails
- The Velodrome Trail section on Burnaby Mountain is closed indefinitely for dirt erosion and aging wood steps while City staff plan restoration — check the trail page before relying on that route. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/walking-hiking-and-biking/burnaby-mountain-conservation-area-trails
- Forest Grove Park's playground was one of three playground replacement projects the City completed in 2024, alongside Lobley Park and Poplar Park West. https://www.burnaby.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2025-04/2024-Annual-Municipal-Report.pdf
- SFU's own directions page describes the 145 bus as daily, from Production Way SkyTrain Station to SFU via Production Way, Broadway, Gaglardi Way and University Drive East — the mountain route that frames this area's south edge. https://www.sfu.ca/content/sfu/genericdept/kenson/about-sfu/maps-and-directions/burnaby-campus-map.html
- The City is also developing a Burnaby Mountain Trail Management Plan to balance conservation and recreation on the mountain. https://www.burnaby.ca/yourvoice

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- Burnaby Mountain Gondola, Production Way–University Station to SFU Burnaby campus, proponent TransLink: 3S (three-cable) gondola, cabins up to 35 passengers, preferred Route 1 straight line. STATUS: business case and engagement stage; Burnaby Council endorsed the project and route in 2022, but TransLink states it is not yet approved or funded and needs a future investment plan plus TransLink Board and Mayors' Council approval; read 2026-11-03. https://www.translink.ca/news/2023/november/translink%20seeking%20feedback%20on%20burnaby%20mountain%20gondola

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Burnaby's 2026 municipal tax rate for Class 1 (residential) property is $1.72223 per $1,000 of taxable value in total, including the Growth Infrastructure Investment Levy of $0.05920, under the Burnaby Rates Bylaw 2026, Bylaw No. 14814. https://bylaws.burnaby.ca/media/14000/14814.pdf
- Council approved a 2.9% property tax increase for 2026, about $61 for the average Burnaby home, plus the 1.9% Growth Infrastructure Investment Levy, about $39 more. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/news/2026-01-28/burnaby-keeps-tax-rates-affordable-latest-financial-plan
- A detached single-family home's 2026 flat water charge is $652.33; its sewer charge is billed as a sewer parcel tax on the property tax notice, not on the utility bill. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees
- The default 180-litre garbage toter costs $159 for 2026 disposal; utility rates get a 5% discount if paid in full by the due date. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies here: the Province lists the City of Burnaby as a taxable area in the Metro Vancouver Regional District. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The 145 bus is the mountain connector: TransLink's timetable, effective June 8, 2026, runs it from Production Station via Production Way, Broadway, Gaglardi Way, the tunnel, University Drive East and East Campus Road to SFU Exchange. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt145.pdf
- The 144 bus links SFU and Metrotown Station through the Sperling and Deer Lake corridor south of this area; TransLink publishes its stop-by-stop schedule on its route 144 page. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/144/direction/1/schedule
- The Trans Canada Trail's Burnaby section runs 12.1 kilometres across the north end of the city and links into the Burnaby Mountain Conservation Area trails above these streets. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/walking-hiking-and-biking/trans-canada-trail

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the Riparian Management Areas layer before offering on a lot near Stoney Creek: the City's geographic information system (GIS) layer description says it flags lots crossed by a 10-metre or 30-metre SPEA measured from top of bank or top of ravine bank. https://gis.burnaby.ca/arcgis/rest/services/Other/BBOT/MapServer/25
- The map layer is a screening tool only; the City's guide says the parcel-specific SPEA is set by a Qualified Environmental Professional's assessment, which the map does not give you. https://www.burnaby.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2026-06/Preparing-Environmental-Assessment-Report.pdf
- Slope is the second check on these mountain-foot streets: the City's trail page records trail erosion closures on Burnaby Mountain, but it does not rate any individual lot's slope stability — a geotechnical report is a private purchase, not a City page. https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/walking-hiking-and-biking/burnaby-mountain-conservation-area-trails
