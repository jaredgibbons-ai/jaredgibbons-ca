# Langley 55+ Condos — Extra Local Notes
Page: langley-55-plus-condos-for-sale (Langley; 55+ condos).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Langley is two municipalities sharing one condo market: the City of Langley, a compact urban city around 200 Street, 56 Avenue and the Fraser Highway, and the Township of Langley surrounding it, so a condo's tax rate, bylaws and development plan depend on which side of the municipal line it sits. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026taxrates
- The City of Langley's condo core is the downtown and Fraser Highway corridor, where the City's amenity-contribution policy notes densities are already rising with SkyTrain under construction, while Township condos cluster in Willoughby and Walnut Grove, a different plan area entirely. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/policy-co-80-amenity-contributions-policy
- School District No. 35 (Langley) covers both municipalities, so school catchment does not change at the City and Township line even though taxes and bylaws do. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026taxrates

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The general British Columbia Strata Property Act 55+ framework is not repeated here; see the chilliwack-55-plus-townhomes-for-sale note for the full framework, and read the specific Langley building's filed bylaws for any age bylaw, because neither Langley municipality publishes a list of 55+ buildings. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- Development cost is a City of Langley rule a new-build buyer meets indirectly: the City's Amenity Contributions and Density Bonusing Policy CO-80 collects cash-in-lieu from multi-unit developments, with rates rising by floor-area ratio and updates on January 1, 2025 and January 1, 2026 tied in the policy to SkyTrain construction and rising densities. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/policy-co-80-amenity-contributions-policy
- The same policy lets Council consider reducing or waiving amenity contributions for affordable housing units, which is why some Langley seniors and affordable projects proceed as rental housing-society product rather than strata condos. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/policy-co-80-amenity-contributions-policy

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Which Langley am I buying in, City or Township? **A:** Read the tax notice, because the two Langleys charge differently. The City of Langley's 2026 total residential rate is $4.2067 per $1,000 of assessed value on its own rate summary, while a Township condo is taxed by the Township at a rate that is UNCONFIRMED in this pass, so the municipal line changes the bill for the same-priced condo. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026taxrates
- **Q:** How are condo utilities billed in the City of Langley? **A:** By a City utility charge split from property tax. The City's 2026 insert reports multi-family water rates up 7.44% and sewer rates up 5.07% for 2026, with Metro Vancouver taking 58% of water charges and 57% of sewer charges, so most of the increase is regional, not City, cost. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026-property-tax-insert-half-page-utilities
- **Q:** Is there a hospital near Langley's condo core? **A:** Yes, Langley Memorial Hospital is the Fraser Health hospital serving Langley. A 55+ buyer should still confirm the clinic and pharmacy pattern for their specific building, because hospital proximity on a listing card is UNCONFIRMED and the practical check is the building's own address against services they will actually use weekly. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/service-directory/locations/langley/langley-memorial-hospital
- **Q:** Will SkyTrain change my condo area? **A:** The City already plans as if it will. Langley's amenity policy states residential densities are increasing with SkyTrain now under construction, and contribution rates were updated for January 1, 2025 and January 1, 2026, so a downtown Langley condo sits in a corridor the City expects to densify around the future stations. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/policy-co-80-amenity-contributions-policy
- **Q:** How much of my City tax stays in the City? **A:** Just over half. Langley City's 2025 annual report states only 58% of property taxes go to the City, with the rest going to provincial school tax, TransLink, Metro Vancouver, the British Columbia Assessment Authority, the Downtown Langley Business Association and the Municipal Finance Authority. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2025-annual-report

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Langley City collects tax for many other bodies on one notice: its annual report lists provincial school taxes, TransLink, Metro Vancouver, British Columbia Assessment, the Downtown Langley Business Association and the Municipal Finance Authority as the other 42% of the bill, so a condo owner disputing a line is often disputing another government's levy, not the City's. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2025-annual-report
- Utility money mostly leaves the City: Metro Vancouver takes 58% of Langley City water charges and 57% of sewer charges in 2026, per the City's own utility insert, so condo utility rises track regional treatment costs. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026-property-tax-insert-half-page-utilities
- The City funds much of its capital program from a non-tax source a buyer rarely hears about: casino proceeds primarily fund capital projects such as road rehabilitation and replacement of aging water infrastructure, per the 2025 annual report. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2025-annual-report
- Tax prepayment is a local habit: the City's 2024 annual report recorded 95% of property taxes collected from 11,584 properties and a prepayment program expanded to 1,998 subscribers, a cash-flow option a condo owner can join. https://www.langleycity.ca/2024AnnualReport
- School catchment for a specific Langley condo address in School District No. 35 is UNCONFIRMED in this pass because the district locator was not run; the district covers both Langleys, but elementary catchments still change by block. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026taxrates

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- The City of Langley's 2026 total residential property tax rate is $4.2067 per $1,000 of taxable value, built on its rate summary from a City general rate plus Metro Vancouver, school, TransLink (Greater Vancouver Transportation Authority), British Columbia Assessment and Municipal Finance Authority components. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026taxrates
- The Township of Langley's residential rate is a separate figure and is UNCONFIRMED in this pass, so this page needs two tax answers, not one. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026taxrates
- City of Langley utilities for multi-family homes rose in 2026, with water up 7.44% and sewer up 5.07% for townhouse, apartment and condo homes, and single-family solid waste up 10.49%, per the City's 2026 utility insert. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026-property-tax-insert-half-page-utilities
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies in both the City of Langley and the Township of Langley, both listed by the Province as designated taxable areas, so owners in either municipality file the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- The two-municipality split is the first risk: City and Township condos carry different tax rates, utility structures and development plans, so a buyer comparing two Langley condos must confirm the municipality for each before comparing carrying costs. https://www.langleycity.ca/media/file/2026taxrates
- Older low-rise strata risk is documentary, not municipal: no City page states a building's depreciation, insurance or special-levy position, so the Form B, bylaws and depreciation report for the specific building are the due-diligence documents, and any 55+ claim is UNCONFIRMED until those bylaws are read. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
