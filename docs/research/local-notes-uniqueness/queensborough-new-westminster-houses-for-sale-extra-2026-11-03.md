# Queensborough Houses — Extra Local Notes
Page: queensborough-new-westminster-houses-for-sale (Queensborough; houses).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Queensborough is the City of New Westminster's island neighbourhood on the eastern tip of Lulu Island — District Lots 757 and 758 in the Zoning Bylaw wording — ringed by the Fraser River main arm to the south and east and the North Arm to the north, and sharing Lulu Island with the City of Richmond to the west. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf
- The only road link to the rest of New Westminster is Highway 91A, the Queensborough Connector, over the Queensborough Bridge across the North Arm to Marine Way and Stewardson Way; Highway 91 connects at the south end in Richmond. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf
- Ewen Avenue is the island's main street — the City's Queensborough Development Permit Areas include the Queensborough Main Street commercial area on Ewen Avenue — and Port Royal is the newer development area at the island's north end, named in City planning materials. https://www.newwestcity.ca/development-policies-and-process/development-permit-areas/sb_expander_articles/1184.php

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Houses in Queensborough sit under a subdivision rule no mainland New Westminster house faces: Zoning Bylaw section 190.36 provides that no Queensborough subdivision may produce a lot under one-half acre (0.20 hectares) or with frontage under 66 feet (20.12 metres) unless the land can connect to the City sanitary sewer system. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/100_Introduction(2).pdf
- The newer house form is plan-named: the (RLR) Residential – Low Rise designation in the Official Community Plan amendment materials (Bylaw No. 8574, 2026) provides compact-lot single detached form, low-rise buildings up to six storeys, and stacked and side-by-side townhouses. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Draft_Queensborough_Land_Use_Designations_and_Map___March_9_2026.pdf
- No City or developer source read for this note states a build-decade split or typical lot size for the existing detached stock, so none is claimed; Port Royal is a City-named development area, not a single building or complex. https://www.newwestcity.ca/ocp/past-event-materials-and-council-reports

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- New Westminster-wide rules are stated in full in this note, as the first New Westminster note in this slice: houses sit in the Single Detached Residential Districts (RS series, section 310) of Zoning Bylaw No. 6680, 2001 — RS-1 sets a minimum site area of 557.40 square metres (6,000 square feet) and a floor space ratio of 0.5 — and the RS districts allow secondary suites, with laneway and carriage houses regulated under the same bylaw. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Zoning_Bylaw_No_6680_2001_as_of_June_30_2022_(Complete).pdf
- Every Queensborough house is inside the Flood Hazard Development Permit Area framework: minimum flood construction levels are 3.53 metres above Geodetic Survey of Canada datum for Area A (generally downstream from Derwent Way on the south side and Wood Street on the north side) and 4.20 metres above that datum for Area B (upstream of Derwent Way and Wood Street). https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Flood_Hazard_DPA.pdf
- The governing plan is the Queensborough Community Plan, a schedule to Our City 2041, the Official Community Plan (Bylaw No. 7925, 2017), together with the Queensborough Eastern Neighbourhood Node Master Plan. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Draft_Queensborough_Land_Use_Designations_and_Map___March_9_2026.pdf
- Queensborough properties are documented in the City's Queensborough Residential Heritage Inventory and Queensborough Industrial Heritage Inventory, so an older house's heritage record is a City file a buyer can check. https://www.newwestcity.ca/heritage/heritage-research-and-resources/sb_expander_articles/585.php

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is Queensborough in a floodplain? **A:** Yes — the whole neighbourhood is Floodplain Zone 1 in the City's floodplain study, a low-lying area with high groundwater, poor compressible soils and poor drainage, protected by a perimeter dyke built in the 1960s covering approximately 350 hectares and a pumped drainage system shared with Richmond. New construction must meet flood construction levels of 3.53 metres (Area A) or 4.20 metres (Area B) above Geodetic Survey of Canada datum. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Flood_Hazard_DPA.pdf
- **Q:** How do I get off the island? **A:** By one bridge to mainland New Westminster, or south into Richmond. Highway 91A, the Queensborough Connector, crosses the Queensborough Bridge over the North Arm of the Fraser River to Marine Way and Stewardson Way, and Highway 91 connects at the south end in Richmond. Every trip to Uptown, Downtown or Royal Columbian Hospital crosses that bridge, which is the fact to test at commute hours before buying. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf
- **Q:** Which schools are on the island? **A:** Queen Elizabeth Elementary and Queensborough Middle School are on the island; secondary school is on the mainland. For the sample address 921 Salter Street, the School District No. 40 locator result is Queen Elizabeth Elementary (kindergarten to Grade 4), Queensborough Middle (Grades 5 to 8) and New Westminster Secondary (Grades 9 to 12) across the bridge. Queensborough Middle School is at 833 Salter Street. Catchments are address-based, so check the exact house address. https://newwestschools.ca/our-schools/
- **Q:** Can a Queensborough lot be subdivided? **A:** Only within the island's sewer rule. Zoning Bylaw section 190.36 bars a Queensborough subdivision from producing a lot under one-half acre (0.20 hectares) or with frontage under 66 feet (20.12 metres) unless the land can connect to the City sanitary sewer system. The connection status of the specific lot therefore decides the subdivision question, and it is confirmed with the City, not assumed from the lot's size. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/100_Introduction(2).pdf
- **Q:** Is there a SkyTrain station in Queensborough? **A:** No — Queensborough has no SkyTrain station, and buses are the transit link. TransLink route 104 runs from 22nd Street Station across the Queensborough Bridge via Highway 91A, Howes Street and Ewen Avenue through the island, per TransLink's timetable effective September 7, 2026. A house buyer's transit test is the walk to an Ewen Avenue or Howes Street stop and the bridge crossing to the station, not a station walk. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt104.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Queensborough has the library branch on the island: New Westminster Public Library's Queensborough Branch is at 920 Ewen Avenue; the Main Branch is at 716 6th Avenue on the mainland. https://www.nwpl.ca/
- The Queensborough Community Centre is the on-island City recreation facility, beside Ryall Park, which the City lists with sports fields, courts, a spray park, a community garden and the Boro All Wheel Park; Port Royal Park has a riverfront trail. https://www.newwestcity.ca/parks-and-recreation/book-facilities-fields-and-parks/articles/5540.php
- Electricity here is not BC Hydro's retail service: New Westminster owns and operates its own electric utility, governed under the City's Utility Commission Bylaw No. 8029, 2018 — a Queensborough house's power bill is a City of New Westminster bill. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Bylaw_8029_2018_Utility_Commission.pdf
- The dike is being repaired in named spots: the City received $800,000 from the provincial Community Emergency Preparedness Fund for the Queensborough Dike Shoreline Protection Rehabilitation project, restabilising eroding dike slopes behind 345 Gifford Street and behind 805 Boyd Street, on the northeast side of Lulu Island. https://www.newwestcity.ca/2023/02/14/city-of-new-westminster-receives-provincial-funding-for-queensborough-dike-rehabilitation.php
- Secondary students cross the bridge daily: New Westminster Secondary is on the mainland, so a Queensborough family's school run includes the Queensborough Bridge from Grade 9 onward. https://newwestschools.ca/our-schools/

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- New Westminster's total 2026 residential property tax rate is $4.79312 per $1,000 of assessed value across all taxing authorities, per the City's 2026 property tax model and mill rates file. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/26_CNW_Mill_Rates_.pdf
- 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty on current taxes remaining unpaid on July 3, 2026 and a second 5% penalty on any unpaid balance on September 3, 2026. https://www.newwestcity.ca/services/whats-happening/index/main/175/site_post/page10.php
- 2026 utility charges for a single-family dwelling are flat annual rates, billed on a statement mailed in late February and discounted 5% if paid in full by Monday, May 4, 2026: net of discount, water $881.20, sewer $1,364.21 and solid waste (120-litre cart) $508.32, a $2,753.73 subtotal; a single-family dwelling with a secondary suite nets $4,143.46. https://www.newwestcity.ca/propertytaxes-utilities/sb_expander_articles/1271.php
- A registered owner aged 65 or over living alone in a single-family residence can claim the City's senior waiver of 25% of the annual water, sewer and solid waste charges, bringing the 2026 single-family net to $2,174. https://www.newwestcity.ca/propertytaxes-utilities/sb_expander_articles/1271.php
- New Westminster is a designated taxable area for the provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax, so residential owners here must complete the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Lead every Queensborough file with flood diligence: the City states the city covers twelve square kilometres, of which five are within the flood boundaries on the provincial floodplain map, and that Queensborough's approximately 350 hectares of designated floodplain are the highest priority for flood protection improvements in the city. http://www.newwestcity.ca/services/public-safety/flood-management
- Check the lot's flood construction level before pricing a rebuild: 3.53 metres above Geodetic Survey of Canada datum in Flood Hazard Development Permit Area A and 4.20 metres in Area B, split at Derwent Way on the south side and Wood Street on the north side. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Flood_Hazard_DPA.pdf
- Check the soil and drainage record, not just the dike: the City's floodplain study describes Queensborough as a low-lying area with high groundwater, poor compressible soils and poor drainage, protected by a shared dyking and pumped drainage system with Richmond — a geotechnical review belongs in the purchase file. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/NWRFP_20_23_Addendum_1.pdf
- Check the sewer connection status under Zoning Bylaw section 190.36 before promising subdivision: lots under one-half acre or under 66 feet of frontage cannot be created unless the land can connect to the City sanitary sewer system. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/100_Introduction(2).pdf
