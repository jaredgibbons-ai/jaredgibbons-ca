# Pitt Meadows Area Guide — Extra Local Notes
Page: pitt-meadows-neighbourhood-guides (Pitt Meadows; area guide).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Pitt Meadows is one small city between three rivers — the Fraser, Pitt and Alouette — and its neighbourhood guides split the urban core into Central, Mid and South Meadows inside the dikes, with North Meadows farm country running toward Pitt Lake. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/docs/our-community/community-plan/section_4_-_policies_-_4_10_utilities.pdf
- Harris Road is the city's main street: the Official Community Plan (OCP) describes local shops and amenities accessible by walking along Harris Road, and focuses higher-density development along the Harris Road corridor. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/media/8989
- The whole city sits on the traditional, unceded territory of q̓ic̓əy̓ (Katzie) First Nation, and the North Lougheed Area Plan records documented q̓ic̓əy̓ occupation and use of the North Lougheed area for over four thousand years. http://pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/2026-05/DRAFT-NLAP.pdf
- Pitt Meadows Regional Airport (YPK) anchors the southwest: the City places it north of the Fraser River between the Pitt River and Golden Ears bridges. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/our-community/ypk-airport

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Most of the city is not housing land at all: the OCP states more than three-quarters of Pitt Meadows is still farmland, known for greenhouses and small-fruit production, so the urban stock is concentrated in the Central, Mid and South Meadows pockets inside the Urban Containment Boundary. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/media/8989
- Growth is directed, not spread: the OCP as amended in 2025 focuses higher-density development along the Harris Road corridor, near the West Coast Express stations and near the Civic Centre, while Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) is permitted as-of-right in urban neighbourhoods. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/2025-12/Dec_2_2025_OCP_2025_Update_Amendment_Bylaw_3034%2C_2025.pdf
- The airport lands are their own stock: 257 hectares (636 acres) in five legal parcels owned and operated by the Pitt Meadows Airport Society, zoned I-5 (Airport), with civic addresses on Baynes Road and 18799 Airport Way. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/docs/city-hall/hearings/Staff%20Report%20as%20presented%20on%20March%205%2C%202024.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Flood construction levels are set by bylaw, not by the seller: Floodplain Designation and Construction Control Bylaw No. 2384, 2008 sets the Designated Flood Level at 1.85 metres Geodetic Survey of Canada (GSC) datum for the Schedule A Rural Floodplain and 5.15 metres GSC for the Schedule B Urban Floodplain. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/2024-09/Floodplain Designation and Construction Control Bylaw No. 2384, 2008 - Consolidated.pdf
- The current plan is Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 2864, 2020 ("I See Pitt Meadows 2040"), adopted September 27, 2022 and amended by Bylaw No. 3034, 2025 on December 2, 2025 to align OCP policies with provincial SSMUH legislation and designate Transit-Oriented Areas beside the Pitt Meadows and Maple Meadows West Coast Express stations. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/media/8989
- Airport-adjacent lots carry federal height limits: under the Aeronautics Act, height restrictions over much of the airport lands range from 9 metres (30 feet) to 45 metres (150 feet) above airport elevation, overseen by the airport itself. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/docs/city-hall/hearings/Staff%20Report%20as%20presented%20on%20March%205%2C%202024.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** How much of Pitt Meadows can actually flood? **A:** Most of it, on paper. The Official Community Plan states approximately 90 percent of Pitt Meadows lies within floodplains, and the community is protected by a series of dikes against spring freshet flooding from the Fraser, Pitt and Alouette Rivers. The City's floodplain bylaw then sets the build level — 5.15 metres geodetic for the urban floodplain — so the question for any lot is its flood construction level and covenant, not whether the city floods. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/docs/our-community/community-plan/section_4_-_policies_-_4_10_utilities.pdf
- **Q:** What is the Harris Road rail crossing issue? **A:** It is the city's main road blocked by one of the busiest rail segments in British Columbia. The City states trains block the Harris Road and Kennedy Road crossings up to 3.5 hours per day, and the Pitt Meadows Road and Rail Improvements Project is planned to separate road and rail traffic at Harris Road with a four-lane underpass. Until it is built, Harris Road travel time depends on train traffic. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/railways
- **Q:** Which plan governs a lot north of Lougheed Highway? **A:** Since September 2026, the North Lougheed Area Plan. Council adopted it on September 29, 2026 through Official Community Plan Amendment Bylaw No. 3060, 2026, covering about 50 hectares (125 acres) on the north side of Lougheed Highway between Meadows Gardens Golf Course and Harris Road. Development there is expected to proceed gradually, and specific projects still need further planning, technical studies and Council approvals. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/community-projects-and-initiatives/projects-and-studies/north-lougheed-area-plan
- **Q:** Do Pitt Meadows kids change schools at middle school? **A:** No — School District 42 (Maple Ridge–Pitt Meadows) has no middle tier. Its elementary schools run Kindergarten to Grade 7 and its secondary schools Grade 8 to Grade 12, and catchments are set by address in the district's School Locator, so a Harris Road address and a North Meadows address can feed different schools despite the city's size. https://www.sd42.ca/school-locator/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The dikes are the recreation system: the City is almost entirely protected by 60 kilometres of dikes, divided into four drainage areas — Area 1 (Alouette Pump Station catchment), Area 2 (Fenton), Area 3 (Kennedy, including McKechnie) and Area 4 (Pitt Polder) — each discharging through floodboxes and pump stations. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/node/163
- The same dikes that keep river water out keep storm water in: the OCP notes the dikes also prevent storm water from discharging into the rivers, so drainage runs through ditches, sloughs and culverts to pump stations. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/docs/our-community/community-plan/section_4_-_policies_-_4_10_utilities.pdf
- The airport opened in 1963 and is run by a non-profit: the Pitt Meadows Airport Society owns and operates YPK, reinvests all earnings back into the airport, and the field has one staffed control tower, a terminal, a restaurant, three paved runways and a water runway. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/our-community/ypk-airport
- The airport was built as a Vancouver satellite: the Airport Society records the May 1961 federal announcement of a new airport at Pitt Meadows to serve as a satellite to Vancouver International Airport, on a 704-acre (285-hectare) site with a seaplane base on the adjacent Fraser River. https://www.flyypk.ca/about
- Council is on record against one large proposal: the City's planning projects page states Council is unanimously and strongly opposed to Canadian Pacific Kansas City's (CPKC) proposed Industrial Logistics Park. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/our-community/city-planning-projects

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The Golden Ears Bridge is the south exit: TransLink describes it as a 1-kilometre, six-lane bridge over the Fraser River connecting Surrey and Langley to Maple Ridge and Pitt Meadows, opened in 2009 to replace the Albion Ferry, with protected bike-pedestrian lanes in both directions. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/maintenance-and-upgrade-program/bridge-projects
- Golden Ears Way is being widened at the city's east edge: detailed design is underway to take Golden Ears Way between Lougheed Highway and 210 Street from two lanes to four, with $20 million secured in TransLink's 2026 Business Plan. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/maintenance-and-upgrade-program/bridge-projects
- The commuter rail stop is Pitt Meadows Station on the West Coast Express, TransLink's weekday train between Waterfront Station in downtown Vancouver and Mission City; the 2025 OCP update designates the lands beside Pitt Meadows and Maple Meadows stations as Transit-Oriented Areas. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/west-coast-express

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Start with the dike map for the lot's drainage area: the City publishes Pitt Meadows North and South Diking Maps from the provincial Lower Mainland Dike Inventory, and which of the four drainage areas a lot sits in decides which pump station and floodboxes serve it. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/node/163
- Read the title for a flood covenant, not just the bylaw: a City report on a Sturgeon Slough Road site records a registered covenant requiring a minimum flood construction level of 5.2 metres, with the site to be filled from about 2 metres elevation to meet it. https://pub-pittmeadows.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=17999
- Airport parcels show how the levels stack: the City's airport report records a covenant requiring buildings other than hangars to meet a minimum flood construction level of 5.33 metres, or the City bylaw requirement of 5.75 metres depending on use — a lot-specific figure the bylaw's general 5.15-metre urban level does not tell you. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/docs/city-hall/hearings/Staff%20Report%20as%20presented%20on%20March%205%2C%202024.pdf
- Lowland soils need checking as well as water levels: the OCP warns that development in the lowlands must consider deep peat and bog soil conditions that can require considerable fill and/or preloading before development. https://www.pittmeadows.ca/sites/default/files/docs/our-community/community-plan/section_4_-_policies_-_4_1_natural_environment.pdf
