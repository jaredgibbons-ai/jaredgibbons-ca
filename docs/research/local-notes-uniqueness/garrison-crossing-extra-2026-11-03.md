# Garrison Crossing — Extra Local Notes
Page: garrison-crossing (Garrison Crossing; neighbourhood page).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Garrison Crossing is a 153-acre redevelopment on former Canadian Forces Base (CFB) Chilliwack land in the City of Chilliwack, and that former-base origin is the boundary fact that defines the neighbourhood. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- Canada Lands Company acquired the property, formerly part of CFB Chilliwack, from the federal government in 2011, and developed it in cooperation with the City of Chilliwack. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- The neighbourhood's central boulevard links to a network of bikeways, pathways and nature trails, and to the retail centre called Garrison Village. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- The City's Sardis-Vedder parks list places Garrison-named parks inside this area's park set, including All Sappers Memorial Park, Chehalis Park, Mitchell Gardens and the Garrison Woodlot nature park. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=1754&dowhat=areaView&plID=165&backTo&areaID=6
- The Sardis Neighbourhood Plan treats Garrison Crossing as the area to the south of Sardis, connected along the Vedder Corridor, rather than as part of the Sardis neighbourhood itself. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=6230

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The stock is a deliberate mix of refurbished base housing and new housing: Garrison Crossing includes more than 1,700 residential housing units, comprising refurbished single-family homes, new and restored townhouses and condominium apartments. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- Permanent Married Quarters (PMQs) that originally existed on the site were refurbished, with care taken to preserve the residences' original fir floors. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- The community was modelled on the principles of new urbanism and was intentionally designed to be pedestrian-friendly and to encourage travel without a vehicle. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- In 2010 Garrison Crossing secured official Stage 2 Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design – Neighbourhood Development (LEED-ND) certification under a pilot program led by the U.S. Green Building Council. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Heritage on this site is a design fact a buyer inherits, not a marketing line: Canada Lands preserved elements of the former military base, built the Legacy Walk honouring those who lived and worked at CFB Chilliwack from 1942 to its closure in 1995, and renovated Sappers Cenotaph Park on the eastern edge of the site with local veterans. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- Which Official Community Plan (OCP) neighbourhood-plan schedule covers a specific Garrison Crossing lot must be read on the City's OCP page and webmap, because the OCP lists the Sardis plan as Schedule B5 and the South Vedder plan as Schedule B6 as separate schedules. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2387
- City-wide zoning, suite and small-scale multi-unit housing rules for Chilliwack are in full in the chilliwack-houses-for-sale note — see the chilliwack-houses-for-sale note. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Was this really a military base? **A:** Yes, the housing area was part of Canadian Forces Base Chilliwack. Canada Lands states it acquired the property from the federal government in 2011, refurbished Permanent Married Quarters already on the site, and built the Legacy Walk to honour the men and women who lived and worked at the base from its inception in 1942 to its closure in 1995. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- **Q:** Is Garrison Crossing finished building? **A:** Canada Lands describes Garrison Crossing as a now-completed development. Its own project page states the completed community includes more than 1,700 residential units in refurbished single-family homes, new and restored townhouses and condominium apartments, plus the Garrison Village retail centre, so a buyer here is buying resale housing in a completed master plan rather than a future phase. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- **Q:** Can children walk to school inside the neighbourhood? **A:** The community was designed so residents can walk to the local elementary school, community centre and Garrison Village by the central boulevard, bikeways, pathways and nature trails, per Canada Lands. Catchment assignment is still by home address in School District No. 33 (SD33), so confirm the exact address in the SD33 School Locator rather than relying on walking distance alone. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69 https://sd33.bc.ca/index.php/find-your-school
- **Q:** How is this different from the Sardis streets next door? **A:** The difference is the single master plan. Sardis grew street by street under the Sardis Neighbourhood Plan, while Garrison Crossing was one 153-acre redevelopment that kept refurbished base houses, added townhouses and apartments, and centred the area on a boulevard and the Garrison Village retail centre, all under new urbanism principles stated by Canada Lands. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69 https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=6230

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Walking and cycling are the designed first mode inside Garrison Crossing: the central boulevard links to bikeways, pathways and nature trails to the elementary school, community centre and Garrison Village. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- The Sardis plan describes a frequent bus line on the Vedder Corridor, stated in the plan as 15-minute service, connecting Sardis to downtown, Garrison Crossing and the Canada Education Park to the south. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=6230
- Route 51 Vedder, in the BC Transit Chilliwack Rider's Guide, runs the Vedder Road corridor between downtown Chilliwack and the University of the Fraser Valley Chilliwack campus, the corridor a Garrison Crossing rider uses to leave the neighbourhood. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/chw_rg_jan2024-V1.1.pdf https://www.bctransit.com/chilliwack/schedules-and-maps/route-overview/?route=51&schedule=2024-07-05

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the title of a refurbished PMQ carefully: the homes were base housing refurbished in a single redevelopment, so a buyer should have the title, any covenants and the building's renovation permits reviewed rather than treat the house as a conventional resale of its apparent age. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- Check which OCP schedule and development permit areas apply to the specific lot on the City's OCP page and webmap, because the OCP's Schedule D permit areas include drinking water, hillside, riparian and residential form-and-character areas that apply differently by location. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2387
- Check Peach Creek and Vedder River edge lots against the OCP's Schedule D-3 Riparian Area development permit area, listed in the OCP's Schedule D contents. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2387
- City-wide Chilliwack floodplain and flood construction level checks are in full in the chilliwack-houses-for-sale note — see the chilliwack-houses-for-sale note. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2387
