# River Springs Houses — Extra Local Notes
Page: river-springs-coquitlam-houses-for-sale (River Springs; houses).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- River Springs is a house pocket on the east bank of the Coquitlam River in northeast Coquitlam, and the City publishes no official boundary for the multiple listing service sub-area name. https://coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Hockaday-Park-46
- Shaughnessy Street is the main street through the pocket, David Avenue runs along its south side, the Coquitlam River and Coquitlam River Park are on the west, and the land rises toward the Burke Mountain and Port Coquitlam side, around Oxford Street and Lincoln Avenue, on the east and north. https://www.coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Coquitlam-River-Park-28
- The addressed river anchor is Coquitlam River Park at 1190 Shaughnessy Street, and the City's Hockaday Park page is the City page that names the River Springs area and its river setting directly. https://coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Hockaday-Park-46

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Houses on the Shaughnessy Street pocket are the stock for this page; no City, School District No. 43 (Coquitlam) or BC Assessment public page read for this note states a typical build decade, lot size, subdivision name, developer or house style for River Springs, and no master-plan developer claim is made, because no City page read names one for this pocket. https://coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Hockaday-Park-46
- The documented age in this pocket is in its street names, not a build-era statement: the City records that several River Springs streets were named after 1930s Hollywood visitors to the river, when the Oxbow Ranch on the Coquitlam River was renamed Steelhead Lodge, a fishing lodge visited by film stars of the era. https://coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Hockaday-Park-46
- The surrounding plan context is older established northeast Coquitlam, not a new neighbourhood plan area: the Northeast plan's own neighbourhood plans — Lower Hyde Creek, Upper Hyde Creek and Smiling Creek — are on Burke Mountain, north of River Springs. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/4149/How-Development-Happens-PDF

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide zoning, small-scale multi-unit housing, 2026 tax and utility figures and Speculation and Vacancy Tax applicability are in full in the scott-creek-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note; this note covers the river and northeast rules that touch Shaughnessy Street lots. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit
- Watercourse permit distance is wider here than in City Centre: land within 50 metres of the top of bank or top of ravine bank of a watercourse in the Northeast Coquitlam Area Plan falls in the Watercourse Protection Development Permit Area. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/316/Watercourse-Protection-Development-Permit-Application-Guide-PDF
- A Riparian Areas Protection Regulation assessment can be required for development within 30 metres of a stream, or within 30 metres of a ravine under 60 metres wide, to set the watercourse setback where no development or land alteration is allowed. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/316/Watercourse-Protection-Development-Permit-Application-Guide-PDF
- Floodplain siting under Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996 section 519: no building within 30 metres of the natural boundary of the Coquitlam River, and no building below the flood construction level in the designated floodplain shown on Schedule G. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/1170/Part-5-General-Regulations-PDF
- Plan coverage: the pocket sits in the City's northeast sector under the Northeast Coquitlam Area Plan, formerly Chapter 11 of Citywide Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 3479, 2001, now carried with the City's plans into the Interim Official Community Plan, Bylaw No. 5511, 2026 — check current. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1436/Official-Community-Plan-Review

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is River Springs a new master-planned community? **A:** No City page read for this note describes it that way. The City treats it as an existing river pocket on Shaughnessy Street under the Northeast Coquitlam Area Plan, with a documented 1930s history: the Oxbow Ranch became Steelhead Lodge, a fishing lodge visited by Hollywood stars, and several streets here were named after those visitors. The newer Burke Mountain neighbourhood plans — Lower Hyde Creek, Upper Hyde Creek and Smiling Creek — are separate plan areas north of this pocket. https://coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Hockaday-Park-46
- **Q:** Which schools serve a River Springs house? **A:** No School District No. 43 school building sits inside the pocket itself in the Ministry directory. Schools listed around it are Leigh Elementary at 1230 Soball Street on Burke Mountain, École Coquitlam River Elementary at 4250 Shaughnessy Street in Port Coquitlam south across David Avenue, Glen Elementary at 3064 Glen Drive across the river, and Pinetree Secondary at 3000 Pinewood Avenue. None is confirmed as the catchment school for a specific River Springs address; catchment is by permanent home address in the district School Locator. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/043
- **Q:** How close can I build to the Coquitlam River? **A:** Two separate checks apply on the Shaughnessy Street side. Zoning Bylaw section 519 bars buildings within 30 metres of the natural boundary of the Coquitlam River and below the flood construction level in designated floodplain. Separately, land within 50 metres of the top of bank or ravine bank in the Northeast Coquitlam Area Plan is in the Watercourse Protection Development Permit Area, so altering land there needs that permit before work starts. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/1170/Part-5-General-Regulations-PDF
- **Q:** Does the Coquitlam River flood here? **A:** The City designs Coquitlam River flood levels on the 200-year design flood and publishes Year 2200 floodplain maps with flood elevations including freeboard, and its river park page states the river is at its highest and most dynamic from November to March with heavy precipitation and spring thaw. That is a lot-level check: read the specific Shaughnessy Street lot against the City's floodplain maps and the Schedule G floodplain, rather than relying on the pocket's elevation generally. https://www.coquitlam.ca/224/Floodplain-Dikes
- **Q:** What is Coquitlam River Park, at the pocket's west side? **A:** A City park at 1190 Shaughnessy Street with a network of forest walking trails extending 9.8 kilometres within 165 acres of municipal parkland along the Coquitlam River, with fishing and trails listed as its features. Hockaday Park, at the north end of the pocket on the river, adds a viewing platform, a small sand beach, interpretive signage, a playground with climbing structures and swings, and open grass. https://www.coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Coquitlam-River-Park-28

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the Year 2200 Floodplain Maps and the Coquitlam, Fraser and Pitt Rivers Floodplain Mapping Report, August 2017, for the specific lot: the City's flood analysis accounts for climate change and lists flood elevations for 2014, 2100 and 2200 events including freeboard, but the maps do not replace a lot survey. https://www.coquitlam.ca/224/Floodplain-Dikes
- Check dikes and pump stations on the City's Coquitlam Pump Station and Dike Map: the City states several dikes protect low-lying properties and those properties require pump stations to move water into the river when levels are high. https://www.coquitlam.ca/224/Floodplain-Dikes
- Check the Watercourse Protection Development Permit Area at the wider northeast distance, 50 metres from top of bank or ravine bank, and whether a Riparian Areas Protection Regulation assessment is triggered within 30 metres of the river or a ravine. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/316/Watercourse-Protection-Development-Permit-Application-Guide-PDF
