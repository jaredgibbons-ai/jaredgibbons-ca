# BC Land Buying Rules — Extra Local Notes
Page: bc-land-buying-rules (British Columbia; land / topic page).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- This page covers buying land anywhere in British Columbia: vacant parcels, acreages, farm land, Crown land and bare-land strata lots. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/
- Title is registered with the Land Title and Survey Authority of BC (LTSA); BC's Torrens register is conclusive evidence that the person recorded on title is the owner, subject to statutory exceptions. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/land-title-and-survey-authority-ltsa-information
- Property transfer tax is calculated on fair market value (land and improvements) on the day the transfer is registered with the Land Title Office. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Fee simple is the standard freehold: a title lists the registered fee simple holder, the legal description and the parcel identifier (PID). https://ltsa.ca/property-owners/how-can-i/search-for-a-title/
- Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) land is a provincial zone where agriculture is the priority use, run by the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) under the Agricultural Land Commission Act. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/02036_01
- Crown land is usually used, not bought: upland Crown land can be sold at market price, but aquatic land including foreshore cannot be sold under the Land Act, so use is by lease (up to 30 years), licence of occupation (normally 10 years) or temporary licence (up to two years). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/crown-land-water/crown-land/crown-land-uses/commercial-uses/general-commercial

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- ALR land cannot take a non-farm use or be subdivided unless the ALC Act, the regulations or an ALC permission under section 25 allows it; a registrar must not accept a subdivision plan the Act does not permit. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/02036_01
- Every subdivision needs an approving officer: the municipal officer inside a municipality, and a Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure district officer for rural land. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/driving-and-transportation/funding-engagement-permits/subdividing-land/rural_subdivision_guide.pdf
- The Land Owner Transparency Registry (LOTR) requires a transparency declaration from the transferee on every application to register an interest in land, plus a transparency report when a corporation, trust or partnership is involved. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/land-owner-transparency-registry
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) declaration duty applies only to property classed as residential in a designated taxable area on the Province's published list. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Does property transfer tax apply to bare land? **A:** Yes. The tax is based on the fair market value of the property — land and improvements — on the day the transfer is registered, and the general rate applies to all taxable transactions, so a vacant parcel is taxed on its land value at the same tiers. Home-based exemptions are aimed at dwellings, not bare lots. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/
**Q:** Can a non-Canadian buy vacant land in BC? **A:** The federal prohibition does not apply to vacant land. Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC) states that effective March 27, 2023 the Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act does not apply to vacant land; the ban on the homes it does cover was extended to January 1, 2027. BC's foreign-buyer transfer tax is separate. https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/housing-research/consultations/prohibition-purchase-residential-property-non-canadians-act?guide=EXCEPTIONS%20CONTAINED%20IN%20THE%20ACT%20AND%20REGULATIONS
**Q:** Can I subdivide ALR land after buying it? **A:** Only with permission. The ALC Act bars non-farm use and subdivision of agricultural land unless the Act, the regulations or a Commission permission allows it, and the registrar of titles cannot accept a plan that subdivides ALR land contrary to the Act. Treat ALR subdivision as an ALC application, never a right of purchase. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/02036_01
**Q:** Who approves a rural subdivision? **A:** A provincial approving officer. The Province's Guide to Rural Subdivision Approvals states every subdivision must be approved by an approving officer appointed under the Land Title Act, and for rural subdivisions that officer sits in a Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure district office and checks provincial acts, regulations and local bylaws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/driving-and-transportation/funding-engagement-permits/subdividing-land/rural_subdivision_guide.pdf
**Q:** Must I disclose who really owns the land? **A:** Yes, by declaration on every registration. LOTR is a publicly searchable registry of beneficial ownership of BC land, administered by the LTSA; the transferee files a transparency declaration with any application to register an interest in land, and corporations, trusts and partnerships may also file a report naming interest holders. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/land-owner-transparency-registry

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- A title search cannot run by street address: the LTSA says the nine-digit PID is the best search key, found through ParcelMap BC or BC Assessment. https://ltsa.ca/property-owners/how-can-i/search-for-a-title/
- A title shows current owners, legal description, PID and registered charges — not past owners. https://ltsa.ca/property-owners/how-can-i/search-for-a-title/
- BC Assessment's database supplies the PID, assessed value and classification behind the tax bill, and links onward to LTSA records through myLTSA. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/look-up-property-assessment/BC-Online
- The provincial Site Registry records contaminated-site investigations and remediation, but the Province warns that no record is no assurance a site is clean. http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/site-remediation/site-information

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- General property transfer tax (Province's page, read 2026-11-03): 1% of fair market value up to and including $200,000; 2% on the portion over $200,000 to $2,000,000; 3% above $2,000,000; plus a further 2% on residential value over $3,000,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/
- LTSA fee listing, April 1, 2026: registering an indefeasible title (a transfer of title) costs $83.82. https://ltsa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Fee-Listing-April-1-2026.pdf
- Crown commercial leases rent at 8% of land value in urban areas, minimum $850 a year plus GST. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/crown-land-water/crown-land/crown-land-uses/commercial-uses/general-commercial

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Title information has three routes: an LTSA Explorer account, a State of Title Certificate by mail, or a registry agent ordering it for you. https://ltsa.ca/property-owners/how-can-i/search-for-a-title/
- Closing is a registration sequence: transfer registered at the Land Title Office, transfer tax assessed on the registration-date value, LOTR declaration filed with the same application. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/ https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/real-estate-bc/land-owner-transparency-registry

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Run the ALR check first on any rural parcel, on the ALC map, because ALR status limits use and subdivision whatever the municipal zoning says. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps
- Order the title and read every charge; use BC Assessment for the PID and classification, and the Site Registry for contamination records, remembering its no-record warning. https://ltsa.ca/property-owners/how-can-i/search-for-a-title/ http://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/site-remediation/site-information
- Check flood, well and septic questions lot by lot with the local government and the Province's well records service; no provincial land page maps one parcel's flood risk. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/water/groundwater-wells-aquifers/information-for-property-owners/well-records-registration

## 10. HOW IT COMPARES

| Purchase path | Who approves a subdivision or non-farm use | Fee simple available | Title at the LTSA |
|---|---|---|---|
| ALR land | Agricultural Land Commission permission | Yes | Yes |
| Non-ALR private land | Approving officer, municipal or provincial | Yes | Yes |
| Crown land | Province, by disposition or tenure under the Land Act | Only on a provincial sale; foreshore cannot be sold | Yes, once granted |

- All three paths share one registry and one transfer-tax system; they differ in who must say yes before the land's use changes. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/
