# Sardis East / Vedder — Questions for Jared

Five questions only Jared can answer, for a voice memo. Muse does not answer these.

## Questions

1. In your deals, do buyers treat Vedder and Garrison Crossing as part of Sardis, or as their own market? When a listing sits near the Sardis–Vedder boundary, which name do you market it under, and why?
2. Who buys on the east side — families aiming for Sardis Secondary and Sardis Elementary catchments, military families with a Garrison Crossing connection, or commuters who want the Route 66 stop at Luckakuck Way? What are they leaving behind?
3. What sells fast and what sits — Garrison Crossing townhouses and refurbished PMQ houses, older Vedder Road detached homes, or the newer South Vedder infill? Where do buyers draw the line on Vedder Road traffic noise?
4. What surprises buyers — Vedder River sediment-removal trail closures at Vedder Park, the Tzeachten boundary along Keith Wilson Road, catchment lines that split one street between Sardis and Vedder schools, or Luckakuck Way congestion?
5. One story: an east Sardis or Vedder deal that taught you something — a street, complex or pocket you now recommend or avoid, and why.

## Shot list

1. Sardis Park pond and the 655-metre loop trail from School Lane — the east Sardis neighbourhood landmark, shot across the pond toward the playground side.
2. The Vedder Bridge crossing at Vedder Park, looking south up Vedder Road toward Promontory — the river gateway between Sardis and Vedder.
3. Cheamview Crescent in Garrison Crossing, shot down the curb-less PMQ loop — the preserved base streetscape that makes Garrison look different from every other Chilliwack subdivision.
