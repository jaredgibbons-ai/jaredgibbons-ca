# Royal Heights Area Guide — Extra Local Notes
Page: royal-heights-guide (Royal Heights; area guide).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- The City-defined core of Royal Heights is the Royal Heights Park neighbourhood: Royal Heights Park to the north, 116 Street to the east, properties along 96A Avenue to the south and Princess Drive to the west — 127 residential lots in northwest Surrey. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf
- The City tags the area "Whalley and City Centre" on its park pages, not a separate town centre — Royal Heights Park's address is 9797 115A Street, in Whalley. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/royal-heights-park
- The guide area's transit edge is 120 Street (Scott Road): the neighbourhood streets in the City's own map run from Princess Drive and Crown Crescent east to 116 Street, and the Scott Road corridor one block further east carries the R6 RapidBus — see section 8. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf
- Guide-level history in two sentences: the surrounding subdivision was built out as Royal Heights Park was established in the mid-1960s, and the City records most homes in the park neighbourhood as built from the late 1950s to the early 1970s, with only 11 percent built in the last 20 years. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/royal-heights-park https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The defining stock fact is the downzoned enclave: all 127 lots in the Royal Heights Park neighbourhood sat under one Comprehensive Development (CD) Zone, Bylaw No. 16419, which capped house size and height — stock detail in full in the royal-heights-surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the royal-heights-surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf
- Outside the enclave the guide area is standard northwest Surrey detached stock designated Urban in the Official Community Plan (OCP), with the park itself — over 2.2 hectares, forest in the west and open field in the east — as the centrepiece the streets curve around. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/royal-heights-park
- The school anchor is Royal Heights Elementary, Kindergarten to Grade 7, at 11665 97 Avenue — inside the neighbourhood, not on an arterial. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/school/03636077

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide Surrey zoning and Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) rules are written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. The Royal Heights difference is the enclave history below.
- Royal Heights Park is one of only five downzoned areas in Surrey: the CD zones were created by resident petition to preserve neighbourhood character by limiting maximum house size and height, alongside St. Helen's Park (2006), Crescent Park Annex (2008), Kwomais Point Park Area 1 (2015) and the Bolivar Park neighbourhood (2016). https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf
- The July 2024 provincial housing amendments reached inside the enclave anyway: secondary suites, garden suites, coach houses, duplexes and houseplexes became permitted in the Royal Heights Park neighbourhood, while the CD cap on single-family house size and height stayed unchanged until the 2026 rezoning. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** What zone is a Royal Heights Park lot in now? **A:** Urban Residential (R3), since April 2026. The 127 lots were rezoned from the Comprehensive Development Zone (CD Bylaw No. 16419) to R3 by Surrey Zoning By-law Amendment Bylaw, 2026, No. 21930, after owners petitioned the City in January 2025 and 68 percent of properties supported the change in the City's survey. Council considered final adoption at its April 13, 2026 meeting, with no public hearing because the rezoning complies with the Official Community Plan. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/council-minutes/regular-council-public-hearing-minutes-april-13-2026
- **Q:** Why were houses here smaller than in other Surrey neighbourhoods? **A:** Because residents asked the City to cap them. In February 2008 the neighbourhood was downzoned to limit the maximum permitted size and height of single-family houses to preserve its character, and in January 2012 the CD zone was amended again over double-height floor areas and covered decks. That 2008 cap — not the building era — is why rebuilds here stayed modest until the 2026 return to R3. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf
- **Q:** Which elementary school is in the neighbourhood? **A:** Royal Heights Elementary, at 11665 97 Avenue. The British Columbia Ministry school listing records it as a Kindergarten to Grade 7 school in School District 36 (Surrey). Catchments are set by the district by address, so a home on Princess Drive versus 116 Street should still be confirmed in the district's school locator before an offer depends on it. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/school/03636077
- **Q:** How do I get downtown without driving Scott Road? **A:** Use the R6 Scott Road RapidBus on 120 Street. TransLink's R6 runs from Scott Road Station to Newton Exchange along the Scott Road corridor at the area's east edge, and local routes 314 and 316 run through the 96 Avenue and 116 Street streets inside the guide area toward Surrey Central. Frequencies are stated on TransLink's route pages, not estimated here. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/r6-scott-road-rapidbus

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The park was renovated in 2016 — open field, picnic areas, playground and walking paths — and its looping gravel trail continues into the west forest, with trail entries at the north ends of Crown Crescent and Princess Drive and the south end of Royal Crescent. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/royal-heights-park
- There is no library branch in Royal Heights: the City Centre Branch at 10350 University Drive is the branch the City lists for the Whalley side, and the district's family-of-schools grouping pairs Royal Heights Elementary with L.A. Matheson Secondary at 9484 122 Street for the secondary years. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/036
- The 2025 owner survey is a due-diligence document: 93 percent of enclave properties responded, 86 percent knew the area had been downzoned, and support for larger houses was highest among owners who bought after the 2008 downzoning — a split worth knowing before assuming neighbours welcome a maximum-size R3 rebuild. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R017.pdf
- Surrey Memorial Hospital, 13750 96 Avenue, is the hospital for this side of Surrey — reached by driving south on 120 Street or 128 Street, not through the neighbourhood's crescents. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Surrey/surrey-memorial-hospital

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The R6 Scott Road RapidBus is the area's rapid transit: TransLink runs it between Scott Road Station and Newton Exchange, putting the King George corridor and Newton within one ride of the 120 Street stops at the guide area's east edge. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/r6-scott-road-rapidbus
- Route 314 runs from Surrey Central through the 96 Avenue corridor, and route 316 runs Surrey Central to Scottsdale via 96 Avenue, 116 Street and Scott Road — the two local routes that actually enter the Royal Heights street grid. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt314.pdf https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt316.pdf
- Driving, the exits are 96 Avenue west to Delta and River Road, and 120 Street (Scott Road) south to Newton or north to Scott Road Station and the Pattullo Bridge corridor. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the lot in the City's mapping (COSMOS) against Surrey's Development Permit Areas (DPAs) before designing a rebuild: the Hazard Lands (Steep Slopes) DPA covers parcels with slopes over 20 percent gradient, or within 10 metres downslope or 30 metres of the top of such a slope — relevant on the bench lots above the Fraser flats at the area's north and west edges. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/surrey-official-community-plan/development-permit-areas
- Confirm the title predates or postdates the zone change correctly: a Royal Heights Park lot bought on CD Bylaw No. 16419 assumptions (size and height caps) is now an R3 lot — verify the current zone on the specific parcel rather than relying on a pre-2026 information sheet. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2026-R037.pdf
- The City's coastal floodplain page does not name Royal Heights among Surrey's lowland floodplain areas — the bench position is the reason — but flood status is still confirmed per property, not assumed from the neighbourhood name. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
