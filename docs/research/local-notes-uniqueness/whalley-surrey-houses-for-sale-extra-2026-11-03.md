# Whalley Houses — Extra Local Notes
Page: whalley-surrey-houses-for-sale (Whalley; houses).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Whalley sits in Surrey's northwest corner, with the Fraser River on its northern and western edges, Newton to the south, and an eastern border that runs much of its length along 144 Street. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- The downtown core inside Whalley is called Surrey City Centre in City of Surrey planning, but the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board sub-area name for the whole area is Whalley — "Surrey City Centre" is not a board sub-area name. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- The name comes from Arthur Whalley, who settled near the corner of King George Boulevard and 108 Avenue in 1925, when the crossroads was known as Whalley's Corner. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- Whalley surrounds City Centre, so a detached-house search here is really two searches: older residential blocks ringing a tower core around Surrey Central and King George stations. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Detached houses are the older stock outside the core, while the core itself holds the emerging residential high rises, offices and research facilities the City describes for City Centre. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- The first City Centre plan dates to 1991, and the City states that development after it was sporadic and not always focused around SkyTrain — which is why house blocks and towers sit side by side here rather than in separate districts. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- Two universities anchor the area — Simon Fraser University's Surrey campus and Kwantlen Polytechnic University — alongside Surrey City Hall and the City Centre Library, so rental demand near the stations is part of the house market's context. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The City Centre Plan, approved by Council in 2017, is the land-use plan that governs what can replace a Whalley house near the core, and an update to it is underway. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- The plan update enables additional transit-oriented development around SkyTrain stations and allows growth in Bolivar Heights, including a mixed-use neighbourhood centre — both change what a nearby house lot may become. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is Surrey City Centre a different area from Whalley? **A:** No — City Centre is the downtown core inside the larger Whalley community. The City lists Whalley as the community that surrounds Surrey's City Centre in North Surrey, and the real estate board's sub-area name for the whole area is Whalley, not Surrey City Centre. A listing labelled either way can describe the same streets, so compare by address and by which side of the tower core the house sits on. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- **Q:** Which SkyTrain stations serve a Whalley house? **A:** Four Expo Line stations serve Whalley, and no other Surrey community has any. The City lists Scott Road at Scott Road and King George Boulevard, Gateway at 108 Avenue and University Drive, Surrey Central at City Parkway and 102 Avenue, and King George at King George Boulevard and 100 Avenue. Which station a house walks to depends on which side of the core it sits on, so check the specific address rather than the area name. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey
- **Q:** Could the house I buy near a station be redeveloped or overshadowed? **A:** Possibly, because the City is updating the City Centre Plan specifically to enable more transit-oriented development around the SkyTrain stations. The same update establishes a Central Business District and identifies a future Entertainment District, which Council endorsed work on in October 2025. Before buying for quiet or for redevelopment value, check the lot's current plan designation and the update's maps, because both the upside and the construction around a house here follow the plan. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- **Q:** Which schools serve Whalley houses? **A:** School District 36 (Surrey) sets the catchments, and they are address-specific. The provincial school list records A.H.P. Matthew Elementary at 13367 97 Avenue in this area, and a district locator run for that address returned A.H.P. Matthew Elementary and Queen Elizabeth Secondary. École Kwantlen Park Secondary at 10441 132 Street is also in the wider Whalley area. Run the exact address in the district locator before writing an offer that depends on a school. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/036
- **Q:** Is a Whalley house in the floodplain? **A:** The City Centre and Whalley core are upland, away from the floodplain areas the City maps. Surrey's coastal floodplain stretches along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers toward Cloverdale and Newton and includes the Little Campbell River area in the south — a different part of the city. A Whalley buyer should still confirm the individual lot on the City's mapping, because the floodplain page describes areas, not single parcels. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The Chuck Bailey Recreation Centre in Whalley City Centre is being expanded, with new indoor and outdoor amenities and more recreation, sports and arts programming — the City lists it as coming soon. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- Holland Park, Green Timbers, Bear Creek Park and Whalley Athletic Park are the major parks the City names inside Whalley — Green Timbers is an urban forest, not a neighbourhood park. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- The Downtown Surrey Business Improvement Association, formed in 2003 and previously the Whalley Business Improvement Association, has more than 1,400 members and publishes the area's business directory and restaurant guide. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- Scott Road Station has the Park and Ride location the City lists for Surrey, and King George Station has the bike parkade location — useful detail for a house bought for a park-and-ride commute. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey
- The City's bus-exchange list for Surrey names Surrey Central Station, King George Station and Gateway Station — the exchange name TransLink and the City use at the core is Surrey Central Station. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Property tax in Whalley is the city-wide Surrey tax — there is no separate Whalley rate. The full 2026 rate, parcel taxes and utility figures are carried in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5 percent penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5 percent penalty after September 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies in Whalley because the Province lists the City of Surrey as a designated taxable area, so every residential owner here files the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Whalley is the only Surrey community on SkyTrain today, with four Expo Line stations: Scott Road, Gateway, Surrey Central and King George. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey
- TransLink's R1 King George Boulevard RapidBus runs from Newton Exchange to Guildford Exchange through Surrey Central and King George stations, linking Whalley houses to Guildford and Newton without a car. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- The City lists Surrey Central Station, King George Station and Gateway Station among Surrey's bus exchanges, so most Whalley bus trips start at a station exchange rather than a roadside loop. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the lot against the City Centre Plan before valuing a Whalley house, because the plan update changes designations around the stations and in Bolivar Heights while the house is being marketed under today's zoning. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- Confirm the parcel on the City's COSMOS mapping for zoning, plan area and flood status — the City's floodplain page describes the coastal floodplain along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers, not individual Whalley lots, so it cannot clear a specific address. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
