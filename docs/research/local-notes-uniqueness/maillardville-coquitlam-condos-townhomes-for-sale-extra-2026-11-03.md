## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Maillardville is Coquitlam's oldest and most historic neighbourhood, and its attached stock fronts Brunette Avenue, the plan's commercial Main Street, with Laval Square and Heritage Square at Brunette Avenue and King Edward Street as the historic core. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- The Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan names four specific policy areas a condo or townhouse buyer will meet in documents: the Neighbourhood Centre, the Heritage Character Areas of Laval Square and Allard-LeBleu, Schoolhouse East, and Lougheed Brunette. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- The plan area's south edge is the Lougheed Highway and Brunette Interchange side, and its east-west greenway is planned to connect Braid Street SkyTrain Station, the Neighbourhood Centre and Laval Square. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The plan's attached-housing direction is a high-density, mixed-use Neighbourhood Centre on Brunette Avenue, set against low-rise residential districts around Laval Square and Allard-LeBleu — so the condo stock here is centre-fronting apartment and townhouse form, not plateau subdivisions. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- Heritage houses are part of the stock a buyer walks past, and one is built into a strata-era development: the Bédard Residence at 311 Laval Square, built in 1913 for a French-Canadian mill-worker family, was integrated into a larger residential development around Laval Square under a Heritage Revitalization Agreement, Bylaw No. 3949, 2008. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1021/Built-History-Coquitlams-Heritage-Homes
- The City's current building-permit list shows the small-scale end of today's attached stock on a Maillardville street: a Housing Choices quadruplex application at 358 Laval Street, filed March 2026. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/193/Current-Multiple-Family-Commercial-Industrial-and-Institutional-Building-Permit-Applications-PDF

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Heritage designation is a hard rule on named Maillardville properties: the City states a designated structure or property cannot be altered, moved or subdivided without a heritage alteration permit, and Maillardville addresses on the designated list include 830, 838 and 828 Laval Square and 1120, 1123 and 1432 Brunette Avenue. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1021/Built-History-Coquitlams-Heritage-Homes
- The Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan, Schedule A to Bylaw No. 4465, 2014, is the plan that governs what can be built next to an existing condo or townhouse here, including conservation of heritage buildings in the Laval Square and Allard-LeBleu Heritage Character Areas. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- The Neighbourhood Centre Design Guidelines (July 2008) set the form rules for new Brunette Avenue buildings a buyer will live beside, including the Brunette Avenue streetscape, Clock Tower Plaza, Woolridge Street and Adair Street sections. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/723/Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Centre-Design-Guidelines-PDF
- City-wide Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) and zoning detail is held in full in the coquitlam-condos-for-sale note in this batch — see that note; the Maillardville-specific overlay on top of it is the heritage and plan rules above. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Why do some Maillardville buildings face heritage rules and others do not? **A:** Because designation is property by property. The City has designated 27 buildings city-wide, and the Maillardville ones include Our Lady of Lourdes Church at 830 Laval Square, Saint Anne's Hall at 838 Laval Square, the Rectory at 828 Laval Square and the Ryan House (Place des Arts) at 1120 Brunette Avenue. A designated property cannot be altered, moved or subdivided without a heritage alteration permit, so check the specific address, not the street. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1021/Built-History-Coquitlams-Heritage-Homes
- **Q:** What is actually planned around the Brunette Avenue condos? **A:** More density in the centre, protected heritage at the edges. The Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan calls for a walkable, high-density mixed-use Neighbourhood Centre on Brunette Avenue, an additional 6,000 residents in the plan area in the draft-plan report, and conservation of heritage buildings in the Laval Square and Allard-LeBleu Heritage Character Areas. A buyer is purchasing into a centre that is planned to keep growing, beside streets that are planned not to change form. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- **Q:** Is there new attached housing being built in Maillardville now? **A:** At the small end, yes. The City's current multiple-family building-permit list, updated to October 1, 2026, records a Housing Choices development of one quadruplex (4 units) at 358 Laval Street, in the application stream since March 2026. That is the in-neighbourhood permit activity on the list read for this note; larger Brunette Avenue projects, if any, were not named on that list for a Maillardville address. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/193/Current-Multiple-Family-Commercial-Industrial-and-Institutional-Building-Permit-Applications-PDF
- **Q:** How do I get downtown from a Maillardville condo without a car? **A:** By bus to a SkyTrain station at either end. TransLink route 153 runs from Braid Station via Brunette Avenue — with stops at Bernatchey Street, Blue Mountain Street, Lougheed Highway, Lebleu Street, Nelson Street, Marmont Street, Laval Street and Schoolhouse Street in Maillardville — then Laurentian Crescent, Austin Avenue, Poirier Street, Como Lake Avenue and Mariner Way to Coquitlam Central Station. The timetable read is effective June 23, 2025. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/207/tt153.pdf
- **Q:** What is the French-Canadian history a buyer keeps hearing about? **A:** It is the founding story, and it is documented in the buildings. The City's heritage page describes the Bédard Residence at 311 Laval Square, built in 1913, as one of the few remaining French-Canadian mill-worker houses in the core of the original French-Canadian community at Laval Square, and Place des Arts at 1120 Brunette Avenue is the former Fraser Mills manager's residence, Ryan House. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1021/Built-History-Coquitlams-Heritage-Homes

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Place des Arts, the arts centre and music school at 1120 Brunette Avenue, is itself a designated heritage building — the Ryan House, the former Fraser Mills manager's residence — so the cultural anchor on the condo strip is a protected 1900s house with a modern addition. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/407/Fraser-Mills-Managers-Residence---1120-Brunette-Avenue-PDF
- The City's Statements of Significance record that the manager's residence at 1120 Brunette Avenue was completed by 1908, faced the Mackin Residence across King Edward Avenue, and flanked the original northern entry to Fraser Mills. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/407/Fraser-Mills-Managers-Residence---1120-Brunette-Avenue-PDF
- Millside Elementary School at 1432 Brunette Avenue is a designated heritage property, which is why a working school building sits on the City's protected list in the middle of the condo corridor. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1021/Built-History-Coquitlams-Heritage-Homes
- The City's 2026 capital reporting puts Phase 3 of the Southwest Austin Heights Sewer Main inside the Maillardville sewer catchment area, built to support current and future development of Austin Heights — sewer capacity work is part of this area's growth story. https://www.coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/home/detail/1921
- The Neighbourhood Centre Design Guidelines name a Clock Tower Plaza and a Woolridge Street and Adair Street streetscape — small streets south of Brunette Avenue where new condo frontages are design-reviewed, not standard. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/723/Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Centre-Design-Guidelines-PDF
- The plan's greenway route is east-west: it is meant to connect Braid Street SkyTrain Station to the Maillardville Neighbourhood Centre, the residential areas north of Brunette Avenue and Laval Square. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check heritage status first on any Maillardville purchase: the City's Built History page lists the designated addresses and states the heritage alteration permit rule, and the Community Heritage Register Statements of Significance give the per-address history — a register listing is recognition, while designation is the legal protection. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1021/Built-History-Coquitlams-Heritage-Homes
- Check the Brunette Creek side on the City's environmental mapping: Schedule B Environmental Development Permit Areas maps watercourse and slope layers city-wide, and the page shows layers, not a lot-level conclusion for a specific strata lot. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/1527/Schedule-B---Environmental-Development-Permit-Areas-PDF?bidId=
- Check flood siting under Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996 section 519 and Schedule G, the designated 200-year floodplain of the Brunette, Coquitlam, Fraser and Pitt rivers — the Brunette River is one of the four named rivers, and Maillardville sits on its corridor. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/1170/Part-5-General-Regulations-PDF
