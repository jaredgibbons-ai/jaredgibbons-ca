## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Metrotown is Burnaby's downtown: the City's Burnaby 2050 project page describes Metrotown as the city's downtown and lists it with Brentwood, Lougheed and Edmonds as the four town centres that provide higher-density housing in urban settings. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/burnabys-official-community-plan-project
- The condo and townhome market sits on the tower lots around Metrotown Station and Metropolis at Metrotown, and on the residential streets that step down from the station — Kingsway, Central Boulevard, Wilson Avenue, Silver Avenue, Telford Avenue and Nelson Avenue are the addresses the City's current applications name. http://www.city.burnaby.bc.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2024-08/Major-Development-Projects.pdf
- Neighbourhood-shared facts (boundaries, house stock, community plan history in full): see the metrotown-burnaby-houses-for-sale note. City-wide Burnaby facts: see the vancouver-heights-burnaby-houses-for-sale note.

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Tower strata are the stock here: the City's current applications for the Metrotown plan area are high-rise towers — a 72-storey proposal at 4940 Kingsway, a 40-storey proposal at 5978 Wilson Avenue, and two towers of 32 and 28 storeys at 6470 and 6508 Silver Avenue — plus tower-base townhouse forms stated inside some applications. http://www.city.burnaby.bc.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2024-08/Major-Development-Projects.pdf
- Townhouse-form stock in Metrotown is mostly two forms: street-fronting townhouses at the base of a tower (the Wilson Avenue application states townhouse-style streetfronting units and the Telford Avenue application states townhouse-style units), and ground-oriented units on the residential streets west of the station. http://www.city.burnaby.bc.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2024-08/Major-Development-Projects.pdf
- Named existing towers with addresses and years built could not be confirmed on an allowed municipal, developer or strata-own source in this pass; see NOT CONFIRMED. The older towers' systems, not their names, are the buyer's real check.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026 (Bylaw No. 14815) gives the tower stock its districts: Low-Rise Apartment (R3, R4), Mid-Rise Apartment (R5, R6) and High-Rise Apartment 1 and 2 (R7, R8), with Townhouse District (R2) separate for ground-oriented forms. https://www.burnaby.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2026-07/Zoning-Bylaw-Consolidated-Without-Schedules.pdf
- A Metrotown strata cannot enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw: the Province states that as of November 24, 2022 all strata residential rental-restriction bylaws are invalid, only a 55-or-older age bylaw is allowed, and short-term rental bylaws remain allowed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- The contingency reserve fund (CRF) rule is a tower-budget fact: the Province states a strata must contribute a minimum of 10% of its annual operating fund to the CRF when it approves its budget (from November 1, 2023), which is the line to compare across Metrotown towers on their Form Bs. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- Burnaby 2050, adopted December 9, 2025, takes precedence over older community-plan land-use maps where they conflict; Metrotown's plan-area applications are still filed by the City under a "Metrotown" plan area on its major-projects list. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/strategies-and-plans/official-community-plan/community-plans

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Which station and buses serve a Metrotown condo?
**A:** Metrotown Station, on the Expo Line, is the station, and its bus exchange is where local routes such as the 116 to Edmonds Station begin. Since September 7, 2026 the R2 Marine–Willingdon RapidBus also terminates at Metrotown Station, running every 6 to 7 minutes at weekday peak and every 10 to 15 minutes off-peak to the North Shore via Hastings Street and Willingdon Avenue. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects

**Q:** Is the tallest Metrotown proposal really 72 storeys?
**A:** Yes, on the City's list: a rezoning at portion of 4940 Kingsway and 6446 and 6450 Nelson Avenue (REZ #24-16, applicant DIALOG) proposes a 72-storey high-rise with 169 market strata homes, 130 non-market rental homes and 170 market rental homes. The City records it at Third Reading on March 25, 2025, so it is not yet at Final Adoption — a buyer should not treat it as built stock. http://www.city.burnaby.bc.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2024-08/Major-Development-Projects.pdf

**Q:** What is Concord Metrotown, and is it finished?
**A:** Concord Metrotown is Concord Pacific's multi-tower master plan on the former Sears site between Nelson Avenue and McMurray Avenue; the developer's own disclosure statement names the Central Tower and West Tower at 4866 Kingsway and 4833 Metro Way, with strata lots numbered 1 to 722, and a Sixth Amendment dated August 14, 2026 — evidence the project was still in disclosure-amendment stage in 2026, not a completed file. https://concordpacificliving.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/CMCW-Sixth-Amendment-to-Disclosure-Statement-w-Exhibits_Filed-1.pdf

**Q:** Can a Metrotown strata ban long-term rentals?
**A:** No. The Province states that as of November 24, 2022 all strata residential rental-restriction bylaws are invalid, and no bylaw change was required. A Metrotown tower can still restrict short-term rentals by bylaw, so an investor's check is the short-term clause in the registered bylaws, not the rental clause. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals

**Q:** What does a Metrotown condo's utility bill look like?
**A:** A Multiple Family Dwelling in Burnaby pays the City's 2026 line of water $374.54 and sewer use $436.00 per year, with a 5% discount if the full payment is received before the due date. The strata's own levy is separate and is the figure on the Form B; the City line is the utility charge underneath it. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The R2 extension made Metrotown Station a two-region terminus on September 7, 2026: TransLink states the extended R2 runs from Park Royal to Metrotown Station all day, seven days a week, and replaced the peak-only 222 Willingdon Express, so the station now serves a North Shore commute, not only an Expo Line commute. https://buzzer.translink.ca/2026/09/major-r2-rapidbus-extension-provides-all-day-service-from-park-royal-to-metrotown/
- A new pedestrian rule is being built at the station: the City states construction began in May 2026 on Burnaby's first pedestrian scramble crossing, at Central Boulevard and the Metrotown underground parkade access, one of the city's busiest pedestrian areas. https://www.burnaby.ca/news-category/projects
- The City's residential water-meter program reaches tower strata too: the City states installation began in June 2026 with about 5,500 meter-ready homes, meters are mandatory and free, and flat-rate billing continues while data is collected. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/water-and-sewers/residential-water-meter-program
- Rental-replacement is part of the Silver Avenue file: the City's Silver Avenue application (REZ #25-12, 6470 and 6508 Silver Avenue) lists about 784 units including 83 rental-replacement units, which is the document a renter in the existing stock checks, not a marketing sheet. http://www.city.burnaby.bc.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2024-08/Major-Development-Projects.pdf

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Metrotown Station (Expo Line) is the rapid-transit anchor; its bus exchange is where the local routes for the south-side streets begin, including the 116 to Edmonds Station. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt116.pdf
- The R2 Marine–Willingdon RapidBus now terminates at Metrotown Station and runs 5 a.m. to 2 a.m. seven days a week, every 6 to 7 minutes at weekday peak and every 10 to 15 minutes off-peak. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- The 116 runs between Metrotown Station and Edmonds Station, so the south-side streets have a direct bus to Edmonds without the SkyTrain. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt116.pdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Construction adjacency is the dated risk here: the City list updated October 27, 2026 shows Third Reading and Final Adoption on that same date at 5978 Wilson Avenue, so the file beside any Metrotown tower can change status in a single council meeting — check the list, not a past summary. http://www.city.burnaby.bc.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2024-08/Major-Development-Projects.pdf
- For an older tower, the depreciation report is the systems check: the Province tracks updated depreciation report requirements effective July 1, 2024, and the October 27, 2025 expansion of who may prepare them, so a report dated before that change was prepared under the older rules. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- A buyer of a tower-base townhouse should confirm the strata plan: the unit is a strata lot in the tower strata, with the tower's parkade, elevators and envelope in its depreciation report, even though the unit's entrance is at street level.
