## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The Hazel Street towers are this page's defining stock: a run of concrete high-rises on one short street west of Metrotown Station, built in waves from the 1990s to the 2020s, which is why two Forest Glen condos of the same size can sit in buildings 30 years apart in age and systems. The earlier Forest Glen program verified the tower addresses street by street, but those verifications rested on listing sources banned under this brief, so the building-by-building list sits in NOT CONFIRMED rather than being restated here.
- The low-rise counterweight is on the residential streets south of the towers: per the earlier Forest Glen program, the Denbigh Avenue, Nelson Avenue and McMurray Avenue stock is low-rise and ground-oriented, including townhome-style ground units inside an apartment strata — a unit's listing form does not change the strata it belongs to.
- Named buildings, addresses and years built for the Hazel Street towers could not be confirmed on an allowed developer, municipal or strata-own source in this pass; earlier internal research held them from listing sources banned under this brief. See NOT CONFIRMED before relying on any tower list.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The towers and the low-rise stock fall in different districts of the Burnaby Zoning Bylaw 2026 (Bylaw No. 14815): its district list runs from Townhouse District (R2) and Low-Rise Apartment (R3, R4) to High-Rise Apartment 2 District (R8), so the Hazel Street towers and a Denbigh Avenue low-rise are not under the same district rules. https://www.burnaby.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2026-07/Zoning-Bylaw-Consolidated-Without-Schedules.pdf
- A Forest Glen strata cannot enforce a long-term rental-restriction bylaw: the Province states that as of November 24, 2022 all strata residential rental-restriction bylaws are invalid, only a 55-or-older age bylaw is allowed, and short-term rental bylaws remain allowed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- Depreciation reports are the age-split issue here: the Province's strata page tracks updated depreciation report requirements effective July 1, 2024, so a 1990s Hazel Street tower and a 2020s tower are read on the same five-year cycle but start from very different building systems. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- Burnaby 2050, adopted December 9, 2025, takes precedence over older community-plan land-use maps where they conflict, and Metrotown is one of the four town centres the City's community-plans page lists as providing higher-density housing in urban settings. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/strategies-and-plans/official-community-plan/community-plans

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Are the Hazel Street towers in Metrotown or Forest Glen?
**A:** Both labels are used, for different things. Listing feeds file the Hazel Street addresses under the Forest Glen board sub-area, while the City's planning documents treat the ground as part of the Metrotown town-centre area — the City's major-projects list has no Forest Glen plan area and files nearby applications under Metrotown. For strata documents, taxes and zoning, the City treatment governs; for a listing search, the board tag is what returns the towers. http://www.city.burnaby.bc.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2024-08/Major-Development-Projects.pdf

**Q:** Is there a townhouse complex in Forest Glen, or only towers?
**A:** No standalone named townhouse complex was confirmed on an allowed source in this pass. What the earlier program verified is townhome-style ground units inside a low-rise apartment strata on Denbigh Avenue, which are bought as strata lots in that apartment complex, with its Form B, bylaws and depreciation report — not as a townhouse complex purchase. Named-complex confirmation is listed in NOT CONFIRMED.

**Q:** Which SkyTrain station serves a Hazel Street condo?
**A:** Metrotown Station, on the Expo Line, is the station for the Hazel Street cluster, and since September 7, 2026 the R2 Marine–Willingdon RapidBus also terminates at Metrotown Station, running via Hastings Street and Willingdon Avenue. TransLink states the R2 runs every 6 to 7 minutes at peak and every 10 to 15 minutes off-peak, so a Hazel Street owner now has a one-seat bus to the North Shore as well as the Expo Line. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects

**Q:** What should I check first in a 1990s Hazel Street tower?
**A:** The depreciation report and the contingency reserve fund (CRF) first. The Province requires a strata's budget to contribute a minimum of 10% of the annual operating fund to the CRF (from November 1, 2023) and tracks depreciation report requirements updated July 1, 2024. In a tower of that age those two documents, not the listing, show how envelope, elevator and plumbing work has been funded. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The bus runs on Forest Glen's own streets, not only at the station: TransLink route 116 runs between Metrotown Station and Edmonds Station — the local route a Nelson Avenue or McMurray Avenue owner uses for trips that do not start at the SkyTrain. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt116.pdf
- Burnaby's water-meter program started with meter-ready homes in June 2026: the City states meters are mandatory, installed free outside the home, and flat-rate billing continues while usage data is collected — a strata owner may see a meter installed without any billing change. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/water-and-sewers/residential-water-meter-program
- A Metrotown pedestrian change is under construction at the station: the City states construction began in May 2026 on Burnaby's first pedestrian scramble crossing, at Central Boulevard and the Metrotown underground parkade access, one of the city's busiest pedestrian areas. https://www.burnaby.ca/news-category/projects

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Building age is the risk split on Hazel Street: towers from different decades carry different envelope, elevator and plumbing cycles, and the only published check is each strata's depreciation report and Form B — no municipal source publishes a tower-by-tower condition figure.
- Form and character of any new multi-family project here is governed by the Zoning Bylaw 2026's Form and Character Development Permit Area guidelines, which state a development permit is not required for permitted uses in the R1 Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing District or for R1 uses proposed in an R2 Townhouse district, except supportive housing — relevant to small infill beside the low-rise stock. https://bylaws.burnaby.ca/media/14000/14815.pdf
