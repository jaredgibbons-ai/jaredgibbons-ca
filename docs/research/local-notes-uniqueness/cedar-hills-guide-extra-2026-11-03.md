# Cedar Hills Area Guide — Extra Local Notes
Page: cedar-hills-guide (Cedar Hills; area guide).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Cedar Hills has no City-published neighbourhood boundary — the City of Surrey tags its Cedar Hills parks "Whalley and City Centre," and the name is anchored by Cedar Hills Elementary at 12370 98 Avenue and Cedar Hills Park at 12761 87A Avenue rather than by a plan map. https://www.surreyschools.ca/cedarhill https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/cedar-hills-park
- The commercial anchor is named in City documents: a 2026 City land-exchange document identifies the Cedar Hills Shopping Centre at 12830 96 Avenue, at the southeast corner of 96 Avenue and 128 Street, on a 14.6-acre site. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/26.06.26-City-of-Surrey-Land-Exchange-Final.pdf
- A 1976-era City planning file already calls this ground "the Cedar Hills neighbourhood": Land Use Contract (LUC) No. 118 regulated one commercial lot at the northeast corner of 128 Street and 96 Avenue, with the Cedar Hills shopping centre across 96 Avenue to the south. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7917-0057-00.pdf
- The east edge is 120 Street (Scott Road): a February 2025 City planning report for 12248 97 Avenue places the site about 400 metres from 120 Street and its R6 RapidBus stops. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0285-00.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Cedar Hills stock is detached houses on standard urban lots being split smaller: the February 24, 2025 planning report for 12248 97 Avenue (a 0.2-hectare site) proposed rezoning from Urban Residential (R3) to Small Lot Residential (R4) with a variance to reduce lot widths from 12 metres to 11.76 metres, creating four small lots with side-by-side garages and access from a future 96A Avenue. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0285-00.pdf
- The pattern is older than the current zone names: a 2013 City report for 12215–12229 96 Avenue proposed retaining an existing duplex and subdividing into six lots under RF-12 and a Comprehensive Development (CD) zone based on RM-D, with new access through a rear lane instead of 96 Avenue driveways. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7911-0320-00.pdf
- The neighbourhood's green structure is older parkland: Moffat Memorial Park at 9434 122 Street was acquired in 1967 to provide park amenity and sports fields for the adjacent secondary school. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/moffat-memorial-park

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide Surrey zoning and Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) rules are written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. The Cedar Hills-specific rules follow.
- Small-lot subdivision here runs through rezoning plus variance, not as-of-right: at 12248 97 Avenue the R3-to-R4 rezoning, the lot-width variance and a 10-metre dedication for the future 96A Avenue were all conditions of creating four lots, per the City's February 2025 report. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0285-00.pdf
- Old Land Use Contracts still surface on Cedar Hills titles: LUC No. 118, adopted by Authorization Bylaw, 1975, No. 4472 on August 16, 1976, regulated the commercial lot at 128 Street and 96 Avenue, and provincial law terminated all Land Use Contracts on June 30, 2024, bringing the underlying C-8 Zone into effect. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7917-0057-00.pdf
- The next plan layer is not yet written: the 9572 120 Street report notes the site sits within the future Scott Road Corridor Neighbourhood Concept Plan (NCP), whose timelines have not been determined — the east edge of Cedar Hills is planned under the Official Community Plan (OCP) alone until that NCP starts. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7924-0229-00

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is Cedar Hills an official Surrey neighbourhood? **A:** Not with a published boundary. The City uses the name in its own planning files — a 2017 report describes a commercial lot as being in "the Cedar Hills neighbourhood" at 128 Street and 96 Avenue — and the elementary school and park carry the name, but no City plan map read in this pass draws the neighbourhood's edges. Buyers should fix a property by its street and zone, not by the neighbourhood label. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7917-0057-00.pdf
- **Q:** Which schools serve a Cedar Hills house? **A:** Cedar Hills Elementary is the named local school, at 12370 98 Avenue, and the City's planning reports use it directly: a 2013 report projected two elementary students from a six-lot project to Cedar Hills Elementary, and the 2025 report for 12248 97 Avenue discusses Cedar Hills Elementary capacity. The paired secondary school beside Moffat Memorial Park is L.A. Matheson Secondary at 9484 122 Street. Catchments are still confirmed by address in the district locator. https://www.surreyschools.ca/cedarhill https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0285-00.pdf
- **Q:** Can a Cedar Hills lot be split into small lots? **A:** Yes, but it took a rezoning and a variance in the worked example. At 12248 97 Avenue the owner proposed moving from R3 to the R4 small-lot zone, reducing lot widths to 11.76 metres, and dedicating 10 metres for a future 96A Avenue along the south property line. A buyer pricing a Cedar Hills lot on a four-way split is pricing an application, not an existing right. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0285-00.pdf
- **Q:** What is happening with the Cedar Hills Shopping Centre site? **A:** The City has named it in a proposed land swap. A June 2026 City document lists the Cedar Hills Shopping Centre property, 12830 96 Avenue, 14.6 acres at the southeast corner of 96 Avenue and 128 Street, as one of three proposed disposition properties in a land exchange connected to a proposed Arena and Entertainment Hub, with 10355 King George Boulevard as the proposed acquisition property. The document is a proposal analysis, not an approval. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/26.06.26-City-of-Surrey-Land-Exchange-Final.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Moffat Memorial Park is a memorial, not just a sports field: the City records that volunteer John Thomas Moffat died in April 1969 when a concrete dugout being built by volunteers collapsed, and the park at 9434 122 Street — baseball diamond, multi-use field, parking from 94A Avenue — was named to commemorate him. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/moffat-memorial-park
- There are two "Cedar Hills" park names to keep straight: Cedar Hills Park itself is at 12761 87A Avenue, well south of the 96–100 Avenue core most buyers mean by Cedar Hills, and the City tags it Whalley and City Centre. https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/cedar-hills-park
- A future street is already on the plans: lots along the south side of the 12248 97 Avenue block face a planned 96A Avenue, and the 2025 application required a 10-metre dedication to build it — rear-yard plans on that block should assume the lane-street arrives. https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0285-00.pdf
- The Scott Road edge has its own commercial application: 9572 120 Street, at the southeast corner of 96 Avenue and 120 Street on the Surrey–Delta border, is proposed as a 4-storey commercial building with ground-level commercial, offices and a childcare centre, rezoning from CG-1 to C-15 (application 7924-0229-00, report dated April 27, 2026). https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7924-0229-00

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The R6 Scott Road RapidBus is the rapid link at the east edge: the City's 12248 97 Avenue report places the site about 400 metres from 120 Street stops served by route R6, route 319 (Scott Road / Newton Exchange) and route 312 (Scottsdale / Scott Road Station). https://www.Surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0285-00.pdf
- Route 316 (Surrey Central–Scottsdale) crosses the neighbourhood on 100 Avenue, 96 Avenue and 116 Street — the local route that connects Cedar Hills streets to Surrey Central Station without going to Scott Road first. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt316.pdf
- Driving, 96 Avenue is the east–west spine to Scott Road and Delta, 128 Street runs south to Newton, and King George Boulevard is reached west via 96 Avenue or south via 120 Street. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check for a terminated Land Use Contract on title before assuming the printed zone governs: on the 128 Street and 96 Avenue commercial lot, LUC No. 118 (1976) regulated use until provincial termination on June 30, 2024 brought the underlying C-8 Zone into effect — older Cedar Hills parcels can carry the same layered history. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7917-0057-00.pdf
- Check the lot against Surrey's Development Permit Areas (DPAs) in the City's mapping (COSMOS): the Hazard Lands and Sensitive Ecosystems DPAs in Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 18020 can add geotechnical or streamside requirements a zone check alone will not show. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/surrey-official-community-plan/development-permit-areas
- A streamside check is part of the same DPA screen: the Sensitive Ecosystems DPA covers streamside areas, so a Cedar Hills lot backing onto any creek or ditch should have its streamside setback area confirmed before a building envelope is assumed. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/surrey-official-community-plan/development-permit-areas
