## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The resale shelf is three wood-frame low-rises: Glenkirk Court at 7436 Stave Lake Street (built 1975, 38 homes, strata plan NW380), Mission Manor at 33165 2nd Avenue (built 1992) and Briskham Manor at 7554 Briskham Street (built 1993, 33 homes, strata plan LMS1049, a 55+ complex) — years and counts from the earlier program files' data-sheet and directory sources, flagged below.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Mission replaced its Official Community Plan (OCP) this year: OCP Bylaw 6405-2026 was adopted April 21, 2026, replacing Bylaw 6350-2025, and zoning runs under Zoning Bylaw 5949-2020 — any condo document or listing citing the old OCP is a bylaw out of date. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-rates
- Only a 55-or-older residency bylaw is legal in a British Columbia strata (Strata Property Act sections 123.1 and 123.2), with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and, since May 1, 2023, younger spouses or partners and defined children — the frame for Briskham Manor's and Luther Place's 55+ rules, to be confirmed on the Form B. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- Long-term rental restriction bylaws have been unenforceable since November 24, 2022, so a directory line saying "rentals not allowed" at an older Mission building is old bylaw text, not current law; short-term rentals can still be restricted, and one Glenkirk Court listing states "No short term rentals." https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- Every Mission strata of five or more lots must now obtain a depreciation report every five years with no deferral vote (in force July 1, 2024), and must contribute at least 10 percent of the operating fund to the contingency reserve fund — the two rules that drive levies in the 1975–1993 stock. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- Developer sales at Montgomery and Silver & Cedar are presale contracts under the Real Estate Development Marketing Act (REDMA), which gives a seven-day rescission period from the later of signing and acknowledging the disclosure statement, with deposits held in trust.

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** How much condo stock does Mission actually have? **A:** Very little, and it is concentrated. The verified apartment shelf is three resale low-rises from 1975 to 1993 — Glenkirk Court, Mission Manor and Briskham Manor — plus the Redekop Faye buildings Preston on Fourteenth (2023), Montgomery and Silver & Cedar. A buyer who needs choice among buildings should expect a short list and compare across eras rather than across many buildings. https://montgomerycondos.ca/?option=com_content&task=view&id=17&Itemid=33
- **Q:** Is Cherry Lane a condo or a townhouse complex? **A:** A townhouse strata. Cherry Lane at 32165 Seventh Avenue (built 1989, 22 homes) is filed as townhouse product, and Luther Place is the same — its records state dwelling type Townhouse outright. Listing feeds that file either under "condos" use the word loosely, which matters because the strata documents, insurance and maintenance splits differ by form. https://www.bccondosandhomes.com/building/cherry-lane-32165-seventh-ave
- **Q:** What does the 55+ rule at Briskham Manor actually restrict? **A:** Residency, under the only age bylaw British Columbia allows. Sections 123.1 and 123.2 of the Strata Property Act permit a 55-or-older residency bylaw, with exemptions for legacy residents, live-in caregivers and younger spouses or partners since May 1, 2023. Confirm on the Form B that the registered bylaw at 7554 Briskham Street is that 55+ bylaw before writing an offer that depends on it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/bylaws-and-rules/age-restriction-bylaws
- **Q:** What is the penalty if I miss Mission's tax deadline? **A:** A flat 10 percent on any current balance outstanding. The City's property tax page sets the 2026 due date at Thursday, July 2, 2026 and states a 10 percent penalty is added to current balances outstanding on July 3, 2026 — there is no staged 5-plus-5 scheme inside the City, and the tax bill also carries a diking levy line. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes
- **Q:** Can I commute to downtown Vancouver from a Mission condo? **A:** Yes, on the train's schedule only. The West Coast Express runs from Mission City Station to Waterfront Station on weekday mornings and back in the afternoon, with no weekend or statutory holiday service, per TransLink's timetable — a commute outside those windows means driving or the BC Transit and TransLink bus chain instead. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt997.pdf

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Mission runs a three-tier school system — elementary Kindergarten to Grade 6, middle school Grades 7 to 9, secondary Grades 10 to 12 in School District 75 — so a family buying a two-bedroom condo here feeds a middle school that a Maple Ridge buyer would not expect; run the building's address through the district locator.
- Preston on Fourteenth's rentals rest on a housing agreement, not just the 2022 law change: its presale record states condo rentals were permitted for 100 years, a project-level term that survives independently of the province voiding rental bylaws.
- Briskham Manor's strata fee includes gas and hot water per its listing record — a real offset when comparing its fee against Mission Manor or Glenkirk Court, and a detail no fee table shows.
- Mission's tax penalty is a single flat 10 percent, while some Fraser Valley rural areas use a staged scheme — the figure quoted for a Mission address must be the City one. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes
- The City mails the 2026 tax notice in the last week of May with a July 2, 2026 due date, and owners are responsible for paying whether or not the notice arrives. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Mission sets its municipal rates by a tax rate bylaw adopted before May 15 each year, and levies additional taxes for diking — the diking levy is a Mission-specific line a condo buyer sees on the tax bill. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-rates
- The City's 2026 utility figures in its budget material are flat-rate water $627.24, sewer $776.64, refuse collection $164.04 and recycling/compost $292.80 for a serviced household — from the City's 2026 budget presentation, read as budget figures, not a condo's strata-billed share. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/budget-2026-oct-26-meeting-presentation
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) applies in the Fraser Valley Regional District communities the Province lists, including Mission — confirm the current listing on the Province's taxable-areas page for the exact property. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The West Coast Express is the rail link: Mission City Station at 33200 North Railway Avenue is the line's eastern terminus, trains run weekday mornings to Waterfront Station and back in the afternoon, and TransLink's timetable states no weekend or statutory holiday service. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt997.pdf
- BC Transit's Central Fraser Valley system runs Mission's local buses, including route 31 Valley Connector to Abbotsford via the Abbotsford-Mission Highway, route 32 West Heights, route 33 Cedar Valley, route 34 East Side, route 35 Hatzic and route 39 Shopper Shuttle. https://www.bctransit.com/central-fraser-valley/schedules-and-maps/
- TransLink route 701 also provides limited weekday trips to and from Mission City Station, per the West Coast Express timetable notes. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt997.pdf
- Mission is outside TransLink's tax area for most services — there is no TransLink levy line on a Mission tax bill, unlike a Surrey or Langley bill. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-rates

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood and diking are the local checks: Mission's south edge along the Fraser River is floodplain, Hatzic Prairie sits behind the Dewdney Dyke, and owners pay a diking levy — the named central buildings are not on the river edge, but flood status is parcel-specific and must be checked for the exact building, not assumed from this note. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-rates
- For the 1975–1993 resale stock, the Form B Information Certificate and the current depreciation report are the central diligence documents — they show the contingency reserve fund balance and planned work that decide future special levies. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- Confirm zoning and OCP designation for a specific condo parcel against Zoning Bylaw 5949-2020 and the new OCP Bylaw 6405-2026, not the replaced Bylaw 6350-2025.
- What the City pages do not tell a buyer: a specific strata's insurance deductible for water damage or flood — that figure is in the strata's own documents obtained on a purchase.
