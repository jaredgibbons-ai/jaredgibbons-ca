# Lower Mary Hill Neighbourhood Guide — Extra Local Notes
Page: lower-mary-hill-port-coquitlam (Lower Mary Hill, Port Coquitlam; neighbourhood page).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Lower Mary Hill is the low, flat foot of Mary Hill: the Pitt River Road grid of Pooley Avenue, Morgan Avenue, Knappen Street, Humber Crescent, Western Drive and Eastern Drive, between Shaughnessy Street on the west and the Mary Hill Bypass on the east. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/floodplain
- Shared neighbourhood facts — floodplain position, schools, the Pitt River Road lot-split file and city-wide rules — are in full in the lower-mary-hill-port-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note; this page adds the street-level detail below. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/media-centre/port-coquitlam-reminds-residents-prepare-possible-flooding
- The east edge is a highway, not a street: the Mary Hill Bypass separates these lots from the Pitt River, and the City's floodplain map labels the bypass, the dike and the Fremont Connector across the low ground at the neighbourhood's east side. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/floodplain
- The south edge is water: the Pitt River and Fraser River meet below the hill, and Colony Farm — now ƛ̓éxətəm Regional Park — sits across the Coquitlam River to the west, reached by the PoCo Climb trail and the Shaughnessy Street crossing. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/media-centre/check-out-port-coquitlams-latest-annual-report

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The pattern is post-war suburbia on the flat: the City's Housing Action Plan records that in the 1950s the older grid pattern "was replaced by a subdivision design creating larger lots fronting curvilinear streets and no lanes in the Lincoln Park and Mary Hill neighbourhoods" — Lower Mary Hill's curving Western Drive and Humber Crescent streets are that pattern, at the hill's foot. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/housing-action-plan
- The change pressure is at the bypass corner: a City council file for nine lots at Harbour Street, Prince Street and 1155 Pitt River Road — the junction of Pitt River Road and the Mary Hill Bypass — proposed rezoning from RS1 (Residential Small Scale 1) to RTh3 (Residential Townhouse 3) for a 43-unit townhouse project. https://pub-portcoquitlam.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11402
- The same file fixes the ground truth for that corner: the site "slopes downward north to south from Harbour Street to the Mary Hill Bypass," and 1155 Pitt River Road and the Prince Street lots sit within the floodplain — house lots and townhouse sites here are read lot by lot, not by neighbourhood label. https://pub-portcoquitlam.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11402

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide Port Coquitlam rules and §7 figures point to the mary-hill-port-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note; flood, dike and watercourse rules for this low ground are in full in the lower-mary-hill-port-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/flood
- The lot-level rule the council file adds is a number: the floodplain lots in the Harbour Street assembly carry "a minimum flood construction level of 5.15 m elevation" — a rebuild or new build on the low streets is designed to a stated elevation, checked in the City's floodplain specification for the lot. https://pub-portcoquitlam.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11402
- Noise is a regulated condition at the bypass edge, not just a fact of life: the Harbour Street rezoning conditions required legal agreements for buildings "designed to incorporate recommendations of the Acoustical Evaluation and for the construction of a sound attenuation fence along Mary Hill Road." https://pub-portcoquitlam.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11402
- The plan frame is the Official Community Plan Bylaw, 2013, No. 3838, which notes the Mary Hill Bypass and the Fraser River "help shape the distribution of land uses within the City" — the bypass corridor is a planned land-use edge, not an accident. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2024-10/3838%20-%20Official%20Community%20Plan.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** What is the PoCo Climb, and does it change life on these streets? **A:** It is a completed City trail, not a proposal. The City's capital program describes "a challenging new 6.5-km loop trail linking Mary Hill area with ƛ̓éxətəm Regional Park (formerly Colony Farm), with a new crosswalk at Shaughnessy Street, steep inclines and 200 timber stairs," and the City's 2025 annual report lists "Completed the PoCo Climb, providing a fully connected trail experience in Mary Hill." Lower Mary Hill streets are now on a signed loop between the hill and the river flats. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/media-centre/2024-2025-capital-program-continues-focus-infrastructure-renewal
- **Q:** Which elementary schools are actually on this side of the hill? **A:** Two School District No. 43 schools sit in or at the foot of Mary Hill: École Mary Hill Elementary, kindergarten to Grade 5, at 1890 Humber Crescent, and Kilmer Elementary, kindergarten to Grade 5, at 1575 Knappen Street. The houses note lists the middle and secondary schools (École Pitt River Middle, École Riverside Secondary); catchment for a specific address is confirmed in the SD43 School Locator, not by proximity. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in/port-coquitlam
- **Q:** Is the big park across the river part of Port Coquitlam? **A:** ƛ̓éxətəm Regional Park (formerly Colony Farm Regional Park) is a Metro Vancouver regional park on the Coquitlam River flats west of Shaughnessy Street — regional, not City, jurisdiction. What the City built is the connection: the PoCo Climb loop and the Shaughnessy Street crosswalk tie Mary Hill's streets into the regional park's trail system. Fremont Village, the newer development area across the Pitt River side of the city, is a different neighbourhood and its facts are not Lower Mary Hill facts. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/media-centre/check-out-port-coquitlams-latest-annual-report
- **Q:** How does the bypass affect a house on the east-side streets? **A:** In two documented ways. The Mary Hill Bypass is the neighbourhood's east boundary and its highway link toward Lougheed Highway and Highway 1, and the City has required a sound attenuation fence and acoustical design conditions for new building on the bypass-edge lands at Harbour Street. An east-edge lot gets the access and the noise file together; both are in the council record for the specific site. https://pub-portcoquitlam.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11402

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Routley Park, 1570 Western Drive, is the hill-foot park with the outdoor pool: the City's capital program funded upgrades to Routley Outdoor Pool, and Routley Park is one of the parks the City dedicated by Park Dedication Bylaw No. 1563 back in 1977. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/recreation-parks/parks-trails/parks-directory?page=2
- Kilmer Park at 1844 Pooley Avenue and Thompson Park at 1842 Morgan Avenue are the grid's two interior parks — both legal parcels assembled from the original subdivision lots, per the same 1977 park dedication bylaw's lot descriptions. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2024-10/1563%20-%20Park%20Dedication.pdf
- Marian Kroeker Park sits west of Pitt River Road, named in the Harbour Street council file as the green edge between the road and the townhouse lands east of it. https://pub-portcoquitlam.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11402
- The trail under your feet is one named system: the Traboulay PoCo Trail rings the city along the Coquitlam, Pitt and Fraser rivers, and its Mary Hill section is the dike-top path the flood rules in the houses note close at high water — recreation and flood infrastructure are the same corridor here. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/our-government/media-centre/port-coquitlam-reminds-residents-prepare-possible-flooding
- The hill above has a name for its top: Citadel Heights and Castle Park sit above Lower Mary Hill's east side — "lower" in the name is literal elevation against the Citadel slope, not a price label. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/sites/default/files/2024-10/3838%20-%20Official%20Community%20Plan.pdf

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Route 159, Braid Station to Coquitlam Central Station, runs the Mary Hill Bypass and Shaughnessy Street at the neighbourhood's edges — the bus that drops riders at the Colony Farm / ƛ̓éxətəm Regional Park trail entrance at the bypass and Shaughnessy. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/159/direction/1/schedule
- Routes 170 (Port Coquitlam South loop via Pitt River Road, Eastern and Western Drives) and 188 (Pitt River Road to Coquitlam Central Station), and Port Coquitlam Station's West Coast Express at 2125 Kingsway Avenue, are in full in the lower-mary-hill-port-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt170.pdf
- By car the bypass is the point: Mary Hill Bypass connects Pitt River Road and Shaughnessy Street to Lougheed Highway and the Highway 1 corridor at Cape Horn — the reason the east-side streets carry highway traffic noise conditions in City files. https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/floodplain

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Start on the City's floodplain map, which shows this neighbourhood's ground plainly: the dike line, the Mary Hill Bypass, Pitt River Road and the Fremont Connector across the 1:200 annual exceedance probability floodplain (Ministry of Environment, 1992 base mapping, City map dated April 14, 2022). https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/floodplain
- Get the lot's flood construction level in writing: the Harbour Street file states 5.15 metres for its floodplain lots, but the level is set lot by lot in the City's floodplain specification — the map shows the boundary, not your lot's number. https://pub-portcoquitlam.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=11402
- Watercourse and tree rules for the creek and ditch lots are in full in the lower-mary-hill-port-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note (Watercourse Development Permit; Tree Bylaw, 2019, No. 4108). https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/development-permits/watercourse-development
