## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- “Pacific Douglas” is the board name; the City calls the neighbourhood simply Douglas, Surrey's most southern urban neighbourhood, between the Peace Arch and Pacific border crossings and adjacent to the United States. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/douglas-ncp
- The Douglas Neighbourhood Concept Plan (NCP) area is about 61 hectares (150 acres), east of Highway 99, west of Highway 15 (176 Street) and north of the United States, bordered by the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) and the Glades Garden Park to the north. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/douglas-ncp
- The City describes Douglas as mostly residential, with two small commercial nodes, a neighbourhood park and an elementary school. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/douglas-ncp

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Do not rely on the 1999 Douglas NCP designations alone: the City states Douglas's land use designations have been superseded by the Surrey 2050 Official Community Plan (OCP) update, while Douglas continues to guide public amenities and infrastructure. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/douglas-ncp
- The north edge is Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) land: a house backing onto the ALR / Glades Garden Park edge on the north side of Douglas is beside provincially protected farmland, not future subdivision land confirmed by the plan page. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/douglas-ncp

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is Pacific Douglas the same place as Douglas? **A:** Yes. Pacific Douglas is the board sub-area name and Douglas is the City of Surrey name for the same neighbourhood. The City calls it Surrey's most southern urban neighbourhood, located between the Peace Arch and Pacific border crossings adjacent to the United States, and its plan area sits east of Highway 99 and west of Highway 15. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/douglas-ncp
- **Q:** How close is the border, in practical terms? **A:** The neighbourhood's south edge is the United States border itself. Peace Arch Park sits at the Douglas Border Crossing, at the junction of Highway 99 in British Columbia and Interstate 5 in Washington State, and BC Parks warns visitors they may be asked for identification by Canada Border Services Agency or U.S. officers in the park. https://bcparks.ca/peace-arch-park/
- **Q:** Can I use Peace Arch Park like a normal local park? **A:** Partly. BC Parks states the parking lot gate is open from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. daily, camping is not allowed anywhere in Peace Arch Park, and there is no access from the park to the beach. The park was dedicated in 1921 and the Canadian side became a provincial park in 1939. https://bcparks.ca/peace-arch-park/
- **Q:** Will the farmland north of Douglas be developed? **A:** Not on the evidence in the plan. The Douglas NCP page states the area is bordered by the Agricultural Land Reserve and the Glades Garden Park to the north. ALR land is provincially protected farmland, so a buyer should not price a Douglas house on the assumption that the north farmland edge will become housing. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/douglas-ncp

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Douglas sits between two different border crossings — Peace Arch (Highway 99) to the west and Pacific (Highway 15) to the east — so border traffic can affect either Highway 99 or 176 Street, not one road only. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/douglas-ncp
- Peace Arch Park's parking lot is on Semiahmoo First Nation land, subleased by BC Parks for public use, and visitors may only use it when the gate is open, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. daily. https://bcparks.ca/peace-arch-park/
- The Douglas plan was a two-stage process: Council approved the Stage 1 draft land use plan in July 1996 and the Stage 2 plan, Corporate Report C429, in July 1999 — the servicing strategies behind the houses date from that process. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/douglas-ncp

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Highway 99 runs on Douglas's west side to the Peace Arch / Douglas Border Crossing, where it meets Interstate 5 in Washington State. https://bcparks.ca/peace-arch-park/
- Highway 15 (176 Street) runs on Douglas's east side to the Pacific border crossing — the second crossing the City names in its Douglas description. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/south-surrey-land-use-plans/douglas-ncp
