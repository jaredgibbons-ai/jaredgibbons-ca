# Mission Acreages — Extra Local Notes
Page: mission-acreages-for-sale (Mission; acreages).
## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Mission acreage falls into two landscapes: hillside rural land north of the built-up area and flat Hatzic Prairie land to the east. The City's zoning material places Hatzic as a neighbourhood of smaller Rural Residential 7 (RR7) and Urban Residential 930 (R930) lots, while Stave Falls, Steelhead and Ferndale carry the larger rural zones. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-rao26-2
- The eastern edge at Ferndale meets Hatzic Lake. A City rezoning report for 34971 Ferndale Avenue describes the property as on the edge of Ferndale, with the municipal boundary and Hatzic Lake to the east. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-rsd26-1
- Shared Mission civic facts are owned by the earlier mission-farms note; this note keeps hillside and Hatzic Prairie acreage, rural zoning and septic facts only.

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The parcel fabric is set by three Rural zones with very different sizes. Mission's Rural zone sheet states RU80 requires 8.0 hectares, RU36 requires 3.6 hectares and RU16 requires 1.6 hectares for lots created by subdivision. https://map.mission.ca/Mission/images/ZoningBylawZones/RU16.pdf
- Bare-land strata lots are allowed but must average the full zone size. The same sheet gives bare-land strata minimum and average lot areas of 6.8 and 8.0 hectares in RU80, 3.0 and 3.6 hectares in RU36, and 1.35 and 1.6 hectares in RU16. https://map.mission.ca/Mission/images/ZoningBylawZones/RU16.pdf
- The intended house form in those zones is one single-family dwelling with agriculture. The zone intent states the RU zones provide one single-family dwelling along with a range of agricultural uses and compatible accessory uses on land designated Rural. https://map.mission.ca/Mission/images/ZoningBylawZones/RU16.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- A second detached dwelling in a Rural zone needs a rezoning to the secondary-dwelling version of the zone, not just a building permit. The City processed 34971 Ferndale Avenue as a rezoning from Rural 16 Zone (RU16) to Rural 16 Secondary Dwelling Zone (RU16s) to permit a detached secondary dwelling. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-rsd26-1
- A secondary family dwelling by covenant is the other Rural-zone route, and it has a size floor. Mission's Rural zone sheet limits that use to lots of at least 1.6 hectares (4.0 acres) where the principal use is not a duplex or a manufactured home. https://map.mission.ca/Mission/images/ZoningBylawZones/RU16.pdf
- Minimum width and depth also control whether a split works. The Rural zone sheet requires 40.0 metres width on a corner lot, 35.0 metres on a non-corner lot and 50.0 metres depth for RU80, RU36 and RU16 lots created by subdivision. https://map.mission.ca/Mission/images/ZoningBylawZones/RU16.pdf
- Development Permit Area H can apply on a rural parcel as well as in town. The City report for 34971 Ferndale Avenue states Development Permit Area H (DPAH) is applicable to that RU16 site. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-rsd26-1
- Small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH) is an urban-serviced policy, not a rural acreage right. The City publishes its SSMUH work as a separate planning page, and buyers should not assume it adds units on RU land. https://www.mission.ca/planning/small-scale-multi-unit-housing-ssmuh

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** How big does a Mission acreage have to be to subdivide? **A:** It depends entirely on which Rural zone it is in, and the gaps are large. A new lot needs 8.0 hectares in RU80, 3.6 hectares in RU36 or 1.6 hectares in RU16, plus 35 to 40 metres of width and 50 metres of depth, so a 2-hectare RU36 parcel cannot split even though a 2-hectare RU16 parcel might meet area alone. https://map.mission.ca/Mission/images/ZoningBylawZones/RU16.pdf
- **Q:** Can I add a coach house for family on an RU16 acreage? **A:** Expect a rezoning unless a covenant-based secondary family dwelling already fits. Mission rezoned 34971 Ferndale Avenue from RU16 to RU16s specifically to permit a detached secondary dwelling, and its Rural zone sheet separately allows a secondary family dwelling by covenant only on lots of 1.6 hectares or more and not where the principal use is a duplex or manufactured home. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-rsd26-1
- **Q:** Is a Hatzic Prairie acreage the same as a hillside acreage? **A:** No — Hatzic is the low, flat eastern area near Hatzic Lake with smaller RR7 and R930 lots nearby, while the larger RU parcels are in places such as Stave Falls, Steelhead and Ferndale. The City's own zoning comparison lists Hatzic under the smaller-lot zones and Stave Falls, Steelhead and Ferndale under the larger suburban and rural zones, which changes flood, septic and subdivision checks. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-rao26-2
- **Q:** What development charges apply if I build or subdivide? **A:** Mission charges both Development Cost Charges (DCCs) and Amenity Cost Charges (ACCs) under 2025 bylaws. The City states DCC Bylaw 6348-2025 and ACC Bylaw 6349-2025 were both adopted on May 5, 2025, and that ACCs are paid at subdivision approval or building permit issuance, replacing the former Community Amenity Contribution for developers. https://www.mission.ca/planning/development-amenity-cost-charges

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Mission has a Rural Mission Strategy as a live City project. The City announced the strategy as live and lists it among its current engagement projects, which is the document to check for changing rural policy. https://www.mission.ca/council-government/news/rural-mission-strategy-now-live
- A pre-application review is the City's required first step for a development proposal, and it is paid. Mission's planning page states a Pre-Application Review form and a $404.25 payment are needed to be listed on the next meeting agenda, with meetings every two weeks and a maximum of six applications per date. https://www.mission.ca/planning
- Current applications are published as a quarterly spreadsheet, not a live map. The City planning page states its spreadsheet of all current planning applications was current as of August 11, 2026 and is updated quarterly. https://www.mission.ca/planning
- Shipping-container storage is being restricted to larger rural lots. City staff recommended limiting that use to suburban, rural residential and rural zones with lot areas of 0.2 hectares (0.49 acres) or larger, which would cover most properties in Stave Falls, Steelhead and Ferndale. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-rao26-2
- Septic and well service is the rural default assumption to verify, not a City guarantee. No City page read for this note stated municipal sewer or water for RU-zone parcels, so a buyer must confirm services parcel by parcel — see NOT CONFIRMED. https://www.mission.ca/planning

The City states Council endorsed the Focused Land Use option on July 20, 2026, implementation is expected over 10 to 20 years, and infrastructure and road-network work was underway in fall 2026. https://www.mission.ca/lougheed-highway-corridor

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Mission's total residential tax rate is 4.43688 per $1,000 of assessed value for 2026 on the City's tax-rates schedule, as summarised from that schedule. The City publishes its current rates on its tax-rates page. https://www.mission.ca/services/property-taxes/tax-rates
- DCCs are the main land-specific charge for a new acreage build or split. DCC Bylaw 6348-2025 states the charges fund sewage, water, drainage, fire protection, police, highway and solid waste facilities and park land for growth. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/6348-2025-development-cost-charge-0
- Building permit costs can stack with DCCs, ACCs and School Site Acquisition Charges. The City's building permits page states a project may be subject to all three and that they are payable at the stage the page specifies. https://www.mission.ca/building-permits
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) applies in Mission. The Province lists the District of Mission among its taxable areas, separate from the Metro Vancouver list. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The West Coast Express (WCE) Mission City Station is the commuter rail terminus for the whole line. TransLink lists its park and ride at 7218 Mershon Street with 254 spaces, open 7 days a week from 4 a.m. to 2 a.m. https://www.translink.ca/parkandride?utm_source=direct&utm_medium=shorturl&utm_campaign=parkandride
- A one-way WCE trip spans 69 kilometres and takes approximately 75 minutes, according to TransLink's 30-year WCE release. That is the operator's stated figure for Mission City to Waterfront, not a drive-time estimate for a rural parcel. https://www.translink.ca/news/2025/november/all%20aboard%20translink%20celebrates%2030%20years%20of%20west%20coast%20express
- No transit route to rural parcels in Stave Falls, Steelhead, Ferndale or Hatzic Prairie was confirmed from an operator page read for this note, so those acreages should be treated as car-dependent until TransLink or BC Transit confirms a route for the specific address. https://www.translink.ca/parkandride?utm_source=direct&utm_medium=shorturl&utm_campaign=parkandride

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Slope and geohazard review is a hillside-acreage check in Mission. Development Permit Area H applied even to the flat Ferndale RU16 rezoning site, so a buyer should ask which development permit areas attach to the specific lot before designing. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-rsd26-1
- Flood risk is the Hatzic Prairie check, distinct from hillside risk. Hatzic lots sit by Hatzic Lake and the municipal boundary to the east, as the City describes the Ferndale edge, so floodplain and diking questions belong in the offer conditions for low parcels. https://www.mission.ca/media/file/information-package-rsd26-1
- On-site sewerage rules are provincial health-authority rules where there is no sewer. Fraser Health publishes the regulatory requirements for on-site sewerage systems that a septic designer must meet. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/health-topics-a-to-z/onsite-sewerage-systems/regulatory-requirements-for-onsite-sewerage-systems
- Check ALR status before assuming farm or housing rights. The Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) limits an ALR parcel to one principal residence of 500 square metres or less, a suite inside it and an additional residence only in defined cases. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
