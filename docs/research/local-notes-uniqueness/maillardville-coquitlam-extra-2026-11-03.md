# Maillardville Neighbourhood — Extra Local Notes
Page: maillardville-coquitlam (Maillardville, Coquitlam; neighbourhood).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Maillardville sits on the lower south slope of southwest Coquitlam, and the Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan location map bounds the plan area by Austin Avenue to the north, Lougheed Highway to the south, Blue Mountain Street to the west and Mundy Road to the east. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- Brunette Avenue is the neighbourhood's commercial main street, and the plan's town centre sits in the southwest corner of the plan area on Brunette Avenue between Lougheed Highway and Woolridge Street. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- The plan names four specific areas inside the boundary: the Neighbourhood Centre, the Laval Square and Allard-LeBleu Heritage Character Areas, Schoolhouse East, and Lougheed Brunette, which includes the Brunette Interchange and the Brunette, Lougheed and Blue Mountain intersection. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- Laval Square is the original town-site core around Laval Street, Schoolhouse Street and Cartier Avenue, and its street names, and Maillardville's street names generally, honour the early French-Canadian pioneers, a point the City makes on its heritage page. https://coquitlam.ca/797/Heritage-and-History

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The stock is an eclectic blend of old and new, in the plan's own wording: single-family heritage homes on the original town-site lots, plus duplex, triplex and quadruplex Housing Choices forms, townhouses and apartments in the residential areas the plan designates. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- The oldest houses are mill-worker houses, and the City's Built History page lists designated examples off the main streets as well as on them, including 218 and 225 Begin Street, 1107 and 1207 Cartier Avenue, 801 and 805 Roderick Avenue, and 800 and 804 Gauthier Avenue. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1021/Built-History-Coquitlams-Heritage-Homes
- One east-end house is in the heritage inventory rather than designated: the Booth Farm heritage home at 1746 and 1750 Brunette Avenue, dating to 1901, which the plan proposed designating as park so the house would be conserved. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- No official source read for this note states a typical Maillardville lot size, unit size or build-decade split for the stock as a whole, so none is claimed here.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The governing document is the Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan, adopted as Schedule A to Bylaw No. 4465, 2014, first as Chapter 9.1 of the Citywide Official Community Plan and now carried in the Interim Official Community Plan's Schedule J list of neighbourhood plans. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17458
- The plan sets its own Development Permit Areas for this neighbourhood, with heritage-inspired design guidelines for the Neighbourhood Centre, for the Laval Square and Allard-LeBleu Heritage Character Areas, and for multi-family residential development, applied on top of the city-wide guidelines. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- New Neighbourhood Attached Residential areas, the plan's Housing Choices designation, were drawn for the Laval Square area north of the Neighbourhood Centre up to Rochester Avenue, an area southwest of Bernatchey Street, and the 1600 and 1700 blocks of Brunette Avenue and Sheridan Avenue. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- Two plan areas are deliberately held at their existing designations: Schoolhouse East, part of the Lougheed and Schoolhouse employment corridor, and Lougheed Brunette, where designations stay in place until the Brunette Interchange transportation review with other levels of government is completed. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- The heritage designation rule and the Heritage Revitalization Agreement process are stated in full in the sibling Maillardville notes and are not repeated here; the same applies to Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996 section 519 and the Watercourse Protection Development Permit Area, which are in the maillardville-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Where did Maillardville come from?
**A:** It began as a Fraser Mills mill community. The City's heritage page states Frank Ross and James McLaren opened Fraser Mills on the north bank of the Fraser River, that in 1909 a contingent of 110 French Canadians arrived, recruited for work there, and that with a second contingent in June 1910 Maillardville was born, named for Father Maillard. https://coquitlam.ca/797/Heritage-and-History
**Q:** Which schools are physically in Maillardville?
**A:** Alderson Elementary and École Rochester Elementary are the elementary schools in the neighbourhood, and École Maillard Middle is its middle school. The provincial school list for School District 43 gives Alderson, kindergarten to Grade 5, at 825 Gauthier Avenue, Rochester, kindergarten to Grade 5, at 411 Schoolhouse Street, and Maillard Middle, Grades 6 to 8, at 1300 Rochester Avenue. Assignment is by home address, so a buyer should confirm the catchment for the exact address with the district. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/043
**Q:** Where does the neighbourhood plan put the growth?
**A:** Growth is directed mainly to the centre, not evenly across the house streets. The plan calls for a high-density, mixed-use Neighbourhood Centre on Brunette Avenue, medium-density apartment areas at named edges, and Neighbourhood Attached Residential Housing Choices, including duplex, triplex and quadruplex forms, in areas such as Laval Square north to Rochester Avenue, while retaining existing street, block and lot patterns. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
**Q:** Is there a SkyTrain station in Maillardville?
**A:** There is no SkyTrain station inside Maillardville. The neighbourhood plan notes the nearby Braid Street SkyTrain station as one of the area's key transportation linkages, alongside Highway 1 and Lougheed Highway. The local bus connection through the neighbourhood is TransLink route 153 on Brunette Avenue, which is covered in full in the maillardville-coquitlam-condos-townhomes-for-sale note in this program. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
**Q:** What is Fraser Mills, at the south end of the neighbourhood?
**A:** Fraser Mills is the former mill site at the south end of King Edward Street, now being built as a waterfront community. Developer Beedie Living states the 96-acre master-planned community will include 5,500 homes in strata and rental buildings, and that its first three residential projects, Debut, Encore and Chapter, are under construction, completing in 2028 and 2029. https://www.beedie.ca/2026/07/city-of-coquitlam-and-beedie-living-unveil-first-look-at-new-fraser-mills-community-centre/
**Q:** How do I know if a Maillardville house carries heritage protection?
**A:** Check the specific address, because protection is property by property. The City's Built History page lists designated Maillardville addresses across Brunette Avenue, Laval Square and streets including Begin Street, Cartier Avenue and Roderick Avenue, and states the designation rule in full. That rule and the Heritage Revitalization Agreement process are covered in the sibling Maillardville notes; this page does not repeat them. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1021/Built-History-Coquitlams-Heritage-Homes

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Mackin Park, at 1046 Brunette Avenue, is the neighbourhood park and, on the City's facility page, the home of Festival du Bois, the francophone festival, and of the public art piece Pioneer Spirit. https://www.coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Mackin-Park-51
- Nelson Creek flows through the naturalized area of Mackin Park, so the park's Brunette Avenue frontage is also a creek corridor, not only playing fields. https://www.coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Mackin-Park-51
- The Maillardville Community Centre is at 1200 Cartier Avenue, is 22,000 square feet, doubles the size of the previous community centre, and holds the City's first dedicated gymnasium; the City lists its hours as 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., seven days a week. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1199/Maillardville-Community-Centre
- Brunette Interim Park, at 1123 Brunette Avenue, occupies undeveloped City-owned land on an interim basis, with a sand play area, community garden plots and kids' bike and scooter paths, and the City states it will remain for a number of years before the site is redeveloped. https://coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/Brunette-Interim-Park-185
- Place des Arts, the arts centre at 1120 Brunette Avenue, operates from the Ryan House, a designated heritage building that was the Fraser Mills manager's residence, at the King Edward Street entry to the old mill lands. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1021/Built-History-Coquitlams-Heritage-Homes
- The City's heritage page describes Maillardville as the largest Francophone centre west of Manitoba in its founding era, and states French is still heard on the streets and in homes on the south slope of Coquitlam. https://coquitlam.ca/797/Heritage-and-History
- Mackin Park's north ball diamond was scheduled to close for upgrades starting in May 2026, one of four ball diamonds in the park, alongside two lit tennis courts, two playing fields, a skateboarding facility and a spray park. https://www.coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Mackin-Park-51

## 6. DEVELOPMENT AND NEW PROJECTS
- Maillardville Neighbourhood Plan, City of Coquitlam, plan area as in section 1, land-use plan for the whole neighbourhood — STATUS: implementation framework, read 2026-11-03. The original plan was adopted in 1990, the Neighbourhood Centre policies, zoning and design guidelines were updated in July 2008, and the updated plan was adopted in 2014 as set out in section 3, to guide growth and reinvestment over 20 to 25 years and accommodate an additional 6,000 residents. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- Plan implementation items named in the plan itself are the development of Heritage Revitalization Agreement guidelines and procedures, a park acquisition plan, a Maillardville Servicing Assessment covering park, utility and transportation improvements funded primarily through Development Cost Charges, and heritage-inspired streetscape guidelines for Adair Avenue and the Laval Square area. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- The plan now sits inside a further City process: the City's October 21, 2025 Interim Official Community Plan item describes a streamlined, housekeeping Interim OCP that introduces no new land-use policy, with a comprehensive OCP review anticipated to begin in 2027 ahead of the provincially mandated update due December 31, 2030. https://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Archive/Item/1826?arcId=2721
- Fraser Mills, south end of King Edward Street on the Fraser River, Beedie Living, 96 acres, 5,500 homes in strata and rental buildings as stated by the developer — STATUS: under construction, read 2026-11-03. The first three residential projects, Debut, Encore and Chapter, are under construction, completing in 2028 and 2029, and the amenity plan includes 16.6 acres of park space, a planned new elementary school and more than 400 childcare spaces. https://www.beedie.ca/2026/07/city-of-coquitlam-and-beedie-living-unveil-first-look-at-new-fraser-mills-community-centre/
- Fraser Mills Community Centre, Fraser Mills waterfront, City of Coquitlam with Beedie Living, nearly 60,000 square feet with a leisure pool, a five-lane lap pool and a full-size gymnasium — STATUS: design unveiled by the City and the developer, read 2026-11-03; the developer states the City-owned, City-operated facility will be complete in 2031, next to a central Urban Plaza. https://www.beedie.ca/2026/07/city-of-coquitlam-and-beedie-living-unveil-first-look-at-new-fraser-mills-community-centre/
- Maillardville Community Centre Plaza, 1200 Cartier Avenue, City of Coquitlam capital project, tiered seating and play areas as the centre's public gathering space — STATUS: construction nearing completion in the City's May 16, 2024 capital projects report, with an official opening then slated for late June or early July, read 2026-11-03. https://www.coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Archive/Item/1502?arcId=2220

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The R3 Lougheed Hwy RapidBus runs on Lougheed Highway, the plan area's south boundary, between Coquitlam Central Station and Haney Place Exchange, and TransLink describes RapidBus routes as running at least every 10 minutes at peak times and every 15 minutes off-peak, every day from 6 a.m. to midnight. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- Route 153, the bus through the neighbourhood on Brunette Avenue, is named here once: its full routing and stop list are in the maillardville-coquitlam-condos-townhomes-for-sale note. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt153.pdf
- Driving access at the west end is the Brunette Interchange on Highway 1: the Province's January 19, 2016 release states it was starting the conceptual design phase for a new Brunette interchange at Highway 1, engaging New Westminster, Coquitlam and other local governments, and calls the Brunette overpass critical for moving people and goods and for emergency vehicles travelling to and from Royal Columbian Hospital. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2016TRAN0006-000053
- The plan's greenway and bike network is an east-west connection: the plan document sets out a street, greenway and trail network for walking, cycling and transit use between residential areas and neighbourhood destinations, with bike connections linking Maillard Middle, Alderson Elementary and Millside schools with parks and open spaces. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check flood status at the south end before anything else: the City states Coquitlam fronts four major rivers, the Brunette, Coquitlam, Pitt and Fraser, and that dikes and pump stations protect low-lying properties, with flood levels and Flood Construction Levels set out in its floodplain mapping. https://www.coquitlam.ca/224/Floodplain-Dikes
- The Fraser Mills lands show what that check involves in practice: the developer's community-centre release describes Fraser Mills as a waterfront community on the Fraser River, and a buyer there is buying into a diked riverfront site, so the lot-level flood documents, not the neighbourhood name, carry the answer. https://www.beedie.ca/2026/07/city-of-coquitlam-and-beedie-living-unveil-first-look-at-new-fraser-mills-community-centre/
- Check slope on the upper Brunette Avenue blocks: the plan record states redevelopment of some properties in the 1600 and 1700 blocks of Brunette Avenue will require additional grading and earth works given the presence of steep slopes, with access required off Sheridan Avenue. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/761/06-17-2013-Report-to-Council---Draft-Maillardville-Neighbourhood-Plan-PDF
- Check creek frontage lot by lot: Nelson Creek runs through Mackin Park, and the plan area's other creeks are the reason the watercourse setback and permit-area rules in the maillardville-coquitlam-houses-for-sale note, section 9, must be run against the specific lot; they are not repeated here. https://www.coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Mackin-Park-51
- Heritage status is the remaining lot-level check, and it is address-specific: the designated-address list in section 2 is the starting point, and the designation rule itself is in the sibling notes as noted in section 3. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1021/Built-History-Coquitlams-Heritage-Homes
