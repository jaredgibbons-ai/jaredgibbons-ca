# North Surrey Houses — Extra Local Notes
Page: north-surrey-houses-for-sale (North Surrey; houses).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- North Surrey is the northern half of the city, made up of the Whalley, Guildford and Fleetwood communities, with the Fraser River along its northern edge and Newton to the south. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/fleetwood
- The City treats City Centre only as the downtown core inside Whalley in North Surrey — "Surrey City Centre" is a City planning name for that core, not a Fraser Valley Real Estate Board sub-area name; the board name is Whalley. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- Fleetwood sits at the centre of the northern half of the city, with Whalley to the west and Guildford to the east and north — so "North Surrey" on a house listing can mean three different board areas. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/fleetwood
- Guildford forms the northeast corner, bordered by the Fraser River to the north, Whalley to the west and Langley to the east, with a southern border stretching from 96 Avenue to 84 Avenue. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The detached stock changes character across the three communities: Fleetwood is mostly single family dwellings, Guildford is mature urban neighbourhoods plus the newer Fraser Heights subdivision, and Whalley's houses ring a high-rise core. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/fleetwood
- Fraser Heights, inside Guildford, is the newer subdivision pattern in North Surrey, sloping down toward the Fraser River, while the neighbourhoods around Guildford Town Centre mall are the mature stock. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- Whalley is one of Surrey's oldest neighbourhoods, so its house blocks predate the tower core that now surrounds Surrey Central and King George stations. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- City-wide detached rules, lot patterns and the acreage edge are carried in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note; this note covers only what splits North Surrey internally. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Three different local plans govern North Surrey house lots — the City Centre Plan in Whalley, the Guildford Plan in Guildford, and the Fleetwood Plan in Fleetwood — so the plan check depends on which community the address is in. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/whalley-land-use-plans/city-centre-plan
- The Fleetwood Plan guides growth in Fleetwood's three Transit Oriented Areas and beyond as SkyTrain extends along Fraser Highway, and Council will consider the updated plan in 2026 — Fleetwood designations are still moving. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/fleetwood-land-use-plans/fleetwood-town-centre-plan

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** What does "North Surrey" actually cover when I search for houses? **A:** It covers the Whalley, Guildford and Fleetwood communities in the northern half of the city, not one board area. Fleetwood sits at the centre of that northern half with Whalley west and Guildford east and north, and each community has its own land-use plan and its own house stock. A buyer comparing two "North Surrey" houses should first pin down which of the three communities each address is in, because transit, plans and flood checks all differ by community. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/fleetwood
- **Q:** Which part of North Surrey has SkyTrain for a house commute today? **A:** Only Whalley has SkyTrain today. The City lists four Expo Line stations in Surrey — Scott Road, Gateway, Surrey Central and King George — and all four are in Whalley around the City Centre core. Guildford is served by the Guildford Exchange bus loop and the R1 RapidBus, and Fleetwood's stations are part of the Surrey–Langley SkyTrain project still being built along Fraser Highway, so a Fleetwood house bought for SkyTrain is bought on a project timeline, not a running service. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey
- **Q:** Is Surrey City Centre a separate area I should search instead of Whalley? **A:** No — it is the same ground described two ways. Surrey City Centre is the City of Surrey's planning name for the downtown core, and the City describes Whalley as the community that surrounds it in North Surrey. The Fraser Valley Real Estate Board sub-area name is Whalley, so board-based searches file the downtown and its surrounding house blocks under Whalley. Search Whalley for the houses and treat City Centre as the plan name for the core blocks. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley
- **Q:** How close is a North Surrey house to the hospital? **A:** The Surrey–Langley SkyTrain project's own station page states that its future Green Timbers Station, at 140 Street and Fraser Highway, will facilitate access to Surrey Memorial Hospital, the Jim Pattison Outpatient Care and Surgery Centre and the BC RCMP E-Divisional Headquarters. That station sits at the Whalley–Fleetwood edge of North Surrey. Driving time from any individual house to the hospital is UNCONFIRMED here — no operator or health-authority page read in this pass states one. https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/stations/
- **Q:** Do North Surrey houses share one school catchment pattern? **A:** No — all of North Surrey is School District 36 (Surrey), but catchments are set by the district for each address, not by community name. The provincial list confirms schools such as A.H.P. Matthew Elementary at 13367 97 Avenue in Whalley, and a district locator run is the only confirmed way to tie a house to its schools. Any page that assigns a catchment to a whole North Surrey neighbourhood without an address check should be treated as unconfirmed. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/schools/in-school-district/036

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Green Timbers Urban Forest is the western edge of the Fleetwood plan area and sits inside North Surrey — the Fleetwood plan area stretches from that forest in the west towards the Agricultural Land Reserve in the east. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/fleetwood-land-use-plans/fleetwood-town-centre-plan
- The City's bus-exchange list names Guildford Exchange, Surrey Central Station, King George Station, Gateway Station and Scott Road Station in North Surrey — five of Surrey's exchanges are in the northern half of the city. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey
- Scott Road Station carries the Park and Ride location the City lists, and King George Station carries the bike parkade — both in Whalley, not spread across North Surrey. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey
- Tynehead Regional Park, at the headwaters of the Serpentine River, sits at North Surrey's east side in Guildford, and Metro Vancouver is building a new 280-metre section of its Perimeter Trail with weekday trail closures from September to December 2026. https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/tynehead-regional-park
- The Downtown Surrey Business Improvement Association covers the North Surrey downtown area and has more than 1,400 members — it was previously named the Whalley Business Improvement Association. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/whalley

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- North Surrey has no separate tax rate — houses here pay the city-wide Surrey rate. The full 2026 rate, parcel taxes and utility figures are carried in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Surrey property taxes are due July 2, with a 5 percent penalty if not paid by midnight July 2 and a further 5 percent penalty after September 2. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/paying-your-property-taxes
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies across North Surrey because the Province lists the City of Surrey as a designated taxable area in the Metro Vancouver Regional District. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Four Expo Line stations serve North Surrey today, all in Whalley: Scott Road, Gateway, Surrey Central and King George. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/parking-streets-transportation/walking-cycling-transit/transit-in-surrey
- TransLink's R1 King George Boulevard RapidBus runs from Guildford Exchange to Newton Exchange through Surrey Central and King George stations, tying Guildford's houses to the SkyTrain core along 104 Avenue and King George Boulevard. https://www.translink.ca/plans-and-projects/projects/bus-projects/rapidbus-projects
- Highway 1 and the Port Mann Bridge form the northern driving edge at Guildford and Fraser Heights, where the City notes views of the bridge and the Fraser River along the community's northern edge. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/guildford
- The Surrey–Langley SkyTrain project will add stations at Green Timbers (140 Street and Fraser Highway) and 152 Street on North Surrey's southern Fraser Highway edge; the project page does not state an in-service date, so treat timing as unconfirmed. https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/stations/

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Run the flood check by community, not by the North Surrey label: the City's coastal floodplain stretches along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers toward Cloverdale and Newton, while Whalley and the Guildford uplands sit outside the areas that page describes. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- Confirm Agricultural Land Reserve status at the east end, where the Fleetwood plan area runs towards the ALR — a North Surrey acreage-style lot there carries farm-land rules that an ordinary house lot does not. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/fleetwood-land-use-plans/fleetwood-town-centre-plan
- Check which local plan covers the lot before relying on future density, because the Fleetwood Plan update goes to Council in 2026 and the City Centre Plan update is underway — both can change what a neighbouring house lot may become. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/fleetwood-land-use-plans/fleetwood-town-centre-plan
