# Chineside Houses — Extra Local Notes

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Chineside is in the City of Coquitlam, not Port Coquitlam — it is a Greater Vancouver Regional District (GVR) board sub-area name for the central-Coquitlam slope around Austin Avenue and Como Lake Avenue, and the City publishes no neighbourhood plan or boundary for the name "Chineside". https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit
- Chineside and Harbour Chines are two separate board sub-areas, both in Coquitlam — do not merge them; Central Coquitlam lies to the east, Ranch Park and Maillardville to the south, and Burquitlam to the west. https://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/1838

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Houses are the stock here — the sub-area's houses sit in the City's small-scale residential zones R-1, R-2 and R-3 under Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996, and no City, School District No. 43 (Coquitlam) or BC Assessment public page read for this note states a build decade, typical lot size, subdivision name or house style for Chineside, so none is claimed. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/15337/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-Summary-Guide?bidId=
- Subdivision minimums are the lot-size floor a buyer can verify: 650 square metres with 18.5 metres of width in R-1 and R-3, and 465 square metres with 12.5 metres of width in R-2, under Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/15337/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-Summary-Guide?bidId=

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Coquitlam-wide rules are stated in full in this note, as the first Coquitlam note in this slice: the provincial small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH) rules as adopted in Coquitlam allow up to 3 units on lots 280 square metres or smaller and up to 4 units on lots larger than 280 square metres and under 4,050 square metres, including secondary suites and carriage or laneway homes. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit
- Lots not connected to municipal water or sewer fall outside the SSMUH rules, so a Chineside lot's service connections must be confirmed before unit potential is priced in. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/15337/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-Summary-Guide?bidId=
- A Watercourse Protection Development Permit can be required within 30 metres of the top of bank or top of ravine bank of any watercourse in this part of Coquitlam, and Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996 section 519 sets flood protection setbacks from watercourses while section 523 covers riparian areas. https://www.coquitlam.ca/254/Development-Permits
- No Chineside neighbourhood plan exists in the Interim Official Community Plan's Schedule J neighbourhood plan list — Austin Heights, Burquitlam-Lougheed, City Centre, Hyde Creek, Maillardville, Partington Creek, Smiling Creek and Waterfront Village Centre — so Chineside lots are governed by the city-wide plan, not an area plan. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17458

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Is Chineside in Coquitlam or Port Coquitlam? **A:** Chineside is in Coquitlam. The board sub-area sits in central Coquitlam around Austin Avenue and Como Lake Avenue, the school district is School District No. 43 (Coquitlam), and Coquitlam's taxes, zoning bylaw and utility bills apply — there is no Chineside sub-area in Port Coquitlam, so a listing filed under the wrong city points the buyer at the wrong tax bill and the wrong school locator. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit
- **Q:** Can a Chineside house lot hold more than one home? **A:** Yes, within the City's small-scale multi-unit housing limits. A lot of 280 square metres or smaller can hold up to 3 units, and a lot larger than 280 square metres and under 4,050 square metres can hold up to 4 units, counting secondary suites and carriage or laneway homes. The lot must connect to municipal water and sewer, and the exact zone — R-1, R-2 or R-3 — is confirmed lot by lot. https://www.coquitlam.ca/1361/Small-Scale-Multi-Unit-Housing-in-Coquit
- **Q:** Which schools will my children attend? **A:** The catchment is set by the exact house address, not by the sub-area name. School District No. 43 (Coquitlam) assigns an elementary, middle and secondary school to each address in its School Locator, and no Chineside address-level result is confirmed in the sources read for this note. Programs of Choice — French Immersion, Mandarin Bilingual and Montessori — are separate applications, and cross-catchment requests are decided by a lottery with in-district priority. http://sd43.bc.ca/cross-catchment
- **Q:** Is there a bus on the Chineside streets? **A:** The published bus streets are Como Lake Avenue and Austin Avenue, not the interior streets. TransLink route 153, Coquitlam Central Station to Braid Station, runs Como Lake Avenue and Austin Avenue, and routes 156 and 157 also run Como Lake Avenue on the area's north side. A buyer should check the walk from the specific house to the nearest Como Lake Avenue or Austin Avenue stop against the current timetable. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/221/tt153.pdf
- **Q:** When were Coquitlam property taxes due in 2026? **A:** Coquitlam property taxes were due July 2, 2026, for the January 1 to December 31, 2026 tax year. A 5% penalty applied to any unpaid balance of current taxes after July 2, 2026, and a further 5% penalty applied after September 15, 2026, including balances left unpaid because a Home Owner Grant was not claimed. A buyer closing mid-year should confirm the seller's payment and grant status on the tax certificate. https://coquitlam.ca/544/Property-Taxes

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The "Chines" name is the trap in this sub-area: Chineside and Harbour Chines are separate board sub-areas with separate records, and evidence from one does not describe the other. https://coquitlam.ca/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/1838
- School District No. 43 names Alderson Elementary among its Coquitlam schools south of this slope, but no source read assigns any specific Chineside address to it — catchment is proven only by running the exact address in the district locator. http://sd43.bc.ca/cross-catchment
- Coquitlam offers a Voluntary Residential Water Metering Program: metered properties are billed every four months instead of the annual flat rate, so two otherwise similar Chineside houses can be on different water billing. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17426/2026-Budget-Highlights

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Coquitlam's 2026 Class 1 Residential municipal tax rate is $2.2756 per $1,000 of assessed value — $2.1993 for municipal services plus $0.0763 for drainage — and the grand total with other governments is $3.8520 per $1,000, per the City's Summary of 2026 Tax Rates. https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17264/2026_Tax-Rates
- 2026 property taxes were due July 2, 2026, with a 5% penalty after July 2, 2026 and a further 5% after September 15, 2026. https://coquitlam.ca/544/Property-Taxes
- City utility bills were due March 31, 2026; the 2026–2030 Financial Plan limits 2026 utility increases to 2% for water, 4% for sewer and drainage and 1% for solid waste, and estimates total municipal taxes and utilities for an average residential home at about $4,558. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17424/2026_Financial_Plan_Public_Book
- Coquitlam is a designated taxable area for the provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax, so residential owners here must complete the annual declaration; reserve, treaty and self-governing Indigenous Nation lands are excluded. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check flood information at city scale, not sub-area scale: the City states Coquitlam fronts four major rivers — Brunette, Coquitlam, Pitt and Fraser — that Coquitlam River flood levels use the 200-year design flood, and that dikes and pump stations protect low-lying properties. https://www.coquitlam.ca/224/Floodplain-Dikes
- Check the Zoning Bylaw No. 3000, 1996 section 519 floodplain setbacks on any creek-edge lot: no building within 30 metres of the natural boundary of the Brunette, Coquitlam, Fraser or Pitt rivers, or within 15 metres of another qualifying watercourse, and no building below the flood construction level in designated floodplain. https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/1170/Part-5-General-Regulations-PDF
- Check the Watercourse Protection Development Permit Area before assuming a ravine lot can be built out: the permit area covers land within 30 metres of the top of bank or top of ravine bank of any watercourse. https://www.coquitlam.ca/254/Development-Permits
