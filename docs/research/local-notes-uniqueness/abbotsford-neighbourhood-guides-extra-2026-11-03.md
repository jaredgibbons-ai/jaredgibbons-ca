# Abbotsford Neighbourhood Guides — Extra Local Notes
Page: abbotsford-neighbourhood-guides (City of Abbotsford; Neighbourhood Guides).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Abbotsford's neighbourhoods are mapped three different ways, and this hub page is where the three lists meet: the City's demographic profile areas, the Official Community Plan (OCP) neighbourhood plans, and the real estate board's sub-area names. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/2020%20McKee%20Profile%20-%20spreads%20final.pdf
- The City's 2020 Demographic Profiles number its communities, including Bradner – Mt. Lehman (area 1), Aberdeen (area 6) and West Abbotsford (area 7). https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/2020%20McKee%20Profile%20-%20spreads%20final.pdf
- The OCP carries four named neighbourhood plans — City Centre, Historic Downtown, UDistrict and McKee — which do not cover the rural west side of the city at all. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2025bschedulea
- Board sub-area names such as Aberdeen are used in Fraser Valley board data and do not always match a City profile area or an OCP plan area of the same name.

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The hub's neighbourhoods split into two stocks: urban neighbourhoods inside the urban development boundary, and rural farm communities such as Aberdeen and Bradner where the minimum subdivision lot area is 8.0 hectares (A1 zone) or 16.0 hectares (A2 zone). https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-10/AgRefresh%20St3%20-%20Parcel%20Size.pdf
- McKee, the OCP's New Neighbourhoods area above Auguston and Highlands, is the city's planned growth neighbourhood: its plan area held about 4,600 people in 1,193 dwelling units when the plan was written. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2016b2600schedulee
- Neighbourhood-level stock detail lives in each neighbourhood's own guide note; this hub note does not repeat it — see the aberdeen-guide note for the rural stock and the central-abbotsford-guide note for the urban core stock.

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide Abbotsford zoning, OCP, tax and utility rules are written in full in the abbotsford-homes-for-sale note; see that note rather than repeating them here.
- Which rulebook applies depends on which map the lot falls in: an OCP neighbourhood plan (City Centre, Historic Downtown, UDistrict, McKee) adds plan-level policy on top of Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014, while rural areas outside those plans run on the zoning bylaw and, on farm land, Agricultural Land Reserve rules. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/bylaw-services/bylaw-listings/zoning-bylaw-2014
- A neighbourhood name alone never settles a lot's zone or floodplain status; the City's WebMap shows both for the specific parcel. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/mapping-data-analytics/webmap

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Why does the same Abbotsford area have different names? **A:** Because three different systems name it. The City profiles its communities as numbered demographic areas, the Official Community Plan names four plan areas (City Centre, Historic Downtown, UDistrict and McKee), and the real estate board uses its own sub-area names in its data. A listing's sub-area name, a City profile area and an OCP plan area can cover different ground, so check which system a claim comes from. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-02/2020%20McKee%20Profile%20-%20spreads%20final.pdf
- **Q:** Which Abbotsford neighbourhoods have their own plan? **A:** Four do: City Centre, Historic Downtown, UDistrict and McKee are the neighbourhood plans named in the Official Community Plan. Everywhere else in the city — including the rural west side communities such as Aberdeen and Bradner — is governed by the city-wide OCP and Zoning Bylaw No. 2400-2014 without a neighbourhood plan layer. Aberdeen's own guide note covers that rural position in full. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2025bschedulea
- **Q:** Where do I check which school a neighbourhood feeds? **A:** In the School District No. 34 (SD34) School Locator, by exact address. Catchments are set under SD34 Policy 18 and follow street addresses, not neighbourhood or sub-area names, so two homes in the same named neighbourhood can feed different schools. The one locator run on record in this program, for 2975 Bradner Road in Aberdeen, returned Aberdeen Elementary, Eugene Reimer Middle and Rick Hansen Secondary. https://www.abbyschools.ca/policy-18-school-catchment-areas
- **Q:** Which neighbourhoods are rural and which are urban? **A:** The west side — Aberdeen, Bradner and Mt. Lehman — is rural farm country under the A1 and A2 agricultural zones, with subdivision minimums of 8.0 and 16.0 hectares. The urban neighbourhoods sit inside the urban development boundary around Clearbrook, the City Centre, Mill Lake and the McKee hillside, under urban residential zones such as RS3 and RS7. The City's own profile areas keep the two sets separate. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2021-10/AgRefresh%20St3%20-%20Parcel%20Size.pdf
Read: 2026-11-03

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The McKee Neighbourhood Plan area's existing neighbourhoods are Auguston and Highlands; "McKee" is the plan name for the hillside above them, not a separate older community. https://laws.abbotsford.ca/civix/document/id/coa/coabylaws/2016b2600schedulee
- Mill Lake Park, in the City Centre, is described in the City's Mill Lake Park Master Plan (2025) as the heart of the city's parks system and its premier urban park. https://hdp-ca-prod-app-abbotsford-letstalk-files.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/2117/8513/8191/b55622a4f3630624e77beb7ded37389a_0_-_Executive_Summary.pdf
- The City's 2022–2026 Strategic Plan records a completed Montrose Transit Exchange and an introduced McKee Neighbourhood Plan among its delivered items. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2025-01/2025 Strategic Plan.pdf
- Highstreet Transit Exchange, at Mt. Lehman Road, serves the west side neighbourhoods on BC Transit route 21; Bourquin Exchange serves the City Centre side. https://www.bctransit.com/central-fraser-valley/schedules-and-maps/route-overview/?route=21&schedule=2026-04-05
- Abbotsford Regional Hospital and Cancer Centre, 32900 Marshall Road, sits in the UDistrict side of the city and anchors that neighbourhood plan area. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Abbotsford/abbotsford-regional-hospital-and-cancer-centre
- Rural neighbourhoods on wells and septic are not charged City water and sewer utility fees; urban neighbourhoods are billed bi-monthly — detail in the abbotsford-homes-for-sale note. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/utility-billing

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Every Abbotsford neighbourhood pays the same city rates: the City's 2026 general municipal residential rate is 1.08284 dollars per $1,000 of taxable value — full city-wide detail in the abbotsford-homes-for-sale note. https://www.abbotsford.ca/sites/default/files/2026-05/2026%20Tax%20Rates%20Summary%20May%2022.pdf
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies in every Abbotsford neighbourhood: the Province's taxable-areas list names the City of Abbotsford as a whole, with no neighbourhood exceptions. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas
Read: 2026-11-03

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- One transit system serves all Abbotsford neighbourhoods: BC Transit's Central Fraser Valley system, with exchanges at Bourquin, Clearbrook Centre, Highstreet and Montrose. https://www.bctransit.com/central-fraser-valley/schedules-and-maps/route-overview/?route=21&schedule=2026-04-05
- Route 21 Fraser Hwy is the cross-city link along Fraser Highway, connecting the west side neighbourhoods to Bourquin Exchange in the City Centre. https://www.bctransit.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/cfv_map_21.pdf
- No Abbotsford neighbourhood has rapid transit; the nearest West Coast Express station is Mission City Station, across the Fraser River in Mission. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/west-coast-express
Read: 2026-11-03

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- The risk layers differ by neighbourhood type: floodplain (Zoning Bylaw Schedule E) on the lowland and Matsqui Prairie side, airport height surfaces (City drawing M-500) in the southwest, and Agricultural Land Reserve status across the rural west. https://www.abbotsford.ca/city-services/mapping-data-analytics/webmap
- A hub page cannot settle any of those layers for a specific home; each is a parcel check in the City's WebMap or, for ALR land, on Agricultural Land Commission mapping. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/alr-maps

## 10. HOW IT COMPARES
| Fact | Urban Abbotsford neighbourhoods | Rural Abbotsford neighbourhoods |
|---|---|---|
| Governing plan layer | OCP plus, in four areas, a neighbourhood plan | City-wide OCP and zoning only |
| Subdivision minimum named in City sources | Set by urban zone (RS3, RS7 and others) | 8.0 ha (A1) / 16.0 ha (A2) |
| City water and sewer billing | Billed bi-monthly | Not charged where not connected (well/septic) |
| School district | SD34 | SD34 |
