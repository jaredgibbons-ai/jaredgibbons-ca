# BC Turkey Farms — Extra Local Notes
Page: turkey-farms-for-sale-bc (British Columbia; turkey farms).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- This is a provincial page: the BC Turkey Marketing Board regulates the production of all turkey grown for meat or eggs in British Columbia. https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/
- The Board's office is in Cloverdale, Surrey: British Columbia Turkey, Suite 106 – 19329 Enterprise Way, Surrey, per the Board's own page. https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/
- The shared provincial supply-management framework (quota, BC Farm Industry Review Board (BCFIRB) supervision, national allocation) is in full in the egg-farms-for-sale-bc note; this note covers turkey-specific facts only.

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- A turkey farm is a quota holding in kilograms, not a bird count: the national quota is set in live weight and allotted to registered producers, per BCFIRB's Sunrise decision. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/sunrise_07-09_alloc_dec_sept21_07.pdf
- The personal-use ceiling is small and specific: any entity growing more than the allotted 50 turkeys per year for personal consumption is subject to the Board's Order and Regulations. https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/
- The Board dates to 1966: before it, each farmer negotiated their own price and secured their own poults, with prices sometimes falling below the cost of raising turkeys. https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- The Board licenses the whole chain: under the Natural Products Marketing (BC) Act and the BC Turkey Marketing Scheme it licenses turkey farmers, hatcheries and processors, sets the minimum live price in negotiations with processors, and maintains the quota system. https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/
- The Board is overseen by BCFIRB, the same supervisor as the egg board. https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/
- Quota transfers fund new entrants in turkey too: BCFIRB's 2009 letter records the Turkey Board moving to a direct 5 per cent transfer assessment, with exemptions for direct family members, businesses and partnerships. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/organizational-structure/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/regulated-marketing/supervisory-reviews/specialty-review-2005/09_sep_02_ltr_to_tmb_re_deemed_to_direct_assessment.pdf
- The land under a turkey barn is usually Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR = Agricultural Land Reserve) land, with the ALR Use Regulation residence caps of 500 square metres (m²) principal and 90 m² additional. https://webfiles.islandstrust.bc.ca/islands/local-trust-areas/south-pender/current-projects/Minor%20LUB%20Amendments%20Project/8%20-%20Public%20Hearing%20Binder/05.2_SP-BL-129_Bylaw%20referral_Responses.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** How is turkey quota different from egg quota?
**A:** Turkey quota is measured in live-weight kilograms, egg quota in layers. BCFIRB's Sunrise decision explains that a national turkey quota is established each year by the Canadian Turkey Marketing Agency (now Turkey Farmers of Canada), allocated to provincial boards, which then allot production to registered producers in their jurisdiction. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/sunrise_07-09_alloc_dec_sept21_07.pdf

**Q:** Can I raise turkeys for my own table without dealing with the Board?
**A:** Yes, up to a hard ceiling. The Board states it regulates all turkey grown for meat or eggs in BC, and any entity growing more than the allotted 50 turkeys per year for personal consumption is subject to its Order and Regulations — so 50 is the no-regulation line. https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/

**Q:** Does buying a turkey farm guarantee a processor will take my birds?
**A:** No — processor allocation is a separate Board system. BCFIRB's 2006 supervisory decision allocated base live-weight shares to named BC processors for the 2006/07 quota year (check current), which shows processing capacity is allotted, not automatic with a farm purchase. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/organizational-structure/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/regulated-marketing/supervisory-reviews/supervisory-review-decisions/bc-turkey-marketing-board-decisions/tmb_alloc_dec_mar21_06.pdf

**Q:** Who runs the Turkey Board?
**A:** A government-appointed Chair plus elected growers. The Board states it is comprised of a BC Government appointed Chair and 3 to 4 turkey farmers elected by their peers, serving staggered three-year terms. https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Utilization is not always 100 per cent: BCFIRB's 2019 accountability summary records BC turkey producers used 85.6 per cent of their quota in the April 29, 2018 – April 27, 2019 period (check current). https://www.llbc.leg.bc.ca/public/pubdocs/bcdocs2024/736339/736339_2018_reporting_period_PARP_summary_report.pdf
- The transfer assessment changed method, not just rate: the Board's earlier deemed (pooled) assessment was replaced by a direct assessment on the transferor, with BCFIRB recording it had no objection to the change in 2009. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/organizational-structure/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/regulated-marketing/supervisory-reviews/specialty-review-2005/09_sep_02_ltr_to_tmb_re_deemed_to_direct_assessment.pdf
- Price is negotiated, not posted by the farmer: the Board sets the minimum live price for turkey in negotiations with processors. https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/
- Normal farm practice protection covers turkey barns as farm businesses, through the BCFIRB farm practices complaint process. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/agriculture-seafood/agricultural-land-and-environment/farm-practices-protection/resolving-farm-concerns-and-complaints

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- The transfer cost to budget is the assessment: a direct 5 per cent quota transfer assessment applies on turkey quota transfers, with exemptions for direct family members, businesses and partnerships, per BCFIRB's 2009 letter. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/organizational-structure/boards-commissions-tribunals/bc-farm-industry-review-board/regulated-marketing/supervisory-reviews/specialty-review-2005/09_sep_02_ltr_to_tmb_re_deemed_to_direct_assessment.pdf
- The land is Class 9 only if it produces: BC Assessment requires a prescribed amount of qualifying primary agricultural products for sale for Class 9, Farm classification. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/understanding-property-classes-and-exemptions?TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=582130f6-038f-4842-ada7-0166ce03a246

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- The Board buyers deal with is in Surrey, not Victoria: Suite 106 – 19329 Enterprise Way, Surrey, with office phone 604-534-5644, per the Board page (times UNCONFIRMED). https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Verify quota in kilograms and its processor allocation together: a quota holding without processing room was the subject of the Sunrise and Rossdown appeals decided by BCFIRB. https://bcfarmindustryreviewboard.ca/app/uploads/sites/996/documents/sunrise_07-09_alloc_dec_sept21_07.pdf
- Verify the licence status of the whole chain for the operation: the Board licenses farmers, hatcheries and processors, so a gap at any stage affects the farm's route to market. https://bcturkey.com/bc-turkey-marketing-board/
- Verify the land separately from the quota: ALR status and municipal zoning are checked on the parcel through the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC = Agricultural Land Commission) process, not through the Turkey Board. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/application-and-notice-process/applications/application-process/
