# Surrey Farms — Extra Local Notes
Page: surrey-farms (Surrey; farms).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Surrey farm land is the lowland ring around the urban core, not one district: about one-third of the city is designated Agriculture in the Official Community Plan (OCP), and 95 percent of that land is in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR). https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0236-00.pdf
- The named farm areas in City files are the Serpentine and Nicomekl lowlands through Cloverdale and South Newton, the Colebrook Road area by Mud Bay, Hazelmere and Port Kells in the east and north, and the 184 Street and 192 Street farm blocks in Cloverdale — a current non-farm use file sits at 4114 184 Street and a driving-range file at 4947 192 Street. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7925-0150-00
- Farm Surrey is in the Fraser Valley Real Estate Board (FVREB) area; the board's own community list for its members is Surrey, Langley, White Rock, North Delta, Abbotsford and Mission. https://www.fvreb.bc.ca/about/about-fvreb/

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The farm dwelling pattern is one principal house on a large parcel, sited on a limited farm residential footprint at the front of the lot: a City report for 17023 88 Avenue in Cloverdale describes a 1.98 hectare A-1 (General Agriculture) lot farmed with cedar trees on about 0.48 hectares, with the house siting rules meant to preserve the farmed area behind it. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7924-0231-00.pdf
- Working-farm parcels in City files are large: the 4114 184 Street property is about 12.2 hectares, designated Agricultural in the OCP and zoned General Agriculture (A-1), with the majority of the land actively farmed and leased for greenhouse operations and feed crop cultivation. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7925-0150-00
- The 4947 192 Street property is 16.1 hectares, of which 12 hectares are used by a golf driving range and mini-golf operation that the Agricultural Land Reserve Use Regulation treats as a non-farm use — an example of the non-farm uses that sit inside Surrey's farm belt under Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) permission. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7925-0264-00
- A second, smaller dwelling for farm help is the other recurring farm-house form: a City committee summary for 17139 40 Avenue describes a new principal dwelling plus an additional dwelling unit under 90 square metres for farm help on a 3.8 hectare property with farm status, used for hay production. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/council-minutes/agricultural-and-food-policy-committee-minutes-january-13-2026

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide Surrey zoning, tax and Speculation and Vacancy Tax facts are written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note; the rules below are the ALR and A-1 rules that apply only to farm land. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities
- One principal residence per ALR parcel is the general rule: the ALC states that land in the ALR may have no more than one residence per parcel unless the owner qualifies for an additional residence, and a local government may regulate or restrict residences further through zoning. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- The principal residence size limit is 500 square metres of total floor area, with no more than 1,000 square metres of fill placed for its construction; a larger residence needs ALC approval through a non-adhering residential use application. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- An additional residence is size-limited by parcel size: under section 34.3 of the ALR Use Regulation, on a parcel of 40 hectares or less one residence can be up to 500 square metres and the second is limited to 90 square metres or less, as the City's report for 17023 88 Avenue sets out. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7924-0231-00.pdf
- A secondary suite is permitted in the ALR only inside the principal residence: the ALC requires the suite to be entirely contained within the principal residence, a detached dwelling cannot be used as a secondary suite, and local bylaws must also permit it. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- Subdivision in the ALR needs ALC approval as a rule: the ALC's Information Bulletin 09 covers subdivision and plans that cause subdivision in the ALR, and the permitted subdivisions an approving officer can approve without a Commission application are narrow — consolidations, boundary adjustments with no parcel under 1 hectare and no increase in parcel count, and similar cases. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- A non-farm use on A-1 land needs both City and ALC steps: the 4114 184 Street wellness facility is a Non-Farm Use application under section 20(2) of the ALC Act, which Council must refer to the Agricultural Land Commission for consideration because the use does not conform to the General Agriculture (A-1) zone. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7925-0150-00
- Surrey's own ALR exclusion policy is Policy No. O-51: the City planning report on its agriculture policies names O-51 alongside ALC approval as governing non-farm uses and exclusions on A-1 land. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7918-0236-00.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Can I build a large house on a Surrey farm parcel? **A:** Only up to a limit without applying. The Agricultural Land Commission permits one principal residence of 500 square metres or less of total floor area per ALR parcel, with no more than 1,000 square metres of fill placed to build it. Anything larger needs a non-adhering residential use application, which the Commission can approve only on its own criteria, and Surrey's A-1 zoning and farm residential footprint rules apply on top of the provincial limit. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- **Q:** Can I add a second house for family or farm help? **A:** One additional residence can be permitted without an ALC application if it meets the size rules: on a parcel of 40 hectares or less the additional residence is limited to 90 square metres or less under section 34.3 of the ALR Use Regulation. A City committee file for 17139 40 Avenue shows the Surrey version — a principal dwelling plus an additional unit under 90 square metres for farm help on a farm-status property. More than that needs Commission approval and must be necessary for farm use. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7924-0231-00.pdf
- **Q:** Can I subdivide a Surrey farm into smaller lots? **A:** Not as of right. Subdivision of ALR land is an Agricultural Land Commission matter, and the Commission's Information Bulletin 09 limits the subdivisions an approving officer can approve without an application to cases such as consolidations and boundary adjustments that create no new parcels and leave no parcel under 1 hectare. A plan to split a farm for residential lots is an application with no guaranteed outcome, not a zoning entitlement. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/assets/alc/assets/legislation-and-regulation/information-bulletins/ib_09_subdivision_and_plans_that_cause_subdivision.pdf
- **Q:** Does buying ALR land automatically give me farm tax class? **A:** No — farm classification is a separate BC Assessment program with an application deadline. BC Assessment states that owners must apply for farm classification, that applications and any lease must be submitted by October 31 for the following taxation year, and that late applications are not accepted. Land can be in the Agricultural Land Reserve and still not hold farm class if the income and production requirements are not met. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0
- **Q:** Can I lease the fields to a farmer and keep farm class? **A:** Yes, if the paperwork is filed. BC Assessment states that land leased to a farmer may qualify for farm class if the owner applies, a lease is in place, the leased land makes a reasonable contribution to the farm operation, and the lessee farmer meets the income and sales requirements; a current lease must be resubmitted every seven years and on expiry. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/classifying-land-leased-to-farmers
- **Q:** Is a lowland farm in the Serpentine or Nicomekl a normal drainage situation? **A:** No — it sits behind dykes and sea dams. The City states the Serpentine and Nicomekl rivers are controlled by sea dams at King George Highway and Elgin Road whose gates open at low tide to let fresh water flow to Mud Bay and close at high tide to keep brackish water out of farm land, and that each agricultural property is serviced by a pump station, flood box or localized filling under the lowlands plan. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- The Surrey Dyking District dates to 1910: the City records that farmers began reclaiming the lowlands with dykes and canals in the late 1800s and that the Surrey Dyking District was formed in 1910 under the Drainage, Ditch, and Dike Act to build sea dams on both the Serpentine and Nicomekl Rivers. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands
- Lowland drainage is still an active City file in 2026: the Agricultural and Food Policy Committee received a Serpentine/Nicomekl Lowlands Drainage Servicing Update on March 10, 2026, covering the system that services each agricultural property with a pump station, flood box or localized filling. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/council-minutes/agricultural-and-food-policy-committee-minutes-march-10-2026
- The coastal floodplain holds over 60 square kilometres of agricultural land and over $100 million in annual farm gate revenue, per the City's coastal floodplain page — the scale behind Surrey's drainage parcel charges and flood work. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- A farm home plate has a depth limit in A-1 practice: the City's report for 17023 88 Avenue describes the maximum depth of the farm residential footprint and of a single-family dwelling as intended to preserve agricultural land, and the application needed a rezoning to a Comprehensive Development zone to relax farm homeplate and dwelling setbacks. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7924-0231-00.pdf
- Fill is regulated on a farm house site: the ALC caps fill for a principal residence at 1,000 square metres including structural aggregate for foundations and driveways, and fill beyond that requires ALC authorization through its notice-of-intent or soil-or-fill application process. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Surrey's 2026 property tax rate and Speculation and Vacancy Tax position are city-wide facts written in full in the surrey-houses-for-sale note — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note; the farm-specific cost is the drainage parcel tax. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Drainage parcel tax for Class 1 (residential) property in Surrey is $250.71 for 2026, collected with the tax notice — farm parcels carrying a residential class component meet this charge alongside the $300.00 Capital Parcel Tax. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- Farm classification changes the assessed value the tax is applied to: BC Assessment states the Classification of Land as a Farm Regulation sets income thresholds a producing farm must meet to obtain and keep farm class, and that BC Assessment must confirm a farm continues to meet them, because farm class brings regulated land rates and lower assessed values. https://info.bcassessment.ca/Services-products/property-classes-and-exemptions/farm-land-assessment/farm-classification-in-british-columbia/Apply-for-farm-classification?bcgovtm=Junewebinarannouncement&TermStoreId=2e29bc59-558d-448c-9c19-d11b487dbf89&TermSetId=0a8def97-2239-4f44-b0fc-f2f10a0ea2d4&TermId=edf88474-1907-4185-b77b-5cd510f603a0

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Farm Surrey is road country: Highway 15 (176 Street, Pacific Highway) runs through the Cloverdale farm blocks to the Pacific Highway border crossing, and Highway 99 runs through South Surrey past the Hazelmere farm area to the Peace Arch crossing, as the City's community descriptions note for South Surrey's two border crossings. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities
- Colebrook Road and King George Boulevard are the lowland farm connectors across the Serpentine and Nicomekl floodplain between Newton, Cloverdale and Mud Bay — the same corridors the City's flood pages name for high-tide and dike work, so a farm viewing after a storm should check road conditions first. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- No rapid transit serves Surrey's farm areas; the nearest stations are the Expo Line stations in Whalley and, on completion of the Surrey-Langley SkyTrain extension, the Fraser Highway stations — see the surrey-houses-for-sale note. https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Confirm ALR status parcel by parcel before anything else: the ALC's housing page states the one-residence rule and size limits apply to land in the ALR, and local zoning can be stricter — a Surrey farm buyer needs both the ALC position and the A-1 zone position for the specific parcel. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- Check the floodplain layer for any lowland parcel: the City states most of Surrey's floodplain is protected by dykes, that sea dams on the Nicomekl and Serpentine were first built in 1912 and 1913, and that spillway cells are typically located on agricultural fields — a field can be a designed flood-holding area, which the listing will not say. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- Check for existing non-farm uses and their ALC decisions before buying a farm with a business on it: the 4947 192 Street range operates under ALC Resolution #442/2021 and a historic restrictive covenant prohibiting new golf facility structures or expansion, per the City report — permissions can be conditional and time-limited. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/council-meetings-documents/planning-reports/planning-report-7925-0264-00
- Soil and fill history matters in the ALR: the ALC regulates fill placement for a principal residence above 1,000 square metres of fill area, so a parcel with extensive placed fill may carry an unauthorized-fill issue the buyer inherits. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
