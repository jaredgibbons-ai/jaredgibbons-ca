## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Sullivan Heights is the detached-house subdivision east of Stoney Creek Park in northeast Burnaby, below Simon Fraser University (SFU). Heritage Burnaby, the City's museum service, places the Sullivan Heights single-family subdivision east of Stoney Creek Park, with higher-density housing west of the park. https://search.heritageburnaby.ca/link/landmark823
- Lougheed Highway bounds the houses on the south and North Road is the Burnaby–Coquitlam municipal boundary on the east. The City counts Lougheed Town Centre as one of Burnaby's four town centres, and the station area sits at the south edge of the house streets. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/about-burnaby/town-centres
- Cameron Street, Austin Avenue and Beaverbrook Drive carry the local house traffic between Lougheed Highway and the Broadway slope. The temporary Cameron Community Centre now operates at 110-9855 Austin Avenue while the permanent centre is rebuilt. https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/cameron-recreation-complex

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The house stock is a 1950s single-family subdivision. Heritage Burnaby dates the Sullivan Heights subdivision east of Stoney Creek Park to the 1950s, separate from the multi-family buildings west of the park developed after 1974. https://search.heritageburnaby.ca/link/landmark823
- Houses here are freehold lots, not strata. Burnaby rezoned its house districts into the single R1 Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) District in June 2024, so a Sullivan Heights house lot is a fee-simple lot under that district unless a strata plan is registered on it. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- Infill on these lots means multiplexes, duplexes and suites, not towers. The R1 district permits single-family homes, duplexes, multiplexes, rowhouses and secondary suites on the same streets as the 1950s houses. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- A standard Sullivan Heights lot can hold 3 to 4 homes, and 6 near frequent transit. Under the R1 SSMUH District, lots up to 280 square metres allow 3 units, larger lots allow 4, and lots of at least 281 square metres within 400 metres of a frequent-transit stop allow 6; a secondary suite counts in the total — see the vancouver-heights-burnaby-houses-for-sale note for the city-wide position. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- A Stoney Creek lot can need a streamside permit before any rebuild. The City requires an assessment for a property within 30 metres of a stream, ravine or wetland to identify the Streamside Protection and Enhancement Area (SPEA) kept clear of new building. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/development-permits-construction/new-home-construction
- Splitting a multiplex into saleable units is allowed, but a suite cannot be split off. Lots in the R1 district with two or more primary dwelling units may be stratified, while a secondary suite must stay on the same title as the primary unit inside it. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Which elementary school serves a Sullivan Heights house? **A:** It depends on the exact street, so check the address first. Cameron Elementary is at 9540 Erickson Drive, and School District 41 (SD41) groups Cameron, Lyndhurst and Stoney Creek Community elementary schools with Burnaby Mountain Secondary in its Cariboo-Lougheed zone. Catchments are address-based, so run the house address in the SD41 School Locator before relying on any school claim. https://burnabyschools.ca/elementary-school-addresses/
- **Q:** Can I rebuild a 1950s house as a multiplex? **A:** Yes, under the R1 SSMUH District, without a rezoning. A larger lot allows 4 homes and a lot of at least 281 square metres within 400 metres of a frequent-transit stop allows 6, with forms including duplexes, multiplexes, rowhouses and secondary suites. Confirm the lot's zoning and any streamside area on BurnabyMap first. https://www.burnaby.ca/our-city/projects/zoning-bylaw-rewrite
- **Q:** Does the Stoney Creek ravine limit what I can build? **A:** It can on lots near the creek. A property within 30 metres of a stream, ravine or wetland needs an assessment that sets the protected SPEA, and new building and land disturbance are kept out of that area. Ask the City whether the specific lot is subject to streamside protection before pricing a rebuild or addition. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/development-permits-construction/new-home-construction
- **Q:** How do I get downtown without driving Lougheed Highway? **A:** Use Lougheed Town Centre Station at the south edge of the neighbourhood. TransLink route 136 runs from Lougheed Station via Gatineau Place, Austin Road and Lougheed Highway, linking the Cameron and Austin Road streets to Production Way–University and Brentwood stations, and the station itself is the Expo and Millennium Line interchange for the town centre. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/213/tt136.pdf
- **Q:** Can I remove a large tree to fit a bigger house? **A:** Not without checking the Burnaby Tree Bylaw first. The City decides tree-cutting permits against set criteria, protected trees removed for development need replacement trees shown on the site plan, and driveways and construction must sit outside tree protection areas. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/trees/tree-removal-and-replacement

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Cameron Elementary sits inside the house area at 9540 Erickson Drive. SD41 lists Cameron among its elementary schools with an attendance-area map per school, so two houses a few streets apart can sit in different catchments. https://burnabyschools.ca/elementary-school-addresses/
- The Cameron Community Centre and Library are in a temporary home inside The City of Lougheed Shopping Centre at 110-9855 Austin Avenue during construction of the replacement facility, with extra classes shifted to Charles Rummel Centre. https://www.burnaby.ca/recreation-and-arts/recreation-facilities/cameron-recreation-complex
- A rented suite changes the utility bill on a Sullivan Heights house. The City's 2026 residential utility table charges a rented suite in a single-family dwelling $326.17 for water and $403.00 for sewer use, on top of the house charges. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees
- Stoney Creek Community School students work on the creek itself. SD41 reports Stoney Creek classes cleaning the creek banks and writing to support preservation of the creek and its spawning salmon, which is why creek-side lots carry the streamside rules in Section 3. https://burnabyschools.ca/honouring-the-planet/

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Burnaby property taxes for 2026 were due July 3, 2026, with a 5% penalty after the July due date and a further 5% after the September due date — see the vancouver-heights-burnaby-houses-for-sale note for the city-wide rate position. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/property-taxes/fees-charges-and-penalties
- A detached Sullivan Heights house pays the 2026 single-family water charge of $652.33, with a 5% discount if the utility notice is paid by its due date; for single-family homes the sewer parcel tax is billed on the property tax notice, moving to the utility notice in 2027. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/utility-fees-and-charges/residential-utility-fees

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the Stoney Creek SPEA on any lot backing onto the ravine. The Burnaby 2050 OCP designates a Streamside Protection and Enhancement Development Permit Area, and its guidelines set how the protected area is measured on a specific parcel, by City mapping or by a qualified environmental professional. http://www.city.burnaby.bc.ca/sites/default/files/acquiadam/2026-06/Streamside-Protection-and-Enhancement-Development-Permit-Area-Guidelines.pdf
- Check heritage status and title charges on BurnabyMap before planning a teardown. The City's new-home page directs buyers to BurnabyMap for heritage flags and to the Land Title and Survey Authority of British Columbia (LTSA) for easements and rights-of-way that can limit a rebuild. https://www.burnaby.ca/services-and-payments/development-permits-construction/new-home-construction
