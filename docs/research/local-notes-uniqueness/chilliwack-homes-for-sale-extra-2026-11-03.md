# Chilliwack Homes for Sale — Extra Local Notes
Page: chilliwack-homes-for-sale (Chilliwack; homes for sale).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Chilliwack is one city in the Fraser Valley Regional District (FVRD), split by the Vedder River into a north side (Chilliwack Proper, Downtown, Fairfield Island, Rosedale) and a south side (Sardis, Vedder, Garrison Crossing, Yarrow). https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=1618
- The city's 2050 Official Community Plan (OCP), Bylaw 2025, No. 5500, divides Chilliwack into Schedule B plan areas: B1 Agricultural, B2 Chilliwack Proper and Fairfield Island, B3 Downtown, B4 Eastern Hillsides, B5 Sardis, B6 South Vedder and B7 Yarrow. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2387
- Nearly 70 percent of Chilliwack's land is in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) and not available for development, so the OCP focuses growth on densification inside the urban growth boundary. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=1618
- The city sits on the traditional, ancestral and unceded territory of the Stó:lō Coast Salish peoples, and more than ten First Nations share borders with the city, including Xwchíyò:m (Cheam), Sqwá (Skwah) and Ch'íyáqtel (Tzeachten). https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=1618

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Chilliwack's homes divide by plan area, not by one city-wide pattern: older serviced lots in Chilliwack Proper (Schedule B2), post-1990s subdivisions in Sardis (B5), master-planned Garrison Crossing streets in South Vedder (B6), and large rural lots in Yarrow (B7). https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2387
- Garrison Crossing, the redeveloped former Canadian Forces Base (CFB) Chilliwack married quarters, holds more than 1,700 housing units mixing refurbished single-family homes with new townhouses and condominium apartments on a 153-acre site. https://www.clc-sic.ca/node/69
- Chilliwack's growth strategy directs selective growth to designated hillside areas served by municipal services, including the Eastern Hillsides (Schedule B4), while rural settlement boundaries in areas like Yarrow are held as they are. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=1618

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- City-wide rules that affect every Chilliwack home type — Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000, small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH), and Floodplain Regulation Bylaw 2018, No. 4519 — are written in full in the chilliwack-houses-for-sale note; this note adds only guide-level plan direction.
- The 2050 OCP sets Development Permit Areas (DPAs) city-wide, including DPA 4 Farm Home Plate for housing on ALR valley-floor farms of 2 hectares or more, and DPA 8 Residential, which applies to coach houses, garden suites, SSMUH, townhouses and apartments. http://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2182
- The OCP's floodplain policy discourages development in unprotected floodplain areas and requires new construction in floodplain and alluvial fan areas to meet the Floodplain Regulation Bylaw's floodproofing standards. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/pdfbook/planning/2040OCP/files/assets/basic-html/page-54.html

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** What is the difference between Chilliwack's north side and south side? **A:** The Vedder River divides them. The north side holds the original town — Downtown, Chilliwack Proper and Fairfield Island under OCP Schedule B2 and B3 — plus Rosedale to the east. The south side holds Sardis, the city's second commercial centre, and the Vedder and Garrison Crossing neighbourhoods under Schedules B5 and B6, with Yarrow (B7) in the rural southwest. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2387
- **Q:** Why is so much of Chilliwack farmland instead of housing? **A:** Nearly 70 percent of the city's land base is inside the provincial Agricultural Land Reserve and not available for development. The OCP answers that limit by directing growth into redevelopment and densification of existing urban areas inside the urban growth boundary, rather than outward onto farm land. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=1618
- **Q:** What plan governs what can be built next to a home I buy? **A:** The 2050 Official Community Plan, Bylaw 2025, No. 5500, sets city-wide land use designations, and its Schedule B neighbourhood plans — Sardis (B5), South Vedder (B6), Yarrow (B7) and others — carry the area-specific direction. The City folded each neighbourhood plan's land uses into the 2050 OCP designations in its 2025 update. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2387
- **Q:** Which schools will my children attend? **A:** All of Chilliwack is in School District No. 33 (SD33), and catchments are set by street address, not neighbourhood name, so check the exact address in the district's School Locator. The district is consulting on rebalancing enrolment for September 2027 because several schools are over capacity, so a catchment confirmed today can change. https://sd33.bc.ca/index.php/find-your-school

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Chilliwack began as a river settlement: the Township of Chilliwhack was incorporated in 1873, the third oldest municipality in British Columbia, with settlement first concentrated at Chilliwack Landing on the Fraser River. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=167
- The commercial centre later moved inland to Five Corners, the junction of the New Westminster–Yale Wagon Road, Wellington Avenue and Young Road; the City and Township co-existed as separate municipalities for 72 years before merging in 1980. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=167
- City Hall and most civic services sit at 8550 Young Road, and the City — not a separate diking district — is the local diking authority maintaining the Fraser and Vedder River dike system. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2825
- Chilliwack General Hospital, the city's hospital with emergency care, is at 45600 Menholm Road on the north side; major institutions on the south side include the University of the Fraser Valley (UFV) Chilliwack campus at Canada Education Park, 45190 Caen Avenue. https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Locations/Chilliwack/chilliwack-general-hospital https://www.ufv.ca/peace-and-reconciliation/contact-us/

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- BC Transit, not TransLink, runs Chilliwack transit; Downtown Chilliwack is the main exchange for local routes 51 to 59, and regional routes 66, 71 and 72 also serve the city. https://www.bctransit.com/chilliwack/schedules-and-maps/
- Route 66 Fraser Valley Express is the regional link, running from Downtown Chilliwack to Lougheed Town Centre Station in Burnaby via Abbotsford and Langley, with Chilliwack stops including Vedder Road at Luckakuck Way and the Lickman Park & Ride. https://www.bctransit.com/chilliwack/schedules-and-maps/
- Route 71 Agassiz–Harrison connects Downtown Chilliwack with Rosedale, Agassiz and Harrison Hot Springs via Yale Road and Highway 9, and is administered by the FVRD with the City of Chilliwack as a cost-sharing partner. https://www.fvrd.ca/EN/main/services/transit.html
- Highway 1 (Trans-Canada Highway) crosses the north side of the city with interchanges serving both sides; Highway 9 leaves Highway 1 at Exit 135 for Rosedale, Agassiz and Harrison Hot Springs. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation-projects/other-transportation-projects/agassiz-rosedale-bridge

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- A large portion of Chilliwack lies within the floodplain of the Fraser River and, to a smaller extent, the Vedder River; the City maintains an extensive diking system and publishes flood protection information and freshet updates keyed to the Mission gauge. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2825
- The OCP states that, except for the hillsides and the Sardis–Vedder area, Chilliwack is situated on floodplains, with major flood events in 1894 and 1948 (Fraser River) and 1975 (Vedder River) driving its dike building programs. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/pdfbook/planning/2040OCP/files/assets/basic-html/page-54.html
- Before buying, check the specific lot on the City's floodplain mapping for its flood construction level, and check whether it falls in a Development Permit Area for drinking water supply (DPA 1), hillside environment (DPA 2) or riparian area (DPA 3) under the 2050 OCP. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2387
