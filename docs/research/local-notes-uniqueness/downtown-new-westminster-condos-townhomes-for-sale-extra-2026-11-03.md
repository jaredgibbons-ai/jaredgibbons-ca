# Downtown New Westminster Condos and Townhomes — Extra Local Notes
Page: downtown-new-westminster-condos-townhomes-for-sale (Downtown New Westminster; condos and townhomes).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Downtown is New Westminster's Fraser River waterfront centre, planned under its own Downtown Community Plan, created in 2010, with land use designations separate from the city-wide Official Community Plan (OCP). https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Schedule_C___Downtown_Community_Plan___Revised_Aug_28_2017(1).pdf
- The condo and townhome streets are Columbia Street, Carnarvon Street, Agnes Street and Royal Avenue, between the Pattullo Bridge side at McBride Boulevard and Tenth Street, above the Quayside waterfront on the Fraser River. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Downtown_Land_Use_Designations_and_Map___Feb_2026.pdf
- Downtown is designated a Regional City Centre in Metro 2050, a priority location for higher-density housing, employment, services and amenities serving a Metro Vancouver sub-region. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Proposed_Growth_Management_Chapter___May_2026(1).pdf
- No earlier Downtown page exists in this program, so downtown facts are carried in full here; New Westminster city-wide facts are in the the-heights-new-westminster-houses-for-sale note.

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The downtown stock is towers and low-rise strata along Carnarvon Street and Agnes Street: the local index records 618 Carnarvon at 618 Carnarvon Street, Azure 1 and 2 at 898 and 892 Carnarvon Street, Marinus at Plaza 88 at 888 Carnarvon Street, Ovation at 823 Carnarvon Street and Westminster Towers at 828 and 838 Agnes Street — addresses as indexed, to be confirmed on title. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Downtown_Land_Use_Designations_and_Map___Feb_2026.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Most of downtown sits inside provincial Transit Oriented Development Areas (TOD Areas): land within 800 metres of a SkyTrain station, where the City must allow up to 20 storeys and 5.0 floor space ratio (FSR) within 200 metres, up to 12 storeys and 4.0 FSR at 201 to 400 metres, and up to 8 storeys and 3.0 FSR at 401 to 800 metres. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Oct_27_Bylaw_1__Provincial_Legislation_Requirements.pdf
- A TOD designation is not a rezoning: the City states it is not pre-zoning the TOD Areas, most sites still need a rezoning to reach the provincial heights and densities, and the legislation does not override municipal zoning. https://www.newwestcity.ca/planning-building-and-development/new-westminsters-official-community-plan/provincial-housing-legislation-implementation/sb_expander_articles/7565.php
- In TOD Areas, residential vehicle parking cannot be required — a direct check for any new downtown building's parking count. https://www.beheardnewwest.ca/uploads/8ae80625-458b-4911-8c33-3fe9a89e33eb/files/file/content/3014d05e-c879-4a90-b7a5-c400cdd719d0/611_Agnes_Street___Preliminary_Report_to_Council__June_23__2025_.pdf?Expires=1792174563&Signature=iWaFy9fl4MX3dQpIdDzGRRW-FOeE7Fo3OfBaXqAHN2IX8vQd67VniSNNkZuIdEA4t1W86ki3IGJfgAyQKu310VoAnxnRSoyDPnCpMUAkq1YzbVAhFTI6REeM5oIb7oGH4VxW~ZfppO4vVQZtXqQ4JaKslk55~iijRR6g6dNLW~CCwDbtiKycxmupzHpJlkTyEFzMjhPVxiB~AF4JBS7gR2VXynQJz09GGyiuE9SjitaKolaFIZ-mgnvkrWy-eXKH~wJTCoutIRI9taeoK~-aN~CQZZruv33O44w__&Key-Pair-Id=KDMH7OGHEFBEN

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Which SkyTrain stations are actually downtown? **A:** Two Expo Line stations sit inside the plan area: Columbia Station at Columbia and Fourth Street, and New Westminster Station at Columbia and Eighth Street. That two-station coverage is why almost every downtown condo and townhome falls inside a provincial TOD Area band, and why the station a building is measured from matters for its site's permitted height. https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/skytrain/?page=1
- **Q:** Who supplies electricity to a downtown condo? **A:** The City itself. New Westminster runs its own Electrical Utility under Electrical Utility Bylaw No. 6502, 1998, distributing electricity to residents and businesses and maintaining the city-owned distribution system, streetlights and traffic signals — so the electricity account is with the City, not BC Hydro. Overdue closed electrical accounts are transferred to property taxes under the Community Charter. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Consolidated_Bylaw_6502__1998_Electrical_Utility__2025_09_29_consolidation_.pdf
- **Q:** Which elementary school serves downtown families? **A:** École Qayqayt Elementary, 85 Merivale Street, Kindergarten to Grade 5, is the downtown school in School District No. 40 (New Westminster). Catchment is by address, and the district has been adjusting downtown French Immersion arrangements at Qayqayt, so families should confirm the current catchment and program placement with the district for the specific building. https://studentsuccess.gov.bc.ca/school/04040015
- **Q:** Are water and sewer metered for downtown strata? **A:** No — the City bills water, sewer and solid waste as an annual flat rate. The 2026 statement was mailed in late February, and the discounted amount required full payment by Monday, May 4, 2026; multi-family buildings are charged per living unit under the City's rate table. https://www.newwestcity.ca/propertytaxes-utilities/sb_expander_articles/1271.php

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Anvil Centre, 777 Columbia Street, is the City's civic and cultural centre in the middle of the condo district, and Westminster Pier Park at 600 Quayside Drive is the waterfront park below it. https://www.newwestcity.ca/parks/
- Bus routes 105 and 106 both terminate at New Westminster Station and run via Carnarvon Street, so the townhome streets themselves carry the Uptown and Edmonds bus service. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt105.pdf
- The City's electrical utility is a separate bill from its flat-rate water, sewer and solid waste statement, and a separate pre-authorized payment plan applies to electrical charges. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/CNW_DOCS_1196591_v5_PAP___Electrical_Utility.pdf
- Columbia Street is the historic commercial street and is not a truck route through downtown, per the Pattullo Bridge replacement environmental assessment's community chapter. https://projects.eao.gov.bc.ca/api/document/5b734322d496410024db2288/fetch/Section_6.3_Community_Cohesion.pdf

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- New Westminster's city-wide tax rates and due dates are carried in the the-heights-new-westminster-houses-for-sale note; the downtown-specific charges are the two below.
- Water, sewer and solid waste are one annual flat-rate statement: for a single-family dwelling the 2026 net total after the 5% discount was $2,753.73, and multi-family buildings are charged per living unit from the same City rate table. https://www.newwestcity.ca/propertytaxes-utilities/sb_expander_articles/1271.php
- Electricity is a City utility charge under Bylaw No. 6502, 1998, separate from property taxes, with its own rates schedule. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Consolidated_Bylaw_6502__1998_Electrical_Utility__2025_09_29_consolidation_.pdf
- New Westminster is a designated taxable area for the provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- Columbia Station and New Westminster Station, both Expo Line, are the downtown stations, and New Westminster Station is also the bus exchange where routes 105 and 106 terminate. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt106.pdf
- Route 106 runs New Westminster Station to Edmonds Station via Carnarvon Street and Sixth Street, and Route 105 runs Uptown to New Westminster Station via Carnarvon, Eighth and Columbia Streets. https://infomaps.translink.ca/Public_Timetables/218/tt105.pdf
- The Pattullo Bridge at the east end of downtown is the road crossing to Surrey; its replacement project is a provincial project and its current opening status was not confirmed from an official page in this pass — see NOT CONFIRMED.

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the Fraser River side first: downtown sits between Royal Avenue and the river, and the City's Development Permit Areas tool is the place to confirm which permit area, including hazard-related areas, applies to a specific lot — the plan pages do not give a lot-level flood conclusion. https://www.newwestcity.ca/development-policies-and-process/development-permit-areas
- Check heritage on Columbia and Carnarvon Streets per building: the Downtown Community Plan carries its own designations including Mixed Use – Columbia Historic, so a low-rise strata on the historic street can carry heritage conditions a tower two blocks away does not. https://www.newwestcity.ca/database/files/library/Downtown_Land_Use_Designations_and_Map___Feb_2026.pdf
- Check the TOD band and the zoning separately: the City confirms a TOD designation does not change zoning by itself, so permitted height on paper and approved height on a specific site are two different checks. https://www.newwestcity.ca/planning-building-and-development/new-westminsters-official-community-plan/provincial-housing-legislation-implementation/sb_expander_articles/7565.php
