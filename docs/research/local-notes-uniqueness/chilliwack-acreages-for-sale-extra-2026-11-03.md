# Chilliwack Acreages — Extra Local Notes
Page: chilliwack-acreages-for-sale (Chilliwack; acreages).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- Chilliwack acreages trade across the whole City of Chilliwack, a Chilliwack and District Real Estate Board (CADREB) market, with the flat farm parcels on the valley floor and the upland parcels at Ryder Lake, Majuba Hill and the Eastern Hillsides. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- The City states agriculture accounts for 67% of Chilliwack's total land base, and the Official Community Plan (OCP), Official Community Plan Bylaw 2025, No. 5500 ("Chilliwack 2050"), plans that land under Schedule B1, the Agricultural Area Plan, with neighbourhood plans B2 to B7 for the urban and village areas including Yarrow (B7). https://chilliwack.com/ocp

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Acreage zoning is set in Section 6 of Zoning Bylaw 2020, No. 5000: in the AL (Agricultural Lowland) zone the minimum lot area for a subdivision inside the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) is 7.5 hectares (ha), with smaller figures only for an approved boundary adjustment (1 ha, or 0.2 ha where the adjustment improves farm use). https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- The AL zone caps the farm home plate at 4,000 square metres (m²) for the principal residence and 1,000 m² for an additional residence, and allows only one structure for principal residential use per lot. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- In the RS-FHA (Flood Hazard Area) zone the minimum lot area is 8 ha where land is excluded from the ALR or subdivision inside the ALR is approved, and residential use is capped at 150 m² of lot coverage. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- Smaller rural parcels exist in the small-lot agriculture zones: the City's Small Lot Agriculture Study records minimums of 2.0 ha for AS (Agriculture Small Lot) and 1.0 ha for AR (Agriculture Residential, with Agricultural Land Commission approval of a homesite severance) (check current: the study cites the former Zoning Bylaw No. 2800, 2001; confirm figures in Bylaw No. 5000). https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=8630

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Subdivision inside the ALR requires Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) approval on top of the City's process, and ALR residences cannot be stratified: one principal residence per parcel (maximum 500 m²), a secondary suite only inside the principal residence, and an additional residence capped at 90 m² (parcels of 40 ha or less) or 186 m² (over 40 ha). https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/
- A Farm Home Plate Development Permit (OCP permit area D-4) is required when the Zoning Bylaw's farm home plate regulations are not met, and an ALC application may also be required for a new house, coach house or suite, for placing fill or removing soil, and for non-farm uses. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- Small-scale multi-unit housing (SSMUH = small-scale multi-unit housing) is an urban tool here only: Bylaw No. 5395 (adopted June 18, 2024) allows 3 to 4 dwelling units on parcels inside the urban growth boundary zoned for single-detached and duplex housing — it does not open up ALR acreages. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
- OPEN CONFLICT on the subdivision bylaw: the City's subdivisions webpage states that all subdivisions are subject to the "Land Development Bylaw 2014, No. 3055," but the adoption of that bylaw could not be verified in this program's earlier ledger, so this note does not assert Bylaw No. 3055 as governing — confirm the servicing bylaw with the City in writing. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=653
- Hillside land is regulated by slope: the City defines hillside areas as all lands with slopes of 20 percent or more, under its Hillside Development Standards, and most subdivisions also need a Tree and Green Space Management Plan under the Tree Management (Land Development) Bylaw 2008, No. 3585. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=653

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
**Q:** Can I subdivide a Chilliwack farm acreage into smaller lots?
**A:** Almost never on ALR ground. The AL zone's minimum lot area for a subdivision inside the ALR is 7.5 ha, any subdivision inside the reserve also needs ALC approval, and the smaller figures (1 ha, or 0.2 ha) exist only for boundary adjustments that improve farm use. Price an acreage as one parcel unless the ALC has approved otherwise. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf

**Q:** Can I build a second home or a coach house on my acreage?
**A:** Only inside the ALC's caps if the land is in the ALR: one principal residence up to 500 m², a secondary suite only inside that residence, and one additional residence of at most 90 m² on parcels up to 40 ha or 186 m² above 40 ha. A Farm Home Plate Development Permit (D-4) is also required where the home-plate rules are not met. https://www.alc.gov.bc.ca/use-of-alr-land/housing-in-the-alr/

**Q:** How do I check whether a parcel is in the ALR?
**A:** Start with the City's own direction: the City points owners to its Webmap for ALR status, and the ALC's ALR Property and Map Finder checks the parcel by PID or civic address. With agriculture at 67% of the city's land base, most large parcels are in or beside the reserve, and ALR status decides the subdivision, housing and fill rules. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009

**Q:** What do the flood rules mean for a low-lying acreage?
**A:** They set where a house can sit. Floodplain Regulation Bylaw 2018, No. 4519 sets minimum building setbacks of 60 m from the Fraser River, 75 m from the Vedder River and 30 m from the Chilliwack River and Vedder Canal; the City maintains diking for the Fraser and Vedder systems, and drainage and dyking taxes are levied on top of municipal rates. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=999

**Q:** Can an urban Chilliwack lot carry three or four homes instead?
**A:** Yes, but only inside the urban growth boundary. Bylaw No. 5395 allows 3 to 4 dwelling units on parcels within the boundary zoned for single-detached and duplex housing. That right does not extend to ALR acreages, where the ALC caps govern instead. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/377/ZBA%5F5000%5F%2D%5FZoning%5FBylaw%5F2020%2Epdf
Read: 2026-11-03

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Farm-edge deals run through a standing agricultural review: the Agricultural Area Plan was created in 2012 in partnership with the Chilliwack Agricultural Commission, and the Agricultural and Rural Advisory Committee still monitors its implementation by reviewing agricultural development applications. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=3009
- Development cost and tree rules bite on acreage subdivisions too: the Tree Management (Land Development) Bylaw framework requires a tree density of at least 50 trees per hectare on development sites, planted or retained. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=653
- Chilliwack bills water and sewer quarterly, not on the tax notice: the flat rate for a 20-millimetre connection is $33.99 for water and $55.18 for sewer per three months, with metered rates of $2.00 and $4.09 per 100 cubic feet. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/2527/Water%5F%5FSewer%5FRates%5Ffor%5FWeb%2Epdf
- Solid waste moved bylaws in 2026: collection is now billed quarterly under the Solid Waste Management Bylaw 2026, No. 5541, which repealed the 2019 bylaw. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/363/BL5541%5F1%2Epdf
- School District No. 33 (SD33) catchments are address-based, and capacity is tight enough that the district just removed 11 portable classrooms from Sardis Secondary after its 400-seat addition opened — run the exact acreage address with SD33 rather than assuming the nearest school. https://sd33.bc.ca/news/sardis-celebrate-70th-anniversary-new-addition

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Chilliwack's 2026 Class 1 Residential rate is 3.00943 per $1,000 for the municipal portion and 4.66727 per $1,000 in total, plus a drainage charge of 0.12024 per $1,000 on land only. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/2527/Tax%5FRates%5Ffor%5Fthe%5FWeb%2Dmou9gntw%5Fc%2Epdf
- Taxes are due the first working day after July 1; a 5% penalty applies to taxes outstanding after July 2, 2026 and a further 5% after August 4, 2026, and tax notices are mailed at the end of May. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=2522
- Quarterly water and sewer flat rates (20-millimetre connection): water $33.99 and sewer $55.18 per three months. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/Files/2527/Water%5F%5FSewer%5FRates%5Ffor%5FWeb%2Epdf
- The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies: the Province's taxable-areas list names the City of Chilliwack, so residential owners, including acreage owners, file the annual declaration. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/speculation-vacancy-tax/how-tax-works/taxable-areas

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- BC Transit runs the Chilliwack system: route 51 (Vedder) links downtown and the Vedder Road corridor, route 55 (Yarrow-Greendale) is the rural route serving the Yarrow and Greendale acreage side, and route 66 (Fraser Valley Express) runs Highway 1 to Abbotsford, Langley (Carvolth Exchange) and Lougheed SkyTrain Station. https://www.bctransit.com/agassiz-harrison/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/02/chw_map_54.pdf
- Highway 1 (the Trans-Canada Highway) crosses the city's north side with interchanges at Lickman Road and Vedder Road; most valley-floor acreages reach it by Yale Road or Vedder Road. https://www.bctransit.com/agassiz-harrison/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/02/chw_map_54.pdf

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood is the first check on valley-floor land: Floodplain Regulation Bylaw 2018, No. 4519 setbacks (60 m Fraser River, 75 m Vedder River, 30 m Chilliwack River and Vedder Canal) plus the diking network frame the risk, and the RS-FHA (Flood Hazard Area) zone carries its own 8 ha minimum lot area — the bylaw tells you the setback, not the flood construction level for a specific homesite, which is a parcel-level confirmation. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/attachments/attachView.cfm?attachID=999
- Hillside acreages (Ryder Lake, Eastern Hillsides) sit under the City's hillside frame: hillside means slopes of 20 percent or more, and the OCP's Schedule D lists D-2 Hillside Environment and D-5 Hillside Development permit areas alongside D-3 Riparian Area. https://chilliwack.com/ocp
- Well and septic servicing on rural parcels is a per-parcel check: no City or health-authority page read in this pass states a servicing standard for a specific Chilliwack acreage, so do not assume municipal water or sewer reaches an upland or ALR parcel. https://www.chilliwack.com/main/page.cfm?id=653
