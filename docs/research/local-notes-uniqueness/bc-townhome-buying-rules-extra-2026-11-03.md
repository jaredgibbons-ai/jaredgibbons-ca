# BC Townhome Buying Rules — Extra Local Notes
Page: bc-townhome-buying-rules (British Columbia; topic — townhome buying rules).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- The boundary that matters on this page is which level of government sets which rule. The Strata Property Act, the Home Buyer Rescission Period and Property Transfer Tax (PTT) are provincial; Goods and Services Tax (GST) on new homes is federal; secondary-suite and parking rules are municipal; and pet, smoking and alteration rules for a specific complex are set by that strata corporation's own bylaws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/understanding-stratas/kinds-of-stratas
- A townhouse is usually a strata lot: the Province states strata housing includes townhouses, and that you cannot tell by looking whether a townhouse is strata-titled or a non-strata rowhouse — the legal test is whether a strata plan was filed at the Land Title Office. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/understanding-stratas/kinds-of-stratas
- Where a rule below is municipal, it differs by city; where it is provincial or federal, it applies the same way in every BC municipality. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- Three townhome forms meet different rules. A standard (building) strata townhome is shown on the strata plan, so the strata corporation insures the building; a bare-land strata townhome lot is defined by survey markers, not by the house's walls, and the house on it is not shown on the plan. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol31/consol31/98043_01
- A freehold rowhouse is not strata at all: there is no strata corporation, no strata fees and no Form B (Information Certificate), and the provincial strata rules in this note do not apply to it. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/understanding-stratas/kinds-of-stratas
- In a bare-land strata (called a "strata subdivision"), the strata plan is filed before homes are built, the lot is essentially a plot of land, and common property can include roads, streetlights, septic systems and utility lines running under individual lots. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/understanding-stratas/kinds-of-stratas

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- Strata basics: a strata corporation is created when a strata plan is filed at the Land Title Office; owners own their strata lot individually and own the common property together as the strata corporation. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/understanding-stratas/kinds-of-stratas
- Rental restrictions are gone: as of November 24, 2022, no strata corporation in British Columbia may have a residential rental-restriction bylaw, and all such bylaws are invalid — although short-term rental bylaws are still allowed. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- Age restrictions are narrowed the same way: since November 24, 2022 a strata may only restrict age to 55 years or older, with exemptions added May 1, 2023 for caregivers, previous residents, children (including adult children) and younger spouses or partners. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- Depreciation reports are now mandatory on a cycle: every strata corporation with five or more strata lots, including bare-land stratas, must obtain one every five years, and owners may no longer defer it by an annual three-quarter vote. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- Insurance is mandatory for every strata corporation, including bare-land developments with townhouses: the Strata Property Act requires property and liability insurance, and the Province advises owners to carry their own policy for contents, upgrades and liability because the strata policy is not homeowner insurance. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/finances-and-insurance/strata-corporation-insurance
- Municipal suite rules sit on top of strata rules: for example, the Township of Langley zoning bylaw definition allows one secondary suite per principal dwelling unit, including in a townhouse, capped at 120 square metres — the city you buy in sets its own figure, so check that city's bylaw, not a provincial page. https://www.tol.ca/en/services/resources/bylaw-services/bylaws/Zoning-Bylaws/Section-100-Administration.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** Do I pay GST on a townhome? **A:** Only if it is new or substantially renovated. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) taxes new homes bought from a builder and rebates part of the GST through the GST/HST New Housing Rebate; a resale townhome carries no GST. Eligible first-time buyers can now recover 100 percent of the GST on a new home valued at or under $1 million, reducing to zero at $1.5 million. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/gst-hst-rebates/first-time-home-buyers-gst-hst-rebate.html
- **Q:** Can I change my mind after my offer is accepted? **A:** Yes, for three business days. British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) explains the Home Buyer Rescission Period under the Property Law Act: in force January 3, 2023, it lets a buyer rescind a contract for residential real property, including a residential strata lot, up to three business days after acceptance, on payment of 0.25 percent of the price to the seller, and it cannot be waived. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/home-buyer-rescission-period-information
- **Q:** What is a Form B and how fast must the strata produce it? **A:** The Form B (Information Certificate) is the strata corporation's disclosure about the specific lot and corporation, and a buyer may request one. The Province states the strata must provide it within 7 days of a request, may charge at most $35 plus up to 25 cents per page, and since 2023 Form Bs must also include summaries of the strata's insurance and electrical planning reports. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- **Q:** Does a new townhome avoid Property Transfer Tax? **A:** It can. The Province's newly built home exemption fully eliminates PTT on a qualifying principal-residence purchase at a fair market value of $1,100,000 or less for registrations on or after April 1, 2024, and phases out completely at $1,150,000. Foreign entities and taxable trustees are not eligible, and the buyer claims it on the PTT return using exemption code 49. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption
- **Q:** My bare-land strata townhome looks like a normal house — what is different? **A:** The land, not the house, is the strata lot. Under the Strata Property Act a bare-land strata plan defines lot boundaries on a horizontal plane by survey markers, not by floors, walls or ceilings, so the plan does not show your house. The strata corporation still exists, still charges fees, and is responsible for common-property infrastructure such as roads and utility lines. https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/consol31/consol31/98043_01

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- A depreciation report projects 30 years ahead: the Province states it inventories the strata's common property and assets and projects maintenance, repair and replacement costs over a 30-year span, including roads and amenities in bare-land stratas. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- Timing trap for older complexes: a strata corporation with no depreciation report, or one received before December 31, 2020, in Metro Vancouver or the Fraser Valley had to obtain a new report by July 1, 2026 under the strengthened July 1, 2024 regulations — check current. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- From July 1, 2027, owner developers must contribute toward a new strata's first depreciation report: a minimum of $5,000 plus $200 per strata lot, to a maximum of $30,000. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- Earthquake coverage is optional, not mandatory: the Province states strata corporations are not required to buy earthquake insurance because earthquake is not a listed peril in the regulation, but advises corporations in earthquake-prone areas to consider it, noting earthquake deductibles are typically a percentage of full replacement value. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/finances-and-insurance/strata-corporation-insurance
- Old bylaw packages still print dead rules: a rental cap or a 19-plus age bylaw in a townhome complex's documents is invalid under the November 24, 2022 changes even if the strata never reprinted its bylaws. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/legislation-and-changes/changes-to-legislation?keyword=strata&keyword=rentals
- The long-standing GST/HST New Housing Rebate is small at BC prices: the CRA guide states it is generally 36 percent of the GST paid, to a maximum of $6,300, reducing between $350,000 and $450,000 of value and unavailable at $450,000 or more — separate from the first-time buyer rebate. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4028/gst-hst-new-housing-rebate.html

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- Property Transfer Tax general rates are charged on fair market value at registration: 1 percent on the first $200,000, 2 percent on the portion to $2,000,000 and 3 percent above that, with a further 2 percent on the residential portion above $3,000,000 — check current on the Province's calculator before closing. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption/exemption-amounts
- First-time buyers have a separate PTT program: a qualifying buyer can save up to $8,000, with the tax eliminated on the first $500,000 on homes up to $835,000 — a different exemption from the newly built home exemption, and the two are claimed through the PTT return. https://www2.gov.BC.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/more-homes-for-people
- GST on a new townhome is the 5 percent federal tax, subject to the CRA rebates in section 4; the CRA housing rebate guide (RC4028) is the federal source for how that tax and its rebates apply to a house bought from a builder. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4028/gst-hst-new-housing-rebate.html
- The rescission fee is a closing cost if you use it: 0.25 percent of the purchase price, paid to the seller, under the Home Buyer Rescission Period. https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/home-buyer-rescission-period-information
- The Form B fee is capped provincially at $35 plus up to 25 cents per page for copying. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate

## 8. GETTING AROUND
- This is a topic page, not a place: the "getting around" step is getting the right document from the right office. Title and the strata plan itself come from the Land Title and Survey Authority of British Columbia (LTSA), because a strata corporation exists only once its plan is filed at the Land Title Office. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/understanding-stratas/kinds-of-stratas
- Strata disclosure (Form B, budget, rules, depreciation report) comes from the strata corporation, which must answer a Form B request within 7 days. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- Tax questions split by level: PTT exemptions are claimed on the provincial Property Transfer Tax return at registration, while GST rebates are claimed from the Canada Revenue Agency or credited by the builder. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/property-taxes/property-transfer-tax/exemptions/newly-built-home-exemption

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Read the depreciation report before subject removal: it is the document that shows what common property the strata must repair and what the projected costs are over 30 years, and stratas with four or fewer lots are exempt from obtaining one — a gap to notice on a small townhome complex. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements
- Check the strata's insurance summary on the Form B: since 2023 the Form B must summarize strata corporation insurance, and the deductible the owners could face is part of that disclosure. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate
- Confirm the plan type on the strata plan itself: if the words "bare land strata" do not appear and houses are shown on the plan, it is a building strata, and the strata corporation's insurance and repair duties cover the buildings shown. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/understanding-stratas/kinds-of-stratas
- Municipal rules can block a suite the strata allows, and strata bylaws can block uses the city allows; a townhome buyer needs both answers, from the city's zoning bylaw and the strata's filed bylaws, before counting on rental or suite income. https://www.tol.ca/en/services/resources/bylaw-services/bylaws/Zoning-Bylaws/Section-100-Administration.pdf

## NOT CONFIRMED
- Whether a specific townhome complex allows pets, and in what number or size: that is set by each strata corporation's own bylaws and no provincial page states it — read the filed bylaws for the complex.
- A single provincial figure for strata insurance deductible amounts: deductibles are set policy by policy; only the duty to insure and to summarize it on the Form B are stated provincially.
- Municipal secondary-suite rules for every BC city a townhome buyer might meet: only the Township of Langley example (120 square metres, one per principal unit) was verified in this pass; other cities' figures are UNCONFIRMED here.
- Short-term rental rules: strata short-term bylaws remain allowed provincially, but municipal short-term rental licensing and principal-residence rules vary by city and were not verified city by city in this pass.
- The 2-5-10 home warranty requirements for new townhomes (BC Housing source not read in this pass).
