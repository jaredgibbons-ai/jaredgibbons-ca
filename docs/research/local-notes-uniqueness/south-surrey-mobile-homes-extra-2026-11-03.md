# South Surrey Mobile Homes — Extra Local Notes
Page: south-surrey-mobile-homes (South Surrey, Surrey; mobile homes).

## 1. STREETS AND BOUNDARIES
- South Surrey's manufactured-home communities all sit in the King George Corridor, in the blocks around King George Boulevard, 160 Street, 20 Avenue, and Cranley Drive, between 8 Avenue and the Nicomekl River with Highway 99 on the east side. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2025-R237.pdf
- The corridor's flood setting is defined by two rivers: the Serpentine and Nicomekl River Sea Dams limit ocean tides from backwatering up the two rivers and protect about 51 square kilometres of lowland, including Highways 10, 15, and 99. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2025-R237.pdf
- Surrey's manufactured-home zoning for the corridor is the Manufactured Home Residential Zone (RM-M) under Surrey Zoning Bylaw No. 12000, Part 19, which is the zone the City applies to manufactured home parks. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7916-0081-00.pdf

## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- South Surrey is unusual because three different tenure forms sit within a few blocks of each other: a rented pad, a bare-land strata lot you own, and an own-land community whose title form is unverified. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
- Breakaway Bays, 1840 160 Street, is a pad-rental community where the buyer owns the home and rents the pad; the operator Parkbridge's own page lists the property address as 1840 160th Street, Surrey, British Columbia V4A 4X4, calls it a Residential Retirement Community, and lists an outdoor heated pool open May to September. https://parkbridge.com/en/residential/detail/breakaway-bays
- Sea Ridge Bays, 15875 20 Avenue, is a bare-land strata: the buyer owns the home and the strata lot, pays a strata fee, and pays no pad rent. A City of Surrey planning report confirms 15875-20 Avenue is one large lot on the north side of 20 Avenue between King George Boulevard and Highway 99, with underlying RM-M zoning and Land Use Contract No. 283 dating from 1977. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7915-0403-00.pdf
- The Cranley Drive community, with homes addressed on Cranley Drive off 24 Avenue, is described in the earlier local-notes program as own-land, but its exact title form and community name were not verified on an allowed source in this pass and are listed under NOT CONFIRMED below. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- On a rented pad in Surrey, the buyer owns the home and pays pad rent under the provincial Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act (MHPTA), and the City's own policy brochure explains that split in plain language — see the surrey-mobile-homes note for the full framework. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
- Surrey's Policy O-34A, the Manufactured Home Park Redevelopment and Strata Conversion Policy as revised July 27, 2015, requires prenotification to all park occupants once a redevelopment file is assigned, and an applicant letter to occupants at least 10 days before Council considers the application. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2015-R167.pdf
- Surrey's Tenant Protection Bylaw No. 21788 (2025) does not apply to property governed by the MHPTA, so park residents rely on Policy O-34A and the provincial act instead of the bylaw that covers ordinary rental buildings. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-development-application-process/submitting-a-development-application/rental-housing-redevelopment
- The RM-M zone sets the physical form of a park: a minimum manufactured home space of 225 square metres (2,420 square feet) with a minimum width of 12 metres, a maximum density of 22 dwelling units per hectare, and permitted uses limited to a manufactured home park plus one single-family dwelling for the park manager. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7916-0081-00.pdf

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** What is the real difference between Breakaway Bays and Sea Ridge Bays? **A:** The difference is what you own. At Breakaway Bays, 1840 160 Street, you own the manufactured home only and rent the pad under the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act. At Sea Ridge Bays, 15875 20 Avenue, you own the home and the bare-land strata lot under it, so there is no pad rent and a strata fee applies instead, and strata rules replace park tenancy rules. https://parkbridge.com/en/residential/detail/breakaway-bays https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7915-0403-00.pdf
- **Q:** If a South Surrey park is redeveloped, what notice and compensation do I get? **A:** The park owner may only require you to leave after every permit and approval is in place. The City's policy brochure states the owner must then give 12 months' notice and pay 20,000 dollars in compensation on or before the notice date, and a tenant who finds somewhere else sooner may end the tenancy early with 10 days' written notice. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
- **Q:** Is Breakaway Bays a 55-plus park? **A:** That is not settled, so check the current park rules before you buy. Parkbridge's own page calls Breakaway Bays a Residential Retirement Community, but sources gathered in the earlier local-notes program conflicted — a directory described it as 55-plus while listing text called it a family park with no age restriction — and no source read in this pass states an enforceable age rule. https://parkbridge.com/en/residential/detail/breakaway-bays
- **Q:** How much can pad rent rise at a rented-pad park here? **A:** Only by the provincial percentage, the same as everywhere in British Columbia. For 2026 the limit for a manufactured home site is 2.3 percent plus the proportionate increase in local government levies and regulated utility fees charged on the park's common property, with three full months' notice and no more than one increase in a 12-month period. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/rent-rtb/rent-increases
- **Q:** Does Surrey's Tenant Protection Bylaw protect me in a manufactured home park? **A:** No, and that surprises many buyers. The City states that Tenant Protection Bylaw No. 21788 does not apply to property governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, so a park household's protection on redevelopment comes from the provincial act and from City Policy O-34A, including its occupant prenotification steps, rather than from the bylaw. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-development-application-process/submitting-a-development-application/rental-housing-redevelopment

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- Surrey publishes a plain-language manufactured home park policy brochure that walks through pad rent, the 12-month notice rule, and the compensation rule, which most buyers never see before they start shopping. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/ManufacturedHomeParkPolicy.pdf
- Policy O-34A dates from a July 27, 2015 revision recorded in Corporate Report R167, so Surrey's park redevelopment process is a council policy, not a bylaw, and it can be changed by Council resolution. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2015-R167.pdf
- Sea Ridge Bays still carried Land Use Contract No. 283 from 1977 over its RM-M zoning when the City reported on the property, a layering of an old contract over the current zone that a buyer should have a lawyer explain. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7915-0403-00.pdf
- Replacement of the Nicomekl Sea Dam, one of the two dams holding back tidal flooding near the corridor, is on hold pending direction from the Province, according to a City corporate report dated November 3, 2025. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2025-R237.pdf
- Assigning a pad tenancy to a buyer needs the park owner's prior written consent on Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB) Form 10, and if the owner does not respond by the 10th day, consent is deemed given. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
- The two Surrey sea dams are more than 100 years old and protect about 51 square kilometres that include the Highway 99 corridor east of the parks. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2025-R237.pdf

## 7. COSTS AND TAXES HERE
- The headline residential rate for Surrey is carried in the earliest sibling note in this set; the City's 2026 rate page gives a Municipal Total, including the Roads and Traffic levy, of 1.84251 dollars per $1,000 of assessed value for Class 1 Residential, and a General Municipal Total of 1.68947 dollars per $1,000. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/property-taxes/property-tax-rates
- For 2026, Surrey's annual flat utility rates for a single-family dwelling are 1,195 dollars for water and 1,661 dollars for sewer, a combined 2,856 dollars, while metered accounts are billed every four months. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/utility-billing-services/understanding-your-utility-bill
- At a rented-pad park, ongoing cost follows the provincial percentage rule only: the 2026 pad-rent limit is 2.3 percent plus the proportionate levy and regulated-utility pass-through, and bare-land strata owners at Sea Ridge Bays pay a strata fee instead of pad rent. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/rent-rtb/rent-increases

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Flood due diligence here is about the river system, not just the lot: the Serpentine and Nicomekl sea dams hold back tidal water for about 51 square kilometres of lowland, and the Nicomekl dam's replacement is paused awaiting provincial direction, so a buyer should ask how the specific community is protected. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/CR_2025-R237.pdf
- Redevelopment risk is procedural: Policy O-34A's prenotification to all occupants happens once a file is assigned, which is the earliest formal signal that a park's future is in play. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/corporate-reports/RPT_2015-R167.pdf
- Tenure confusion is the local trap: two communities a few blocks apart run on opposite rules — pad tenancy at Breakaway Bays, bare-land strata at Sea Ridge Bays — so the form of ownership must be confirmed from title and strata or park documents, not from how a home is marketed. https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/planning-reports/PLR_7915-0403-00.pdf
- At a strata community, the buyer should read the strata plan and bylaws; at a pad-rental park, the park rules form part of the tenancy agreement, and the RTB Form 10 consent step must be built into the purchase timeline. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb10.pdf
