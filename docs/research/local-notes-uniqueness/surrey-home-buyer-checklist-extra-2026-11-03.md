## 2. THE HOUSING STOCK
- The Surrey-specific stock question is the suite, not the house form: Surrey treats any self-contained unit with cooking facilities and a bathroom as a secondary suite that must be registered, whether or not it is rented out. http://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/secondary-suites
- Surrey's suite test turns on the kitchen and bathroom alone: the City states the bylaw does not address who occupies a suite or whether it is occupied at all, and that the existence of the cooking equipment and bathroom are the only considerations in deciding whether a secondary suite exists. http://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/secondary-suites

## 3. RULES THAT AFFECT BUYERS HERE
- All Surrey secondary suites must be registered with the City: the City requires the owner to complete a Secondary Suite Declaration Form and submit it to Property & Payment Services, and a $1,000 fee is billed to the property for each unregistered suite the City becomes aware of. http://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/secondary-suites
- A buyer can check a suite's registration before offering: the City provides a Secondary Suite Fee Web Inquiry tool to check whether a suite at an address is registered. http://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/secondary-suites
- Trees are protected by a named bylaw with money held back: Surrey Tree Protection By-law No. 16100 applies, and on a building site the City requires tree protection securities of $3,000 per tree, up to $15,000 per single-family lot, paid before the building permit is issued. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/trees-yard-garden/trees-landscape-building-site-requirements
- Cutting a protected tree costs a permit fee plus replacement security: the City sets a tree cutting permit fee of $108 plus $42 for every tree removed, and replacement tree securities of $550 per replacement tree. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/trees-yard-garden/trees-landscape-building-site-requirements
- Moving soil needs the City's soil rules, not just a truck: Surrey's regulatory bylaw list includes the Soil Removal & Deposition rules in By-law No. 16389, the Surrey Soil Conservation and Protection By-law, 2007, and a separate Erosion and Sediment Control By-law No. 21181. https://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/regulatory-bylaws

## 4. WHAT BUYERS ASK
- **Q:** How do I know if the suite in a Surrey house is legal? **A:** Check the City's registry, because every Surrey secondary suite must be registered. The City's Secondary Suite Fee Web Inquiry tool shows whether a suite is registered; registration is done by filing a Secondary Suite Declaration Form with Property & Payment Services, and the City bills a $1,000 fee to the property for each unregistered suite it becomes aware of. http://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/secondary-suites
- **Q:** Can I remove the big trees and rebuild? **A:** Only under the tree bylaw's process and securities. Surrey's Tree Protection By-law No. 16100 governs the site: the City requires tree protection securities of $3,000 per tree up to $15,000 per single-family lot, a tree cutting permit at $108 plus $42 per tree removed, replacement tree securities of $550 per replacement tree, and arborist supervision for work inside a protected tree's zone. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/trees-yard-garden/trees-landscape-building-site-requirements
- **Q:** Is this Surrey lot in a floodplain? **A:** Check the lowlands first, because about 20% of Surrey is coastal floodplain. The City maps the floodplain from Boundary Bay and Mud Bay along the Nicomekl and Serpentine Rivers toward Cloverdale and Newton; most of it is protected by dykes, with sea dams on both rivers, so a lowland lot's dyke and drainage position is a checklist item, not a detail. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/surrey-coastal-floodplain
- **Q:** What permit steps should I expect after I buy and renovate? **A:** Expect the building permit first and the trade permits second. Surrey states a residential building permit is usually required for building or renovating work such as building a house, and that separate plumbing and electrical permits may be applied for only after the building permit has been issued; before anyone moves into a new structure, the owner must book and pass a final inspection. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/residential-building/demolish-rebuild-house-surrey

## 5. LOCAL DETAILS A BUYER WOULD NOT KNOW
- A registered Surrey suite carries two annual charges: the Secondary Suite Service Fee is $893 per suite, and garbage, recycling and organics for one suite is $172.04, set by the Secondary Suite Service Fee Bylaw No. 17356 and the waste bylaw. http://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/secondary-suites
- Removing a suite has a City inspection step: the City states that once a suite is removed, the owner can request an inspection with Bylaws by calling 604-591-4516 and pressing Option 2, after removing stoves and other cooking equipment and completing any permitted electrical work. http://www.surrey.ca/city-government/bylaws/secondary-suites
- Drawings need a confirmed civic address before Surrey will review them: the City states the correct civic address must be established by an Addressing Clerk and shown on each drawing sheet, and that drawings will not be reviewed with an incorrect or missing address. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/residential-building/residential-building-permits
- Permits and inspections run through one portal: the City's permitting portal, tied to a MySurrey account, is used to apply for permits, pay fees, track a project and request building inspections. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/permitting-portal-guide
Read: 2026-11-03

## 9. RISKS AND DUE DILIGENCE
- Check the Serpentine and Nicomekl lowlands lot by lot: the City states the two river catchments cover 334 km², of which 59 km² is lowland floodplain, much of it very close to sea level because it was historically an intertidal zone. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands
- The rivers are gated, not free-flowing, at the bay: the City states both rivers are controlled by sea dams that open at low tide to let fresh water flow to Mud Bay and close at high tide to stop salt water moving into farm land. https://www.surrey.ca/services-payments/water-drainage-sewer/flood-control/lowlands-floodplains/serpentine-nicomekl-lowlands
- Check for unpermitted work before relying on a suite or addition: the City's permit pages state an owner must hold the required permit before construction, and its residential permit page is the checklist for which projects need one. https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/building/residential-building/residential-building-permits
