In early 2024, residents across South Surrey and White Rock pushed back against a proposed biofuel plant near the Douglas border crossing. Here's what was proposed, what changed, and what it would take for it to go ahead.

## What was proposed

- **The project:** an anaerobic digestion plant to turn organic waste into renewable natural gas, on Semiahmoo First Nation land near the Peace Arch border crossing.
- **Who:** a joint venture between the Semiahmoo First Nation and Taurus RNG, a Vancouver company. It began as a partnership with Andion Global.
- **The concerns:** the Clean Air Alliance and nearby residents raised air quality, odour and truck traffic, and urged people with breathing problems living within 10 kilometres to speak up.

## Federal funding pulled

On February 29, 2024, Natural Resources Canada withdrew the **$14.4 million** it had announced from its Clean Fuels Fund in June 2023, saying it would no longer enable the project ([CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/1.7132337)). Chief Harley Chappell said the Nation was taken aback and would seek answers from Ottawa. The Canada Infrastructure Bank and Indigenous Services Canada stayed involved at the time.

## What it would take to go ahead

The plant still needed a federal impact assessment and an air quality permit before construction, and neither had been granted when the funding was withdrawn. If it comes back, there will be public comment periods; the earlier one drew wide input from South Surrey and White Rock residents.
