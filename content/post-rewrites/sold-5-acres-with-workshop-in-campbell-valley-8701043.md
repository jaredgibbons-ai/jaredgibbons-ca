In May 2025 I sold a 5-acre property with a workshop in Campbell Valley, South Langley: a 4 bedroom, 3 bathroom split-level at the end of a quiet cul-de-sac, with a brand-new 3,600 sq ft shop, a 2017 roof, two driveways and flat, usable land with no creeks. Here's what buyers looked for, and what to check if you're shopping for an acreage there.

## What buyers paid for

- **A big shop:** a large, newer workshop drew trades, hobbyists and horse owners. Buildings like that are expensive to permit and build, so buyers value them.
- **Usable land:** flat ground with no creeks means fewer setbacks and more room for paddocks, gardens or parking.
- **Access:** two driveways made trucks and trailers easy.
- **Privacy, close to town:** Campbell Valley feels rural but is minutes from Aldergrove, Brookswood and Highway 1.

## Zoning in Campbell Valley

Most acreages here are in the Township of Langley's rural zones. Under the Township's zoning bylaw:

- **RU-1 Rural:** agriculture, equestrian uses and residential use, with up to two single family dwellings or mobile homes per lot, subject to the Agricultural Land Commission Act. The minimum new lot is 1.7 ha (about 4.2 acres).
- **RU-2 and RU-3:** larger minimum lots of 8 ha (about 19.8 acres).
- **ALR:** if the lot is in the Agricultural Land Reserve, the Commission's rules on homes, fill and subdivision also apply.

Check any address in my [zoning guide](/zonings.html?m=zd-langley), which covers the Township's farm and rural zones.

## Before you buy an acreage

- **Well and septic:** a flow test and water test for the well, and an inspection of the septic system.
- **Shop permits:** confirm the shop and any suite were built with permits.
- **Watercourses:** a creek on or near the lot can bring setbacks; this property had none, which was a big plus.
- **Trees and fill:** the Township regulates tree cutting and soil deposits, so ask before planning big changes.
