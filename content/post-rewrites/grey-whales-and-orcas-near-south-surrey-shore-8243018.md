In May 2024, over the Mother's Day weekend, a local boater photographed two grey whales close to shore off Crescent Beach, with orcas nearby. It wasn't a one-off. Whales turn up in Semiahmoo Bay and Boundary Bay most years, and seeing one from the beach is one of the things people love about living in South Surrey and White Rock.

## Which whales you might see

- **Grey whales** migrate up the coast each spring, from their winter calving lagoons off Baja California and Mexico to feeding grounds in the Bering Sea, and a few stop to feed in the shallow bays. Spring is the best time to look.
- **Orcas (killer whales)** come into Boundary Bay and Semiahmoo Bay too. Most seen here are Bigg's (transient) killer whales, which hunt seals and sea lions.
- **Humpback whales** have come back to the Salish Sea in growing numbers and are seen farther out in the Strait of Georgia.

## Where to watch from shore

- **White Rock Pier and promenade:** the 470-metre pier gets you well out over the water, and the promenade runs about 2.19 km from East Beach to West Beach.
- **Crescent Beach:** look west and south across Boundary Bay from the beach and Blackie Spit.
- **Ocean Park bluff:** the higher viewpoints, such as Kwomais Point Park, look over Boundary Bay.

Bring binoculars, look for a blow first, and check local sighting groups on social media; sightings get posted within minutes.

## The rules if you're on the water

- **All killer whales:** in southern BC coastal waters, boats must stay at least **400 metres** away from all killer whales year-round and must not get in their path ([Government of Canada, 2025 measures](https://www.canada.ca/en/transport-canada/news/2025/06/the-government-of-canada-outlines-2025-measures-to-protect-southern-resident-killer-whales.html)). Fisheries and Oceans Canada has proposed raising that to 1,000 metres for Southern Resident killer whales.
- **Other whales:** stay well back, slow down, and never chase or circle. In June 2025 a BC jet skier was fined $5,000 for deliberately approaching marine mammals too closely ([Fisheries and Oceans Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/fisheries-oceans/news/2025/06/bc-jet-skier-fined-5000-for-intentionally-approaching-marine-mammals-too-closely.html)).
- **If you see a whale hit, tangled or harassed:** report it to Fisheries and Oceans Canada.
